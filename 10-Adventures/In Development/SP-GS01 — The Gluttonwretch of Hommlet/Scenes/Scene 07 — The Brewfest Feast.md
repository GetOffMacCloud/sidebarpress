# Scene 07 — The Brewfest Feast

[[10-Adventures/In Development/SP-GS01 — The Gluttonwretch of Hommlet/Scenes/00 — Scene Index|00 — Scene Index]] · ← [[Scene 06 — The Hunt on Emridy Meadows]] · Next → [[Scene 08 — The Last Toast]]

## Purpose
The climax. The village sits down to the Brewfest feast, names its thanks, and the Wretch walks in through the Open Door. All three ways of ending it stay live in the same fight: the First Fruits, the Wishbone, and the Carving.
**Budget:** 70 minutes. Running long? If phase 2 triggers with under 50 minutes left, skip Second Helpings and let the Wretch fall.

## Climax at a Glance
| Solution | What it takes | Seeded in | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The First Fruits** | 6 Offerings delivered to the Wretch (at most 3 from PCs); Jaroo's rite | Scenes 02, 03, 04 | Time in the aura; PCs who stand in pay permanently |
| **The Wishbone** | Two creatures pull the bone apart | Scenes 03, 05 | Old Gobbler's spirit destroyed; the grove's blessing lost |
| **The Carving** | Bring it to 0 hit points, then do it again | Scene 06 (carried damage) | The hardest fight; the village's debt unpaid |

## Part A — The Naming of Thanks
> *Long tables run the length of the green under two hundred candles. Every bench is full: farmers in clean shirts, children with their hair wet-combed, Pudding the goat under the head table. The Welcome Wench's door stands wide open at the end of the green, spilling yellow light onto the grass. Baskets of first fruits wait at the households' feet. Canon Terjon stands and raises his cup.*

Hommlet's custom: before anyone eats, each household stands and names one thing it is thankful for. **[[Canon Terjon]]** turns to the guests of honor first.
> "Our heroes first. Not your victories, mind. Something you're grateful for. Saint Cuthbert prefers plain speech."

Go around the table and let each player answer in character. **A sincere answer earns inspiration.** On stream, invite chat to type theirs. Then the villagers:
- **[[Pip Haddle]]** (if cured): "I'm thankful I'm not hungry any more."
- **[[Widow Marta Bree]]:** "For forty Brewfests with that ridiculous bird."
- **[[Ostler Gundigoot]]:** "For a full house. Nobody's cancelling Brewfest."
- **[[The Elder of Hommlet]]**, last and stumbling (comedy-free): "I'm thankful… that we still have the chance to set it right."

That is the cue. Every candle flame leans toward the open door.

## Part B — The Guest Arrives
> *Something fills the Welcome Wench's doorway from the inside, though nothing went in. It ducks under the lintel and comes out onto the green: slate-grey feathers, wattles dripping gravy-dark ichor, eyes like coals in a banked oven. It is taller than the inn. It looks down the length of the feast, and opens its beak.*

The **Gluttonwretch** speaks in Sylvan, a wet gobbling rasp: *"Gwolloch… ne'feddh… gwolloch."* Anyone who understands Sylvan hears: ***"You did not feed me."***

Roll initiative. The Wretch starts with any damage carried over from [[Scene 06 — The Hunt on Emridy Meadows]].

**Who else is here.**
- **Hollow Guests:** **Hob Tanner** (1), missing since Scene 02, rises from the bench where he was sitting a moment ago (a Gorger). If the traders' "preserves" reached the green, a **Flinger** (1) climbs out of the barrels as well.
- **[[Deacon Mungo Brindlewick]]**, if he is still free, stands up from the crowd with the Wishbone and starts saying grace.
- **The Offering-Heap**, if calmed in Scene 04, waits at the edge of the green.
- **The village:** two hundred people, most of them frozen. Those holding baskets look at the party.

## Part C — The Three Ways

### 1. The First Fruits
An **Offering** is delivered by bringing first fruits from a won household to the Wretch's beak: **an action within 15 feet**, inside the aura. **A creature carrying an Offering is ignored by the Aura of Endless Hunger;** the Wretch wants what it carries, not the one carrying it. A villager can carry their own basket if a PC escorts them (the PC moves with them and takes the Dodge or Help action); otherwise a PC carries it.
- **Each Offering** costs the Wretch **1 legendary action per round**.
- **At 3 Offerings** it **loses all its damage resistances**, and it will not rise in Second Helpings.
- **At 6 Offerings** it stops fighting, eats, and lies down. **[[Jaroo Ashstaff]]**'s rite (1 round, uninterrupted, within 30 feet) lays Old Gobbler to rest. Ending A.
- **Old Gobbler's last feather** (Scene 04), placed by Jaroo, counts as one Offering.
- **Winning households mid-feast:** a PC can use an action and a DC 18 Charisma (Persuasion) check, or a good story, to win a household not yet won, right there at the table.
- **Making up the shortfall:** a PC can stand in for one household by freely giving something that costs them: a magic item, a permanent reduction of 1d10 to their hit point maximum, or a true memory spoken aloud. **No more than 3 Offerings can come from PCs.**
- **The Offering-Heap:** if calmed in Scene 04 and given one Offering (which then does not count toward the six), it fights beside the party for 3 rounds, Slamming Hollow Guests.

> [!note] DM Note: explaining the shortfall to the players
> Say it in Jaroo's voice first, then plainly out of character.
> - **Jaroo:** "The Old Faith counts what is given, not what is spared. If the village can't fill his bowl, fill it with something it hurts you to lose."
> - **Out of character:** "If the village comes up short, any of you can stand in for a household. Use your action within 15 feet of the Wretch, name what you're giving up, and tell us why it matters. That counts as one Offering."
>
> Spell out the costs before anyone commits, because all three are permanent:
> - **A magic item:** it's gone. The Wretch eats it.
> - **Vitality:** your hit point maximum drops by 1d10, and only a *wish* restores it.
> - **A true memory:** speak a real memory from your backstory aloud, and your character forgets it for good. The player chooses which.
>
> Explain the cap: at most 3 Offerings can come from the party. The other 3 must come from Hommlet, because the point is the village paying its own debt. If a player just says "I give my +1 dagger," ask "Why does it matter to you?" One sentence is enough. Only refuse an obvious throwaway.

### 2. The Wishbone
Two creatures, each holding one end and using an action, make a **contested Strength check**.
- **Snap:** the Wretch loses Legendary Resistance, Incorporeal Movement and its necrotic immunity, and its current hit points are halved. It will not rise in Second Helpings. Whoever wins the contest keeps the big half and gains a single ***wish***, limited to duplicating a spell of 7th level or lower (see [[Gluttonwretch]], Spoils).
- **The cost:** Old Gobbler's spirit is destroyed, not laid to rest. The First Fruits can no longer end the fight (there is no one left to feed). Obad-Hai's blessing leaves the grove, Hommlet's 577 harvest fails, and Jaroo never forgives it. Ending B.
- **If Brindlewick holds the Wishbone,** he has to be pried off it first (a grapple, or dropping him). He will snap it himself only if he is about to be captured: "If she can't have the village, she'll have the bird."

### 3. The Carving
Bring the Wretch to 0 hit points. **Unless 3 or more Offerings were made or the Wishbone was snapped, it splits its own skin and rises as the Gluttonwretch, Second Helpings** (a separate statblock and Fantasy Grounds record; see [[SP-GS01 — Bestiary]]), at its own initiative, legendary actions reset. Bring that to 0 too. Ending B.

## Lair Actions
On initiative count 20 (losing ties), the Wretch takes one lair action. It can't use the same one two rounds running.
1. **Gravy Flood.** A brown tidal wave sweeps down the green with a single crouton riding the crest. A 20-foot radius within 120 feet becomes difficult terrain; each creature there makes a DC 16 Dexterity saving throw or is restrained until the end of its next turn.
2. **The Table Turns.** Plates, carving knives and candlesticks whirl; the carving knife has clearly been waiting years for this. Each creature on or beside the feast table makes a DC 16 Dexterity saving throw, taking 14 (4d6) slashing damage on a failure, or half as much on a success.
3. **Second Hunger.** Every stomach in range growls at once, in harmony. Each creature that has eaten at the feast makes a DC 16 Wisdom saving throw. On a failure it takes 7 (2d6) necrotic damage and loses its reaction until its next turn.

## Pacing at a Seven-Player Table
A round with seven players, the Wretch, its legendary actions and a lair action takes about 20–25 minutes at a stream table. The 70-minute budget is about three rounds. Keep the Naming of Thanks under 10 minutes, use a turn timer if your table likes one, and treat Second Helpings as optional: run it only if you are ahead of the clock.

## The Clock
- **After round 3,** if no Offering has yet been made, one more household gorges at the end of each round. Cross it off the households; its members join the fight as one more Gorger (700 XP each).
- **Canon Terjon** casts *spirit guardians* once (his safety valve). Use it the first time two or more PCs drop.
- **Jaroo's last gift** (safety valve): if the party is losing badly, Jaroo can complete the rite with only **four** Offerings by giving his own life. Ending B: the bird rests, the druid dies.

Lair actions and Second Helpings come on top of every row. At APL 13, add one Flinger to each row.
**Terrain:** long tables (half cover; standing on one counts for The Table Turns), benches (difficult terrain), the open door, two hundred panicking villagers (difficult terrain anywhere on the green until round 3, when most have fled).
**Tactics:** the Wretch opens with Gobble of Gluttony to drag PCs into its aura and stun them, then Pecks the stunned and uses Feast on Fear on anyone frightened by a Gorger's Dreadful Glare. It goes after anyone carrying a basket. Brindlewick stays in the crowd casting.
**DM direction:** keep pointing at the baskets. The players should always know the First Fruits are on the table.

## Endings
Read the one that fits, then go to [[Scene 08 — The Last Toast]].

**A — The First Fruits.**
> *The Gluttonwretch lowers its head to a bowl of cranberries picked from the grove bog that morning. It eats. The rot runs out of its feathers like mist off a pond. For a moment it is only a turkey, enormous and ridiculous and holy. Then it is gone, and Pudding is eating the rest of the cranberries.*

**B — The Carving or the Wishbone.**
> *The Gluttonwretch falls across the head table and does not get up. Its feathers slough away into black rot that soaks into the grass. Where it fell, the candles go out one by one down the length of the green. Nobody moves for a long time.*

**C — The Feast Lost.** The Wretch eats until the green is empty, then walks back out through the Open Door and is gone. Run Scene 08 for the survivors, without the toast.

## Clues Available Here
- The Wretch eating an Offering and the rot leaving its feathers → **the First Fruits work** (last resort for Fact 2).
- Brindlewick with the Wishbone → **the Temple's binding** (last resort for Fact 3).

## Sensory Palette
- **Sight:** two hundred candles leaning one way; a turkey taller than the inn ducking under the lintel; baskets of apples rolling across the grass.
- **Sound:** a fiddle stopping mid-note; benches going over; Gundigoot shouting "Second barrel's tapped! Nobody leaves!"
- **Smell:** roast goose, gravy, rot, crushed apples.
- **Touch:** gravy to the ankles; a child's hand gripping a sleeve.
- **Taste:** cider spilled everywhere, and ash.

## Alternates & Re-hooks
- **If the Wishbone was snapped in Scene 05:** the Wretch arrives weakened (halved hit points, no Legendary Resistance) and the First Fruits are closed.
- **If the party killed the Wretch in Scene 06:** it rises at the Open Door as Second Helpings.
- **If nobody won any households:** they can still be won here, one action and one DC 18 Persuasion check at a time.
