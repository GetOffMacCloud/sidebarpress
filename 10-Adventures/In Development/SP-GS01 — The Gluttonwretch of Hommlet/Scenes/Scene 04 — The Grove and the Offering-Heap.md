# Scene 04 — The Grove and the Offering-Heap

[[10-Adventures/In Development/SP-GS01 — The Gluttonwretch of Hommlet/Scenes/00 — Scene Index|00 — Scene Index]] · ← [[Scene 03 — The War Council]] · Next → [[Scene 05 — The Smokehouse]]

## Purpose
The First Fruits path. The party wins the households' offerings in the village and gets past the grieving compost heap at the grove, learning there how the rite works. Every household won is one Offering banked for the feast.
**Budget:** about 25 minutes of Act 2's shared 65. Running long? Calm the Heap with one check, and run three households, not eight.

## Part A — The Households

Jaroo can lay Old Gobbler to rest only if the offering is *freely given by Hommlet*. The party must win over **6 of the village's 8 households** to carry their first fruits to the feast table, in the Wretch's presence, knowing what it is.

**The challenge.** Each household is won by one success: a DC 15 Charisma (Persuasion), Wisdom (Insight) or Charisma (Performance) check, or a **good story** (below), which wins the household outright. Two failures in a row with the same household means it gorges and is lost.

| Household | First fruits (576 harvest) | What holds them back |
|---|---|---|
| [[The Elder of Hommlet]]'s household | First sheaf of barley from the council field | Shame: giving means admitting the vote in public (won automatically if he confessed in Scene 02) |
| The Haddles ([[Pip Haddle]]'s family) | First cranberries from the grove bog; Pip picked them | Terror for Pip |
| The Gundigoots, Welcome Wench | First pour of the 576 Brewfest ale | Business: the feast must go on |
| [[Mytch the Miller]] | First sack of flour from the new wheat, tied with a red ribbon | Money |
| [[Widow Marta Bree]] | First honeycomb from her skeps | Grief (won by a sincere promise; see Scene 02) |
| The Thistlewoods | First squash and pumpkins | Bitterness: their pigs are already gone |
| The Rheddens, a young family | First apples and new cider | Fear of the monster |
| Dunwold the farrier's family | First ears of corn | Thinks it's all superstition |

**Arlo Rhedden's orchard** (gorging 8). When the party reaches the Rheddens, the young father **Arlo Rhedden** is sitting under his best tree with the family's basket of first apples, eating them core, stem and all. His wife is trying to pull the basket away; their children are watching from the door.
> "Just one more. They're ours, aren't they? They're *ours*."

- He is Peckish: a DC 12 Wisdom (Medicine) or Charisma (Persuasion) check stops him, and the Rhedden household is **won outright**, in thanks.
- If the party takes more than a minute or so to act, he is Gorging (DC 15 to cure) and the basket is half gone. Enough remains for an Offering only if he is stopped this round.
- Uncured, he finishes the apples. The Rhedden household is lost.

**What a good story looks like.** It is true to the character, it speaks to that household's fear, and it asks them to give. Examples the players might find:
1. **To the Elder (shame).** A paladin tells of an oath they once broke and what it took to make it right. "I didn't fix it by hiding it. I fixed it by walking back through the door and saying it out loud."
2. **To the Haddles (Pip).** "Pip has carried your cranberries to that grove every year since he could walk. Let him carry them one more time, with me right beside him."
3. **To Mytch (money).** "A cursed harvest costs you the mill. A blessed one fills it. One sack now, or every sack next year."
4. **To Widow Bree (grief).** A bard retells Old Gobbler's first Brewfest, the year he ate the feather off Canon Terjon's hat. She laughs, then cries, then goes to fetch her honeycomb.
5. **To the Gundigoots (business).** "The Welcome Wench has fed every traveler since sixty-nine. Tonight there's one guest still waiting at your door."
6. **To the Rheddens (fear).** A fighter or cleric swears to stand between the family and the Wretch while they give. It counts only if the PC keeps the promise in Scene 07. Break it, and the household drops its offering and runs.

Record each household won on the [[SP-GS01 — The First Fruits|First Fruits tracker]].

## Part B — The Grove
> [!art]- Map — Jaroo's Grove (M3)
> ![[SP-GS01 - M3 Jaroos Grove - Print (300dpi, 7.5in).png|500]]


> *A ring of standing stones encloses a clearing of brown autumn grass. At its center, a flat stone shrine is carved with birds, sheaves and open hands, and a moss-covered feeding trough stands beside it. A great empty roost of woven branches sags from the largest oak. Behind the oak, a mound of rotted vegetables taller than a barn is moving. It is breathing.*

**[[Jaroo's Grove]]** is the Old Faith's offering place. The shrine's carvings show the rite in pictures: villagers carrying baskets, a great bird eating first, and the village eating after.

**Clue.** A DC 15 Intelligence (Religion) check, or a druid's glance, reads the carvings: *the bird eats first; the village gives; the giving is the blessing.* This confirms Jaroo's six-offering rite.

**Jaroo** (comedy-free), at the empty roost:
> "Forty years. I'd come up at dawn with a bucket of corn and he'd be sitting on that branch like a lord, waiting, offended I was late." *(He touches the trough.)* "He waited all last Brewfest. I watched him wait."

### The Offering-Heap
**Creatures:** **the Offering-Heap** (1). It is forty years of rotted offerings — gourds for knuckles, a corn-husk mane, a pumpkin where a head should be — pulled upright by grief and the Temple's rot. It isn't evil. It is confused, hungry and lonely, and it guards the empty roost because nobody told it to stop. It understands Sylvan but cannot speak; it answers by handing over objects, usually a rotten pumpkin, which it offers as a gift. Statblock: [[SP-GS01 — Bestiary]].

**It never attacks first.** It looms, shuffles between the party and the roost, and presents pumpkins.

**Calming it.**
- **Talk it down:** a DC 18 Intelligence (Nature) or Wisdom (Animal Handling) check, with advantage if Jaroo helps. Speaking Sylvan to it gives advantage too.
- **Feed it:** give it one household's first fruits and it settles at once. **This spends one banked Offering** (cross it off the tracker). That trade is the point of the scene; let the players weigh it.
- **Comedy beat:** once calm, it apologizes at length, in mime, for "the whole lurching business," then hands over Old Gobbler's last fallen feather (see Treasure) and points one vine down the High Road toward the Smokehouse.
- **The Wishbone test:** if anyone shows it the Wishbone, it shudders away and will not touch it. *(Clue: the bone is wrong.)*

**Fighting it.** Allowed, and a mistake. It hits for 87 (12d10 + 21) a round if all three Slams land and buries people under its Harvest Avalanche. It is **vulnerable to fire**, fire stops its regeneration, and lightning heals it. A DC 15 Intelligence (Nature) check tells anyone looking that it is a compost heap: "wet, but it'll burn." Expect two or three rounds. Jaroo will not help kill it, and its death rattle is a long, sad wheeze of escaping cider.

**Development:** Killed, the Heap leaves nothing but compost; Jaroo goes back to the village without a word, and the Grove's households need one more success each to win (they have heard). Calmed, it follows the party to the feast at a distance and stands at the edge of the green, where in Scene 07 it can be given one Offering to fight beside the party for 3 rounds.

**Terrain:** standing stones (half cover), the great oak, soft ground. **Tactics:** it Slams the nearest attacker, uses Harvest Avalanche on clusters, and never pursues past the stones. **DM direction:** play it as a sad, enormous dog that has been hit.

## Treasure
- **Old Gobbler's last feather** (from the calmed Heap). Laid on the feast table, it counts as a **seventh Offering slot** that only Jaroo can fill: if he places it, the rite needs one fewer household Offering.

## Clues Available Here
- The shrine carvings → **six offerings, freely given** (Clue Ledger).
- The Heap calming when fed → **feeding is the cure** (Clue Ledger).
- The Heap refusing the Wishbone → **the bone is wrong**.
- Jaroo at the roost → **the Wretch is Old Gobbler**.

## Sensory Palette
- **Sight:** standing stones, an empty roost, a mound of pumpkins that breathes.
- **Sound:** crows in the oak, a wet sigh like a cider press.
- **Smell:** sweet rot, wet leaves, crushed apples.
- **Touch:** cold moss on the trough; a squashy pumpkin pressed into your hands.
- **Taste:** cider in the air thick enough to taste.

## Alternates & Re-hooks
- **If the party never goes to the grove:** Jaroo's rite still works at the feast, but every household must be won there (DC 18 Persuasion, an action each).
- **If the party tries to buy the offerings:** a household that is paid gives nothing that counts. Jaroo knows at a glance.
- **If a PC is a druid of the Old Faith:** they can read the carvings without a check and talk to the Heap with advantage.
