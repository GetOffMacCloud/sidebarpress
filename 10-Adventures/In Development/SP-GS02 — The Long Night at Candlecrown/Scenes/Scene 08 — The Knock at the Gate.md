# Scene 08 — The Knock at the Gate

[[10-Adventures/In Development/SP-GS02 — The Long Night at Candlecrown/Scenes/00 — Scene Index|00 — Scene Index]] · ← [[Scene 07 — The Rehearsal]] · Next → [[Scene 09 — The Short Day]]

## Purpose
The avoidable problem. A polite stranger asks to come in from the storm, and only a villager can say yes. The party decides whether he's a danger, a resource, or both, and the village decides with them.
**Budget:** 20 minutes. Running long? Have Tansy open her mouth (beat 3) at once.

## Arrival
> *Outside the gate, in the lantern light, stands a pale man in a good traveling cloak, bareheaded in the snow, his empty hands held open. "Forgive me," he says. "I'm a pilgrim, two days lost on that cliff in the storm; is there a fire? I'd ask for nothing else." Snow is settling on his shoulders and not melting.*

**To the table:** *"Dob is looking at you. It's your call, he says. Is it?"*

## Who Is Here
- **[[Aurel Vasch]]**, outside the gate. *Wants:* an invitation. *Knows:* immoths (the Passage); the Suel on the chapel wall; the true name of his dead master. *Reacts:* see the escalation script in his dossier: ask → plead → bargain → leave and try another door (the cliff side, a child, the wool loft window).
- **[[Dob Kettle]]** (mute if he lost his voice), **[[Tansy Kettle]]**, the gate-watch; by beat 2, **[[Brannoc Tull]]** and **[[Old Mother Sedge]]**.

## Beats
**1. The ask.** Aurel asks to come in. He doesn't step forward. If anyone beckons, opens the gate or steps aside, he still doesn't move: *"You're kind. May I come in?"* He waits for the words. **Fact 4 tells:**
- **(route b)** He never steps over the gate line, even with the gate open and a PC gesturing him through. His footprints in the fresh snow stop exactly at the salt.
- **(route c)** He casts no shadow in the gate lanterns (if they didn't see it in the mine).
- **(route a)** Mother Sedge, arriving, says, "Don't you dare, Dob Kettle," and tells the party the old law if they don't know it.

**2. The bargain.** If the party has met him (Scene 05), he reminds them of their talk. If not, he offers, unasked: *"The thing in the storm. I've seen one before. I can tell you how to deal with it, and read you your miners' chapel walls, if you'll ask me in."* He wants:
- an invitation from a villager (a PC's doesn't count, and he knows it);
- or, failing that, safe passage down the Stair after the storm, sworn, and *he'll give half of what he knows now.*
What he can give is in his dossier. The best of it: his master's true name, Ilvan Dross's secret name from the Passage, "a word no one living has spoken." That is worth something to Hrimvald (see [[Scene 11 — The Seventh Verse]]).

**3. The child.** If the party hesitates long, or seems about to let him in, or gets it wrong, Tansy Kettle, at her father's side, says loudly: *"He's waiting for someone to say yes."* (**Fact 4, last resort.**) And then, worse: *"I could say yes. I give him milk."*

**4. The decision.** Somebody decides. Let the players argue it out; let the village argue too.

## Development
- **Invited in (by a villager):** Aurel is courtly, grateful, and hungry. He sits by the hall fire all night, and on Needfest 3 night he drinks from a sleeper (Pell by default; DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) for a PC on watch to notice him move). On Midwinter Night, if that feeding went unnoticed, Pell is his thrall, and during wave 3 Pell puts out the lanterns on the workshop side while Aurel attacks the singers from inside. He keeps any bargain he made, to the letter.
- **Refused, with a bargain struck:** he gives what he promised from outside the gate (he is honest about deals) and waits out the storm on the cliff. On Midwinter Night he watches. If the party promised him passage, he walks down the Stair on Needfest 5 and is never seen in the Tors again.
- **Refused, no bargain:** he goes to the cliffs. On Midwinter Night, during wave 2, he tries the wool loft window and asks whoever sleeps nearest (Corin, who can't answer, or Tansy, who can). Track it.
- **Destroyed:** by sunlight (keep him talking till dawn; he leaves an hour before), by staking in his trunk by day, or in a fight on the cliff (he flees, Spider Climb). The village is relieved. Mother Sedge says a prayer to Telchur for him anyway.
