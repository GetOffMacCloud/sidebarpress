# Handout — The Apprentice's Carving

*The walls of the miners' chapel ([[SP-GS02 — The Old Workings]], M5). The Common half is given here as the party reads it (the old letters need a DC 10 Intelligence check, no proficiency). The Suloise half is the counter-verse; show it as lines of strange characters (Art Brief H2) and give its meaning only to a PC who can read it (Aurel, or* comprehend languages*).*

> **In the common letters:**
>
> I who cut this was the apprentice of a man whose name I will not cut. He made the Curse of Binding in the last days of the Empire, and he spoke it, and the words went out on the wind, and the cold ones that were bound by it went after them.
>
> I took one verse of it with me under the mountains and into this valley, so that the cold ones would never have it all. I have hidden it in the Frost-Father's song, in our old tongue, where it will be sung every winter and kept alive, and never be found.
>
> SING IT. NEVER WRITE IT. What is written, they can take without a voice.
>
> If the cold ones come for it, they will bargain first. One came for a verse at Kendeen, and they gave it a better word, and it took the words and went.
>
> Here beside this is the word that unbinds them. Give it only if you would free what hunts you.
>
> Fortubo keep the deep.

> **In the Suel characters** (the counter-verse; for a reader of Suloise):
>
> *You were made to gather. Put it down. You were wind before you were a curse; be wind. The words are not yours and you are not theirs. Go where you like. Go.*

**For the DM:** spoken or sung to an immoth, the counter-verse frees it from the curse ([[Scene 11 — The Seventh Verse]], The Bargain). A DC 15 Intelligence (History) check (or a day's work with a rubbing) gives its sounds without its meaning, which is enough to sing it. It is the one thing in this adventure that is safe to write down.
