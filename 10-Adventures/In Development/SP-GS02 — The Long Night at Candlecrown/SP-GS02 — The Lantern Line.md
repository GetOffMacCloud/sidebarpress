# The Lantern Line

Back to [[SP-GS02-The-Long-Night-at-Candlecrown]] · Index: [[10-Adventures/In Development/SP-GS02 — The Long Night at Candlecrown/Scenes/00 — Scene Index|00 — Scene Index]] · Statblocks: [[SP-GS02 — Bestiary]]

> *"No lantern dark, no verse unsung."* — said in Candlecrown at every door on Midwinter Night

This note holds the adventure's standing rules: the laws of the mesa, the storm, the lantern line, the choir, stolen voices, and the safety valve. Scenes point here rather than repeat them.

## Laws of the Mesa
- **One way up.** The Candle Stair is the only path from the valley road to the crown: two miles of switchbacks cut into a 400-foot cliff, 5 to 10 feet wide, with a sheer drop on the outside. In snow it is difficult terrain. The old mine is the only other way in, and almost no one living remembers it.
- **The edge.** A creature shoved or knocked prone within 5 feet of the Stair's outer edge or the mesa's rim must succeed on a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw or go over. A creature that goes over falls 1d4 × 10 feet to the switchback below (1d6 bludgeoning per 10 feet, landing prone in snow) or, from the crown's north and east rims, 400 feet onto the scree, which kills. A creature that fails by 5 or less catches the edge and hangs on; climbing back up is a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check as an action.
- **Shoving off the edge** is the tyrgs' favorite trick and the party's best one: a tyrg that goes over the Stair's edge is out of the fight.
- **Cold.** Outdoors between dusk and dawn, a creature without cold-weather gear or cold resistance makes a DC 10 Constitution saving throw each hour or gains a level of exhaustion (DMG p. 110, extreme cold). The DC rises with the storm (below). Every hearth in Candlecrown is open to the party.
- **Fire.** Candlecrown is a lantern village: every household has 4–6 lanterns, and the workshop holds 40 gallons of lantern oil in 2-pint flasks (160 flasks). A flask of oil works as in the *Player's Handbook* (p. 152): thrown, then lit, 5 (and against an ice-rune guardian, its Rune Heart). Lanterns are weapons and wards in this adventure; let the players use them freely.

## The Storm (alert states)
Hrimvald's storm rises over three nights. Each stage has written effects; announce the change to the players at dusk.

| Stage | When | Visibility (outdoors) | Cold save DC | Other effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gathering** | Needfest 1–2 | Lightly obscured beyond 300 ft. | 10 | Snow 1 foot deep; the Stair is difficult terrain. The wind carries a sound like a choir a long way off (DC 13 Wisdom (Perception) to make out the tune of the Long Night Carol). |
| **Howling** | Needfest 3 | Lightly obscured beyond 60 ft.; heavily obscured beyond 300 ft. | 12 | Ranged weapon attacks beyond normal range automatically miss. Fires outdoors gutter: a lantern left unattended goes out on a 1 on a d6 each hour. Hrimvald's voice can be heard in the wind at dusk. |
| **The Long Night** | Needfest 4 (Midwinter Night) | Heavily obscured beyond 60 ft., except within 10 ft. of a lit lantern or fire | 15 | Hrimvald's siege actions are active (see [[Immoth]]). Luna is dark; only Celene shows, a green-blue coin behind the snow. |
| **Breaking** | Needfest 5, after the climax | Clears by noon | 10 | If Hrimvald is gone or bargained with, the snow stops at dawn. If it left with an incomplete verse, the storm returns each dusk (see [[Scene 12 — Dawn at Candlecrown]]). |

## The Lantern Line
The crown of the tor is ringed by **24 iron lantern posts**, set by the founders along the rim and the head of the Stair. Every Midwinter Night the village lights them all and keeps them lit till dawn. This winter, the posts are the party's wall.

**The track.** Keep a count of lit posts: ☐☐☐☐☐☐ ☐☐☐☐☐☐ ☐☐☐☐☐☐ ☐☐☐☐☐☐ (four sides of six: **Stair**, **East Rim** (Corin's fold), **North Rim** (the cliff above the mine mouth), **Workshop**).

**What a lit lantern does:**
- **Slows the tyrgs.** Tyrgs are Flame-Shy: the space within 10 feet of a lit lantern is difficult terrain to them, and they can't howl there. A side with all six posts lit can be crossed only by climbing past the light or with the alpha's lead.
- **Shows the approach.** If 12 or more posts are lit when Hrimvald comes over the cliffs, the party sees it coming: no one is surprised, and anyone with a readied action can use it as it crests the rim.
- **Pushes back the storm.** In the Long Night, the area within 10 feet of a lit lantern isn't heavily obscured.

**Putting them out.** A tyrg adjacent to a post can use its action to knock the lantern into the snow. Hrimvald's Snuff siege action puts out 1d4. A post can be relit as an action by a creature adjacent to it with a flame (a lit lantern, a torch, a tinderbox and 1 extra action). Villagers sent to tend the line relight one post per round on their side, but they are in the open.

**Lighting the line.** On Needfest 1–3, the village lights the line at dusk with 12 posts (it is short of oil-wicks; the rest are for Midwinter Night). In the preparation phase ([[Scene 09 — The Short Day]]), each watch spent on the line by a PC (or by six villagers) lights and storm-shields 6 more posts, up to 24, and shielded lanterns ignore the Howling stage's 1-in-6 chance of going out.

## The Carol and the Choir
On Midwinter Night the whole village sings the **Long Night Carol** in the workshop hall: seven verses, sung slowly, one verse to each watch of the night. The first six verses are in Common (the words are in [[SP-GS02 — Handout — The Long Night Carol]]). The seventh is in old Suloise, learned by ear, never written.

**The choir track.** Count the singers: the hall holds **40**. Each voice stolen, each singer killed, each singer who leaves the hall comes off the count.

| Choir | Singers | Effect on the defenders |
|---|---|---|
| **Full** | 30–40 | When a verse ends, every defender who can hear the hall gains **5 temporary hit points** and **Inspiration** (if they don't have it). |
| **Thin** | 15–29 | When a verse ends, every defender who can hear the hall gains Inspiration (if they don't have it). |
| **Broken** | 14 or fewer | No benefit. If the choir is Broken before the seventh verse, Hrimvald comes at once (start [[Scene 11 — The Seventh Verse]]). |

"Can hear the hall" means within 300 feet of the workshop and not deafened. Outdoors in the Long Night that covers the whole crown.

**The verse track** times the siege: ☐ I · ☐ II (wave 1) · ☐ III · ☐ IV (wave 2) · ☐ V · ☐ VI (wave 3) · ☐ VII (Hrimvald). See [[Scene 10 — The Long Night]].

## Stolen Voices
- **What it is.** A creature whose voice Hrimvald (or one of its guardians' Hush, briefly) has taken can't speak, sing or provide verbal components. It can still write, gesture and hear. It feels nothing; that is the eerie part.
- **Where it goes.** Each voice Hrimvald takes becomes an ice rune on its body (see [[Immoth]]). The rune holds the voice's words, and Hrimvald can speak with it: a villager may hear their own voice speaking from the snow.
- **How it comes back.** When its rune melts (10 or more fire damage to Hrimvald in one turn melts the newest rune), when Hrimvald spends it (Spend a Word), when Hrimvald dies, or when Hrimvald gives it back in a bargain. *Remove curse*, *greater restoration* and *wish* restore a voice too, but the rune stays on the immoth and Hrimvald can speak with it.
- **If Hrimvald escapes with runes,** they last until the spring thaw. See [[Scene 13 — The Ice Library]].

## The Safety Valve
**Mother Sedge's shrine** to Telchur is a windbreak cave cut in the rock beside the hall. Tyrgs won't enter it (they are Telchur's beasts, and they know his house), and the cold save is never needed inside. A character dropped to 0 hit points within 30 feet of the shrine can be dragged in by any villager, and Mother Sedge stabilizes them. The shrine doesn't stop Hrimvald: it is not Telchur's creature, and it walks where it likes.
