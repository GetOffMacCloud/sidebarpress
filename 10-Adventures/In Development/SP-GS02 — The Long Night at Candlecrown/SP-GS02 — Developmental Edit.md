# SP-GS02 — Developmental Edit (structure, pacing, clues, balance, canon)

[[SP-GS02-The-Long-Night-at-Candlecrown]] · [[00 — Scene Index]] · [[SP-GS02 — Classic Review]] · [[SP-GS02 — Bestiary]] · [[Lantern Line]]

> [!info] How to use this note
> This pass builds on the Classic Review of 2026-11-22 (12/12 musts, 42/43 other items) and does not repeat what that pass already fixed. Nothing here has been applied: every item is unticked, and every scene, the overview and the bestiary are unchanged. Tick an item when you apply it. Items marked **Needs you** at the end are choices that change the adventure's shape; the rest are fixes with one obvious answer.

## Verdict

The bones are strong. Candlecrown is a real situation (a village, a hymn with a curse in it, a hunter that wants a word, a vampire spawn in the coal store) and the Clue Ledger already gives every essential fact three roads and a last resort. The climax has eight solutions and every one is seeded earlier. Three things hold it back from being table-ready:

1. **The siege's numbers don't add up.** The pack is too small to fill the Scene 10 waves at 5–6 PCs, and fights in Scenes 01 and 06 shrink it further, so the better a party plays early the emptier the climax gets.
2. **The Long Night has no single clock.** "One verse to each watch," "about 20 minutes a verse" and "dawn comes round 30" can't all be true, and which one wins decides whether the party ever gets a short rest in a four-fight night.
3. **The magic's rules are fuzzy where the plot leans on them.** Aurel's threshold, which voices Hrimvald can take and why it came this year are each stated two ways. Players will find the seam because they'll try to use the rules.

Fix those and the rest is polish.

**Musts:** 12/12 (unchanged from the Classic Review). **New findings:** 7 major, about 30 scene fixes, 6 encounter-math corrections and 10 canon checks (3 with real conflicts).

## Major findings

- [ ] **1 · Balance — the pack can't fill the siege.** The Bestiary gives Hrimvald 16 tyrgs and the alpha, and says to cut wave 1 if the pack drops below 8 before Midwinter Night. The Scene 10 waves need 4 + 5 + (4 + 3 + 3) = 19 or more tyrgs at 5 PCs, and 6 + 7 + 6 + 4 + 4 = 27 at 6 PCs, before counting the ones the party kills on the Candle Stair (S01) and at dusk (S06). A party that fights well early guts the climax, and the safety rule then cuts it further. **Fix:** scale the roster by party size (4 PCs: 13 + alpha; 5 PCs: 17 + alpha; 6 PCs: 24 + alpha) and add one line to the Bestiary: "Tyrgs killed in Scenes 01 and 06 come off the roster. Hrimvald calls fresh hounds down from the high Tors at dusk on Needfest 3: add back half the dead, rounded up." That keeps early kills meaningful without emptying Scene 10. Replace the "cut wave 1" rule with "if fewer than 10 remain, merge waves 1 and 2." *(Bestiary; S01; S06; S10)*

- [ ] **2 · Pacing — one clock for the Long Night.** The Lantern Line says the choir sings "one verse to each watch of the night." Scene 10 says each verse takes about 20 minutes and a lull is too short for a short rest. Scene 11 has dawn at round 30 and the choir singing until dawn. Seven verses at 20 minutes is under two and a half hours, not a solstice night, and a 30-round climax is five minutes. The adventuring-day math (17,600 or 21,750 adjusted XP against a 20,000 budget, "no time for a short rest") rests on whichever reading the DM picks. **Fix:** make it one verse per watch, seven watches from dusk to dawn, about 2 hours each; the singers rest between verses and the lantern line holds the gap. Put the waves in watches I, II, IV and VI, leave III and V as lulls long enough for a short rest (or let a Full choir grant each PC one Hit Die back at each lull, if you want the tension kept), and start Scene 11 in watch VII with dawn as a stated trigger ("dawn breaks at the end of round 10 of the climax, or when the seventh verse is finished, whichever comes first"). Update the Run Sheet's Session 3 timings to match. *(Lantern Line; S10; S11; Bestiary adventuring day; Scene Index run sheet)*

- [ ] **3 · Rules — Aurel's threshold.** The crown is hallowed as "one house" (S02 C1, S03, Bestiary: "the mesa edge is inside the line"), so Aurel can't enter any of it uninvited. But S03 beat 5 has a goat dragged from the Ambersons' shed across the crown to the coal store, the Front Matter says he "takes a goat from the coal store," S08 and S10 have him trying the wool-loft window (inside the crown), and Ash "has a key" to a hatch that S05 describes as nailed shut. **Fix:** define the line once, in the Lantern Line note: "The hallowing runs along the wall and the rim and over every door and window in it. The Old Workings are outside it. The coal-store hatch is a threshold; Aurel can't cross it inward unless invited." Then: the goat strays to the hatch on its own, or is led there by **Tansy**, who has been feeding "the cold man" (this also gives Tansy's subplot a payoff). Move the wool-loft window to the rim face so Aurel can reach it from outside. Make the hatch locked, not nailed, and keep Ash's key. *(Front Matter Villain's Scheme; S02; S03 beat 5; S05; S08; S10; Bestiary riders)*

- [ ] **4 · Motive — why Hrimvald came this year.** The Front Matter says Hrimvald "heard the verse carried on last year's midwinter wind." The village has sung the carol every Needfest for centuries, so this year needs a reason. The adventure already has the better one: Mother Sedge says the hunter hears writing "the way you hear your name across a room," and Wenna has written the seventh verse down and kept it in her sewing box. Written once, it called the hunter; that is why Wenna's paper says "I'M SORRY." See **Needs you A.** *(Front Matter Background; S03; S07; Wenna Tull)*

- [ ] **5 · Rules — which voices Hrimvald can take.** Corin, Dob and Wenna were each singing the seventh verse when they were taken. Scene 07 speaks of "the six of us who know it." The Broken-choir rule has Hrimvald take the six by Word-Theft at once. If it can do that, it has no reason to wait for Midwinter Night. **Fix:** state the rule in the Lantern Line note and in Hrimvald's Named Example: "Hrimvald can take a voice only while that voice speaks or sings the seventh verse, or a spell with a verbal component, within sight of it." The six know the verse but are safe unless they sing it, which is why it needs Midwinter Night, when the carol is sung whole. The Broken-choir result then becomes: "the six falter, sing the seventh verse ragged and alone, and Hrimvald takes them one by one." *(Lantern Line; S07; S10; S11; Immoth)*

- [ ] **6 · Endings — what "Silenced" does.** The Silenced box has all forty singers mute and S12 says the village "stopped speaking," but under Scene 11's rules gathering the verse's lines doesn't take voices. **Fix:** pick one and write it into S11: (a) when Hrimvald gathers the whole verse it takes every voice that sang a gathered line that night, which is the choir; or (b) the village isn't voiceless, only silent, and won't sing again (rewrite the box). Option (a) is stronger and matches the cover's promise. *(S11; S12)*

- [ ] **7 · Endings — "Driven Off" against Scene 13.** S12 says a driven-off Hrimvald "returns every dusk" to the village. S13 says it sits in its ice library and won't leave its home. If it returns every dusk, the party walking two days each way to the library leaves Candlecrown exposed for four nights. See **Needs you C.** *(S12; S13)*

## Canon Check

Checked on 2026-11-23 against the Greyhawk wiki at greyhawkonline.com, which indexes the TSR and WotC books through the Encyclopedia Greyhawkania. The wiki summarises the books; it is not the books. Page numbers below are the wiki's citations and are still marked *(verify in print)* until you check them against your own copies. Tick an Overview Canon Check box only after that.

**Results at a glance:** 3 conflicts (Fortubo, tyrgs, the Passage date), 2 house extensions to label (Telchur's feast, Bleak Gate), the rest confirmed.

### 1 · Fortubo — conflict
- **Canon says:** Lawful good (lawful neutral tendencies), lesser god of **stone, metals, mountains and guardianship**. Symbol: a warhammer with a glowing head. He began in the **Suel pantheon** but **left the Suel when he learned they had made the derro**, and now favours dwarves above all; a close ally of Moradin, who gave him his hammer Golbi; brother of Jascar. Clergy is about three-quarters dwarven, and his high priest is always a dwarf. He is worshipped in the Yeomanry.
- **Where:** Lakofka, "Gods of the Suel Pantheon," *Dragon* #88 (1984) pp. 8–11; *Living Greyhawk Gazetteer* (2000) p. 21 and others; *Player's Guide to Greyhawk* (1998) pp. 20–21 *(verify in print)*.
- **What the vault has:** the SP-GS02 lore note's stance is "Fortubo stays with the miners," with the elder, the workshop and the sealed chapel (M5) his. That fits his portfolio but misses the break with the Suel. A second, older Fortubo note in the deity compendium says gnomes adopted him, gives him Forge and Light domains, Mount Celestia and two holy days ("Day of the First Strike," "Festival of the Deep Vein"). None of that is in the canon summary; treat that note as unverified and don't use its holy days in SP-GS02.
- **Fix:** keep the chapel and make the break into texture. The founders brought Fortubo's rite through the Passage; the god has since "turned his face from the Suel," so the chapel is sealed and the elder keeps the old forms out of habit and hope. If a priest of Fortubo ever appears, make them dwarven. Add a line to the Fortubo lore note's Sources table for *Dragon* #88. See **Needs you D.**

### 2 · Telchur — confirmed, with one house extension to label
- **Canon says:** Oeridian lesser god of **winter, cold and the north wind**, chaotic neutral; symbol a leafless tree in a field of snow; the Velaeri sibling of Atroa, Sotillion, Wenta and Velnius. Needfest week is especially important to him. Needfest is called the **Feast of Telchur in the Bandit Kingdoms and Highfolk** (a Living Greyhawk usage). The wiki is inconsistent on his father (Procan in one section, Velnius in another).
- **Where:** *Dragon* #265 (1999), "Oeridian Lesser Gods III," pp. 56–59; *Frostburn* (2004) pp. 84–85; *Living Greyhawk Gazetteer*; Needfest feast name from Miller, "Campaign Holidays," *Dragon Annual* #4 (1999) pp. 18–19 *(verify in print)*.
- **Fix:** the vault already flags the Yeomanry use of "Feast of Telchur" as a house extension; keep it and say so in the book's Deviations list. The Yeomanry's listed deities don't include Telchur, and its people are mostly Suel, so give the carol one line of history (for example, Oeridian herders brought the hymn up the Candle Stair and the Suel miners kept it). Don't swap in the Suel winter god Vatun: canon says he wasn't worshipped in the Imperium and his priests are Telchur's enemies. Leave his father unnamed in the book.

### 3 · Needfest and Midwinter Night — confirmed
- **Canon says:** Needfest is the seven-day festival that opens the year; **Midwinter Night is Needfest 4 (Godsday)**. Needfest 1 is New Year's Day; Needfest 7 has the Feast of Fools.
- **Where:** Needfest entry, citing *Greyhawk: The Adventure Begins* (1998), *Dragon Annual* #4, *Living Greyhawk Gazetteer* p. 2, *From the Ashes* pp. 4, 6 *(verify in print)*.
- **Not confirmed online:** the vault's "Luna is dark; only Celene shows" and the other names (Dim Night, Dark Time, Handmaiden's Glory). Check the 1983 boxed set's calendar before printing the moon detail; if you can't, cut "Luna is dark" from the storm stage and keep only the darkness of the storm.

### 4 · The Tors and the tyrgs — conflict (tyrgs)
- **Canon says (Tors):** mesa-like hills with flattened crowns coming off the Crystalmists, forming the southern edge of the Yeomanry, with the Hool Marshes at their eastern end; goblins and bandits there, and monsters that steal livestock. No tyrgs are named. *Living Greyhawk Gazetteer* pp. 134–135, 146; *From the Ashes* Atlas pp. 60, 73 *(verify in print)*.
- **Canon says (tyrg):** a Large magical beast, **"a cross between a tiger and a wolf,"** white with grey, black and tan spots, cat-like in stance with canine jaws. Its howl mildly stuns. It lives in **forested hills and the lower slopes of forested mountains**. Tyrgs are social **but prefer to hunt alone, because other tyrgs aren't immune to the howl.** First appearance *WG5 Mordenkainen's Fantastic Adventure* (1984); *MC5 Greyhawk Adventures Appendix* (1990); *Monstrous Manual* (1993) pp. 241–242 under Mammal *(verify in print)*.
- **What the vault has:** the Tors lore note says tyrg packs harrying the Tors' livestock is canon; it isn't, in these sources. The Tyrg creature note calls them "white wolves of the far cold" hunting in packs of four to eight behind a breeding female, and the 5e block makes every tyrg immune to the others' howls. Those are all house choices, and the pack of 16 that the whole siege rests on runs against canon's lone hunters.
- **Fix:** keep the packs (the siege needs them) and make the deviation the point. One line in the Tyrg lore and in Mother Sedge's dialogue: "Tyrgs hunt alone. A pack means something is driving them." The Yeomanry saying already in the note ("the winter has a master this year") does the rest. Under Hrimvald, the pack's howls don't stun each other because its runes hold their ears; when Hrimvald dies or flees, the pack breaks apart and scatters, which also gives Scene 11 a clean end-of-fight rule. Re-describe them as the tiger-wolf of canon (white coat, grey and tan spots, cat's stance), update the Tors note's Canon Account to remove the tyrg claim, and list "tyrgs in packs" under Deviations.

### 5 · Melkot and the Sons of Marchanter — confirmed as Living Greyhawk / fan tier
- **Canon says:** Melkot is an independent city-state in the Tors, founded by the Suel as a mining outpost; Theodain Eriason based himself there in 576 CY. The Sons of Marchanter are an arcane (or occult) order based in Melkot, enemies of the Silent Ones of Keoland. Both were created by Denis Tetreault and enter print through *Living Greyhawk Journal* #0 p. 11 and #4 p. 16, and the *Living Greyhawk Gazetteer* pp. 135, 146 *(verify in print)*.
- **Note:** the Melkot entry says "southern Tors" while the Tors entry says "western Tors." The vault's stance (western) is fine.
- **Fix:** none; it's a sequel hook only. In the book, keep the Sons' motive (they want the curse-verse) and add that they would be glad to anger the Silent Ones with it.

### 6 · The Yeomanry and Loftwick — confirmed
- **Canon says:** democratic republic; capital Loftwick (pop. 8,400); a Spokesman (the Freeholder) and a Council of Common Grosspokesmen. Coins: **freegold** (gp), **halfgold** (ep), **silver** (sp), **common** (cp). Listed deities include Fortubo, Phaulkon, Heironeous, Allitur and others. *Living Greyhawk Gazetteer* pp. 134–136 *(verify in print)*.
- **Fix:** check that the Front Matter's Yeomanry coin uses these four names. Keep the Spokesman unnamed: the named Spokesman (Marius Lindon) is the 591 CY state.

### 7 · The Passage of Slerotin, Dark Gate and Bleak Gate — conflict (date), house extension (Bleak Gate)
- **Canon says:** the Passage runs under the Hellfurnaces from the Sea of Dust to the foot of the Crystalmists, **over 200 miles**. Slerotin, "the last Mage of Power," led twelve (some say eighteen) Suel tribes through it after the Rain of Colorless Fire and sealed it, trapping the Lerara. Its Yeomanry mouth was **found between 576 and 579 CY** near the township of **Dark Gate**, about twelve miles from Westburn; the *Gazetteer* gives 579 CY. No source checked names a "Bleak Gate." *Living Greyhawk Gazetteer* pp. 135, 143, 155, 163; *Living Greyhawk Journal* #0; *The Adventure Begins* pp. 38, 55; *The Scarlet Brotherhood* pp. 3–4, 23–24; 1983 boxed set p. 27 *(verify in print)*.
- **What the vault has:** the Passage lore note gives 150 miles and has Aurel's master, Ilvan Dross, dying "at a sealed door in the Passage" in Goodmonth 575 CY, a year or more before anyone found the mouth. M8's trunk holds earth "from Bleak Gate," which the note takes from the C12 conversion.
- **Fix:** change 150 miles to "over 200." Have Dross die **looking for** the Passage, at a sealed door of an older Suel working in the Crystalmist foothills near Dark Gate; Aurel's letter can say he was sure it was the way in. That keeps the C12 link and leaves the canon discovery date alone. Label Bleak Gate as a house name in Deviations, or change the trunk's earth to "from the foothills above Dark Gate."

### 8 · The Rain of Colorless Fire — confirmed
- **Canon says:** **-422 CY (5094 SD)**, the Twin Cataclysms. The Suel called down the Invoked Devastation on the Baklunish; the Baklunish answered with the Rain of Colorless Fire, which made the Sea of Dust. *Living Greyhawk Gazetteer* / Guide p. 9 (as cited) *(verify in print)*.
- **Fix:** none. Make sure no scene has the Suel cast the Rain; it fell on them.

### 9 · Immoth — confirmed, with deviations to list
- **Canon says:** 3e *Monster Manual II*: CR 9, a **Large** elemental (air, cold, water), **usually neutral**, Int 14, speaks Aquan, Auran and Common. Ice runes that hold "frozen words" released as spell-like effects; a freezing tail venom that paralyses and then drains Intelligence; casts as a 12th-level sorcerer with no fire spells; icewalking; damage reduction 5/magic and bludgeoning; spell resistance; takes extra damage from fire. Earlier appearances: Planescape *Monstrous Compendium Appendix III* and *The Inner Planes*, as horned, tailed giants of ice. The wiki gives no MM2 page number.
- **Fix:** the 5e conversion is faithful in spirit. Add to Deviations: **Huge** (canon Large), **neutral evil** (canon usually neutral), Int 18 (canon 14), the Suel curse origin and the word-hoarding (canon's "frozen words" are spells, not stolen voices), and resistance to piercing and slashing (canon DR is bypassed by magic bludgeoning). Confirm the pp. 127–128 citation against your MM2.

## Needs you

**A · What brought Hrimvald this year?**
1. **Wenna's paper (recommended).** Wenna wrote the seventh verse down this autumn so it wouldn't be lost when the last singers die. Written words carry to the hunter; the paper called it. Her "I'M SORRY" pays off, Mother Sedge's rule is proven in play, and burning the paper becomes one more climax lever.
2. Keep "last year's wind," and add why this year was different (the winch broke and the village sang late, outside the shrine).
3. Leave it a mystery for the DM.

**B · How to fill the siege.**
1. **Roster by party size, with fresh hounds at dusk on Needfest 3 (recommended).** Early kills still matter, and the climax stays full.
2. Fixed roster, with the waves shrinking to whatever survives. Simpler, but a strong party gets an easy finale.

**C · What a driven-off Hrimvald does.**
1. **Goes home to its ice library to heal until next Needfest (recommended).** The village is safe for the year, and Scene 13 is a hunt in its home, played whenever the party likes.
2. Returns every dusk until it gets the verse. Scene 13 then becomes urgent, but the village is undefended while the party travels; you'd need to write who holds the line.

**D · Fortubo and the Suel.**
1. **Keep him as the founders' god, with canon's break as texture (recommended):** the chapel is sealed because the Stern turned his face from the Suel.
2. Make the chapel and its warden dwarven (a hill-dwarf family who came up with the founders), so Fortubo's favour still runs through Candlecrown.
3. Replace Fortubo with a Suel god who stayed (needs its own canon check).
