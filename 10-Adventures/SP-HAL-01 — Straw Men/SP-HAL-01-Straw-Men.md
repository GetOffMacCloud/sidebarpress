# Straw Men

**APL 2 (levels 1–3) · 4–5 characters · one 4-hour session**

> *Scarecrows wearing villagers' clothes keep turning up in the fields around Hollow Ford. The villagers whose clothes they wear are still walking around. They aren't quite right.*
>
> A creeping-paranoia one-shot for **average party level 1–3** (written for APL 2, with scaling for 1 and 3). A green hag has signed a contract to deliver twelve folk of Hollow Ford to a fey collector by midnight on All Hallows' Eve. She has seven. The party arrives on market day, with the closing reel after dark, and has until the twelfth bell to work out which of their new friends are stuffed with straw. Part 1 of the [[SP-HAL-00-Hallows-Eve-Quartet|Hallow's Eve Quartet]].

## Adventure Code
`SP-HAL-01` — never reuse codes, even for scrapped adventures. The `SP-HAL` series is the setting-agnostic Halloween line, kept apart from the Greyhawk `SP-PBS` sequence.

## Bundle
Part 1 of four. Standalone, with a hook out to [[SP-HAL-02-The-Hollow-Lantern]] (the ring and the ledger). Overview and chain rules in [[SP-HAL-00-Hallows-Eve-Quartet]]. Chain level: run at 2nd; the party should reach 3rd by the end. If a PC eats the apple in Scene 02, that choice pays off in [[SP-HAL-04-The-Tithe-of-Thornbury]].

## Date
**All Hallows' Eve**, dusk to midnight. Setting-agnostic: drop into any calendar's autumn festival of the dead.

## Origin
Written for the Hallow's Eve Quartet bundle (October 2026), on the Hommlet blueprint: a friendly inn, two rival faiths, enemies hidden among the townsfolk, and a ruin down the road.

## Design Frame
- **Thesis:** You can't tell your neighbors from your enemies by looking. You tell them by what they remember.
- **Expectation broken:** *The monster is defeated by killing it.* Auntie Chaff is a deadly encounter for the band, and she is not meant to die. The win is stopping the wheel, and the best win is reading a fey contract more carefully than a hag did.
- **Second break:** the sinister hedge-witch is the only one telling the truth, and the kindly priest leads the mob.
- **What this adventure refuses to do:** Nobody in Hollow Ford is wicked. The doubles are copies of innocents, the mob is frightened parents, and the only villain is a stranger.
- **Session spine:** the **Midnight Clock** (see Scene Index, Standing Trackers). Every hour the party doesn't push, Chaff takes another villager.
- **Tone:** harvest-gold by day, pale-flour glow by night. Cider, fiddle music, and the smell of wet hay where it shouldn't be.

## What the Locals Know
- The mill has been "closed for repairs" for three weeks. The miller says so himself, in the same three phrases.
- The scarecrows in the stubble fields have multiplied, and nobody remembers putting the new ones up.
- Captain Voss has been very calm about everything lately. She doesn't take her gauntlets off at table.
- Mother Nettle on the wood's edge is a witch, and Father Corwin has said so from the pulpit twice.
- Widow Haskell, the tinker, sells the prettiest corn dollies anyone has seen, for a copper each.

## What's Really Happening
**Auntie Chaff**, a [[Auntie Chaff|green hag]], has signed a contract with a fey collector known only as [[The Gentleman in Grey]]. He collects *doll villages*: real people, shrunk into enchanted corn dollies and arranged on his shelves forever. The contract calls for "twelve folk of Hollow Ford, delivered at the stroke of midnight, All Hallows' Eve."

Three weeks ago, Chaff took over the old mill at the ford. Its waterwheel now turns backward and powers her workshop. She snatches villagers at night and keeps them in an enchanted sleep in the mill loft. Before she takes each one, she runs a few of their memories through the millstone and stuffs them into a scarecrow wearing that person's clothes. The result is a **Straw Double**, a glamoured copy that walks into the victim's life and keeps people from noticing anyone is missing.

She has taken seven villagers and needs five more. Tonight is the autumn market and its closing reel, and the whole village will be out after dark.

Chaff walks the village openly, disguised by Illusory Appearance as **Widow Haskell**, a cheerful traveling tinker who sells corn dollies at the market. Each dolly she sells marks its buyer as a target.

## Hook
- **The Missing Boy.** [[Brann Tully]], keeper of the Sheaf & Sickle, hires the party because his son [[Pip Tully|Pip]] hasn't come home. Captain Voss insists the boy is just hiding, but Pip has never stayed out past dark.
- **Harvest Hands.** The party is passing through and is offered room and board for helping with the market stalls and the closing reel.
- **The Hedge-Witch's Letter.** [[Mother Nettle]] sends a letter to a temple or guild the party knows: *"Something is wearing my neighbors. Send someone who isn't from here."*

## PC Objectives (the plot, as goals rather than events)
1. **Find out what's wrong with Hollow Ford.** Routes: spot a double's tells; follow the straw trail from the reel; win Nettle's trust; eat the apple.
2. **Find Pip Tully.** Routes: the apple vision; Nettle's answer about "damaged goods"; the trail of wet straw upstream; Pip's empty scarecrow post in the fields.
3. **Identify the hag.** Routes: Nettle's "one stranger nobody suspects"; the hair braided into the dollies; green fingernail scrapings on the barrel; confronting Haskell at her stall.
4. **Keep the village from tearing itself apart.** Routes: talk down Corwin's mob; unmask Voss in front of the militia; prove a double is straw in public.
5. **Save the sleepers before midnight.** Routes: stop the wheel; destroy or break the doubles; cut sacks loose at the landing; outbid or out-read the Gentleman.
6. *(Optional door)* **Deal with the Gentleman in Grey.** Routes: bargain on the contract's wording; accept his calling card; refuse him and let the carriage leave empty.

## Subplots (keyed to PCs; assign at the table)
Pick one or two before the session. A subplot that belongs to a PC turns a rescue into a personal stake.
- **A face in the loft.** One of the sleepers is a PC's relative, old mentor, or childhood friend, in Hollow Ford for the market. Their Straw Double greets the PC by name in Scene 00, and gets a detail wrong.
- **The dolly with your hair.** Widow Haskell already has a corn dolly braided with a PC's hair. She bought it from someone the PC knows, which means someone in their past has dealt with a hag.
- **The contract has a familiar name.** A PC with a fey, warlock, or noble background finds their own family name among the Gentleman in Grey's prior customers.

## Act Structure
### Act 1 — Market Day and the Reel (≈60 min)
Scene 00 arrival, hooks, and the first tells. Scene 01, the closing reel and the Harvest Dancers. The straw trail points upstream.
### Act 2 — Something Is Wearing My Neighbors (≈95 min)
Scene 02, the Bobbing Game. Scene 03, Mother Nettle and the 10 PM mob. Scene 04, the fields, the stall, and Captain Voss. Three or more clues point clearly to the mill.
### Act 3 — The Mill at Hollow Ford (≈75 min)
Scene 05, the mill keyed M1–M6, the fight over the sacks, the wheel, and the midnight carriage.

- [[Pip Tully]] — Brann's son, asleep in a sack in the mill loft; remembers "a lady with green fingers."
- [[Hedda Voss]] — militia captain. **Straw Double.** The real Hedda is in the loft.
- [[Aldous Crane]] — the miller. **Straw Double.** "Closed for repairs."
- [[Father Corwin]] — priest of the Sower; honest, frightened, wrong about Nettle.

- [[The Gentleman in Grey]] — the fey buyer; arrives at midnight in a carriage drawn by black hares.

- **The Sheaf & Sickle** — inn and gossip hub; the Bobbing Game happens in the yard.
- **Chapel of the Sower** — Father Corwin's stone chapel, with a bell.
- **The Green** — market stalls by day, bonfire and closing reel after dark.
- **Mother Nettle's Cottage** — on the wood's edge, hung with herbs and rumors.
- **The Fields** — stubble fields where the extra scarecrows stand.
- **The Mill at Hollow Ford** — a quarter mile upstream; its wheel turns the wrong way. Keyed M1–M6 in [[Scene 05 — The Mill]].

## Treasure & Rewards
- **Treasure (M6):** 140 gp in mixed coin, 3 *potions of healing*, and Widow Haskell's battered tinker's hat, which is a *hat of disguise*.
- **The ledger:** the newest entry, in fresh ink, reads *"One reflection, sold to the Gentleman in Grey."* If a PC ate the apple, that's theirs. It pays off in [[SP-HAL-04-The-Tithe-of-Thornbury]].
- **The Gentleman's calling card:** a grey card good for one small favor. A fey debt the DM can call in later; in the chain, it opens the "doll for the field" ending.
- **The village's thanks:** free room and board at the Sheaf & Sickle for life, and Mother Nettle's friendship (spellcasting services at cost).
- **Story XP:** 200 per sleeper saved (max 12), 300 for stopping the wheel, 300 for out-reading the contract, 100 for talking down the mob without a blow.
- **Advancement:** in the chain, the party reaches 3rd level (4th if they started at 3rd).

## Denouement
The glamours fail at dawn. Doubles crumble to chaff in the street, and villagers wake in the loft, groggy and alive. Brann Tully carries Pip home. Father Corwin, if he saw a double unmasked, walks to Nettle's cottage and stands in the door a long time before he says anything.

### Endings at a Glance
| Ending | How | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **The wheel stops** (intended) | DC 14 Strength (Athletics) at M1 or a wedged object | Every double falls, every sleeper wakes. Chaff flees down the chute with a grudge. |
| **The hag dies** | Reduce Chaff to 0 before she flees | Same result at dawn. The carriage arrives at midnight and finds no one to collect. |
| **The contract is out-read** | Hold the contract; DC 15 Charisma or the loophole | Chaff has lived in Hollow Ford three weeks, so she counts. The Gentleman leaves with a hag-shaped corn dolly. |
| **Arrived late** | Any sleeper reaches the landing at midnight | That villager vanishes as a corn dolly. Their double collapses. The rest wake. |
| **Walked away** | The party leaves before midnight | Twelve villagers gone; Hollow Ford doesn't notice for a week. Chaff moves on. |
