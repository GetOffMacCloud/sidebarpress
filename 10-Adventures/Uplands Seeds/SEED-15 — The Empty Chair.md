# SEED-15 — The Empty Chair

**APL 5 (levels 5–6) · 4–6 characters · one 3–4 hour session**

**Pitch.** The students are guests at a Yati wedding where, by custom, a seat is kept empty for "the guest from the hill." This year someone sits in it, and every rule of hospitality suddenly has teeth.

---

## Background

### What the Locals Know
- Corrish weddings keep an empty chair, a salted cup, and an unbroken loaf for "the guest from the hill."
- No one remembers a guest ever coming. Old Fergus claims to remember the rules, and gets half of them wrong.
- Folklore students are welcome: the bride's father wants the old ways written down.

### What's Really Happening
- **Lord Thistleby**, a fey noble of the hill, comes once in a century to test whether mortals still keep faith with the old bargain.
- His rules: salt before speech, the first cup to the guest, no thanks given (thanks implies a debt), no names asked, and no iron at table.
- Each rule broken lets him "collect": a voice, a memory, a year, or the couple's luck.
- If enough rules break, his retinue comes down from the fairy ring at midnight to take what is owed.

### If Nobody Interferes
1. **The feast:** a cousin thanks the guest and loses his voice.
2. **The dance:** someone asks his name. The bride's memory of her groom fades.
3. **Midnight:** the retinue arrives, and the Corrish luck is taken for a hundred years.

## Adventure Outline (under 100 words)
The students arrive as guests and take field notes on the customs (1). The guest arrives, and the rules turn out to be real (2). A drunk cousin breaks one, and the collecting begins (3). The students must make things right by riddle, feast, or challenge at the fairy ring before midnight (4). The couple's luck rides on how they do it (5).

1. **Invitation (default).** The Folklore department pays 20 credits each for field notes on a Yati wedding.

## Running Notes
- **Clock:** midnight. Each broken rule moves the retinue's arrival an hour earlier.
- **Reaction tools:**
  - **Debt track:** each broken rule adds a debt. At 3 debts the retinue comes.
  - Thistleby escalates: amused → collecting → offended → summons his retinue.
  - The satyrs dance first and fight only if iron is drawn.
- **Nudges:** (1) a singer suddenly mute; (2) the bride forgets her groom's name; (3) pipes from the hill.

## Climax
- **Set piece:** the fairy ring on the hill above the steading at midnight, with lanterns, the retinue, and Thistleby on a stone throne.
- **Choices:**
  - **Riddle-feast:** three riddles, three dishes, three truths. Win a boon for the couple.
  - **Service:** a PC trades a year and a day of service. Everything is restored.
  - **Cold iron:** banish him. The Corrish line loses its luck.

## Concluding the Adventure
- **A — boon won:** 20 credits plus 10 extra credit each (exceptional field notes); a fey boon (one *potion of healing* that never empties fully; `50-Items`).
- **B — debt paid:** a PC owes service. Everything is restored. Plot thread.
- **C — banished:** the night is safe, but the Corrish luck fails for a hundred years.

## Key NPCs
**Lord Thistleby**, fey noble. [Old Faith, Obad-Hai's court]. *Wants:* proof that mortals keep faith. *Knows:* every rule, and every guest's secret name. *Reacts:* amused → collecting → offended → retinue. *Stats:* green hag reskin, or non-combat.

**Siobh Corrish**, the bride. [Beory]. *Wants:* her wedding, and her memory. *Reacts:* brave and grieving.

**Old Fergus Corrish**, keeper of the rules. [Beory]. *Wants:* to be right. *Knows:* the rules, half wrong. *Reacts:* argues, then admits.

**Grandmother Áine Corrish**. *Knows:* the true rules and the redemption tale.
