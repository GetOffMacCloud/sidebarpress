# Scene 01 — The Hundredth Feast

[[10-Adventures/SP-HAL-04 — The Tithe of Thornbury/Scenes/00 — Scene Index]] · ← [[Scene 00 — Harvest Home]] · Next → [[Scene 02 — The Long Night]]

## Purpose
Get someone welcomed at the table, and let the Empty Chair warn them while it happens. By the end, the loaf has been eaten, by a PC or by Ned, and the party has heard the dead guest speak.
**Budget:** 40 minutes. Running long? Cut the toasts; keep the chair and the loaf.

## Arrival
> *The Bountiful Board's long room is laid for a hundred. Candles stand in rows down the table, and at its head sits one carved chair, garlanded in wheat and set with a silver plate. Nobody sits in it. The whole village files in behind you and waits, standing, until Prudence Thorne lifts her hands. "The Guest's Chair," she says. "Kept empty a hundred years, for luck. Tonight it needn't be."*

**To the table:** *"She's looking at one of you. Which one, and do you sit?"*

## Who Is Here
- **[[Prudence Thorne]]** at the Elder's place, beside the Empty Chair. *Wants:* a PC in the chair and the loaf in their hand.
- **[[Barnaby Reap]]** carrying the Hundredth Loaf on a silver board. *Reacts:* his hands shake when he sets it down.
- **[[Old Ned Tallow]]**, two seats down, already on his second helping.
- **[[Tamsin Reap]]**, serving. *Reacts:* when the loaf comes round she goes still behind the PC she likes best, and says nothing.
- The Sheaf: ninety villagers, every one smiling.

## Beats

### The Empty Chair *(Eerie Encounter)*
Prudence invites the most honored PC to sit. A PC who sits makes a **DC 15 Wisdom saving throw**; on a failure they are frightened for 1 minute. Either way, they hear the last words of the guest given to the field a hundred years ago, whispered from inside the wood:

> *"They smiled the whole time. They cried, and they smiled. Don't eat the bread."*

Only the sitter hears it. If nobody sits, the chair speaks anyway when the loaf passes it: the nearest PC hears the last four words.

### The Hundredth Loaf
Barnaby sets it down with flour to the elbows and tears in his eyes, which he blames on the oven. The loaf is passed around the table from the Guest's Chair outward. Eating from it while seated at the table makes that creature *one who was welcomed at the table*, which fulfills the pact's terms.

- *Detect magic* shows a faint aura of enchantment on the whole loaf.
- **Intelligence (Arcana or Nature) DC 15** identifies it as a binding, old and simple: a tie between the eater and a place.
- **Refusing** is a serious insult in Thornbury. The whole hall goes quiet. Prudence's smile does not move. Then she says, *"Well. There's a hundred of us. Someone will,"* and Ned takes a second slice to cover the silence.
- A PC who palms the slice, feeds it to a dog, or hides it in a sleeve has **not** eaten. **Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) DC 13** against ninety pairs of eyes; on a failure Barnaby gently puts another slice on their plate.

### The toasts
Three if you have time, one if you don't. Each toast is a clue dressed as courtesy:
1. *"To the founders, who went into the field and came back with a promise."*
2. *"To ninety-nine good years, and the hundredth best of all."*
3. *"To our guest. Whoever that may be."* Laughter, and Prudence looks at the chair.

### Tamsin
When the loaf passes her, she leans over the shoulder of the PC she has watched since the green and says, very low, *"Don't—"* and then Barnaby says her name across the room, and she straightens and pours cider. **Wisdom (Insight) DC 12:** she was warning them, and she is afraid of her father.

### Bundle: setting a place for the bell
If the party carries the Vesper Bell and asks for a seat for it, Prudence is baffled but hospitable; a chair is brought and the bell set on it, and a slice of the loaf is laid on its rim. She does not understand what she has just done. This is the seed for the Reaper-as-guest ending; don't explain it.

## Encounter (if any)
None. If a PC draws steel at the table, the Sheaf does not fight back. Prudence says, *"Please. Not in front of the children,"* and the room empties. The flameskull on the step wakes (Scene 02).

## Clues Available Here
- The chair's whisper → the village has done this before, and the bread is the trap
- *Detect magic* / Arcana DC 15 on the loaf → the loaf is a binding
- The third toast → the guest hasn't been chosen yet
- Tamsin's *"Don't—"* → there is a doubter in the Sheaf

## Sensory Palette
- **Sight:** a hundred candles; a carved chair garlanded in wheat; ninety smiles that don't reach the eyes after 10 PM
- **Sound:** the whole room going quiet at once; a knife on a silver board
- **Smell:** fresh bread, hot and sweet, under it something like turned earth
- **Touch:** the chair's arms are warm; the loaf is still steaming
- **Taste:** the best bread any of them has eaten, with a faint grit of soil

## Alternates & Re-hooks
- **If a PC eats:** the sheaf-mark appears on their palm at 10 PM (Scene 02). They are the guest unless the party changes it.
- **If no PC eats:** Ned is the guest. Make it personal; he toasts the PC he knows.
- **If the party exposes the loaf publicly** (casts *detect magic* and says so): Prudence doesn't deny it. *"Yes. It's how we keep you. Please sit down."* The village's honesty is its horror.
- **If the party skips the feast:** the loaf is brought to their room on a tray. Ned eats in the hall anyway.

## Transition
- → [[Scene 02 — The Long Night]]: the bonfire is lit on the green, and every turnip lantern on every step turns its face toward the inn door as the party comes out.
