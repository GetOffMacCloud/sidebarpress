# Scene 05 — The Tithe Barn

[[10-Adventures/SP-HAL-04 — The Tithe of Thornbury/Scenes/00 — Scene Index]] · ← [[Scene 04 — The Procession]]

## Purpose
The ending, chosen by the players from the solutions they were given. Keep the guest out of the pit, or put something else in it, or decide not to. This scene is never cut.
**Budget:** 50 minutes. Running long? Shorten Scene 04 instead. If you must, drop the lofts (T3).

## Arrival
> *The barn is older than the village, a cathedral of black timber with its doors thrown open to the night. Inside, wheat grows up through the floorboards in gold waves that sway with no wind. In the dark beyond the rafters something vast creaks like a tree in a storm, and the whole floor breathes.*

**To the table:** *"It's five minutes to midnight. Where are each of you standing when the singing stops?"*

## Who Is Here
- **[[The Harvest Lord]]**, rooted in the pit at T5. See [[SP-HAL-04 — Bestiary]].
- **[[Prudence Thorne]]** at the pit's edge with her sickle. She is here to speak, not to cast. She fights only if attacked.
- **The guest**, carried in by six men, or walking in under their own power.
- **The Sheaf**, filling the threshing floor, singing.
- **[[Hesper Gloam]]** at the rear door if asked; **[[Tamsin Reap]]** holding it open.
- **Bundle:** the **Gentleman in Grey**, if called; **Thorne**, if free; allies from earlier villages, if sent for.

## Beats

### Midnight
The singing stops. Prudence stands at the pit's edge and speaks the giving, plainly: *"One who came uninvited, and was welcomed at our table. We give you to the field, and we thank you."* Six men carry the guest to the lip. Unless the party has a better idea, the Harvest Lord's roots take the guest and pull them under. It takes one round. Nobody screams; the wheat closes over.

### The Harvest Lord
It does not want to fight. It wants what it is owed. It speaks through the wheat, in a rustle that is almost words, and it will talk for as long as the party is willing to listen: patient, ancient, and faintly puzzled that anyone objects. If attacked, or if the guest is pulled from the lip, it fights.

- **Lair actions** on initiative 20: *Grasping Wheat* (creatures of its choice on the threshing floor, DC 15 Str save or restrained) and *Harvest Bounty* (it regains 20 hp).
- **Harvest Bounty's cost.** Each use withers a field outside to black stubble, visible through the open doors, and the singing falters. Tick the tracker. After the **third** use, the Sheaf on the floor can see the pact eating its own promise. The hymn stops dead. A PC can turn the village with a **DC 12 Charisma (Persuasion) check**: Prudence calls the field's name to stop it, and the Harvest Lord loses its lair actions for the rest of the fight. Make the withering visible; it is the fight's social lever.
- **Animate Trees:** two of the barn's great timbers tear free as **awakened trees** and fight for it.

### The five endings
Run whichever the party reaches for. Each was seeded earlier; if they reach for one that wasn't, price it honestly.

1. **Break the pact.** Kill the Harvest Lord. The fields rot overnight. The party wins the fight and loses the valley's plenty, and the Sheaf knows exactly whose fault it is. If the village was turned, Prudence lays down her sickle first; if not, the Sheaf drags at the party's legs in the wheat (no damage, but each PC adjacent to three or more villagers has disadvantage on attack rolls until they move).
2. **The Reaper as guest** *(bundle)*. If the Vesper Bell was seated at the feast and given a slice, a PC can carry it to the lip and give it to the field: *"One who came uninvited, and was welcomed at the table."* The Harvest Lord's roots close over the bell. From inside, very faintly, Thorne says *"Oh, well done."* The pact is fulfilled and ends. Thornbury keeps its fields. Future harvests are ordinary.
3. **A doll for the field** *(bundle)*. A PC tears the Gentleman in Grey's card. A carriage drawn by four black hares stops at the great doors, and the Gentleman steps out, faceless and polite. He makes a corn-dolly double of the guest in the time it takes to say so, because he already holds that guest's reflection, or because he takes one now. The field accepts its own kind, given. The pact closes. The guest casts no shadow afterward.
4. **Pay the pact.** A guest is given. A PC can volunteer, and take the seat from Ned or from a marked friend. The roots are quick. Thornbury has a hundred more years, and the party has an empty chair at their own table.
5. **Burn it all.** Fire at the base of the Harvest Lord, or a burning Colossus at the doors, or the lofts' century of cloth and wood. The barn goes up in 10 minutes. It works like breaking the pact, and the Sheaf blames the party forever; Thornbury becomes an enemy with a long memory and a hungry winter.

### Bundle payoffs at the pit
- **The Vesper Bell**, rung as an action: every awakened tree and shambling mound within 60 feet makes a **DC 15 Wisdom saving throw** or is frightened of Thorne's presence until the end of its next turn.
- **Thorne free:** the wraith goes for the guest, not the party. Reduce the Harvest Lord to 110 hp; Thorne and the tree are competing for the same meal and will not cooperate.
- **Allies sent for:** Mother Nettle, Tobias Marrow, Ambrose Hale, or Annick Rook's crows. One useful action each: a door held, the cage opened, the hymn broken, the field's name called.

### After
Whichever ending, the sun comes up on Thornbury. Read the Endings at a Glance in [[SP-HAL-04-The-Tithe-of-Thornbury]] and give the party the morning: a village that has to learn ordinary seasons, or a village that got its hundred years, or a village that will never forgive them. Hand out the rewards, and the epilogue if this closes the bundle.

**Terrain:** the threshing floor (restraining wheat, DC 14), the lofts (30-ft drop, cover from the rail), the root cellar (flanking route to the heart, out of sight), the pit (the Harvest Lord's 20-ft reach covers the whole lip). · **Tactics:** it grabs the nearest creature to the guest and drags them away from the lip; it uses Harvest Bounty whenever it's below half hit points, and does not understand why that makes the singing stop. The awakened trees stand between the party and the guest. · **DM direction:** the number on paper is above the deadly line. Three things keep it fair: the Colossus is **never** in this fight; Prudence does not cast; and Harvest Bounty's cost turns the village. If the party is losing, let the Sheaf's turn come one Bounty early. If they're cruising, Thorne arrives.

## Clues Available Here
- The lintel → the pact's words, one last time
- The lofts' labelled piles → ninety-nine names (the sequel)
- The withering fields → the pact's plenty was never free

## Sensory Palette
- **Sight:** gold wheat swaying with no wind; a treant the color of bone; a field going black through the open doors
- **Sound:** two hundred voices stopping at once; roots moving under boards; the bell, if it rings, like a struck anvil
- **Smell:** warm soil; a century of old cloth in the lofts; if it burns, every harvest at once
- **Touch:** the floor rising and falling underfoot; roots closing on a leg, warm and patient
- **Taste:** chaff; soil on the lips when the wheat closes over

## Alternates & Re-hooks
- **If the party is in the root cellar at midnight:** they're under the lip, out of sight, 10 feet from the heart. Surprise round on the Harvest Lord, or a quiet word with it before Prudence speaks.
- **If the guest is already dead** (killed on the road, or by the party): the pact wants a guest *given*. A corpse welcomed at the table counts (*"one who came uninvited"* doesn't say alive). Prudence gives it, and the pact is paid. Grim, and legal.
- **If the party refuses every ending and simply leaves:** the field takes Ned, or a child by lots. The fields stand. The party walks out of a valley that got what it needed, and the Gentleman's card is still in their pocket.
- **If a player invents a sixth ending:** say yes, then price it. The pact's four phrases are the rules; anything that satisfies them works.

## Transition
- End of adventure. If running the bundle: the four messages of the epilogue, and the grey card.
