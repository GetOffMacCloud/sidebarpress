# Scene 00 — Three Weeks in Aerden

## Purpose
The players leave this scene knowing what Aerden looks and sounds like, exactly how broke they are, and how they came to share a table — because they chose it. They are not friends. Yet. The notice is the last thing that happens.
**Budget:** 20 minutes. Running long? Skip the market and the butts; keep Grete's board and the notice.

## Why They Are Here
Every one of them is going to Ritterstadt, to enroll at Greyhawk University's field station across Lake Quag. The University takes anyone who signs its Oath. Getting there is the problem. The northern packet out of Schwartzenbruin, ninety miles down the shore, is one night to Ritterstadt's quay and 20 marks a head. At the far end the Registrar asks a first-term bond: 80 marks in coin, or a term of labor on the levy. **A hundred marks each to arrive clear**, and none of them has twenty. The free way is the shore road east to Traft and four days north on the caravan track: two weeks on foot, and nobody calls it safe.

**Hand this out first.** Each player picks one or writes their own. No two the same.

| How you came to be stuck in Aerden |
|---|
| The coach to the lake left a day early. Nobody told you. |
| You were hired to guard a wool wagon to Osterschlauss. The wool got there. Your pay didn't. |
| You came over the Yatil passes on foot to save the fare. The map said six days. It was fifteen. |
| A letter of credit drawn on a Krestible counting-house. No one in Aerden will honor it. |
| You were sick a week in a farmhouse outside town. The farmer's wife wouldn't take money, so you paid the innkeeper for the farmer's ale instead. That was most of what you had. |
| You won't say. You've been asked twice. |

## Arrival
> *Aerden sits where the last of the Yatil foothills flatten into meadow. Everything is grey slate and cream plaster, and every doorpost in town carries a painted band at shoulder height: red over black, blue over white, a green so dark it looks black in shadow. Two doorposts are bare. One belongs to the inn. The other belongs to a big slate-roofed house at the top of the hill, with paddocks behind it.*

**To the table:** *"You've eaten at Grete's long table for three weeks. Who at it do you still not trust, and why?"*

## Who Is Here
- **Grete Haslau**, widow, keeper of the Mountainview Rest. *Wants:* her board squared and her house quiet after vespers. *Knows:* everyone's debts to the pfennig, and that Hessel has never bought his own ale in fifteen years, because every market day for a year he has paid a Vuurzward man at the well instead. *Reacts:* she refuses thanks, makes arrangements instead of giving credit, and has already decided the party are decent.
- **Tilo**, her yard boy, nine. *Wants:* pfennigs. *Knows:* everything. *Reacts:* tells it for a pfennig, in the order he thinks of it.
- **Old Hartwin**, tollkeeper at the Erlen bridge, at the small table by the door every third night. *Wants:* an audience. *Knows:* every wheel that crosses the bridge by its sound. *Reacts:* talks about the bridge until stopped.
- **Kaspar Roodberg**, the farrier by the well, a Roodberg cousin who shoes in the open and takes Derby bets between horses. *Wants:* your bet. *Knows:* the odds on every horse he's heard of. *Reacts:* offers longer odds the less a horse is known.
- **Meerijder drovers**, up from the bridge camp most evenings: red-and-black on their sleeves, loud, honest, and good at cards.

## Beats

### The town, as they look
Read the box, then give these one at a time as the players look around, not as a list.
- **The bands are clan colors, and painting them is law.** A house with no band has no clan. The people in the house on the hill wear grey; the dress-law says they may wear nothing else.
- **The green** has arrow-chewed straw targets at the far end. On Godsday mornings the militia shoots there, longbows on foot and short bows from the saddle, and the whole town comes out with small beer to watch.
- **Cheese** is the smell of the place: caves cut into the hill behind the market, milk carts before dawn. Under it, when the wind is west, the burnt-metal smell of the copper smelters at the foot of the pass.
- **Half the men over forty** walk like soldiers and wear a foreign coin on a cord: Furyondy, Veluna, Ket. Perrenland's export is its sons. They notice armed strangers the way a carpenter notices a door hung wrong.
- **The chapel bell** tells the day: first bell at dawn, the noon bell, the third bell when the market closes, vespers at dusk. Nobody owns a clock.
- **Every conversation** gets to the Derby. Duffkyyn is a week off and ninety miles down the shore, and it is the only thing anyone wants to talk about.

### Grete's board
Chalked above the bar where everyone can read it. Fill it in from the players' choices and let them read it.

| | Owes | Worked off |
|---|---|---|
| *(PC)* | 2 marks 6 | 3 days, caves |
| *(PC)* | 1 mark 9 | yard watch, 4 nights |
| *(PC)* | 3 marks — | — |
| *(PC)* | 1 mark 2 | wood, roof, one chimney |

Let one of them notice who has worked off nothing, and let that be a conversation or not.

*Grete, to anyone who tries to thank her:* "Don't. I'll only put it on the board."

### Three weeks at the long table
They know each other's names and what each one eats. Between them they have unloaded milk carts, mucked the Halbritter paddocks for a day-rate (3 kleinmarks, and a look from the woman who runs the yard that said *you've done this before*), stood a night watch on the cheese caves, and each lost four kleinmarks to a drover at cards. The University came up the first week, and since then it is the one thing they don't joke about. If a player wants to say what they mean to study, let them. Don't make them.

### Gossip, if they ask
| Ask | Answer (Tilo, for a pfennig) |
|---|---|
| The grey house? | *"Herr Otwin's got the gout and the Jungfer runs the horses and the Junker's got a new saddle he doesn't let anyone see."* |
| The sergeant? | *"Sergeant Amtmann. His wife was a Vuurzward. She still paints her mother's door green. And he didn't pay the green man at the well this week."* |
| The Derby odds? | Kaspar, the farrier by the well: *"Roodberg bay, three to one. You want longer odds, you want a horse nobody's heard of, and there isn't one."* |

### The notice
Dusk on Wealsun 26, their twentieth day at the Rest, at vespers. A man in a plain grey house-guard's jerkin, no band on the sleeve, nods to Grete, pins a sheet to the post by the bar, and **buys his own ale**. He drinks half and speaks to the room, not to them:

*"That's an honest job for honest folk. The Junker pays on arrival and I'll vouch for it. Anyone who can walk ten miles and sit a deck for two nights without being sick over the side — Erlenhof, first bell, day after tomorrow. Ask for Amtmann."*

He finishes the ale and leaves. Grete watches him go and says, to nobody: *"Hessel Amtmann. He's never bought his own ale in this house in fifteen years. Every market day for a year he's paid a Vuurzward man at the well instead. Not this week."*

Hand over SP-PBS-00 — Handout — The Notice.

**Then read the table.** Thirty marks is more than any of them owes Grete, and passage is worth another forty. It puts them on the quay the northern packet sails from, with the fare in hand and seventy short of arriving clear. It is also a great deal of money for walking a rich boy to a horse race. Let them talk. Let them not quite trust it. Don't help.

## Clues Available Here
- Hessel buys his own ale tonight, and has stopped paying the Vuurzward man → **his debt to the Vuurzwards is gone** (Hessel sold the route)
- Tilo: the sergeant's wife was a Vuurzward → **Hessel and Vuurzward are connected**
- Tilo: the new saddle nobody sees → **the Junker is entering a horse**

## Sensory Palette
- **Sight:** grey slate, cream plaster, clan bands at shoulder height, Grete's chalk board
- **Sound:** cowbells in the meadows at all hours, the chapel bell, a fiddle played badly at vespers
- **Smell:** cheese, woodsmoke, wet wool, the smelters when the wind is west
- **Touch:** slate underfoot, cold even in summer
- **Taste:** small beer, thin and sour; rye bread hard enough to knock on

## Alternates & Re-hooks
- **Don't name the Halbritters before the notice.** "The grey house at the top of the hill" is enough. The name comes with the job.
- **The table refuses the job.** Adalinde finds them at the Rest that night with her own money and a straighter story, and comes with them. Otwin's men are now looking for *her*; see Scene 01 — Erlenhof.
- **Pregens.** The arrival table is the whole session zero. Don't skip it.

## Transition
- → Scene 01 — Erlenhof: first bell, the morning after next, they walk up the hill.

# Scene 01 — Erlenhof

## Purpose
Hire them, and plant the lie where they can find it. The third wagon must not be handed over; it is there to be found.
**Budget:** 25 minutes. Running long? One question to Adalinde, not three.

## Arrival
> *The grey house sits at the top of the hill with its back to the mountains. The doorpost beside the gate is bare, old wood scrubbed pale by forty years of rain. Behind it the land opens into paddocks full of horses, and a woman in a grey riding coat walks the fence line with a bucket, not hurrying. Three wagons stand in the yard. The third is shuttered and rides low on its springs. A man in a house-guard's jerkin comes out to meet you before you reach the door, and steers you away from it.*

**To the table:** *"The sergeant says the Junker is at the stable. Who looks at the boy, and who looks at the horses?"*

## Who Is Here
- **Hessel Amtmann**, sergeant of the house guard. *Wants:* the debt gone and his hands clean. *Knows:* everything; he sold the route ten days ago. *Reacts:* answers a different question than the one asked; says "I couldn't spare a man" three different ways.
- **Corwin Halbritter**, *Junker*, 19. *Wants:* to be taken seriously once. *Knows:* the horse, the forged seal, the rented colors; not the leak. *Reacts:* talks past the end of his own sentences; goes quiet when someone competent questions the plan.
- **Adalinde Halbritter**, *Jungfer*, 24. *Wants:* to ride that mare at Duffkyyn, and will not say so. *Knows:* everything about the mare, nothing about the note. *Reacts:* says less than she knows and never less than the truth; watches which of them looks at the horses instead of the boy.
- **Maus**, in the third wagon, being quiet in the dark.

## Beats

### Hessel, walking them out
*"You'll find the Junker enthusiastic. That's not a warning. He's nineteen. Answer what he asks and don't volunteer, and you'll get on."*

| If asked… | Hessel says |
|---|---|
| Why the house guard isn't going | *"Six men. Four are cousins to the tollkeeper, and the tollkeeper is Herr Otwin's man to the bone. You see the difficulty."* He never says what the difficulty is. |
| What's in the wagons | *"The Junker's baggage. He travels heavy. Nobles do."* He looks at the third wagon while he says it. |
| Who else knows about the trip | *"Nobody. That's rather the point."* This is the one lie he tells badly: **Insight DC 12** catches that he is frightened, not why. |

### Corwin, the hiring
He comes out of the stable too fast, wiping his hands, talking before he has crossed the yard: *"You're the ones from the Rest. Good. It's a simple thing. You walk beside the wagon, you look like men who've killed someone, and in Schwartzenbruin I pay you. Ten miles to the water, two nights on a boat, and then it's the Derby. I'll want you sober at the quay. That's the only condition."*

**The players decide the terms.**

| Push | Corwin |
|---|---|
| Once | *"Forty, then. Forty. It's not — fine. Forty."* |
| Twice, or ask for a share | *"A share of — "* He looks at the stable door. *"One part in five of whatever she — of whatever the trip earns. If it earns."* |

*That slip is free:* any player who catches it has **advantage on Insight** for the rest of the scene. Pressing for the share is the first thing that makes Adalinde look at them twice.

### The third wagon
Shuttered, bedded with straw, low on its springs, and it shifts when nothing is touching it. Asked what's in it, Corwin says *"Baggage,"* then *"My baggage,"* then (a third ask, or **Insight DC 13**) *"Why does everyone keep asking me that?"*

**If they open it:** straw, warmth, and a grey mare near enough to white at the flank, who turns her head and looks at them with enormous patience. Corwin folds at once and badly: *"All right. All right. She's a horse. She's* the *horse. If you walk away now I understand. But you'd be walking away from forty marks, and I'd be walking to Schwartzenbruin alone, and I am going to Schwartzenbruin."*

A party that opens the wagon in the first five minutes has not broken anything. Corwin folding early is a better Act 1, and Adalinde sizes them up sooner. **Leave it shut, and it opens itself at the bridge.**

### Adalinde at the fence
She does not come to them; they come to her, or they don't meet her.

*"You took the job, then."* (Beat.) *"He told you it's luggage."*

| If asked… | Adalinde says |
|---|---|
| (They say yes, luggage) | *"It weighs nine hundredweight and it kicked the boards out of that wagon last Godsday. You may draw your own conclusions. I'd draw them before the bridge, myself."* |
| Whose horse it is | *"The farm's."* (A pause just long enough to notice.) *"I bred her. I broke her. Her name is Maus, and if anybody at Duffkyyn calls her that I'll know where they heard it."* |
| Whether the plan will work | *"No."* (If they wait:) *"But he's going anyway, and he's going alone if you don't. So."* She hands the bucket to whoever is nearest, which is the first small test. |
| Whether she rides | *"I ride."* That is the complete answer. **Note who asked**: that player has found the climax three scenes early and should get the payoff in Scene 7. |
| Her father | *"He's signing a remount contract in Schwartzenbruin at noon on Derby Day. Thirty head over three years. It's a good contract."* She says *good contract* the way you'd say *good weather at a funeral*. |

## Development
- The party is employed at 30 or 40 marks, with or without the one-in-five share. Record it; it decides the reward table.
- Hessel watches the party leave from the gate. He does not come.

## Clues Available Here
- The wagon rides low and shifts; Adalinde's nine hundredweight → **there is a racehorse in the third wagon**
- *"I ride."* → **Adalinde should ride**
- Hessel's bad lie → **Hessel is frightened of someone**

## Sensory Palette
- **Sight:** a bare doorpost, thirty horses in long morning light, a woman in grey with a bucket
- **Sound:** hooves on packed earth, a wagon spring creaking with nobody near it
- **Smell:** straw, horse, neatsfoot oil
- **Touch:** the warm side of the shuttered wagon
- **Taste:** dust from the yard

## Alternates & Re-hooks
- **The party refuses the job.** Adalinde finds them at the Rest that night with her own money and a straighter story, and comes along. This removes the Scene 7 rider problem and replaces it: Otwin's men are looking for *her*, and at post time the problem is Otwin, not Corwin.
- **The party takes the job and never looks.** Fine. The bridge reveals it.
- **A PC tries to tell Otwin.** He is in the house with his ledger and the gout. He hears them out, thanks them, and sends the writ an hour sooner. Run Scene 2 with Boedic already standing in the road.

## Transition
- → Scene 02 — The Erlen Bridge: the wagons roll at first bell; the road drops toward the river, and there are cows on it.

# Scene 02 — The Erlen Bridge

## Purpose
The first real decision, and the reveal if it hasn't happened. **This scene is built to be talked through.** A fight is legal and expensive.
**Budget:** 30 minutes. Running long? At 25 minutes Boedic says *"I've cows to move,"* and takes the bribe.

## Arrival
> *The road drops toward the water and there are cows on it, forty or fifty head strung along the verge, with men in red-and-black walking beside them. Beyond them is a stone bridge, a toll house, and the road to the lake. One drover has been watching you come for a while. He is older than the rest, and he holds a folded paper the way you hold something you'd rather not have been given. He steps into the road, hands well away from his belt. "Morning. Would one of you be the Junker Halbritter?"*

**To the table:** *"He hasn't asked you anything. He's asked the boy. Who answers for him?"*

## Who Is Here
- **Boedic of the Meerijder**, drover. *Wants:* to obey a lawful writ and go home to his cows. *Knows:* the writ says hold the wagons and the Junker until the Ratsherr comes; it says nothing about the party. *Reacts:* explains himself, asks questions, apologizes while not moving.
- **Five Meerijder drovers** (three at APL 1) with cattle goads, and (APL 3–4) two mastiffs trained to hold cloth.
- **Old Hartwin**, 68, in the toll house. Will not fight; remembers faces.

## Beats

### The writ
*"I've a writ here from Ratsherr Halbritter. Ink's not dry; it came down on a pony an hour past. It says hold the wagons and hold the Junker until the Ratsherr comes himself."* (He looks at the party, honestly puzzled.) *"It doesn't say a word about you lot. So I'd as soon you stood over there and had some bread while we sort it. Nobody wants a Fehde over a horse."*

He says **horse**. If nobody has opened the wagon, that is the reveal, and Corwin's face does the rest.

*The players decide* whether to open the wagon in front of Boedic. He is an honest witness, which cuts both ways.

### Getting past — pick one approach, not a gauntlet
Any **one** of these ends the scene.

| Approach | Check | Boedic |
|---|---|---|
| Claim clan kinship (a plausible surname) | **Automatic** | He takes his hat off: *"Cousin. You should have led with that."* He stands aside and tells his kin at the Flats a clansman was running a horse for the clanless. A favor *spent*. |
| The jurisdiction argument: Aerden's tolls, Aerden's council, a Halbritter family quarrel with no Meerijder interest in it | Persuasion DC 13, or **automatic** if the player makes the argument aloud | *"You're telling me I'm a Meerijder drover stood in the middle of a Halbritter family quarrel."* (Beat.) *"Aye. That's about the size of it."* He wants it on record that he is not happy. |
| Bribe | 6 marks is his day-fee; he takes **12**, no roll | He counts it without looking at anyone. *"I was never here, and neither were you."* |
| Forge a counter-writ | DC 15 with a forgery kit, DC 17 without | Failure: he sends a rider to Erlenhof, and Otwin comes at dusk |
| Spook the cattle across the road | Animal Handling DC 14 | Buys a crossing, and they are now cattle thieves in a canton of stockmen |
| Sneak the wagons over at night | Stealth DC 13 (group) | Costs a day and the *Kortheim Maid*; see Alternates |
| Fight | — | See Encounter |

### If they're held: Otwin at dusk
**This is a scene, not a wall.** The cart comes at dusk with a lantern on the rail, and the old man in it doesn't get down, because getting down would cost him something. He looks at the third wagon, then at his son. *"Corwin."* That's all. He waits.

Corwin, with nothing prepared: *"She's the best horse we've ever had and you're going to sell her to the levy."*

Otwin: *"Yes. Thirty horses over three years, paid in silver, quarterly, by the canton. That is a future. A race is an afternoon. I have buried a wife and a son in this ground and I will not bury the farm for an afternoon."*

**The winning move is asking to see the ledger.** He has it under the seat. Of course he does. Investigation DC 14, or automatic for a PC with trade or bookkeeping: the remount contract pays 90 marks a quarter, the tolls it is set against are worth 60, and **the place purse at Duffkyyn is 500**. Tell him that and he goes quiet. *"Five hundred. And if she loses?"* Any honest answer gets them past: Persuasion DC 13, with advantage if they showed him the number.

- **Win it:** *"Go on, then. Go. Tell my daughter — no. Tell her nothing. She'll have worked it out."*
- **Lose it:** Otwin takes the mare home. At midnight the party reaches Osterschlauss to find Adalinde on the quay with the mare she led out of the barn an hour after her father brought it in: *"He put her in the near stall and went to bed. I have a key to the near stall. I've had it since I was twelve. Are you coming, or shall I find the boat myself?"*

**Keep the writ.** It names Otwin, and it is evidence later (Otwin's Writ).

## Encounter (if any)
| APL | Creatures | Difficulty (4 PCs) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Boedic + 3 drovers | Deadly (1,500) |
| 2 | Boedic + 5 drovers | Deadly (1,900) |
| 3 | Boedic + 5 drovers + 2 mastiffs | Deadly (2,500) |
| 4 | Boedic + 5 drovers + 2 mastiffs | Deadly (2,500) |

Boedic uses the **bandit captain** block reflavored; drovers are **thugs**; mastiffs hold, they don't bite. See SP-PBS-00 — Bestiary.

**Terrain:** a stone toll bridge three wagons wide, waist-high walls, a toll house at the near end (half cover), and forty-odd cattle on the road behind. The river is four feet deep and slow: a shove over the wall is 1d6 falling damage and a wet, furious drover, not a drowning. **The cattle are terrain:** any loud noise (a shout, a scream, a thunderous spell) sets them milling, and the road is difficult terrain for 2 rounds; everyone in it makes a **DC 11 Dexterity save or falls prone**.
**Tactics:** round 1 they form a line across the bridge and do not advance. Boedic spends his first turn talking. They target the wagon team, not the party: the goal is to stop wheels. **Morale breaks at two drovers down:** *"Enough. Enough! Take the bloody bridge, and when the Ratsherr asks, you tell him I bled for it."*
**DM direction:** say it before initiative: **knocking out is free** (any melee attack), and **killing a clansman without a declared Fehde is murder**. A party that kills drovers has started a feud with the Meerijder and will meet it at the Flats. Most parties take the hint.

# Scene 03 — Osterschlauss

## Purpose
One clean problem-solving beat; a breather with teeth. Get sixteen hands of grey aboard a passage booked for one man and his baggage. This is the moment Corwin stops being the employer.
**Budget:** 15 minutes. Running long? Narrate the loading and keep only the clerk's one question.

## Arrival
> *The road comes over a rise and the lake is just there, filling the whole of the east: grey-green, flat, with the far shore only a smudge that might be cloud. Osterschlauss is a hundred houses and a stone quay. Tied up against the quay is a fat little cog with one mast and her name painted on the stern,* Kortheim Maid. *At the head of the gangway stands a clerk with a roll of paper, writing down everything that goes aboard.*

**To the table:** *"Corwin has stopped at the foot of the gangway. He's looking at you. Who goes up first?"*

## Who Is Here
- **Captain Marda Vosk**, master of the *Kortheim Maid*. *Wants:* declared cargo and paid fare. *Knows:* what selling a horse to the Rhennee means on the Flats. *Reacts:* asks nothing about weight that's on the roll and everything about weight that isn't; prices everything, including silence.
- **Ebbo Kühn**, the Hafenmeister's clerk, with the *Frachtrolle*. *Wants:* every entry in its column. *Knows:* that a roll read aloud in Aerden is a roll Otwin hears. *Reacts:* asks about anything undeclared, takes a bribe and asks why, and remembers. The roll goes up to Aerden with the evening carter and is read aloud in the market, which is why Corwin couldn't book the horse: booking her would have been telling his father.
- **Corwin Halbritter**, out of plan.

## Beats

### Corwin runs out of plan

# Scene 04 — Two Nights on the Quag

## Purpose
The adventure's breathing room, its biggest temptation, and its first fight. The party leaves knowing Vuurzward's name and the phrase *ten days*.
**Budget:** 40 minutes (evening one 10, evening two 15, the small hours 15). Running long? Cut the card game, keep the offer, or open on the second night.

## Arrival
> *The shore slides past on the right hand all afternoon and never comes closer or goes further. Every captain on this lake keeps land in sight. Out in the middle there is grey fog that doesn't lift. There are sixteen passengers: a cheese factor and his wife, two Roodberg grooms already drunk, a woman traveling alone who reads, and a thin man near the mast who has said nothing to anyone and was aboard before you were.*

**To the table:** *"Two nights, sixteen strangers, one canvas stall. Who's sleeping next to the horse?"*

*The thin man is Anse Krul. Do not point at him. Include him every time you list the passengers.*

## Who Is Here
- **Anse Krul**, farrier. *Wants:* his forty marks. *Knows:* Vuurzward's man paid him, and gave him the boat and the day ten days back; not who told them. *Reacts:* runs before he fights; cornered, names his employer without much pressing.
- **Captain Marda Vosk**. Knows the price of everything, including the Rhennee offer.
- **The Rhennee**: three long barges, and **Zinka of the Kestrel** at the rail, who knows the mare's stable name. *Wants:* the mare, for someone she won't name. *Knows:* only that she was told to watch this boat. *Reacts:* makes the 600-mark offer once, reads a fortune for anyone who crosses the plank, and lets the barges drop back if refused.
- **Corwin Halbritter**, talking too much on the first evening.

## Beats

### Evening one (10 min)
The boat, the passengers, Corwin talking. Seed Krul as a face. A PC who knows horses notices the thin man has farrier's hands (scarred, a burn across one palm) and that he looks at the deck stall the way a buyer looks at a sale ring.

### Evening two — the Rhennee (15 min)
> *Three long barges come out of the dusk without a sound, lantern-light strung along their awnings. They don't hail. A woman comes to the rail, looks straight at your deck stall, and says in a voice that carries too well across the water: "That's a good little mouse you've got there."*

Nobody on the boat told her that name. She is Zinka, of the barge *Kestrel*. There is a card game on the afterdeck (lose four kleinmarks, win four, it doesn't matter) and a fortune for anyone who crosses the plank.

**The offer**, made to whoever seems to be in charge, which by now isn't Corwin: *"Six hundred marks. Tonight, in coin, and you can count it on your own deck before you hand her over. You look like people who could use six hundred marks."*

| If asked… | She says |
|---|---|
| Why | *"Because she's worth it, and you know she's worth it, or you wouldn't have her in a canvas box in the dark."* |
| Pressed harder | *"I was told to watch this boat. I wasn't told why, and I've stopped asking."* Deliver it and move on. |

**Vosk, if asked:** *"Clan houses don't sell bloodstock to bargefolk. Not* don't like to. *Don't. Do that, and the boy's name is worth less than the wagon it came in. Now. It's not my name, and it's not my six hundred marks."*

*The players decide.* Six hundred marks clears the note with two hundred over, which Corwin splits with the party, and it finishes the Halbritters on the Flats for good. It is a real choice with a real price. **Let them take it.**

### The small hours (15 min)
*Read this to whoever is on watch when Krul is detected.*

*The players decide Krul's fate:* hand him to Vosk (she puts him ashore at the next landing, and he walks to Schwartzenbruin for Scene 6); keep him bound aboard (a witness for the stewards' tent and for Roodberg); or let him go for the name.

# Scene 05 — The Stewards' Tent

## Purpose

# Scene 06 — The Stable Rows

## Purpose

# Scene 07 — Post Time

## Purpose
The whole adventure. Twenty minutes to the third bell, and three things are true: Corwin can't ride well enough to win and now knows it; Adalinde is standing in the row in riding boots she hasn't explained; the book is unsigned and the nomination is Corwin's. **Neither sibling will raise it. The party has to say it.**
**Budget:** 25 minutes. **Never cut this scene.**

## Arrival
> *Derby Day. The stands are full, the bell for the second race has gone, and the course is a green mile with a crowd four deep along its rail. The morning packet has come in. Otwin Halbritter stands at the end of the row with two house men, looking at the mare, not his son. Adalinde is beside the stall in riding boots. A steward's boy puts his head around the post: "Ostvelt Grey. The book's still not signed. Whoever signs names the rider. Third bell."*

*Now stop talking. Let the table look at it.* If a player asked Adalinde in Scene 1 whether she rides, she looks at that player, not at Corwin, when the steward's boy, Benno, speaks.

## Who Is Here
- **Corwin Halbritter**. *Wants:* to be taken seriously. *Knows:* he can't win. *Reacts:* won't offer the ride.
- **Adalinde Halbritter**. *Wants:* to ride. *Knows:* she can place. *Reacts:* won't ask.
- **Otwin Halbritter**, here for the noon signing and staying for the race. *Wants:* the farm to outlive his children. *Reacts:* says nothing yet.
- **Jorick Weisel**, five minutes away at The Tanner's Rest, drinking.
- **Steward Alric Roodberg** and his book.

## Beats

### If they raise the rider question
**Corwin**, after a silence that should be allowed to be uncomfortable: *"She's better than me. She's always been better than me. Everyone here knows it. I knew it in the yard when I was twelve and I've spent seven years — "* He stops. He turns to his sister, and this is the first grown-up sentence of his life: *"Linde. Will you ride her."*

**Adalinde:** *"Yes."* She is already taking the silks out of his hands. *"Give me those. And Corwin, go and stand where Father can see you."*

### If the table sits silent
Do not have Adalinde ask. Prompt with an action: she crouches and starts unbuckling her spurs. She is putting them away, to go and stand in the public stand with her father and watch her brother lose her horse. If the table still doesn't move, they have made a choice. Run it.

### Changing the nomination
Adalinde riding needs the nomination changed. **Every route was seeded earlier:**

| Route | What it takes |
|---|---|
| **Weisel signs** | The Tanner's Rest, five minutes at a run. Find him: Investigation DC 12, or automatic if they asked about him before. Make him sign: Intimidation DC 15, Persuasion DC 17, or 50 marks. Blackmail him with the rented-colors scheme: automatic, and he hates them forever, which costs nothing today. *"I rent cloth. I don't rent promises. If the boy told you I'd stand in a stewards' tent and swear for him, the boy misheard."* |
| **A new sponsor** | A Roodberg or Meerijder breeder willing to sign for a share of the mare's first foal. Automatic with a clan-kin PC who spends the favor; otherwise Persuasion DC 15 in the enclosures. |
| **Roodberg bends the rule** | Persuasion DC 17 (DC 12 with a clan name vouching): the rule serves the race better bent than kept. He does it once, and he writes down why. |
| **Otwin signs** | Only after the party has said Adalinde should ride, never before. Persuasion DC 13 if the ledger argument was won in Scene 2 or he has watched the swap; DC 17 otherwise. He signs for a dead house in his own hand, which is the closest a Halbritter has ever come to colors. |

### The other routes
- **Buy the note down:** stake money at 4-to-1 to place (the purse share, the bet, or both).
- **Scratch her and take the offer:** Corwin says yes if the party tells him to.
- **Run Corwin anyway:** nothing needed.

### Otwin, watching the swap
He doesn't say anything. He looks at his daughter in the silks, and at his son standing in the row with empty hands, and something in his face does arithmetic and comes out different than it went in.

### The race — three sentences, no rolls
**Adalinde rides, Maus Sound:**
> *They go off in a wall of noise and she's nowhere for half a mile: buried, boxed, a grey nothing at the back of a clan field. Then at the turn there's a gap that isn't really a gap, and she takes it. She doesn't win; the Roodberg colt wins by a length and a half. But a grey farm mare in a dead house's colors comes second at Duffkyyn, and up in the public stand an old man with the gout is on his feet without knowing he stood up.*

**Corwin rides:**
> *He rides well. That's the thing nobody expected and nobody will remember: he rides well, for a boy who's never ridden a race, and he finishes sixth in a field of eleven. The mare comes back sound and blowing and pleased with herself. Sixth pays nothing. He knows it before he's out of the saddle, and he doesn't look for his father in the stand.*

**Maus Sore, and they run her** (roll the 1-in-6 in the open, first):
> *No breakdown:* *She runs fourth on three good legs and a lie, and every horseman on that rail knows what they just watched.*
> *Breakdown:* *At the last furlong something goes out of her stride, the whole rail makes one sound, and she pulls up. She'll walk. She won't race. Adalinde is over the rail before anyone can stop her.*

## Development
- **Adalinde rides, Sound** → second; the 500-mark place purse clears the note. Maus is now a broodmare, not a remount, and Otwin strikes her from the levy string in his own hand.
- **Corwin rides** → sixth, sound. The note falls due; Erlenhof goes to the Day 6 ring unless the party has arranged otherwise.
- **Past the third bell** → scratched. Vuurzward's offer stands at Derby-evening prices, which are worse.
- **Sore** → she can run or she can be kept whole, not both. Adalinde says so and lets the party decide: *"She can run. I don't know that she should."* Fourth pays nothing and does not clear the note.
- **Lame** → she cannot run. The entry is scratched, and Vuurzward's offer is the only route left. He knows it.
- **Vuurzward** plays at full intelligence: odds bought down, gone before the last furlong. He has done nothing a court can hang him for. If the party laid Krul and the odds-book before Roodberg (Scene 5), his own horses are barred and he is not here at all.

## Clues Available Here
- The steward's boy → **whoever signs names the rider** (last resort)
- The boots → **Adalinde should ride**
- If someone mentions the purse, Corwin works it out aloud: *"Second's five hundred. The note's four."* → **second place is enough** (last resort)

## Sensory Palette
- **Sight:** green silks over riding boots, the crowd four deep, a steward's boy at the post
- **Sound:** the bell for the second race, a crowd coming apart at the turn
- **Smell:** crushed turf, horse sweat, a thousand people's beer
- **Touch:** silk still warm from the person who handed it over
- **Taste:** dust off the course

## Alternates & Re-hooks
- **Adalinde came instead of Corwin** (they refused the job): the problem at post time is Otwin, who arrives to take the mare home. The same routes work on him; the ledger argument is the strongest.
- **Otwin has been here since Derby eve** (the roll): he stands in the row the whole scene. He speaks only if a PC speaks to him first.
- **The mare was sold to the Rhennee** (Scene 4): a 10-minute beat. Otwin and Adalinde come off the morning packet to an empty stall and a struck line. Adalinde looks at the stall, then at the party: *"You sold her to the water."* She says nothing else until the sale ring, where she still delivers the letter. Otwin says nothing at all; the note is paid, and that is the worst part. Go to Scene 08 — The Sale Ring.
- **Maus failed to settle at Spook 3** (Scene 6): she won't stand for the first ten minutes. Adalinde walks her, so whoever runs for Weisel goes without her to vouch for them.
- **A PC cut a cord in Scene 6:** Roodberg has already struck the line. The only routes left are a very good reason (Persuasion DC 20, with the bravos' cut cords as evidence) or Vuurzward's offer.

## Transition
- → Scene 08 — The Sale Ring: the morning after, the ring half empty, and an old man sitting on the rail.

# Scene 08 — The Sale Ring

## Purpose
Land it. Hand them the evidence about Hessel, let them decide whether to tell Otwin, and give them the patron's letter on almost every branch.
**Budget:** 10 minutes. Running long? The letter only.

## Arrival
> *The ring is half empty the morning after. Yearlings go through at a hundred marks a head, a man with a ledger calls prices in a bored singsong, and the whole Flats smells of packing up. Otwin Halbritter is sitting on the rail alone, his stick across his knees.*

He signed the remount contract at noon on Derby Day, thirty head over three years, then watched the last two furlongs from the public stand and told nobody.

**To the table:** *"He doesn't look at you. He says, 'I saw. I saw the whole of it.' Who sits down next to him?"*

## Who Is Here
- **Otwin Halbritter**. *Wants:* quiet, to redo the sums. *Knows:* what he saw. *Reacts:* to his daughter, without turning: *"Don't. Don't say anything, girl. I've the ledger in my head and I'm doing the sums again and I'd like quiet for it."*
- **Adalinde Halbritter**, with a folded paper.
- **Corwin Halbritter**, wherever his father can see him.

## Beats

### The truth about Hessel
Krul's *ten days*, the odds-book account opened ten days ago, Grete's ale line, and Tilo's word about the sergeant's wife: together they name **Hessel Amtmann**. Leave it as evidence, not a confession. **Whether to tell Otwin is the last decision in the adventure, and it is the party's.**

If they tell him, give them the moment and don't rush it:
> *He listens all the way through without interrupting, which is worse than shouting. "Hessel." He says the name like he's checking a number he already knew was wrong. "Fifteen years. He carried my son on his shoulders at the Godsday fair." He gets down off the rail, slowly, because of the gout. "Right. Thank you. I'd rather have known."*

### The stewards' chest
By Duffkyyn custom, the company that brought a starter to the post draws one prize from the stewards' chest. Benno carries it around the ring. Any branch where the mare reached the Flats under the party's escort qualifies, whether she ran or was scratched; the Rhennee sale does not. Roll on the **Derby prize table** in SP-PBS-00-Nobodys-Colors (Treasure & Rewards).

### The letter
However it ended, even if they took Vuurzward's offer, Adalinde finds them with a folded paper and a face that gives nothing away:

*"The Roodbergs have sold remounts to the University's field station since its charter. The Registrar knows the seal."* She hands it over. *"It's a letter of field expertise. It won't buy your passage, and you don't need it for the door; they take anyone who signs. It's for after. She'll write it in a different column from everyone else's."*

*(If Adalinde rode and the farm stands:)* *"Father's paying your passage, and half your bond. He says he's buying your absence from my stable. He isn't. He just can't say the other thing."*

*(If anyone asked her, in Scene 1 or at post time, whether she rides:)* *"Whatever else happened out there, thank you for asking me whether I ride. Nobody ever thought to ask."*

Hand over SP-PBS-00 — Handout — The Patron's Letter with the commendations they earned written in.

### What happens next
- **The farm stands** (Adalinde placed, or the note bought down): Maus goes home a broodmare. Her first foal is three years out.
- **The note fell due:** Erlenhof goes through this ring this afternoon. Vuurzward bids. The party can stay and watch, or help Adalinde buy the mare back (she has 60 marks; the mare goes for 150).
- **The Rhennee sale:** the farm stands and the name is gone. Nobody in the Roodberg enclosure will look at Corwin.
- **The northern packet** leaves on Godsday: one night along the shore to Ritterstadt's quay.

## Development
- **Told Otwin** → the last commendation in the patron's letter. Hessel is dismissed by the end of Reaping.
- **Didn't tell him** → Hessel keeps his post, and his Kettenhaus paper is struck out. That is all.
- **Left open:** six hundred marks for a farm mare is far too much. Who on the barges wanted her, and how did they know she'd be on that boat? Sivert's 400 and Hessel's 60 both trace to the Kettenhaus of Traft, which holds Ritter Konrad von Eisfeld's Schuldbrief. The party will hear that name again in Ritterstadt.

## Clues Available Here
- Adalinde, last resort: *"Hessel booked the passage. Nobody else knew the day."* → **Hessel sold the route**

## Sensory Palette
- **Sight:** a half-empty ring, a ledger, an old man on a rail
- **Sound:** the auctioneer's singsong, pavilions coming down
- **Smell:** trodden straw, cold cook-fires
- **Touch:** a folded letter with a Roodberg seal
- **Taste:** the last of the Derby beer, flat

## Alternates & Re-hooks
- **The party left the Flats after the race.** Adalinde finds them at the lake-gate before the packet sails.
- **Otwin isn't speaking to them** (they took Vuurzward's offer): the Hessel evidence goes to Adalinde instead, and the choice is the same.

## Transition
- → **Ritterstadt.** The northern packet, one night along the shore, and The Landing at dawn. *(The campaign continues there.)*
