# Scene 02 — The Erlen Bridge

[[10-Adventures/SP-PBS-00 — Nobody's Colors/Scenes/00 — Scene Index]] · ← [[Scene 01 — Erlenhof]] · Next → [[Scene 03 — Osterschlauss]]

## Purpose
The first real decision, and the reveal if it hasn't happened. **This scene is built to be talked through.** A fight is legal and expensive.
**Budget:** 30 minutes. Running long? At 25 minutes Boedic says *"I've cows to move,"* and takes the bribe.

## Arrival
> *The road drops toward the water and there are cows on it, forty or fifty head strung along the verge, with men in red-and-black walking beside them. Beyond them is a stone bridge, a toll house, and the road to the lake. One drover has been watching you come for a while. He is older than the rest, and he holds a folded paper the way you hold something you'd rather not have been given. He steps into the road, hands well away from his belt. "Morning. Would one of you be the Junker Halbritter?"*

**To the table:** *"He hasn't asked you anything. He's asked the boy. Who answers for him?"*

## Who Is Here
- **[[Boedic of the Meerijder]]**, drover. *Wants:* to obey a lawful writ and go home to his cows. *Knows:* the writ says hold the wagons and the Junker until the Ratsherr comes; it says nothing about the party. *Reacts:* explains himself, asks questions, apologizes while not moving.
- **Five Meerijder drovers** (three at APL 1) with cattle goads, and (APL 3–4) two mastiffs trained to hold cloth.
- **[[Old Hartwin]]**, 68, in the toll house. Will not fight; remembers faces.

## Beats

### The writ
*"I've a writ here from Ratsherr Halbritter. Ink's not dry; it came down on a pony an hour past. It says hold the wagons and hold the Junker until the Ratsherr comes himself."* (He looks at the party, honestly puzzled.) *"It doesn't say a word about you lot. So I'd as soon you stood over there and had some bread while we sort it. Nobody wants a Fehde over a horse."*

He says **horse**. If nobody has opened the wagon, that is the reveal, and Corwin's face does the rest.

*The players decide* whether to open the wagon in front of Boedic. He is an honest witness, which cuts both ways.

### Getting past — pick one approach, not a gauntlet
Any **one** of these ends the scene.

| Approach | Check | Boedic |
|---|---|---|
| Claim clan kinship (a plausible surname) | **Automatic** | He takes his hat off: *"Cousin. You should have led with that."* He stands aside and tells his kin at the Flats a clansman was running a horse for the clanless. A favor *spent*. |
| The jurisdiction argument: Aerden's tolls, Aerden's council, a Halbritter family quarrel with no Meerijder interest in it | Persuasion DC 13, or **automatic** if the player makes the argument aloud | *"You're telling me I'm a Meerijder drover stood in the middle of a Halbritter family quarrel."* (Beat.) *"Aye. That's about the size of it."* He wants it on record that he is not happy. |
| Bribe | 6 marks is his day-fee; he takes **12**, no roll | He counts it without looking at anyone. *"I was never here, and neither were you."* |
| Forge a counter-writ | DC 15 with a forgery kit, DC 17 without | Failure: he sends a rider to Erlenhof, and Otwin comes at dusk |
| Spook the cattle across the road | Animal Handling DC 14 | Buys a crossing, and they are now cattle thieves in a canton of stockmen |
| Sneak the wagons over at night | Stealth DC 13 (group) | Costs a day and the *Kortheim Maid*; see Alternates |
| Fight | — | See Encounter |

### If they're held: Otwin at dusk
**This is a scene, not a wall.** The cart comes at dusk with a lantern on the rail, and the old man in it doesn't get down, because getting down would cost him something. He looks at the third wagon, then at his son. *"Corwin."* That's all. He waits.

Corwin, with nothing prepared: *"She's the best horse we've ever had and you're going to sell her to the levy."*

Otwin: *"Yes. Thirty horses over three years, paid in silver, quarterly, by the canton. That is a future. A race is an afternoon. I have buried a wife and a son in this ground and I will not bury the farm for an afternoon."*

**The winning move is asking to see the ledger.** He has it under the seat. Of course he does. Investigation DC 14, or automatic for a PC with trade or bookkeeping: the remount contract pays 90 marks a quarter, the tolls it is set against are worth 60, and **the place purse at Duffkyyn is 500**. Tell him that and he goes quiet. *"Five hundred. And if she loses?"* Any honest answer gets them past: Persuasion DC 13, with advantage if they showed him the number.

- **Win it:** *"Go on, then. Go. Tell my daughter — no. Tell her nothing. She'll have worked it out."*
- **Lose it:** Otwin takes the mare home. At midnight the party reaches Osterschlauss to find Adalinde on the quay with the mare she led out of the barn an hour after her father brought it in: *"He put her in the near stall and went to bed. I have a key to the near stall. I've had it since I was twelve. Are you coming, or shall I find the boat myself?"*

**Keep the writ.** It names Otwin, and it is evidence later ([[Otwin's Writ]]).

## Encounter (if any)

| 4 | Boedic + 5 drovers + 2 mastiffs | Deadly (2,500) |

Boedic uses the **bandit captain** block reflavored; drovers are **thugs**; mastiffs hold, they don't bite. See [[SP-PBS-00 — Bestiary]].

**Terrain:** a stone toll bridge three wagons wide, waist-high walls, a toll house at the near end (half cover), and forty-odd cattle on the road behind. The river is four feet deep and slow: a shove over the wall is 1d6 falling damage and a wet, furious drover, not a drowning. **The cattle are terrain:** any loud noise (a shout, a scream, a thunderous spell) sets them milling, and the road is difficult terrain for 2 rounds; everyone in it makes a **DC 11 Dexterity save or falls prone**.
**Tactics:** round 1 they form a line across the bridge and do not advance. Boedic spends his first turn talking. They target the wagon team, not the party: the goal is to stop wheels. **Morale breaks at two drovers down:** *"Enough. Enough! Take the bloody bridge, and when the Ratsherr asks, you tell him I bled for it."*
**DM direction:** say it before initiative: **knocking out is free** (any melee attack), and **killing a clansman without a declared Fehde is murder**. A party that kills drovers has started a feud with the Meerijder and will meet it at the Flats. Most parties take the hint.

Use this when the fight needs more bodies: a pack of 4–8 **Meerijder Drover** minions, built from the **thug** (MM p. 350), as Boedic's drovers, with the [[Brute Minion]] template at **APL 3**. More of Boedic's drovers come over the bridge rail. Overwhelm can push a PC off the bridge. Each minion is worth **175 XP**, and every four count as one creature for the encounter multiplier ([[04 — Minions]]). Add them four at a time and recheck the encounter's adjusted XP. **Caution:** they are clansmen; knocking out is still free, and killing one is still a Fehde.

## Development
- **Bloodless crossing** → the bridge commendation in the patron's letter. Say so at the table.
- **Drovers killed** → Fehde. Roodberg hears by Scene 5 (his attitude drops one step), and Boedic's kin are in the stable rows on Derby eve.
- **Clan kinship used** → Boedic's kin at the Flats know a clansman is running a horse for the clanless.
- **Held, then argued free** → Otwin's message for his daughter; Otwin arrives at the Flats on the morning packet anyway.

## Clues Available Here
- Boedic says *horse* → **there is a racehorse in the third wagon**
- Otwin's ledger → **second place is enough** (500 against 400)
- [[Otwin's Writ]] → Otwin knew by dawn; who told him? (Hartwin, about the chain-dragging stranger)

## Sensory Palette
- **Sight:** red-and-black sleeves, a folded paper, cattle strung along the verge, the toll house's slate roof
- **Sound:** cowbells, the river under the arch, Boedic's patient voice
- **Smell:** cattle, crushed grass, river water
- **Touch:** warm stone on the parapet
- **Taste:** the bread Boedic offers, if they take it

## Alternates & Re-hooks
- **A three-day hold** misses the Derby. Don't let it happen: Otwin comes at dusk on the first day.
- **A one-day hold** (night crossing, or a failed forgery) misses the *Maid* and catches the slower *Agal Wind*: they arrive Day 4 at dusk, Scenes 5 and 6 squeeze into Derby eve, and nobody gets a long rest before the race. Late costs; it doesn't end the game.
- **Old Hartwin**, asked, will say a stranger in a dark Traft coat dragged a surveyor's chain across his bridge yesterday, counting links. *"Kettenhaus man. They measure what they mean to take."* (Color for later; it changes nothing today.)
