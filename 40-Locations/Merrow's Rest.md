# Merrow's Rest

> A roadside mortuary chapel with catacombs below, where the dead of the road have been buried for forty years.

## Description
A squat stone chapel behind a lychgate hung with funeral bells. Fresh earth on every grave in the yard. Candles in the nave at noon.

## Notable Features
- **Nave and vestry.** The burial register is in the vestry. The bone drop (the bone chute) opens in the apse floor beside the trapdoor.
- **C1. The Bone Gallery.** Skulls turned to face the wall; slime on the ceiling.
- **C2. The Embalming Room.** Hooks, salt barrels, cured and dated limbs, the larder ledger.
- **C3. The Columbarium.** Urn niches; the mouth of the burial crawls.
- **C4. The Iron Doors.** Barred from the outside; gouged high on the outside.
- **C5. The Saint's Ossuary.** 40 × 60 ft. vault under black water: eight sarcophagi, bone shelves, a drain channel, the altar and the Urn of Saint Merrow.
- **The burial crawls.** 2-ft. tunnels between C3 and C5; too narrow for anything Large.

## Inhabitants
- [[Brother Ossian]]
- [[Sexton Harrow]], now a wight, who keeps his memory and his shame
- The larder: the Widow Brisk, Tam the cooper, and Wil and Ned Collier, all buried this spring and all ghouls now
- Ossian's things: husks raised from old graves, ossuary crawlers that clean up after him, gibbering mouthers he couldn't digest
- Pell Brisk, the widow's son, who lives a mile up the road and won't come past the lychgate
- The chapel cat and Harrow's dog, who know exactly what Ossian is

## History
*Setting-agnostic: the only history here is the place's own.*
- **The founding.** About a hundred years ago, a gravedigger named Merrow buried the road's dead for nothing, and buried them well. When she died, the people she had served raised a chapel over her grave and kept her ashes in a plain grey urn. The locals call her Saint Merrow, patron of the quiet dead (swap in any patron of the dead in your setting).
- **The catacombs.** A plague winter filled the yard twice over, so the brothers dug down. Below the chapel they broke into burial crawls older than anything they had built: two-foot tunnels running down into the deep earth. They walled off the deepest, raised the Saint's Ossuary around the rest, and carried the urn below to keep watch. They cut a bone drop from the apse to the ossuary to lower old remains, and no one has used it since the plague.
- **The last forty years.** Brother Ossian kept Merrow's Rest with only Sexton Harrow, who dug the graves. They were quiet years: a funeral a month, the register in Ossian's cramped, shaking hand, and Harrow's dog asleep under the yews.
- **Eight months ago.** Something came up the old crawls. The register's handwriting changed that spring to a neat, steady hand, and there have been more burials than deaths ever since.
