# General

Name: `= this.file.name`

### Character Descriptor
An eighteen-year-old Flan girl, four thousand years dead, who has just been let out. She was an apprentice stone-setter at the Waymark — she cut the small work, the trim nobody writes about — and she died there before her pair-binding, which meant nobody ever performed her release. She has been standing in the tunnels ever since, minding her diggers, waiting for the work to resume.

Then six students came back and did it properly, and the door she had been leaning on for four millennia simply opened.

She followed [[Bethany McCockin-Simmons|Bethany]] home.

### 📝 Appearance
Translucent and faintly warm-toned rather than cold — she reads more like sunlight through amber than like a corpse. Barefoot. A short work-apron over a plain shift, both cut in a style no living person has seen. Stone dust on her hands that never comes off, because she died with it there. Her hair is bound back the way you bind it when you are going to be lying on your side in a tunnel for six hours.

She is unmistakably a *young person*, and this is the detail that undoes people. She is not a terrible figure in a shroud. She is somebody's daughter, roughly the age of half the Questers, hovering three inches off the cider-house floor with her chin on her fists, watching the press turn.

### 🎭 Mannerisms
- **Walks through things mid-sentence** and keeps talking, so half her conversations happen through a wall. She does not understand doors as social objects. She understands them as *stone she was taught not to damage.*
- **Cannot say her own full name any more.** It was longer — *Tamerakeleth,* or something with more of it — and the last syllables are simply gone. She finds this funny in a way that is difficult to be around. "Tamera is what's left. It's plenty."
- **Formal register for absurd things.** Archaic Flan grammar mapped clumsily onto new Common: she will say *"I petition the orchard for one more hour of the bees"* and mean *can I stay outside a bit longer.*
- **She counts.** Six lecterns, six spokes, six nodes, six students. Apprentices were taught to count the work aloud. When Tamera starts counting things under her breath, **she is frightened** — and it is often the only warning you get.
- **She smells everything.** She cannot taste, which she reports is the genuinely unfair part of being dead, worse than the rest of it combined. Cider is a running tragedy.

### 💡 Key Motivation
To have the year she didn't get. Not vengeance, not rest, not answers — she wants to see a harvest, learn to read the new letters, be at a festival, and find out what people do now. She is *delighted* by the present tense and has no interest in being laid to rest until she has had a proper go at it.

Under that, quieter: she wants someone to finish her paperwork. She was never released because she was never paired. Somewhere in her, that is still an open file.

## 🗺️ Town Role & Function

**Where she stays: [[The McCockin Orchard]].** Specifically the cider-house rafters by day and the oldest tree on the property — Chuck McCockin's first seedling, up on the high ground — at night. She chose it herself within an hour of arriving, on the grounds that it was the oldest living thing in the village and therefore the closest thing to a neighbour.

**Grant McCockin** took her in with almost no fuss, which tells you something about Grant. He then asked Brandt, in complete sincerity, whether housing a primary source qualified as an endowment gift. **Winifred Simmons** wants the arrangement in writing. There is now a document. Tamera has signed it, badly, in a script that predates the alphabet it is written beside — and Winifred has filed it, because Winifred files things.

### 🏪 Services
- **Harvest work.** She can't lift a bushel, but she can find every wormed apple in a row without touching one, and birds will not come near the trees she's in. The pickers have gone from terrified to territorial about her in under a month.
- **Ur-Flan read-aloud.** She can pronounce Waymark script correctly — cadence, stress, the lot — *without knowing what it means.* She was taught the sounds so the work-songs stayed true. For Brandt this is the single most valuable service in the Sepia Uplands.
- **The old work-songs**, including one Ritterstadt has been singing to its babies for three hundred years without knowing what it is.

## 💬 Conversation Guide

She answers questions eagerly, at length, and in the wrong order. She is not evasive; she simply has no idea which of the things she knows are important. Ask her about the binding network and you may get eleven minutes about a supervisor who was unkind about her chisel angles.

**Play her as an apprentice, not an oracle.** She knows:

| She knows | She does not know |
|---|---|
| The *feel* of the site — where it was warm, where you didn't linger | What the network is for |
| The order of work, the songs, the counting | Any theory, any cosmology |
| Names of people four thousand years dead | Which of those names matter |
| How the sockets were dressed and seated | Why they had to be |
| That the grown-ups were frightened of something | What it was |

> [!quote] On being asked what the Waymark was built to hold
> *"They didn't tell the apprentices. You don't tell the apprentices. I asked once and Sefer put his hand flat on the stone and said 'we are building a very good wall, and the wall is the whole of your business,' and then he gave me the rim-dressing to do, which was a punishment, and it took me two months."*

### 🔑 Trust-Gated Information

| Trust | What she offers |
|---|---|
| **Any** | The work-songs, the counting, the names, the daily life of the build |
| **+1** | That she died there, and roughly how — she is matter-of-fact about it and it lands harder for that |
| **+2** | The mural's empty final position is **hers**. It is a memorial. Later generations read it as an invitation. |
| **+3** | What she will not say about the Wachturm *(see GM Notes)* |

## 🎯 Quest Hooks

1. **The accession fight.** The glyph fragment recovered at the Waymark is her tether, and it is on the audit manifest as a University find. **Professor Casimir Vogelsang** wants it in the Registry lockbox. Bethany is carrying it. Nobody has yet said out loud that accessioning the fragment means filing Tamera as a holding — and the first person to notice that gets to decide whether to say it.
2. **Pair-Day.** She has worked out what the Brewfest cord game is. She has been waiting four thousand years for one. She has not asked, because she has understood — correctly — that asking would be a large thing to ask.
3. **The digger that followed.** One ankheg came down off the escarpment behind her. It is in the orchard's lower rows. Grant's drystone wall has moved eleven feet and he would like a word.
4. **[[Vater Sigmund Wolfherz]] has an unquiet dead in his parish.** He is kind about it and he is not going to let it go. He has spent thirty years noticing the paired graves and saying nothing; here is one of them, in his village, hovering.
5. **She recognises the half-ring on sight.** Put her within ten feet of Hilde Horchen's necklace, or Vater Sigmund's mislabelled reliquary token, and she will say the word *geminel* out loud without being prompted.
6. **The only living witness.** She is the campaign's sole primary source for what stood at the Wachturm's site *before* the Waymark network was built.

## 🔗 Relationship Web
- **[[Bethany McCockin-Simmons]]** — the reason she came. At the cold den, Bethany was the one who worked out the gnolls had left *frightened*, not driven — and Tamera, watching from the scree, saw a stranger care whether some frightened creatures were all right. That was enough. Bethany is a Trithereon paladin; a bound thing being let loose is very nearly a sacrament.
- **[[Professor Wilhelmina Brandt]]** — professionally overwhelmed. A living informant from the build generation contradicts forty years of her own published work, and she has said so, in public, cheerfully. Their friendship is real and slightly exhausting for everyone else.
- **[[Professor Casimir Vogelsang]]** *(Antiquities)* — wants her catalogued. Not cruelly. He is a provenance man and she is unprovenanced.
- **[[Vater Sigmund Wolfherz]]** — the gentlest possible antagonist.
- **The orchard pickers** — collectively adopted her inside a month.

# GM Notes

> [!danger] DM Eyes Only

## 🕵️ Secrets & Hidden Motivations

### The Real Story
Tamera was an apprentice in the generation that **built** the Waymark, not one that maintained it. She cut the trim: the socket's rim-dressing, the bench edges, the six lecterns in the relay loft. Small work, done well, and she was proud of it.

She was in the sub-floor tunnels the day Node 2 first took its load. The seating was done correctly and the node held — and the backlash rolled through the stone and killed her where she lay, eighteen years old, holding a chisel. **She was the site's first casualty.** The builders left the final position on the north-wall mural blank as her memorial.

Eight hundred years later, the last Pair-Walkers looked at that blank space and read it as an invitation to a successor. **They were wrong, and the mistake was load-bearing** — the whole "someone will come after" reading that the University has built its Second Voice research on is a misread grave marker.

She never received a release. Pair-Walkers who died had their rings returned and were let go by their partner; Tamera had no partner, so no one performed the rite. She is not cursed and not vengeful. She is, in the most literal sense available, **unfinished paperwork** — which is a joke the University will make, once, and then stop making.

**What freed her:** Fennel seating the anchor and the party retracing the script. The node took real load for the first time in eight centuries. *The same event that killed her, performed correctly, is what let her go.* She has not said this to anyone. She is not sure how she feels about it.

**The ankhegs** were hers. Her family's line kept burrowers to drive and true the service tunnels — "diggers," bred and whistled to. The ones at the escarpment are their descendants, forty centuries feral, and they still come when she whistles. She set them on the party on the road because six armed strangers were walking toward her site carrying a live anchor stone, and she had no other way to ask what they wanted. **She is embarrassed about this** and will apologise, unprompted, to anyone who was hurt.

### What They Know (But Won't Say)
- **The Wachturm.** She goes quiet and dim near it — the only thing in the world that does this to her. What she eventually says, at high trust, is: ***"We did not build that one. We built around it."***
- That the grown-ups on the build were frightened, that they got more frightened as it went on, and that two of them left in the last season and were not spoken of again.
- The full work-song. She knows the tune and the cadence exactly — Ritterstadt's lullaby is a degraded version of it, and hearing a mother sing it in the Fiend's Rest is the best scene available with this character. **She does not know the closing couplet** (it is on the Aerd–Velka grave) and, never having been bound, cannot demonstrate the rite. *The Negotiation Capstone gate stays intact.*

### What They Fear
Being catalogued. Being made a *holding* instead of a person — given a Registry number, entered in a ledger, and shelved by people who are being perfectly kind about it. She has no framework for institutional ownership and she is picking it up fast, from Winifred's paperwork, and she does not like what she is learning.

Under that: being released before she has had her turn. She wants the rite eventually. She wants the year first.

### How to Reveal It
Slowly, and never in answer to a direct question. She reveals herself by **being around** — a small remark while she's counting apples, a song she doesn't know is famous, a sudden silence when the party walks the north road within sight of the tower. Players should assemble her the way Brandt assembled the network: fragments, out of order, arriving faster than they can be filed.

> [!tip] Playing her
> The whole character is one contrast: **a four-thousand-year-old witness to a cosmic containment, who is eighteen and mostly wants to see the cider press work.** Do not let her become an exposition faucet. Her best material comes out sideways, while she is doing something else. When the party finally asks the big question, the correct answer is usually that she doesn't know, and would you like to see what she can do with the bees.

> [!warning] Don't resolve her
> Resist laying her to rest, or accessioning her, or answering the Wachturm question this arc. She is a standing presence — a way for any Ritterstadt party, including ones who never ran GHU3, to brush against the Waymark mystery from town. That was the point of the folklore in the E3 addendum, and she is now its loudest piece.
