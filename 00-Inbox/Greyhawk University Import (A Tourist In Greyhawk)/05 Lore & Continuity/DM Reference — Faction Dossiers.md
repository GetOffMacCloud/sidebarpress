# Faction Dossiers — What They Know & Want

*Every faction in the Sepia Uplands has a version of events they present to the world, and a version they hold privately. This document is the private version.*

---

## The Wolf Nomads

### What They Present
Territorial nomadic herders following ancestral migration routes. Hostile to encroachment. Unwilling to communicate with outsiders.

### What They Actually Know
The Wolf Nomad outriders are not simply territorial. They are the last organized institutional memory of the Flan shrine-tender lineages — culturally corrupted, ancestrally incomplete, but still there. Their seasonal migration routes follow the binding network node sites. When a band leader speaks of "the ancestor routes," they mean paths that were originally maintained for a purpose the current generation cannot explain but has never stopped following.

They know:
- The standing stones at G5 are sacred and cannot be touched during certain astronomical alignments (the ones that correspond to resonance peaks in the network — they just don't call it that)
- The Wachturm site is "the place that must not be opened" — exact phrasing, carried in oral tradition, no longer paired with any explanation of what it means
- The Quag Mires are spreading and the Bloom years are getting worse — this is their most urgent concern and the reason outriders are watching Ritterstadt with more attention than usual. They want to know if the settlement knows anything useful
- Ritterstadt sits on a threshold marker site — they see the chapel's foundation stone (the old Flan carving) and know what it means. This is why they have not attacked. They are watching to see whether the settlement will acknowledge what it's sitting on

### What They Want
- Acknowledgment of their sacred claim to the moor sites — not cession, just recognition
- The standing stones left undisturbed
- Someone in Ritterstadt to know what the Wachturm is and take it seriously
- Ideally, a working partnership: they have the oral tradition, the party has the resources and freedom of movement. Together they might actually fix this

### How to Bring Them In
The correct approach is through the standing stones at G5 — visiting respectfully, not touching, spending a night, and waiting. A Flan-descended character, or one who demonstrates knowledge of the network, will attract an outrider contact within 48 hours. They don't meet anyone who just walks up to the stones and starts poking them.

A band leader, if the party earns an audience, can provide the oral tradition fragments that fill gaps in Hassenrath's notes — including directional lore toward Nodes 5 and 6.

## The Druid Enclave at Ehlonna's Mount

### What They Present
A neutral, evaluating collective of druids maintaining observational records of the Sepia Uplands' ecological cycles. Neither hostile nor welcoming. Watching.

### What They Actually Know
The enclave has the most sophisticated understanding of the Bloom of any organization in the region — and they are the only group that suspects (correctly) that the Bloom is a symptom of a deeper structural failure rather than a natural cycle. Their records going back four centuries show a clear pattern: each Bloom year is slightly more intense than the last, and the recovery between Blooms is slightly less complete. This is not a natural oscillation. Something is winding up.

They know:
- The Quag Mires are not entirely natural. Their formation was accelerated by magical contamination from the Twin Cataclysms — standard Bloom theory — but the druids have a second hypothesis they do not share publicly: that the Marshes are behaving as if they are responding to something, not just expressing contamination. As if the cold has a direction it is moving toward
- The genius loci at the east forest shrine near Ritterstadt is connected to a broader network. They have identified eleven other minor-node sites in the Sepia Uplands with similar genius loci presences. They have not told Ritterstadt's residents any of this
- Ehlonna's Mount is itself a minor node — one of the eleven. The druids maintain it without fully understanding what they're maintaining, in the same way the Flan shrine-tenders once did. They have inherited the practice without the complete theory
- The Bloom crystals (the translucent formations appearing on stone near affected areas) are a new phenomenon — they did not appear in previous Bloom years. The enclave is alarmed by this and has not yet determined what it means

### What They Want
- The binding network stabilized, because they understand (without the full picture) that its degradation is the cause of the worsening Blooms
- A formal relationship with Ritterstadt that gives them access to expedition findings — the party is operating in their territory and seeing things the enclave cannot easily reach
- The Ur-Flan clay tablet translated. They have one scholar with partial Ur-Flan literacy — enough to work through the schematic with time and the tablet in hand
- Ritterstadt not to make things worse. This is their minimum requirement and it is not confident

### How to Bring Them In
The enclave does not respond to formal petitions or letters. They respond to demonstrated stewardship: a party that protects a genius loci site, restores a damaged node, or shows up at the standing stones with evident knowledge of what they're looking at. Two visits to Ehlonna's Mount with an attitude of respectful inquiry — not academic extraction — will open a genuine relationship. One visit where someone takes a Bloom crystal as a specimen will close it for a year.

The enclave's partial Ur-Flan scholar is an old woman named **Giseth** who will claim she can't read it well enough to be useful, then spend three weeks working through the tablet and produce a 90% accurate translation. She will not charge for this. She will expect something in return that she will not name until later.

## Agents of Iuz

### What They Present
Travelers. Traders. A scholar with academic interest in old ruins. A soldier between postings. Whatever cover the current operative is running.

### What They Actually Know
Iuz's intelligence apparatus in the northwestern Flanaess is more organized than it appears from the outside. There is a network of contacts and dead-drops running from his territory south through the Wolf Nomad borderlands and into the eastern Sepia Uplands. It has been active for approximately fifteen years — since the mid-560s, when someone in Iuz's apparatus identified Ritterstadt's founding and flagged the Fiend's Embrace in the founders' documented cargo.

The Fiend's Embrace is what they're here for. It is a bound demonic fragment — specifically, a fragment of an entity that was part of Iuz's father's domain. Recovering or destroying it has theological significance for Iuz's cult that exceeds its strategic military value. It is not a weapon. It is a relic they want for reasons a mortal party might find difficult to fully understand.

They also know, through separate intelligence channels, that there is "something beneath the old tower in the northern settlement." Their scholar — the one Varek Doss's research has partially intersected — has a theory that the Wachturm's binding apparatus and the Fiend's Embrace are connected. This theory is not entirely wrong.

Current asset in the region: **Ewald Wennerberg** (see NPC file) is providing situational intelligence. His handler is a contact at Kottsmort, the trapper's outpost to the east, who forwards dispatches north via an intermediary whose identity Ewald does not know.

### What They Want
- The Fiend's Embrace — recovery preferred, destruction acceptable if recovery proves impossible
- Information on what the University is finding in the field, specifically anything related to the Wachturm
- Ritterstadt destabilized enough that it cannot resist the above — hence the Hanselmann Consortium angle (which they did not initiate, but have noticed and find useful)

### How They Escalate
Iuz's agents work slowly and carefully in this region. They are not ready for direct action. They will be ready when:
1. They know the Fiend's Embrace's exact location within the settlement
2. They have an asset inside who can create an opening
3. The political situation (Konrad's debt, garrison integrity) is weak enough to exploit

The party can stay ahead of this by addressing the political vulnerabilities, establishing genuine garrison integrity, and keeping the Fiend's Embrace's location and nature out of any document that might be copied.

## The Hanselmann Consortium

### What They Present
A legitimate mercantile consortium based in Traft, currently holding a significant debt instrument against Ritterstadt's Ritter. Their agent Vethkar Graun is conducting a routine foreclosure assessment.

### What They Actually Know
The Consortium is exactly what it appears to be — a mercantile organization with no supernatural agenda. They lent Konrad 800 gold pieces against future timber rights in the uplands territory, secured by the Ritter's land-grant. They want either repayment with interest or the timber rights, which they intend to sell to a Schwartzenbruin logging operation.

What they don't know is that Ritterstadt's timber rights sit directly over several resonance node sites. Logging those areas would physically damage the node apparatus in ways that cannot be repaired. The Consortium would cause catastrophic irreversible damage to the binding network without understanding it or intending to.

They also don't know that one of their current assessors — Vethkar Graun — has been conducting a secondary intelligence operation that they did not commission and would not approve of if they knew about it.

### Vethkar Graun's Actual Position
Vethkar is a Consortium assessor who has been in this region long enough to notice that it is not ordinary frontier. He identified Ewald as a foreign agent within a fortnight. He obtained and read Varek Doss's research notes. He understands approximately forty percent of what those notes mean, which is forty percent more than anyone else in the Consortium does.

He is running a private information-collection operation alongside his official debt assessment, motivated by: genuine intellectual curiosity about what the Wachturm is; awareness that someone with the right information in this region could become very useful to the right buyer; and a self-preservation instinct that tells him knowing what "catastrophic" means is better than not knowing.

He is not Iuz's agent. He is not the Wolf Nomads' agent. He is an ambitious mercantile operative who has stumbled into something larger than his career and is trying to determine whether this is an opportunity or a threat. Currently he leans toward: both.

### What the Consortium Wants (Straightforwardly)
Repayment of 800 gold plus 200 gold accrued interest, or formal transfer of the timber rights. They will accept partial payment plus a payment schedule if Konrad presents a credible plan. They will accept political face-saving if Vethkar recommends it (he controls the report).

### How to Resolve the Consortium Thread
The party has several options:
- **Pay the debt** — 1,000 gold, or enough to satisfy Vethkar's report. The Consortium withdraws. This requires the party to either find that money or help Konrad find it.
- **Leverage Vethkar** — He has enough exposure (secondary intelligence operation, Ewald knowledge he hasn't reported) that a negotiation is possible. He would reduce or eliminate the official debt assessment in exchange for useful information about the Wachturm. He is genuinely curious about it.
- **Political pressure** — If the party can identify what the timber-rights logging would destroy, and make that argument to the Consortium through proper channels, the Consortium might restructure the deal rather than risk legal liability for destroying a historically significant site. This requires Hassenrath's notes or equivalent documentation and a contact in Schwartzenbruin with University standing.
- **Do nothing** — The Consortium files its report. The Ritter faces foreclosure. The timber rights transfer. The logging begins within a year. Node 2 is physically damaged. The network loses another anchor.

## Varek Doss — Independent Scholar

### What He Presents
A Eru Tovar academic with scholarly interest in pre-Migration sites of the northwestern Flanaess. Hired Vethkar to do preliminary regional assessment on his behalf. Has not yet visited personally.

### What He Actually Knows
Doss is the most dangerous person in the Sepia Uplands at present, in the sense that he has partially correct information, significant resources, genuine expertise, and a stated intention to come north himself once Vethkar's report is in.

He has identified a connection between the Wachturm and what Baklunish scholarship calls the Prior Period construction network — a series of binding and containment structures built before Baklunish civilization that the Baklunish later documented, categorized, and officially stopped thinking about. He knows the Wachturm is part of this network. He does not know what the network was built to contain, but he suspects it is large, old, and bad in the way that things sealed by a pre-civilizational coalition tend to be bad.

His notes' use of "catastrophic" with an underline is not melodrama. It is a scholar who has done the math on what an unsealed Prior Period containment site would do to a region and is using the most accurate word available.

### What He Wants
To study it. This is the problem. Doss wants access to the sub-floor chamber, the clay tablet, and the node sites for research purposes. He is not malicious. He is an academic who has found the most interesting thing in his field in a century, and he is constitutionally unable to leave it alone.

If the party meets him before he arrives at Ritterstadt himself, they have the opportunity to bring him in as an ally — someone who can actually read the Prior Period records and understand what the original builders intended. If they don't, he arrives with his own agenda and begins doing things that will accelerate the very timeline he's researching.

### How Doss Becomes an Asset
The party needs to tell him what the network does — what it actually contains, what the stakes are — before he starts poking at it academically. A scholar who understands that this is load-bearing infrastructure, not an archaeological site, becomes an extraordinarily valuable collaborator. He has Prior Period research access. He has Baklunish institutional contacts. He can potentially identify what kind of solution the original builders designed the network to accept.

He just needs to be scared in the right direction first.
