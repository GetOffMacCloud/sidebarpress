# The Annals of the Northwestern Flanaess: A Gazetteer for New Students of Greyhawk University (576 CY)

The current era, designated as 576 Common Year (CY), finds the Northwestern Flanaess in a state of precarious, cold-sweating survival. To the uninitiated observer, the region between Perrenland's Concatenated Cantons and the frozen reaches of the Rovers' Barrens appears to be a sparsely populated frontier, a matter of trappers, stubborn hill-dwarves, and the odd gnomish prospector chasing a copper seam through the fog. Yet for those who have read the pattern of missing caravans, studied the druidic enclave's weather logs, and walked the palisade of Ritterstadt on a Planting-month morning when ice has no business forming on a puddle, the year 576 CY is not a quiet frontier. It is a held breath before something large exhales.

The "Chronicler of the Flanaess" presents this field report as essential pre-enrollment reading for any individual intending to operate under the auspices of Greyhawk University's Ritterstadt field station. The Northern Frontier does not kill students out of malice. It kills them out of indifference, which is considerably worse.

## Historical Foundation: The Weight of Ancient Ruin

The Sepia Uplands cannot be understood without acknowledging the catastrophe that created them. The northwestern Flanaess is not a natural wilderness. It is a graveyard that has partially forgotten what it is burying.

### The Twin Cataclysms and Their Long Shadow

In -422 CY, the twin atrocities of the Rain of Colorless Fire and the Invoked Devastation annihilated the Suel Imperium and the Baklunish heartlands in a single generation of magical hubris. The survivors fled east in the Great Migrations, driving Oeridian, Suel, and Baklunish peoples into the territories of the native Flan. The northwestern corridor, what is now Perrenland and the Sepia Uplands, was one of the primary migration channels. The Flan who had lived peacefully in these uplands for centuries found themselves caught between the displaced peoples and the northern cold. Many retreated into the high moors, the deep valleys, and eventually into the nomadic culture of the Wolf Nomads who travel the region's eastern margins to this day.

The Rain of Colorless Fire left more than political displacement. The northwestern soil itself was poisoned by fallout from the western devastation. Scholars of the Old Faith believe the Quag Mires, that vast, malignant fen system north of the Sepia Uplands, are not a natural formation at all, but rather a centuries-deep wound where corrupted magical residue from the Rain has seeped into the water table, blighting the land, cooling the air, and generating the slow, organic production of undead that makes the region's northern border functionally uninhabitable.

### The Flan Inheritance

The Flan are the oldest human presence in this land, and the Sepia Uplands are, by any ancestral claim, Flan territory. The standing stones on the high moor predate the Great Migrations by centuries. The names the Perrenlanders use for the rivers and the passes are corrupted Flan words, often stripped of meanings the speakers no longer know. The Druids of the Old Faith, still active in the enclave at Ehlonna's Mount, are largely of Flan descent and regard the entire uplands as a sacred trust, not a frontier to be exploited. Any student of the University would be wise to understand this before treating the moor as merely "empty land."

## Perrenland: The Distant Authority

Perrenland is the nearest nation with any nominal claim over the Sepia Uplands. It is also, in practical terms, the least present. The Concatenated Cantons are a loose confederation of semi-autonomous city-states and rural communities that elect a Voormann (High Chief) to coordinate matters of mutual defense and trade. The current Voormann rules from Schwungdorf. His tax collectors reach as far north as the town of Traft. They do not go further.

### The Structure of the Cantons

Perrenland's decentralized structure is a feature, not a bug: the Cantons preserve their independence by ensuring no single authority, including the Voormann, can exercise total control. This political philosophy serves Perrenlanders well in their heartlands. In the Sepia Uplands, it means there is effectively no government at all. The University operates in an administrative void. There is no garrison to call upon. There are no canton courts to appeal to. There is no infrastructure of civilization beyond what Ritterstadt has built with its own hands.

### Perrenland's Neutrality Doctrine

Perrenland has historically survived by trading with everyone and formally allying with no one. This "Armed Neutrality" has kept it out of the major wars that have reshaped the Flanaess, but it has also left it strategically isolated when threats materialize on its own doorstep. The proximity of Iuz's territory to the northeast, the Wolf Nomad migration routes that cross its northern provinces, and the growing evidence of unnatural cold emanating from the Quag Mires are all problems the Cantons have elected, collectively, to describe as "the frontier's concern."

The University's presence in Ritterstadt is, officially, a private educational venture. Perrenland does not endorse it. It does not obstruct it. It is fully prepared to express retrospective concern if something goes badly wrong.

## The Sepia Uplands: A Territory Defined by Its Absence

The Sepia Uplands are named for what they look like in autumn and winter, a tawny, amber-brown expanse of dead heather and ochre grass under a grey sky. They stretch north of the Clatspur Mountains, west to the shores of Lake Quag, east toward the Vesve Forest, and north into the Quag Mires. They have no official governance, no reliable roads beyond established tracks, and no civic infrastructure. What they have is space, mineral wealth, ancient secrets, and an increasingly unstable northern boundary.

### The Terrain in Brief

The high moor at the Uplands' center is open, windy, and treacherous underfoot: peat-soft in wet seasons, fog-choked without warning, and beautiful in a way that tends to distract travelers at inconvenient moments. The western ridge lines, locally called "the Teeth", are bare stone scoured by wind, rising to 3,000–4,000 feet, with dwarf strongholds at the most defensible crests. The eastern river valleys are sheltered and heavily forested, home to gnome prospectors and halfling communities who have survived here by being consistently underestimated. The northern margin bogs, where the Sepia dissolves into the Quag Mires, are the most dangerous terrain in the region and the primary zone of University expedition activity.

Environmental Notice

The current season (12th of Planting, 576 CY) is affected by the Blackfrost Bloom, an active magical cold phenomenon originating in the Quag Mires. Temperatures are running three to four weeks behind seasonal norms. Expect frost at night, killing winds off Lake Quag, and unstable bog margins. Refer to the Regional Guide - The Sepia Uplands & Lake Quag for full seasonal and terrain data.

## Ritterstadt: The Hub at the Edge of the World

Ritterstadt was not founded by planners. It was founded by survivors.

In 565 CY, four individuals, now known as the Four Founders, turned their backs on the relative comfort of Eru Tovar and struck north through the Quag Mire margin to reach the ruins of Cold Stone Keep. What they retrieved from that place, the Fiend's Embrace, has not left Ritterstadt since. The hamlet was built around the obligation to guard it: a palisade, a longhouse, a smith's forge, and a commitment to remain. Everything that has been added since, the Fiend's Rest public house, the University's student quarters, the market stalls, the growing census, has been built onto that original, stubborn act of staying.

### What Ritterstadt Is

Ritterstadt is simultaneously a frontier outpost, an academic institution, and an ongoing experiment in whether civilization can be bootstrapped from scratch at the edge of the known world. Its population of approximately fifty souls includes former soldiers, displaced scholars, gnomish craftspeople, and several individuals whose reasons for being here are conspicuously vague. The University provides the organizational framework, the leveling system, the Job Board at the Fiend's Rest, the mission classification structure, but the settlement runs on collective resilience more than institutional authority.

### What Ritterstadt Is Not

Ritterstadt is not safe. The palisade is partially under repair. The census currently reads one out of a target fifty souls. The active threats listed on the Campaign Timepiece, the Blackfrost Bloom, the Wolf Nomad migration, are not background noise. They are real, proximate dangers that have already shaped the settlement's early history. New arrivals who treat Ritterstadt as a comfortable base from which to casually adventure will discover very quickly that the margin between "base" and "the thing you need rescuing from" is considerably thinner here than in more established settlements.

## The Quag Mires: The Source of Everything Concerning

North of Ritterstadt's palisade, the margin bogs transition into the Quag Mires proper: a vast, fog-choked expanse of fen, channel, and stagnant lake stretching toward the Rovers' Barrens and the territory of Iuz. The Quag Mires are the defining geopolitical and supernatural reality of the Northern Frontier. They produce the undead that press against Ritterstadt's perimeter. They generate the cold that blights the Sepia's growing season. They contain Cold Stone Keep, whose maps the Founders sealed and whose contents have not been publicly inventoried. They are the proximate reason Ritterstadt exists.

### The Blackfrost Bloom: An Active Catastrophe

The Blackfrost Bloom is a cyclical magical phenomenon, emerging roughly every 11–15 years, in which a cold, colorless magical frost spreads outward from the deepest Quag Mire channels. The druids of the Old Faith at Ehlonna's Mount call it a wound in the world: the earth bleeding cold through an old scar left by the Rain of Colorless Fire.

In a Bloom year, several effects become observable and dangerous:

- Black ice forms on standing water in what should be frost-free months, and does not melt until noon
    
- Bloom Crystals, small translucent, faintly luminescent crystals, form on stone and organic matter near affected areas; their alchemical classification is still under University research
    
- Vegetation die-back creates grey patches across otherwise healthy heather moor
    
- Undead pressure increases: bog zombies and marsh-drowned are encountered closer to the palisade than in non-Bloom years
    
- Behavioral changes in predators push them further south and into contact with settled areas
    

The 576 CY Bloom is active. The druids' concern is described as grave.

### The Shadow of Iuz in the Northeast

Iuz the Evil was imprisoned beneath Castle Greyhawk by the Mad Archmage Zagig Yragerne in 505 CY. His cults did not disband at his disappearance. They reorganized. By 513 CY they had produced the Horned Society, and through the 550s the Old One's cambion generals and corrupted high priests kept his territorial apparatus alive in his absence.

In 570 CY, Iuz was released, by whom and how remains disputed, and returned to Dorakaa. In 576 CY he has been back on his throne for six years, rebuilding, recruiting, and settling accounts with the Horned Society next door. His agents cross through the eastern Sepia Uplands with enough frequency to be a documented threat. They are not here in force. They do not need to be. They are mapping, subverting, and watching for anything, a relic, a power nexus, a sufficiently desperate community, that might be turned to use. The Fiend's Embrace in Ritterstadt is, by any theological analysis, exactly the kind of object that Iuz's agents would find interesting.

## Factions of the Northern Frontier

### The Concatenated Cantons of Perrenland

A distant and largely indifferent nominal authority. They want stability, functioning trade routes, and to not be embarrassed internationally. They will express concern about any disaster in the Sepia Uplands retrospectively and diplomatically, after the fact.

Relationship to the University: Ambivalent tolerance. The University generates prestige and keeps the northern roads marginally safer. Both are desirable outcomes that require no Canton expenditure.

### Clan Irongrip and Clan Brock (Clatspur Dwarves)

The two great dwarf clanholds of the western Teeth have maintained their positions for over four centuries. They are the most reliable long-term powers in the region and the most insular. They trade in metal and information. They do not explain their reasoning. The fact that Hogar Irongrip, a Clan Irongrip exile, currently operates out of Ritterstadt as the settlement's master smith is a diplomatic irregularity that both sides have elected not to address formally. For now.

Relationship to the University: Commercially productive, politically sensitive. Never ask a Clatspur dwarf about Bitter Pass politics.

### The Druid Enclave at Ehlonna's Mount

A small, mixed-race enclave of druids and acolytes maintaining a standing stone circle forty miles southeast of Ritterstadt. They hold the most accurate observational records of the Sepia Uplands' natural and supernatural cycles of any organization in the region, including the Bloom. They are neither hostile nor welcoming. They are watching Ritterstadt with the patient attention of people who have seen settlements come and go and are forming an opinion about which category this one belongs to.

Relationship to the University: Neutral, evaluating. A formal relationship with the enclave would be the single most valuable diplomatic achievement available to the current student body. The correct approach has not yet been found.

### The Wolf Nomads

The eastern Sepia Uplands are ancestral Flan hunting grounds, traversed by the Wolf Nomad migration routes that run south in autumn and north again in late spring. The Nomads regard Ritterstadt as an encroachment and the standing stones on the high moor as sacred. They have not attacked the settlement. Individual outriders have been observed watching the palisade from the high moor. In 576 CY, with the migration active and the Blackfrost Bloom disrupting their seasonal patterns, the Nomads are under pressure, which makes them more dangerous, not less.

Relationship to the University: Skeptical, possibly hostile. A formal meeting with a band leader is a high-stakes diplomatic event, not an encounter to be stumbled into.

### The Kron Gnomes and Valley Halflings

The gnome prospectors in the eastern valleys and the halfling communities in the warmer valley floors are the most accessible and commercially useful peoples in the region. The gnomes know the mineral geography of the eastern Sepia better than anyone. The halflings know the valley paths. Both groups have survived by being strategically underestimated, and neither will respond well to being patronized.

Relationship to the University: Cautiously favorable; commercially engaged. Gnome academic curiosity about the University's research activities is a significant potential asset.

### Agents of Iuz

Not a faction in the sense of having a presence one can negotiate with. They are a persistent threat: corrupted humans, humanoid intermediaries, the occasional half-fiend operative, moving through the eastern Sepia with plausible deniability, taking a specific interest in anything with the theological signature of a sealed demonic relic. Students should report confirmed sightings to University leadership immediately and without freelancing a response.

## Cosmic Context: What Lies Beneath the Bloom

The Blackfrost Bloom is the active threat students will encounter first. It is not the largest thing in play.

### Tharizdun and the Cold Scar

The Quag Mires are, by the best available scholarship, a physical consequence of the Rain of Colorless Fire, but the druids of Ehlonna's Mount hold a second theory that has not been formally discredited. They believe the Quag Mires are not simply corrupted soil. They believe something was put there. That the north's perpetual cold, its tendency to generate undead, and the cyclical Bloom are not a wound in the world but a seal on one, and that the seal is slowly failing.

Tharizdun, the Chained God, the Ebon God of eternal entropy, was imprisoned by a near-universal divine alliance after his creation of the Abyss. He cannot act directly upon Oerth. He can, however, influence those who find his sealed places. His followers, the Doomdreamers, believe the path to his liberation runs through the accumulation of suffering, cold, and undeath. Whether the Quag Mires represent one of his ancient prisons, a node of his power, or simply a coincidental resonance with his domain, no one at the University's current staffing level knows. What is known: the Fiend's Embrace responds to the Bloom in ways that have not been documented.

### Callings the Frontier Has Work For

Magic is rare, dangerous, and viewed with bone-deep suspicion by the common folk of the Sepia Uplands, whose grandparents grew up in the shadow of what magic can do to a continent. Steel, woodcraft and a strong back are understood. Every permitted class is playable here; these are the ones the Sepia itself seems to produce.

- **Barbarians** come out of Wolf Nomad warrior culture or the brutal survival logic of the Quag Mire margin. The [[03. Mechanics/Rules & Classes/Barbarian/Uirtag Badger|Uirtag Badger]] and the [[03. Mechanics/Rules & Classes/Barbarian/Thillonrian Berserker|Thillonrian Berserker]] are the local traditions; Zealots are sometimes consecrated by the Old Faith druids as living embodiments of Ehlonna's or Beory's wrath against whatever is fouling the north.
- **Fighters** of the Battle Master and Champion kind are the frontier's backbone: pragmatic, tactical, and trained to survive ambushes on open moor. A Cavalier or a [[Permitted Classes & Subclasses#Reflavored — Bannerets of Holy Shielding|Banneret of Holy Shielding]] is a Shield Lands exile who followed trouble north. The [[03. Mechanics/Rules & Classes/Fighter/Furyondian Crusader|Furyondian Crusader]] shield-wall is the other tradition the dwarves respect.
- **Rangers** are the single most useful thing Ritterstadt can recruit. Gloom Stalkers for the Bloom-darkened margin, Monster Slayers with undead experience, and the [[03. Mechanics/Rules & Classes/Ranger/Wolf Sentinels|Wolf Sentinels]] and [[03. Mechanics/Rules & Classes/Ranger/Mist Walkers of the Burneal Forest|Mist Walkers]] of the northern forests.
- **Druids** are rooted in the Old Faith of the Flan, the living theological tradition of the Sepia Uplands. Land druids here are Moor or Arctic specialists; Moon druids serve the enclave at Ehlonna's Mount as shape-shifting scouts. The [[03. Mechanics/Rules & Classes/Druid/Circle of Winter|Circle of Winter]] has a professional interest in the Bloom.
- **Clerics** of St. Cuthbert (Order) are the pragmatic spine of Perrenlander civic faith; Grave clerics of Wee Jas study what the Quag Mires produce; Life clerics of Pelor or Ehlonna keep students alive; Knowledge clerics of Boccob are University-aligned almost by definition.
- **Paladins** of the Watchers oath are uniquely at home here: they are mandated to guard against planar leaks and fiendish incursions, and the Quag Mires are exactly that kind of thin-boundary country. Of the Greyhawk oaths, [[03. Mechanics/Rules & Classes/Paladin/Oath of St. Cuthbert|St. Cuthbert]], [[03. Mechanics/Rules & Classes/Paladin/Oath of Pelor|Pelor]] and [[03. Mechanics/Rules & Classes/Paladin/Oath of Beory|Beory]] are the ones the frontier recognizes.
- **Rogues** of the Inquisitive kind serve as agents for the Cantons or, more quietly, as the University's intelligence arm in the field. Masterminds in this region are frequently former Scarlet Brotherhood-adjacent operatives who have changed allegiances at least once.
- **Monks** are foreign here. A Shadow monk in the Northern Frontier is almost certainly connected to the Scarlet Brotherhood's intelligence network; an Open Hand monk sometimes emerges from the druid enclave's ascetic traditions. The [[03. Mechanics/Rules & Classes/Monk/Wayfarers Monastic Order|Wayfarers]] pass through on the roads.
- **Wizards** are Guild of Wizardry graduates almost without exception. Divination is the most immediately practical discipline where the terrain actively resists mapping; Abjuration is the appropriate response to a region that generates undead. The Bloom Crystals are the reason the University is here, and the reason a wizard is welcome.
- **Sorcerers** are distrusted. Wild Magic in the northern Sepia is often attributed to exposure to Bloom Crystals rather than bloodline; Storm Sorcerers come off Lake Quag's seasonal gales; an Aberrant Mind is, almost universally, very bad news.
- **Warlocks** are not chosen; they are made. Nobody enrolls at Ritterstadt as a warlock. If the bog's collective dead, an aspect of Iuz, or whatever is interested in the Fiend's Embrace decides to make an offer to a student in the field, the DM will make it, and the price will be part of the story.
- **Bards** of the College of Lore serve as living archives of a region with very few written records; Valor bards sometimes travel with the nomad bands as memory-keepers for cultures that distrust writing.

## House Rules: Survival on the Northern Frontier

Operating in the Sepia Uplands in 576 CY requires an acceptance of the tactical and environmental realities of a cold, ungoverned frontier.

- **Starting Level:** All students begin at **1st Level**.

The campaign's core rules — no feats (ability score increases to a maximum stat of 24), flanking, expanded attack options, **Lingering Shock**, and **Long-Term Injuries** — are shared across every table in the setting and maintained in a single source, **[[Campaign House Rules]]**, transcluded here:

### Environmental Hazards of the Sepia Uplands

These conditions are specific to the Northern Frontier and apply in addition to the core rules above.

- **Cold:** The Blackfrost Bloom suppresses temperatures across the northern Sepia throughout Planting 576 CY. Exposure to the Quag Mire, margin bog water, or unprotected sleep without a cold-weather bedroll constitutes Frigid Water or Extreme Cold conditions, leading to Constitution saving throws against Exhaustion. Cold-weather gear is mandatory kit north of Kottsmort.
- **Bog:** Travel in the margin bogs and Quag Mires without a bog pole and a guide constitutes Difficult Terrain with a periodic DC 12 Dexterity save or become Restrained (submerged in peat). Failure by 5 or more: begin suffocating.
- **Blackfrost Crystals:** Bloom Crystals found in the field are University research property. Students are encouraged, not required, to submit them for classification. Students who pocket them without logging them and subsequently experience unusual magical effects are reminded that they were warned.

## The Knowledge Every Student Should Carry

As of 576 CY, the party should treat the following as operational truths, not speculation:

1. Ritterstadt is not reinforced. There is no Perrenland garrison coming. The University's operational capacity is entirely self-generated.
2. The Quag Mires are expanding. The Blackfrost Bloom is the current mechanism; what is driving the Bloom is an open research question with significant survival stakes.
3. The Fiend's Embrace is here for a reason. The Founders did not build a settlement in the most inhospitable part of the Flanaess for the view. What they found, and sealed, matters.
4. Iuz is free, and has been since 570 CY. The Old One rules from Dorakaa again, and his agents in the eastern Sepia are preparation, not opportunism.
5. Magic is a historical weapon of mass destruction. The Twin Cataclysms are not ancient mythology to the people who live in their shadow. Wild magic, unstable arcane energy, and uncontrolled spellcasting are treated by Perrenlanders with the same instinctive distrust a farmer has for open flame in a hay barn. Act accordingly.
6. The moor will outlast you if you are careless. Bog poles. Cold-weather bedrolls. A guide in the margin bogs. These are not suggestions.

## Narrative Integration: The 576 CY Frontier

The Chronicler observes that the strategic significance of Ritterstadt is invisible to most people outside a thirty-mile radius, which is precisely the problem. The Cantons see a private academic venture on an ungoverned moor. Perrenland's merchant interests see a promising northern supply chain for bog iron and salvage. The Wolf Nomads see an encroachment on their ancestral routes. The druids see an experiment in whether the south's ambitions can coexist with the north's age.

What the Chronicler sees: a palisade, half-repaired, at the edge of a marsh that is being directed southward by something that has not yet been identified. A relic of unknown provenance locked in a settlement of fifty people with no military support. A University leveling system that turns expeditions into a curriculum, which is either the most ingenious approach to frontier survival in the history of the Flanaess or a remarkable way to lose an entire student cohort at once.

The year 576 CY is the window. The Bloom will run its course or it will not. The Wolf Nomads will migrate through or they will not. Iuz's agents will continue mapping the eastern passes until they find something worth reporting. Cold Stone Keep sits in the marsh at an unrecorded location, containing things that four very capable people decided needed to stay there.

The road to understanding what Ritterstadt is actually guarding runs north. Into the fog. Across the black ice. Past the rooftops of Kessmark's drowned dead, just visible above the refrozen channel.

Bring a bog pole. Watch the Bloom Crystals. Keep the Embrace within the palisade.

The marsh does not announce itself before it moves.
