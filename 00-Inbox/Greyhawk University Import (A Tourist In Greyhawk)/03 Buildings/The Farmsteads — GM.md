# GM Secret

> [!NOTE|ghost] GM Secret
> Three of the eleven farmsteads are currently occupied by families who are not entirely what they appear. The **Beckenhaus** family — four adults and two children — moved to Ritterstadt from the east two years ago and have been unremarkable neighbors. However, one of the Beckenhaus adults (their eldest son, Kurt) has been making regular late-night trips to the forest edge, always on nights with no moon. He has not told his family what he's doing. He is leaving offerings at an old tree-shrine — a pre-Pholtine Flan spirit-site — not from malice but from private desperation: his youngest daughter has been ill for months and nothing has worked.

# GM Notes

- **Population density:** Eleven longhouses + The Big Barn. Roughly 30–32 farming/laboring villagers plus their children. The farms extend outward from the village in a rough star pattern, each with a small field and pasture.
- **Winter hardship:** Every winter, 1d4 villagers fall seriously ill. The Ritter is obligated by custom to ensure no one starves; he draws from the emergency grain store when needed. It has been three years since anyone starved. This is considered a good run.
- **The Big Barn:** Large enough to shelter the whole village simultaneously. Contains: stalls for 14 horses and assorted livestock, a hay loft (good cover for ambushes or hiding), a heavy bar on the main doors (from the inside), and two small side doors. It smells exactly as you would expect.
- **Village gossip network:** The farmsteads operate as a tight social web. Any significant event (arrival of strangers, unusual weather, a fight, a death, a new baby) is common knowledge within the farmsteads by the following morning. The party's presence will be known to every farming family by sunset of their first day.
- **Barter economy:** Within the farmsteads, everything operates on barter and favor-debt. Coin is rarely used between neighbors. Players who want to trade with farming families should offer services, goods, or labor rather than gold.
