# ⛓️ The Kettenhaus

> *"Ketten binden beide Enden."*
> **A chain binds at both ends.** — the house motto, which sounds like a reassurance and is not one.

---

## Overview

The **Kettenhaus** — formally *Haus Hanselmann van Traft*, universally called the House of the Chain — is a Perrender lending house seated in **[[Traft]]**, at the foot of the main Clatspur pass. It lends against land and against title, and it lends on the **Schuldbrief**: the sealed debt-letter that is the only instrument it cares about and the only one it has ever needed.

It is not a bank in the modern sense and it does not think of itself as a merchant company. It thinks of itself as a **registry of obligations** — a house that writes down what one party owes another and then makes very sure the writing holds. Everything else the Kettenhaus does, including sending men like [[Vethkar Graun]] four days north into unclaimed country, is in service of that one function.

**Why it matters to Ritterstadt:** the Kettenhaus holds paper on [[Ritter Konrad von Eisfeld]]. See *The Eisfeld Schuldbrief*, below.

---

## 🔗 The name and the device

A chain does two things at once, and the house was named for both of them.

A chain **binds**. And a surveyor's chain **measures** — it is the tool you drag across a field to say how much of it there is, and therefore how much it is worth, and therefore how much can be borrowed against it. The Kettenhaus lends you the length of your own land and then holds the other end.

**The device:** a surveyor's chain coiled into a closed ring, sealed in dark green wax.

**The joke Perrenders make:** eight cantons swore the Covenant of Concatenation and bound themselves together with oaths; the ninth link in the chain is the one that charges interest. Nobody in Traft makes this joke where a Hanselmann factor can hear it.

---

## 🏛️ Structure

The house is built in **links** — the internal word for a posting, and deliberately so. Every man in the Kettenhaus is a link, and a link that fails is replaced rather than repaired.

| Rank | Perrender term | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| House head | **Kettenmeester** | Runs a *kontor* (counting-house). Traft's is **Gerlach Hanselmann**. Signs foreclosures. |
| Field agent | **Schätzer** *(appraiser)* | Travels to the collateral, assesses it, files a report. Vethkar's actual job title. |
| Junior agent | **Glied** *(link)* | Carries papers, watches doors, does not sign anything. |
| Retained muscle | *(unnamed)* | Contracted, not enrolled. Vethkar's two travel with him — one is called **Marek**. |

> [!note] Naming collision to watch
> The house calls its field agents **Schätzer**, not *Assessors* — because **the Assessor** is already taken. That is the node-agent who appears at R3 in [[0006 - The Survey Line]], and the two have nothing to do with each other. Keep the terms apart or the table will connect them.

## 🏔️ Clan backing — the Hussen

The Kettenhaus is **clan-backed, and that is the whole of its political weight.** It is a Hanselmann house by family, but it operates under the patronage of the **Hussen clan**.

This matters enormously *right now*, in 576 CY: **Franz Hussen, Pfalzgraf of the Hussen clan, was elected Voormann this very year.** The house's patron clan has just taken the highest office in Perrenland.

Consequences the DM should hold:

- **In Traft, nobody crosses the Kettenhaus at the moment.** Not the magistrate, not the tax collectors, not a rival house. This is recent and everybody has noticed.
- **The house knows this is temporary.** Voormanns are elected, not crowned. Gerlach Hanselmann is consolidating while the window is open, which is precisely why a Schätzer is four days north assessing a marginal frontier debt that a cautious house would have let sit.
- **The window has a closing date.** Franz Hussen's Voormannship ends badly and publicly within a decade. When it does, the Kettenhaus loses its cover, and every foreclosure signed during these years becomes something a rival can reopen. *If the campaign ever reaches that far, this is a loaded gun already on the table.*

> [!warning] ⚠️ House-canon flag
> **Core Gazetteer:** the eight cantons, the Covenant of Concatenation, Perren's election c. 400 CY, the Sepia Uplands as nominally-Perrender-but-largely-unclaimed.
> **Living Greyhawk / wiki-tier:** the clan roster (Hussen, Weisspeer, Meerijder), the Pax Mercuri at Schwartzenbruin (500 CY), Franz Hussen's election in 576 CY and his later disgrace.
> **Pure homebrew:** the Kettenhaus itself, Gerlach Hanselmann, the Schätzer/Glied structure, the motto and the device.
> Log the middle tier in `house-canon.md` before building further on it. If you would rather not hang the house on a dated canonical Voormannship, swap the patron clan — everything above still works, you lose only the 583–584 timer.

## 📜 The Eisfeld Schuldbrief

The house's business in Ritterstadt, in full.

| Item | Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Debtor** | [[Ritter Konrad von Eisfeld]] |
| **Principal** | **700 gp** |
| **With accrued interest** | **~900 gp** |
| **Security** | Eisfeld timber rights |
| **Status** | **Open. Assessment in progress; nothing filed.** |
| **Agent on site** | [[Vethkar Graun]] |

> [!danger] ⚓ Canon ruling — the debt figure
> **700 gp principal / ~900 gp with interest is now canon**, per the 0004 master run note. Two other files disagree and must be corrected:
> - [[Campaign Threads]] **P1** says 800 gp → change to 700/900.
> - [[DM Reference — Faction Dossiers]] says 800 + 200 accrued = 1,000 → change to 700/900.
> - [[0004 - Level 3 Scaling Notes]] calls the house the *"Ritterstadt Consortium"* → an early-draft slip. It is the **Kettenhaus**, seated in Traft, and it is not Ritterstadt's anything.

**What the house actually wants from Ritterstadt.** Not the money. Konrad cannot pay 900 gp and everyone involved knows it. The house wants a defensible answer to one question: *is the timber worth taking, or is this hamlet a liability we should walk away from with the paper intact?* Vethkar's report answers that. Until it is filed, **the decision has not been made and the party can still change it.**

---

## 🎯 Relevance to the party

The Kettenhaus is not a villain. It is a **creditor with a legal instrument and no particular malice**, which is worse, because there is nobody to defeat and nothing to steal back. It cannot be fought. It can only be *argued with*, *delayed*, or *given a better number*.

Three live pressure points:

1. **The report is unfiled.** What the party does in front of Vethkar between now and his departure is, quite literally, the evidence base.
2. **Konrad does not have a move.** He knows the paper is real, he knows he cannot pay, and he has told nobody. Anything the party brings him — a buyer, a revenue stream, a University endowment, the Field Station's timber contract — is more than he currently has.
3. **The Field Station is collateral-adjacent.** Greyhawk University's presence sits on land whose timber rights are the security on this debt. If the house forecloses, the University has a problem it does not yet know about.

## ⚖️ Open question — Marbrand & Fessel

The Verbobonc counting-house in [[0005 - The Timber Run]] (device: **a scale crossed with a key**) is buying Eisfeld toll manifests and paying the Sepia Verge cell. The Kettenhaus holds paper secured on Eisfeld timber. **Two southern money-houses are working the same valley in the same season.** This is not yet decided. Pick one before 0005 runs:

- **Rivals.** Marbrand & Fessel are moving on collateral the Kettenhaus already has locked, and buying manifests to price it. A quiet war between creditors, with Ritterstadt as the ground. *Strongest option — it gives Vethkar a reason to help the party for entirely selfish reasons.*
- **The same hand.** Marbrand & Fessel is a Verbobonc front the Kettenhaus uses where a Perrender seal would draw notice. Colder, simpler, and makes the house considerably more sinister than "creditor."
- **Unconnected.** Coincidence of geography; the Uplands are simply where cheap collateral is. Cheapest to run, weakest payoff.

# GM Notes

> [!danger] DM Eyes Only

## What the house will and will not do

| Will | Will not |
| :--- | :--- |
| Send an appraiser into unclaimed country alone | Send soldiers. It has none and wants none |
| Retain local muscle by contract, quietly | Order a killing. It orders *assessments* |
| Wait years for an instrument to mature | Renegotiate out of sympathy |
| Buy information about collateral | Buy information about people, as a rule |
| Foreclose on a knight and sleep well | Break its own paper — the Schuldbrief is sacred to it |

**The tonal rule:** the Kettenhaus never threatens, because it never has to. It describes a consequence and lets the other party do the arithmetic. Vethkar's whole manner is the house's manner, scaled down to one man.

## Escalation ladder

If the party obstructs, embarrasses, or attacks the house's interests, it does not retaliate — it **re-prices**.

1. **Bad report filed.** Timeline accelerates. Foreclosure moves from "under consideration" to "scheduled."
2. **A second Schätzer.** Less pleasant than Vethkar, and arriving with the first one's notes.
3. **The instrument sold on.** The Kettenhaus takes its loss, sells the Schuldbrief at a discount to someone with fewer manners, and washes its hands. *This is the genuinely frightening outcome and the party will not see it coming.*
4. **Quiet withdrawal.** If Ritterstadt looks like more trouble than the timber is worth, the house simply stops. Konrad keeps his land and never learns how close it was.

> [!tip] If Vethkar is ever killed
> Per the 0004 dialogue file this is the worst non-fatal outcome in that adventure. The house's response is procedural, not vengeful: a Schätzer is an *asset*, and an asset lost in the field is an incident to be investigated. Someone comes north to find out what happened, and that someone is a link the house does not mind spending.

## Seeds

- **Gerlach Hanselmann has never travelled north.** He signs things. If the campaign ever puts the party in Traft, he is a scene: a courteous old man in a cold room full of sealed letters, one of which has Konrad's name on it.
- **The kontor's archive.** Every Schuldbrief the house has ever written, in order. Somewhere in it is the one that explains how a frontier knight came to owe 700 gp to a Perrender house in the first place — *which nobody has yet asked Konrad about.*
- **The Hussen connection is deniable and provable.** A player who goes looking in Traft can establish it. What they do with it is the interesting part.
