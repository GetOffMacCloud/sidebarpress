# GS2 · The Long Night at Candlecrown

**APL 6 (levels 5–7) · 5 characters (scales to 4–6) · three sessions of 3–4 hours (an optional fourth)**

> *Every Midwinter Night, Candlecrown sings seven verses to Telchur. This year, something has come down out of the cold to collect the seventh.*

## Introduction
*The Long Night at Candlecrown* is a Needfest adventure for the World of Greyhawk, set in 576 CY in the Tors, the mesa-topped hills on the southern edge of the League of the Yeomanry. It is cozy with teeth: a real midwinter festival with toys to paint and a feast to share, and a real threat that wants to take the village's voice.

**How to use this book.** Read the Background and the Running the Adventure section before play. The scenes run in order of play over three sessions. Session 3 is a siege; read [[SP-GS02 — The Lantern Line]] before it.

**Read-aloud text** is in italic boxes. Read it as written or paraphrase it, but keep every clue.

**Conventions.** Creature names in **bold** have statblocks in the [[SP-GS02 — Bestiary]]. Numbers in parentheses after a creature are the count for five characters; each encounter table gives counts for four and six. Deities appear in square brackets after a name ([Telchur]); a deity in braces {Wee Jas} is lapsed or false.

## Background

### What the Locals Know
- **Candlecrown** is a hamlet of about 60 souls on the flat crown of a tor on the Yeomanry's southern edge, a day's ride south of Loftwick. One switchback path, the **Candle Stair**, is cut into the 400-foot cliff.
- It began as a Suel silver-mine head. The silver ran out generations ago. The village now lives by its **lantern-and-toy workshop**: carved lanterns, tin toys and painted Needfest figures, sent down to the valley farms each year on the first day of Needfest.
- Every **Midwinter Night** (Needfest 4, the Dim Night, when Luna is dark) the whole village sings the **Long Night Carol** in the workshop hall: a hymn to Telchur, the winter god, older than anyone's grandmother. Six verses are in Common; the seventh is in a tongue no one speaks. The lanterns stay lit until dawn. "No lantern dark, no verse unsung."
- This winter is wrong. The snow came early and won't stop. Two carters' mules froze on the Stair. Tyrg packs have come up out of the eastern Tors farther than anyone remembers.

### What's Really Happening
- **The seventh verse is a curse.** Before the Rain of Colorless Fire, a mad sorcerer of the Suel Imperium bound a host of cold spirits with a spoken curse. The bound spirits became the **immoths**, and each is driven to gather the curse's scattered words back to the Mountain of Ultimate Winter. The sorcerer's apprentice fled the cataclysm with the miners who founded Candlecrown, and hid one verse of the curse inside the local hymn to Telchur, in old Suloise, where it would be sung (and so kept alive) but never written down. He also carved a warning and a **counter-verse** in the miners' chapel, in the mine under the mesa.
- **Hrimvald the Gatherer**, an immoth, heard the verse carried on last year's midwinter wind. It has come to the Tors. Its storm sits over the mesa; the tyrgs it drives before it are its hunting dogs.
- **Immoths hoard words.** Hrimvald takes the verse *and the voices that carry it*: a singer who meets it loses their voice, the words frozen into an ice rune on its body. If it gets the whole verse, it leaves, and Candlecrown never sings again.
- **Hrimvald bargains before it fights.** It would trade the verse for a word it values more: a true name, a secret no one living has spoken, a confession, or above all the apprentice's counter-verse, the one word that could set an immoth free of the curse.
- **The stranger at the gate.** **Aurel Vasch**, a vampire spawn whose master died in the Passage of Slerotin last autumn, has been sheltering in the old mine shafts. The storm and the immoth have driven him up. He will ask to be let in. He can't cross the gate, a threshold or the workshop door uninvited, and only a villager can invite him. Invited in, he feeds through the night and makes a thrall of a singer.

## Adventure Outline
The party reaches Candlecrown as Needfest begins and a storm that won't break closes the Candle Stair. Over three nights, voices vanish, tyrgs climb the cliffs and a stranger asks to come in from the cold. The party learns that the carol's seventh verse is the prize of an immoth, an ice-giant hoarder of words, and that the old Suel mine beneath the village holds the truth. On Midwinter Night they hold the workshop hall while the village sings: fight the immoth with fire, trick it, or bargain with words worth more than the verse.

## Hooks
1. **The gift wagons.** Ardo Pennick, a Loftwick toy-seller, paid in advance for Candlecrown's Needfest lanterns. The wagons never came down. He'll pay 150 gp to have them fetched before Needfest 7, and 50 more to know why they were late.
2. **Shelter.** The party is on the valley road when the storm hits. Candlecrown's lights, high on the tor, are the only ones in sight. Dob Kettle waves them up the Stair with a lantern on a pole.
3. **The carol.** Sage Imra Vell of Loftwick, a scholar of Suel hymns, wants a copy of "the Telchur carol with the verse in a dead tongue." She'll pay 200 gp for the seventh verse transcribed. The village won't let it be written. (She is honest, and she is the reason Hrimvald heard it: she hummed it to a traveling skald last winter.)
4. **Personal.** A character's kin, aunt or grandmother, sang in the carol as a child, and has just written: "Come for Needfest. Something's wrong with the snow." See [[SP-GS02 — Handout — Aunt Sedge's Letter]]: by default it comes from Mother Sedge herself, the character's great-aunt.
5. **Decline path.** If the party won't go, the wagons never come down. In spring the Yeomanry hears that Candlecrown has gone silent: the village is still there, and no one in it can speak. The immoth's library becomes a known place in the high Tors (see [[Scene 13 — The Ice Library]]).

## Running the Adventure
- **Laws, the storm, the lantern line, the choir, stolen voices and the safety valve** are in [[SP-GS02 — The Lantern Line]].
- **Clocks.** The Villain's Scheme above is the clock. The scenes follow it day by day; the party's choices change who loses a voice, not when the storm comes.
- **The five facts** and their routes are the Clue Ledger in the [[10-Adventures/In Development/SP-GS02 — The Long Night at Candlecrown/Scenes/00 — Scene Index|Scene Index]]; the nudge list is in [[SP-GS02 — The Lantern Line#Nudge List]].
- **Factions.** *Hrimvald* wants the seventh verse and will take voices to get it; it hopes to trade for something better. *Aurel* wants warmth, blood and a way out of the Tors; he hopes a frightened village will open its gate. *The elder* wants the carol sung, as always; he hopes the trouble will pass if no one panics. *Mother Sedge* wants the verse kept safe; she hopes the party is the help her letter asked for. Aurel watches the village from the cliffs; Hrimvald hears everything said aloud in the storm.
