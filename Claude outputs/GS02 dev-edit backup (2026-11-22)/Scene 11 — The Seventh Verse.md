# Scene 11 — The Seventh Verse

[[10-Adventures/In Development/SP-GS02 — The Long Night at Candlecrown/Scenes/00 — Scene Index|00 — Scene Index]] · ← [[Scene 10 — The Long Night]] · Next → [[Scene 12 — Dawn at Candlecrown]]

## Purpose
The climax. Hrimvald the Gatherer comes over the north rim for the seventh verse while the village sings it. The party fights it with fire, tricks it, or bargains with words worth more than the verse, or some mix of all three. Every solution was seeded earlier (see the Climax-at-a-Glance table in the [[10-Adventures/In Development/SP-GS02 — The Long Night at Candlecrown/Scenes/00 — Scene Index|Scene Index]]).
**Budget:** 60 minutes. Running long? Have Hrimvald open with the bargain and accept the first fair offer.

## Arrival
Read the box that fits. If 12 or more lantern posts are lit, use the first; otherwise, the second.

> *Mother Sedge's voice rises alone into the first line of the seventh verse, in words none of you know. Along the north rim, the lantern light catches movement: something climbing up out of the dark, hand over enormous hand, glazed and glittering. It stands. It is twenty feet tall, made of blue ice, and every inch of it is written on. Its eyes find the hall.*

> *Mother Sedge's voice rises alone into the first line of the seventh verse. The north lanterns are dark. Then the dark itself stands up, twenty feet of glittering blue ice an arm's length from the nearest of you, and the snow around it stops falling.*

**To the table:** *"It hasn't attacked. It's listening. What do you do?"*

## Who Is Here
- **Hrimvald the Gatherer** ([[Immoth]]; dossier [[Hrimvald the Gatherer]]). It has 3 runes plus one for every Candlecrown voice it has taken (Corin, Dob and Wenna by default: 6 runes). *Wants:* the seventh verse, all of it. *Will settle for:* a word worth more (The Bargain). *Reacts:* see the round-by-round below.
- **The tyrg alpha** (1), if she survived wave 3, at Hrimvald's side.
- **The choir**, singing the seventh verse over and over until dawn or until Hrimvald is gone. Mother Sedge leads if Wenna is mute.
- **Aurel Vasch**, wherever Scene 10 left him.

## The Seventh Verse Track
The verse has seven lines. The choir sings one line per round, starting at the top of round 1, and starts over when it reaches the end.

**Gathering a line.** At the end of each of Hrimvald's turns, it **gathers the line sung that round** (tick it) unless one of these is true:
- since its last turn, it took **fire damage**; or
- it is more than **120 feet** from the singers; or
- the choir is in the **singing room** (Scene 09) and Hrimvald hasn't broken in (the wall has AC 15 and 60 hit points, or it uses the door, which takes its movement); or
- the choir has stopped singing (see the choir track).

**Seven lines gathered** means Hrimvald has the whole verse: it stops fighting, gives a long courtly bow, and leaves over the north rim with every rune it holds. Go to [[Scene 12 — Dawn at Candlecrown]] (*Silenced*).

**Track:** ☐ 1 · ☐ 2 · ☐ 3 · ☐ 4 · ☐ 5 · ☐ 6 · ☐ 7

The choir track still applies: at the end of each round in which Hrimvald or a tyrg is inside the hall, 1d6 singers flee. Each **Word-Theft** on a singer takes one off the count and adds a rune to Hrimvald. Villagers fail its save automatically. A **Broken** choir stops singing: Hrimvald can't gather lines from silence, so it starts taking the voices of the six who know the verse by heart (Mother Sedge first), one Word-Theft each. When it holds all six, it has the verse (count it as seven lines).

## Round by Round
**Round 1: the offer.** Hrimvald doesn't attack in its first round unless it is attacked. It speaks (everyone hears it, as before):

> *"Lanterns. You have kept the light well. I will be brief, because the verse is short. Give me the words, and I give back the voices I have taken from this hill, all but one: the singer who gives the verse to me. Or offer me something worth more. Or fight, and I take it line by line, and the voices too."*

This is the bargain on the table. It hears offers until the end of its next turn. If it is attacked during the parley, it fights at once and the round counts.

**From round 2 (or the moment it is attacked): the fight.** Hrimvald's priorities, in order:
1. **Reach the singers.** It moves to within 30 feet of the hall's windows (or breaks into the singing room).
2. **Silence fire.** Word-Theft on whoever is casting fire spells or is the loudest; *counterspell* on any fire spell of 3rd level or higher.
3. **Break the line.** *Sleet storm* on the defenders between it and the hall (concentration; it is also why it has an ice wall: *wall of ice* across the hall door in round 2 or 3 if the party is holding it).
4. **Hold off fire.** It climbs the hall roof or the rim face (Icewalking) to stay out of melee and casts *ice storm* on clustered defenders.
5. **Spend words when hurt.** Spend a Word (*ice knife* or *misty step*) each round once it has taken 50 damage. It spends the three strangers' runes first; after that, each rune it spends gives a Candlecrown villager back their voice, and they start singing at once (put them back on the choir count).

**Siege actions** on initiative 20 (from [[Immoth]]): Snuff on the posts nearest the hall; Whiteout when the party has ranged attackers; Icefall on any group under the eaves; The Pack Answers if the alpha lives.

**At 92 hit points or fewer (bloodied):** it offers again, more generously (**Fact 3, last resort**): *"Enough. You have made your point in fire. Name what you would trade."* It now accepts any offer on The Bargain table worth "the verse" or more, and gives back *all* the voices it holds from Candlecrown.

**At 50 hit points or fewer:** it flees up the north rim face and away into the storm with whatever lines and runes it has (see Development). It doesn't come back tonight.

## The Bargain
Hrimvald keeps a bargain to the letter. It values a word for its rarity and its weight: who else knows it, and what it would cost the speaker to give it up. It is a fair judge; tell the players its reaction honestly.

| Offer | Where it comes from | What Hrimvald gives for it |
|---|---|---|
| **The verse, sung to it once** | Its own offer (round 1) | It takes the verse and the singer's voice; gives back all other Candlecrown voices; leaves the Tors. (*Paid.*) |
| **A word no one living has spoken** | The true name of Aurel's dead master (Aurel, Scene 05 or 08); a secret name of a PC's god, ancestor or familiar (the player's choice, a real secret of the character); a dead language's last word | Worth the verse. It leaves without the verse, and gives back the voices it took from Candlecrown. (*Saved.*) The speaker can never say that word again: it is in a rune now. |
| **A confession** | A PC tells it, freely and truthfully, something they have never told anyone (the player decides what; it can be small and true) | Worth half the verse: it gives back the voices and keeps the lines it has gathered. If it has gathered fewer than 4 lines, it leaves; otherwise it wants the rest too. The PC can never speak of that thing again. |
| **The counter-verse** | The rubbing from the chapel (M5), learned in Scene 09 or read aloud by Aurel or by *comprehend languages* | Worth everything. Sung or spoken to it, it unbinds Hrimvald from the Suel curse. It stops, and listens, and says something no one expected (see below). It gives back every voice it holds, including the three strangers', and walks away into the high Tors, free. (*Unbound.*) |
| **Wenna's paper** (the written verse) | Scene 07 | It is the verse, written. Hrimvald accepts it as the verse itself (see *Paid*), but no voice is lost: the paper pays. Mother Sedge will never forgive whoever hands it over, and the curse-verse is now out in the world. (*Paid*, without a voice.) |
| **Magic, gold, the party's lives** | — | Nothing. *"I do not hoard what anyone can make."* |

**The counter-verse.** When the counter-verse is sung to it, Hrimvald's runes go dark one by one, and it speaks in its own voice for the first time, which no one in Candlecrown has heard, because it has always used someone else's: *"Oh."* Then: *"I had forgotten that I was made."* It gives back every voice. It doesn't thank the party. Before it goes, it asks one thing: *"Will you sing it to the others? There are others."* (That is the sequel; see [[Scene 12 — Dawn at Candlecrown]], Continuing the Adventure.)

## The Trick
Two tricks were seeded. Both need preparation (Scene 09) or quick thinking now.

**The false verse.** The choir sings a false seventh verse, made up in Scene 09 to sound like old Suloise. Hrimvald gathers false lines as if they were real. The first time it gathers one, it makes a Wisdom (Insight) check (+7) against the Charisma (Deception) check of the PC who wrote it, with advantage if Mother Sedge didn't help. If it is fooled, it gathers seven false lines, bows, and goes, delighted, to the Mountain of Ultimate Winter. (*Fooled.*) If it sees through it, its Word-Theft recharges at once and it takes the choir's leader next.

**The written verse (the lure).** An immoth can't resist a written verse; written words are the easiest to take. When Hrimvald sees the written seventh verse (Wenna's paper, or one written by a PC), it must use its next turn to move toward it and seize it (no save; it wants it more than anything). Seizing it with its hand takes its action. Then:
- **If the paper is set on fire** (it is on an oil-soaked lure frame from Scene 09, with a lit fuse; or a PC sets it alight as a reaction or readied action as Hrimvald grabs it): the burning words go into Hrimvald's hand as it takes them. It takes 22 (4d10) fire damage, Fire Melts Words triggers, and it is stunned until the end of its next turn, as the verse it wanted burns inside its own runes. The written verse is gone; the *sung* verse is safe for this round (no line gathered). It will want to bargain afterward, bloodied or not.
- **If the paper isn't burning:** it has the whole verse. It leaves at once. (*Paid*, and no voice lost; the curse-verse is out in the world.)

## Fighting It
- **Fire is the weapon.** Fire damage stops a line being gathered, melts the newest rune (10 or more in a turn), and is doubled (vulnerability). Burning oil from the workshop: 5 fire damage, doubled to 10, so one hit melts a rune. Fire pots: 2d6, doubled. The bonfire: a creature shoved into it takes 3d6 fire damage (Hrimvald takes 6d6).
- **Edges barely bite.** Hrimvald resists piercing and slashing, magical or not. Bludgeoning works normally: hammers from the workshop, mauls, spells.
- **The edge.** Hrimvald is Huge; it can't be shoved off the rim by a Medium creature unless it is prone (then contested Athletics, at disadvantage for the shover). If it goes over the north rim, it falls 400 feet (it has a fly speed; it doesn't fall far) and is out of reach of the singers for 2 rounds.
- **Its freezing tail.** A PC restrained or paralyzed by it is freed by any fire damage: a villager with a lantern can do it, at the cost of 1 fire damage.

## Turning Points
| If... | ...then |
|---|---|
| **the alpha is alive** | She fights at Hrimvald's side and guards it from melee. When she falls, Hrimvald spends a Word at once (it is shaken). |
| **the choir breaks** | Hrimvald turns to the six who know the verse. Mother Sedge walks out to meet it; she will offer her voice and the verse unless a PC stops her. |
| **two or more PCs are down** | Hrimvald stops attacking the fallen and offers terms at once (as if bloodied): it wants words, not corpses. |
| **a PC offers a bargain mid-fight** | Hrimvald finishes its turn, then listens. It doesn't attack a creature that is speaking an offer to it, but its siege actions go on. |
| **the party splits** (some at the singers, some at the rim) | Cut between them film-style each round; the singers' side is where the lines are won and lost. |
| **Hrimvald is shoved over the rim** | It flies back up in 2 rounds; no lines are gathered while it's gone. Use the time. |
| **dawn comes** (round 30, or the DM's call) | Hrimvald withdraws with what it has: *Driven Off* if fewer than seven lines. |

## Scaling and Pacing
- **Four PCs:** Hrimvald has 152 hit points; it flees at 40. Let the militia's oil volley happen twice.
- **Six PCs:** Word-Theft recharges on 4–6; add one tyrg (or four minions) on The Pack Answers.
- **Short slot:** start at round 3 with Hrimvald already at the hall's north wall and three lines gathered; run it for six rounds, then dawn.
- **Long, loving slot:** let the parley in round 1 run as long as the table likes. A bargain scene is a climax too.

## Outcome Read-Alouds

**Destroyed.**
> *The giant of ice cracks from the crown of its head to its tail with a sound like a frozen lake breaking. It falls in pieces across the snow. Every rune on it flares blue and goes out, and in the hall, voice after voice joins the verse: Corin's, Dob's, Wenna's, rough and astonished.*

**Unbound.**
> *The runes on the immoth go dark, one by one, from its tail to its crown. It lowers its hands. For a long moment there is no sound but the choir. Then it turns from the hall and walks to the rim, and it steps off into the snow as if the snow were the ground, and is gone.*

> *The immoth goes up the north cliff faster than anything that size should move, trailing steam where the fire caught it. Its runes glitter as it climbs. Then the storm closes behind it. The choir is still singing, and some of the voices it took are still out there in the cold.*

> *The immoth bows, low and courtly, to the hall. Then it walks to the rim and climbs down into the dark, and the snow follows it. Inside the hall, the singing has stopped. Forty people stand on the benches with their mouths open, and none of them make a sound.*

## Development
Note the outcome and go to [[Scene 12 — Dawn at Candlecrown]]:
- **Destroyed:** Hrimvald reduced to 0 hit points. Its body shatters into blue ice; every rune breaks and every voice it held comes home, the three strangers' included (they go back to their owners, wherever they are, or are lost if their owners are dead). (*Saved*, the hard way.)
- **Unbound, Saved, Paid or Fooled:** as The Bargain and The Trick.
- **Driven off** (fled at 50 hit points or fewer): it has the lines it gathered and the runes it still holds. (*Driven Off.* Session 4 is how to get them back.)
- **Silenced:** it gathered all seven lines.
- **The alpha:** dead, or fled with Hrimvald (the pack scatters at dawn either way).
- **Aurel:** see his dossier and Scene 12.
