# The Blood Rite — DM Only

> [!danger] DM only
> Keep this note out of anything players see. Player-facing rules are in [[The Schools of Blood Magic]]. *(Homebrew. Nothing here is Greyhawk canon.)*

## Canon Account
None. Builds on canon only through the Suel Imperium and the Rain of Colorless Fire (see the Lore section of [[Wizard — School of Cruormancy]]).

## Sidebar Press Account

### The Progenitor

At the top of every lineage stands one mage: **the Progenitor**, the only living master of **all three schools** (Cruormancy, Oblamancy, and the unnamed school of mending). He began the art as a war-art of the Suel Imperium and has outlived the empire that bred it.

- **He knows everything about the tradition.** How many blood mages and apprentices exist, their true names, where they are at every moment, and where every Codex is. No one else knows any of it.
- **He lives in solitude.** His location is known only to the blood mage **masters**. Apprentices and ordinary mortals don't know it.
- **He can't be ended.** When the Progenitor is killed, his spirit and power immediately take over a mortal body with blood in a remote place, and he lives again. His killers gain nothing but time.

### The Thread

Every blood mage's blood is tied back to the Progenitor's through the Rite. That tie, **the Thread**, does two things at once:

- **It is the leash.** The Progenitor follows the Thread to every mage and every Codex. Because the Thread runs through blood, not magic, nothing that blocks magical detection can block it. Being findable is the price every blood mage pays.
- **It is the wellspring.** Blood magic doesn't come from the mage. It flows to him from the Rite, along the Thread. The Codex recognizes its owner's blood because that blood is *of the Rite*.

**Why *wish* cuts a mage off.** *Wish* can hide a blood mage only by cutting his Thread. The leash and the wellspring are the same thing, so cutting one cuts the other: no power flows, his blood is no longer of the Rite, and his Codex treats him as a stranger. Its pages never wake again.

**The snap.** The Progenitor feels every Thread that is cut. He doesn't know where the mage went, but he knows someone ran. The abandoned Codex is still tied to him, so he always finds it, and he wants it destroyed.

### The Lineages

- **One master, one apprentice.** Each lineage is a single chain. When the apprentice dies, the master looks for another. When the master dies, the apprentice becomes the master. Only the Progenitor knows how many lineages exist.
- **What masters know.** Where the Progenitor is, and that he exists as more than rumor. Apprentices learn it only when they become masters.
- **A dead lineage.** If master and apprentice both die, the lineage ends, until a wizard of enough power is found. The Progenitor **leaves a Codex beside a worthy mage**, and the chain begins again with a new master who never had one.
- **Masters don't command.** They choose, train, and let go. A master's power over an apprentice ends when the apprentice starts adventuring, except for the one rule nobody breaks: the secrecy of the art.

### When a Codex Is Lost

- The owner must recover it and kill everyone who held it or might have read it.
- If he fails, he confesses to his master. The master uses the owner's blood to destroy the Codex, wherever it is, and everyone near it. The owner dies.
- If the owner is a **master**, he confesses to the Progenitor, who does the same.

### The Lost Grimoires

A few texts from before the lineages survive: the ***Vermilion Codex***, the ***Lament of House Neheli***, the ***Red Testament***, and a handful of others. They are **quest objects**, not spell sources.

> [!question] Idea (not yet decided)
> The *Red Testament* could predate the Rite, which would make it the one source of blood magic *without the Thread*: power without the leash. The Progenitor would burn the world to recover it.

## Running It

- **The master is an NPC with an agenda.** A PC apprentice's master is the DM's to play: mentor, rival, or the villain of a later arc. He doesn't give orders, but he watches.
- **Promotion is a turning point.** When the master dies, the PC becomes a master and learns where the Progenitor is. That's a mid-campaign reveal you can plan for.
- **A lost Codex is an adventure.** A stolen Codex means a hunt with a deadline and a body count. Failing means the PC dies, so give the player every chance to succeed.
- **The Progenitor shouldn't be killable as a solution.** Killing him buys time and a new body somewhere remote. It's a fine climax for an arc, not an ending to the art.

## Open Questions
- **Rebirth host.** Can the Progenitor take any creature with blood, an animal included, or only a person? Does the host's soul survive inside him?
- **The Progenitor's name**, and whether masters ever meet him in person.
- **"Mend, bind, and remember."** The Cruormancy epigraph names *three other* schools. Either change it to "mend and bind," or make **remembering** the Progenitor's own secret art: the school that makes his rebirth possible, and the one no lineage is taught.
- **The healing school's name.**
