## What They Want
- **Said:** repayment of the Ritter's debt, or the collateral.
- **Meant:** the collateral. The timber rights are worth far more over the years than the debt, and the house already has a buyer.

The house will accept full payment, partial payment with a credible schedule, or the timber rights. It will accept a face-saving settlement if its appraiser recommends one. He writes the report.

## How They Operate
The house is built in **links**: every man in it is a link, and a link that fails is replaced, not repaired.
- **Kettenmeester** — runs a counting-house and signs foreclosures.
- **Schätzer** (appraiser) — travels to the collateral, assesses it, files a report. Vethkar Graun's real title.
- **Glied** (link) — a junior who carries papers, watches doors, and signs nothing.
- **Hired muscle** — contracted, not enrolled. Vethkar travels with two; one is called Marek.

**The device:** a surveyor's chain coiled into a closed ring, sealed in dark green wax. A chain binds, and a surveyor's chain measures land. The Kettenhaus lends you the length of your own land and holds the other end.

## Origin
A Hanselmann family house of Traft that lends against land and title on the sealed debt-letter, the *Schuldbrief*. It thinks of itself not as a merchant company but as a registry of obligations. It operates under the patronage of the **Hussen clan**, and in 576 CY Franz Hussen was elected Voormann of Perrenland. In Traft, nobody crosses the Kettenhaus this year.

## History
- **573 CY.** Konrad borrows 700 gp on a *Schuldbrief* against the timber rights.
- **575 CY.** The house drafts the "dependency form" Traft's council would use to bring Ritterstadt under the canton. Foreclosure is cleaner under canton law.
- **576 CY.** With its patron clan in the Voormann's seat, the house consolidates. Vethkar Graun is sent north to decide whether the debt is recoverable or the rights should be taken.

## Goals
**Major:**
- Recover the debt, or take the timber rights and sell them to a Schwartzenbruin logging concern.
- Bring Ritterstadt under Traft, where the law suits a creditor.

**Minor:**
- Use the Hussen window while it lasts.
- Read the Voormann's post, including Dominie Roth's letters.

## Relationships
- **Useful to:** the Regents' Commission.
- **Rivals:** the Quag Bargemen, whose lake trade undercuts the Traft road.
- **Watched by:** the Pale Ledger.
- **Cautious of:** the Old Kerk Mission.
- **Unaware of:** what the timber rights sit on.

## Resolving the Debt
- **Pay it.** About 900 gp, or enough to satisfy the report. The house withdraws.
- **Lean on the appraiser.** Vethkar has things to hide (below). He would soften or bury the assessment in exchange for real information about the Wachturm.
- **Make the argument.** Show the house what the logging would destroy, through proper channels and with documents. It may restructure rather than risk liability for wrecking a site of historical importance.
- **Do nothing.** The report is filed. Foreclosure follows. Logging begins within a year.

## Hooks
- Traft's dependency form arrives with a clerk and a Needfest deadline.
- Vethkar Graun invites the party to breakfast and quotes their own words back to them.
- A Schwartzenbruin timber surveyor arrives early, with stakes and a crew.

> [!danger] DM Eyes Only
> - **The timber rights sit over Node 2** of the binding network. Logging would damage it beyond repair. The house has no idea. A lender recovers collateral, a logging concern buys it, men cut trees, and the network loses an anchor, with no malice anywhere in the chain.
> - **Vethkar Graun's second job.** He has been in the region long enough to see it is not ordinary frontier. He identified Ewald Wennerberg as a foreign agent within a fortnight and has not reported it. He has read Varek Doss's research notes and understands about forty percent of them, which is forty percent more than anyone else in the house. He runs a private information-gathering operation of his own: part curiosity, part ambition, part self-preservation. He is no one's agent but his own.
> - **The chest under the Ritter's dais** holds letters between Ritter Edmund and the Kettenhaus that could weaken Konrad's title.
> - **The Hussen window closes.** Franz Hussen's Voormannship ends in disgrace within the decade. Every foreclosure the house signs in these years can then be reopened by a rival.
