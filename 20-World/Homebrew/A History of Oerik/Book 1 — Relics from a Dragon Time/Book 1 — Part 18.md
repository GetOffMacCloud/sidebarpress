## Part 18

Now we focus on the civilizations of Eastern Oerik just over 10,000 years ago.

Most of the lands north of what is now the Nyr Dyv were wild lands. Wood elves lived in the Vesve Forest but shunned unnatural magic and were destined to become grugach elves (many sylvan elves live there today, but they are from a later migration).

Some stone-age humans roamed the barrens and the lands south of the Cold Marshes and up into the Burneal Forest. A few dwarven clans lived in the Griff and Corusk Mountains, although goblins, ogres, norkers, xvarts and kobolds were more prevalent, as were various noxious monsters.

Dwarves were plentiful in the Barrier Peaks, the Lortmils, the Iron Hills, the Glorioles and the Hestmark Highlands.

Great elven nations were located in the Adri and Grandwood forests, the Rieuwood, the Gamboge, Silverwood (which was much larger then and incorporated Hornwood, the Dim, Oytwood, Axewood and Dreadwood), the Bramblewood, and the Celadon.

All the lands including the Wild Coast, Celene, the Ulek states, the Pomarj, the Duchy of Urnst and what is now the Bright Desert were the lands of the grey elven Celenians.

East of Celene were the Blemu lands, incorporating the Adri and Grandwood forests (connected as one forest then), and much of what became the Great Kingdom in the 570s CY. The elves there were predominantly high elven.

The boundary between the two elven empires was loosely defined, and, in fact, many elves traveled freely over the whole of Eastern Oerik.

The elves, whether grey, high or wood, tended to be arrogant, hedonistic preeners, confident in their superiority over most other intelligent life. Dragons were a notable exception, which the elves did fear, but the great reptilian wyrms were still licking their wounds after the fall of their epoch, and were seldom seen.

The elven nations were linked to gates that connected Oerik with the elven empires of Kara-Tur (long gone), the elves of Kingsland and Adarus, Mystara, and other worlds including Toril and Krynn.
