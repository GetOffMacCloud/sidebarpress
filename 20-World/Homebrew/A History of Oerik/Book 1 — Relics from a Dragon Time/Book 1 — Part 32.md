## Part 32

How were the demons to be defeated? Travel into the Abyss and confront them in their lair? Suicidal. Besides, how would they get there? Heward shrugged: Win-Daled was pretty good at magic; surely she had a spell? Ye'Cind snorted with good humor: extra-planar travel was rare and the methods were mostly secret. Should Win-Daled ask a dragon for the formula?

The three Heroes ended their meal bickering vehemently.

Eventually, it was decided that Heward would travel to the Singing Lands and look for any weaknesses that could be exploited. What sort of weaknesses? Something that might cause a rift between the elves and the demons, or perhaps weaken the cohesion in their society. Heward was the best fit for the job because as an outsider, neither a Singing One like Ye'Cind nor a Lowly One like Win-Daled, Heward would more likely be able to mix with either class. Heward was a Flan, tanned with brown wavy hair and broad shoulders. His ancestors were a little mysterious; there were rumors one of his grandmothers was part nymph and one of his cousins had pointy ears like an elf. All in all, Heward made a perfect traveling merchant.

The Singing Lands had become openly debauched. The Singing Ones lived for pleasure, a dark pleasure that was destructive. Torture Clubs were commonplace, and noxious herbs were imbibed before the corrupt elves engaged in demonic sacrifices involving unwilling slaves. Overindulgence in food and drink was de rigueur, and the Singing Ones spent less and less time actually working.

Work became the burden of the Lowly Ones. Everything that needed to be done was left for these slaves. Supervision used to be strict, but as hedonism took over, much of the overseeing was done by the Lowly Ones themselves. Consequently, many tasks were left undone or badly done, and many Lowly Ones were able to hide away and form lucrative thieving networks.

The only jobs still enthusiastically managed by the Singing Ones were the secret police, but even they couldn't be everywhere. Sometimes they did uncover thief dens, but there were so many underground, hidden places for the Lowly Ones to gather, and with the majority of Singing Ones being apathetic, the Lowly Ones were actually developing a strong counterculture.

Heward moved through the run-down streets, admiring the architecture and the spontaneous singing that would erupt when more than three Singing Ones gathered together. He saw the occasional demon or daemon trudging along or bouncing about with flapping bat wings, but they ignored him, presumably because they gained their satiation in the Torture Clubs and temples.

Heward had managed to get the name of a local thief guild master. Hopefully he would be able to make contact.
