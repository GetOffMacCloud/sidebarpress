## Part 129

The most zealous cadre of kuo-toan faithful determined to find their Dear Blibdoolpoolp by descending down into the depths of the great Ocean.

To make such a journey, the kuo-toa performed a magical and holy ceremony to protect them from the sharp-toothed monsters, the long-armed monsters and the huge open-mouthed monsters.

The kuo-toa painted their bodies with invisible paint using invisible brushes.

They then tumbled down into the depths.

The invisible kuo-toa chanted silently silent songs as they descended.

The songs would keep them safe.

And they would be heard by Dear Blibdoolpoolp.

Long they fell, long they tumbled; the sharp-toothed monsters couldn't see them, the long-armed monsters couldn't find them, and the open-mouthed monsters couldn't swallow them.

Finally the holy kuo-toa reached the bottom of the ocean.

Once at the bottom they found the surroundings black as pitch.

The kuo-toa prayed very hard, then their eyes became wide and yellow.

They could see.

The kuo-toa wandered about in the depths of Oerth. The crushing weight of the water was no more. Above them were rainbow-colored octopuses gliding lazily through the ink, hunting beautiful bejeweled starfish. It was a dizzying yet wonderful sight and a proof of the loving closeness of Dear Blibdoolpoolp.

<center>* * *</center>

Whether these mystic acolytes actually did meet with their Goddess is entirely unknown, for they never returned to their community.

It is said their silent singing can still be heard by the wisest kuo-toa on the morning of their death.
