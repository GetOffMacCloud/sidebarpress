## Part 59

The swords pointed at Pope Syrul's heart looked very sharp. Syrul decided to play it cool.

"And what can I do for you?" Syrul asked nonchalantly.

Behind the hostile guards, a sleek young man with dark hair pushed forward.

"Dear Holy one, your rule is over," said the man in a kindly yet firm tone. "My name is Norebo, and I represent the true Suloise heir, Thurnfedge V. Your soldiers now answer only to me."

Thurnfedge? mused Syrul. A spindly, stupid runt now in charge of the Suel people? Hardly! Something else was going on.

"Why all this hostility?" asked Syrul. "I am on your side, as is the Goddess. I beg of you, please put those swords away."

"I regret that I cannot do that."

"The Goddess created Thurnfedge. She chose him."

One of the guards grew impatient and his sword touched her blouse. Syrul stood very still.

"So, I take it," said Norebo, "that you do indeed accept Thurnfedge V as the ruler of Risewater and the other Suloise realms?"

Pope Syrul explained that she, as the Pope, was merely an empty vessel filled by the grace of the Goddess. If the Goddess allowed Thurnfedge V to rule the entirety of the Suloise people, then she would naturally acquiesce.

Norebo smiled. "Not that I don't believe you," he said, "but some people might consider you saying something I might like to hear in order to avoid a beheading. May I have it in writing?"

Another guard moved his sword closer. Syrul was aware of a tiny trickle of blood running down her front. She closed her eyes for a moment, then said:

"I am completely sincere. May I ask for one small trouble? That Thurnfedge V, his court, including you, and all the subjects in Risewater at least, pray to the Goddess and Confirm the Appointment."

Norebo thought for a short time then waved the guards away. He bowed.

"As you wish, my lady."

<center>* * *</center>

Risewater was incorporated into the Suel kingdom. Pope Syrul, her priests, bishops and Cardinals were stripped of their wealth but remained in their positions as heads of the church. It did seem that Syrul had been cowed, and it might be reasonable to conclude that she felt humiliated, but if that is how she felt she didn't show it. Pope Syrul retained her calm dignity as she signed the papers in front of her ardent followers.
