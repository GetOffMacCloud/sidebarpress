## Part 10

I would like to say Tristram the wood elf was able to escape the ropes that bound him and then slew the hideous illithid. Alas...

According to the Illithid Researchers of Exquisite Cuisine, sheep and cow minds tasted like rubbery gruel and only rated 2 on the Sensation Scale, dwarves tasted like powdery biscuits and merited a 4, while elven brains were succulent like chocolate soufflé and champagne, rich and decadent, rating a solid 9, but gave the devourer dizzy spells and a queasy stomach afterward. Hallucinatory episodes were also not uncommon, usually involving flowery meadows and laughing tiny people. So, good for small gatherings but not great for regular consumption. Seeking food more nutritious, and still delicious, the Mind Flayer Researchers of Exquisite Cuisine gated from world to world.

11,000 years ago they found a system of planets filled with intelligent short-lived bipeds. They are today known as "humans." Thousands were collected over the following decades and the captives were divided by their general appearance, size, shape and hair color, then bred and crossbred and mixed and sorted and spliced and engineered, all with the goal of discovering new taste sensations by the Researchers. Many flavorsome delicacies were created in their laboratories and breeding pens, which brought fame and wealth to the Researchers and much relish and delicate enjoyment for the illithids. Roast beef, crème brûlée, venison, cognac and peacock; human minds were capable of such tasty sensations. The brains were combined and recombined to intensify the complexity and flavor notes.

Humans had another string to their bow: they bred rapidly and were efficient laborers, scribes and artisans. Clever and adaptable. What a useful commodity they were!
