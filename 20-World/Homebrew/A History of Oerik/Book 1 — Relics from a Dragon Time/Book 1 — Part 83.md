## Part 83

Warring in the Crystalmists during winter was futile, and every dwarf knew that.

Pious Grimm Quartztusk and his army were tantalizingly close to the capital Grawn and the palace of so-called King Silvervein. Only the wide pass lay between them. As it was impossible to attack in this season, King Silvervein had only posted a skeleton force at strategic points through the mountain pass. Bands of ogres, wargs and worse things would be an incidental help.

Sheltering through a blizzard, Pious had time to ponder. He was a mountain dwarf of a slightly shorter height than average, only a little over four feet, but he was unusually bright and very devout.

His qualities had seen him rise through the Hammers quickly, and he was still young. His short beard had but one streak of grey.

The blizzard raged on. Pious Grimm prayed to Moradin for guidance. The great god, the Soulforger, did not answer, but in the morning the winds had died and a pale sun brought uncommon warmth to the mountains.

<center>* * *</center>

The Loyalist guards of the pass were astonished to see marching ranks of rebel Hammers trekking relentlessly across the snow towards them. The fighting began and the Pious army quickly routed the unprepared guard force.

For several days the unseasonal warmth continued. The invaders made good progress, and before long neared the gates of Grawn.

Just as they began to brave the narrow road leading to the palace, the gates opened and a gigantic twenty-foot-tall giant pushed through them.

The giant's bulky legs supported the thick ring-mailed torso of the enormous dwur, who seemed to be unaccustomed to his size, and wobbled a bit after reaching the road.

The giant looked very much like an enormous Silvervein Moradinson, but how?

Then Pious realized. The giant Silvervein wielded a glowing axe, the Fierce Axe, the Axe of the Dwarven Lords, imbued with magic from the Soul Forge itself. It was the power of the Fierce Axe that had transformed the pretend king into a giant, who slowly lumbered towards him and his men.
