## Part 45

Elves had dwelt on the coastline and islands of Eastern Oerik for many thousands of years. Some elves were drawn to the sea, for they liked the physical contact with wind and salty water. Over time these coastal elves relied less upon the sterile travel of magical gates and explored the island chains of the Aerdi and Solnor seas first on rude rafts, then canoes, then boats with sails.

The first non-Draconic people to sail the open ocean, there was little danger on the surface, for there were no pirates, and the intelligent marine beings such as merfolk, ixixachitl and locathah had little interest in the funny things bobbing far above them.

The Spindrift Isles stretched out eastward, joining Oerik with the smaller continent Mystara across the Solnor. The island of Ierendi was a haven for sea-loving elves.

Traveling by boat was not enough for some. A young elf called Sashelas developed magic that allowed him to explore the underwater depths. Spending most of the time under the sea he was eventually nicknamed Deep Sashelas. A group of young elves followed him in his exploits and discovered ancient remnants of aboleth, kraken and dragon undersea realms.

There were great treasures of all kinds to be found. Some were well guarded by the few dragonkind and krakens still lurking in the murk, dreaming of past glories, but plenty more lay forgotten, ignored by the octopi, fish and jellyfish passing by.

Deep Sashelas and his minions grew very powerful from the mysteries uncovered in the depths and gained respect from the ancient ocean dwellers.

In time, they chose to remove themselves from the land permanently. Deep Sashelas presided over a complex magical and religious ritual that gave the elves gills which allowed them a fully marine life, bonded forevermore to the sea.

There was one marine species that resented the seagoing elves. Needing to spend part of their time on land as part of their life cycle, the fish people, the kuo-toa, were pushed aside and treated like beasts. Forced to abandon the lush and nutrient-rich islands to elven sailors, and barely finding space in hidden corners of the more barren islands, the kuo-toa declined rapidly in the Solnor and Adri seas.

The new and young marine elves also made life difficult for the kuo-toa. Arrogant with their newly acquired power, they taunted the unsophisticated fish people and disrupted their peace by chasing them through the kelp forests and coral reefs.
