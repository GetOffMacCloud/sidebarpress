## Part 19

Disoriented and lost, newly freed from the control of the mind flayers, the various humans ("tribes" would be the wrong word) wandered about, striving to understand this new land they had been dropped into.

It was natural that they gravitated towards the Celenian and Blemu elves. Many worshipped them as demigods and entered into willing servitude (slavery). Others were charmed into service.

Now, there were individual elves who were open-minded and kind-hearted. Liaisons were had with the humans, even full relationships and marriages. This infuriated the elf majority, who shunned any offspring from the unions and tried to enslave them with the rest.

The elves caught up in this situation had the choice of abandoning their new family, fighting back (where they were horrendously outnumbered) or leaving.

Many voluntarily exiled themselves to the island of Ierendi, which lay across the Solnor Sea off the southwest coast of Mystara.

Over subsequent centuries, the elves of the loose Blemu Confederation became more benign and helped to "civilize" the "simple ones." Thus, humans in the easternmost parts of Oerik learned metallurgy and agriculture. Some did serve the elves, but many more formed their own small semi-independent nations within Blemu and lived in relative harmony.

The elves around the Adri and Grandwood were the most helpful to humans on all of Oerik. The fertile lands of what would thousands of years later become the Great Kingdom allowed the human numbers to explode. These elvenkind actually urbanized alongside the Fharun humans, sharing their technology and helping to shape the Fharun understanding of the necessity of balancing nature alongside development.

At last, more elves formed close bonds with the humans, and some of the new half-elves fostered the new religion of druidism and became the first druids and rangers.
