## Part 33

Heward hated to admit it, but the Singing Ones certainly had a talent for singing. When in a group they would regularly burst into song. Their voices were sublime and unoerthly. The words were coarse and the sentiments dark, but the sound was intensely evocative and colorful.

Heward always had a yen for music. He could hold a tune and considered his voice rather melodious, but the Singing Ones were beyond comparison and he felt quite inferior now. Is this how the Lowly Ones felt? Surrounded by such effortless talent, was it natural for the Lowly Ones to accept servitude? Yet, that was not the case. Many had escaped their masters and lived under the streets or countryside, becoming thieves, bandits and mercenaries. The Singing Ones' love of absolute selfish pleasure was weakening their hold.

Heward reluctantly moved away from three silvery elves singing their hearts out, the coins in his belt pouch clanking. His contact proved hard to find, and the Lowly Ones seemed sympathetic but noncommittal. Likely he hadn't been circumspect enough, and the thief network didn't trust him.

Heward had been told of a hidden passage under the square nearby where elves were gathering to perform a choral work. He stopped when he observed a couple of Lowly Ones walking around the edge of the square and suddenly disappearing. Heward followed their steps and found a large stone at a strange angle. He pushed it and saw the entrance of a crude tunnel, which he stealthily entered. The tunnel quickly opened up into a square wooden room. It was empty, but Heward's excellent night vision made out the prints of many feet ending at the south wall. He went over to investigate.

Heward went rigid, for a clammy hand grasped his shoulder, and something cold and rigid moved up against his throat...
