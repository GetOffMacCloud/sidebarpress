## Part 73

The Magnate seethed.

He pulled his pallid, elephantine body up to his feet then staggered towards his Investors.

"We buried him upside down!" he bellowed. "Norebo was buried upside down! He was a traitor, why do they honor him?"

One Investor inadvisably said: "Sir, he made many people rich."

The Magnate heaved himself forward, and yelled into the Investor's face, who flinched from the expectorate.

"They stole from us," he raged, "they stole from me!"

<center>* * *</center>

There was a crackdown.

Norebo was labeled as a Suel spy[^1] who had deliberately bankrupted some of the most worthy benefactors of Thel Norusti.

Yet there were the new rich, those gamblers who had been blessed with Norebo's magic and had won every bet they had placed. They made their power known and deposed the poorest of the Conglomerate. They paid their own people to praise the late Norebo.

The Magnate goons clashed with the Norebo goons, and before long, Thel Norusti exploded into a brief civil war.

The Magnate and the Investors, soon out of funds, were defeated. The victors, the Norebo faction, appointed a new Magnate with new Investors. Assassins, thieves and mercenaries happily worked for their new masters.

The new Conglomerate faction then burned every copy of Norebo's magic poem they could find. They did not want a new wave of the newly rich to disrupt the order once again.

Across the mountains and elven lands of the west, the Suloise Oather society, relics of the original society before the rise of Thel Norusti, continued to revere Norebo. The Oathers in time formed a religion that would eventually raise Norebo to godhood.

[^1]: Even though he had clearly been a Flan.
