## Part 34

Heward felt a dagger blade caressing his neck. The other end of the dagger was held by a raggedy human in ill-fitting elvish garb.

"Not a good idea," said Heward and the thief was suddenly on the ground, the dagger out of her hand and a sword poised above her heart.

"You're a spy," snarled the thief.

"Yes, I suppose so," answered Heward mildly, but still pointing the sword, "but not for the Singing Ones. Now, how about we call a truce and you take me to your leader?"

<center>* * *</center>

It did not take long for Heward to smooth his way in with the Lowly One rogues. He was likeable and clever, and he was full of stories about the beautiful forests of Blemu, and of the much nicer elves that dwelled there.

Once Heward had established himself, he came up to the surface and posed as a Flan wine merchant. This excited interest in the Singing Ones and the elves lined up to sample his exotic wares. He hobnobbed with many an elven mark, getting them sozzled before he and the other thieves ransacked their houses.

The valuables were traded across Oerik and bought the Lowly One rebels plenty of armor and weapons. Heward, well established as a successful fighter, and now proven to also be an effective thief, helped to organize the resistance to become a real fighting force. Some outlaws were gated to the Blemu lands to learn magic from Win-Daled, Ye'Cind and a handful of Blemu elves who believed in liberty for all and condemned slavery.

This was all well and good, but Heward knew that the Singing Ones would soon come down hard. Weapons were good, but Heward wanted something more, some sort of powerful magic that would thwart the secret police and their terrifying leader, Dweomyne, the Whip Lady.
