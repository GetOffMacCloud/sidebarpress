## Part 4

### Dwur! — −6,000 SD

Dwarves were stupid!

Why had the dragons ever bothered creating them? The dwarves had been enchanted slaves of the elves for several thousand years now, and the elven magic, wrapped in the Fey Mysteries, was too strong for the dwarves' natural resistance to overcome.

Delphine used her dwarves to dig into the dirt under the mountains to find bright and beautiful things for her. Is that what the dwarves were for? Did the dragons create them to be thralls?

She wasn't the only one using the dwarves in this way. Really, there wasn't much else that dwarves were good for. Their cooking was atrocious, they were slow and clumsy, physically and mentally, their art was obvious and very simple, so no point employing them for that, and they were boring company. And it was pathetic that Olve magic enslaved them so easily. Dwarves had no dignity either.

Yet, there were a few dwarves who resisted, a tiny number. In fact, only a few hundred dwarves in each generation managed to break the charms.

Why? Not because they grew a spine, or because their esteem demanded freedom. Darlene scoffed. No!

It was base avarice. These few dwarves fell under the spell of gold fever instead of Olve. She heard stories that these degenerates tunneled deep into the Underdark, far away from elvish eyes.

Sages spoke of the deep dwarves forming sinister cults that worshipped the slithering creatures of the darkness. Elder Things, not Dragon, cast-offs from the old empires.

Darlene shuddered. Thank the Dragons that she was Olve!

<center>* * *</center>

Moradin felt alone.

He was born differently, a dwur that could think for himself. He had never been affected by elvish magic, nor was he struck with gold fever. Alone amongst the dwur, he grew up independent and angry, unable to relate to the charmed dwarves all about him, hiding from the elves and stealing from them as he needed to survive. He was achingly lonely. Why had the Dragons made him? Why give him a fierce intelligence and a steely will if all he could be was a vagabond, a rutterkin?

He should be more.

He should be a leader for his people.

He saw an aura about them, and he felt their pain.

The dwur were treated as shells, bodies with no brains.

Moradin knew that their minds were clever. Their will was staunch.

They couldn't help the magic that suppressed it. Moradin would find a way to break it.

He would be more.

The dwur would be free.

And their overlords would pay!
