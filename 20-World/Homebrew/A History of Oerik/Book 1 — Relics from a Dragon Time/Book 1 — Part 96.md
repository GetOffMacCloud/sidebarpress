## Part 96

The druids were naive in their collaboration with the Ur-Flan.

The druids sought to understand the true essence of Existence in order to resonate with and within Nature.

The Ur-Flan sought power over Life itself.

The Ur-Flan corrupted the spells and rituals that the druids used to transform their bodies into wild animals. The druids wandered under the Moons, Luna and Celene, as wolves, bears, tigers, boars, ravens... but upon the rise of Sol, and as light bathed yellow upon a new day, the druids, scattered across the Northlands, awoke alone, with little memory of their night crawls.

They would find themselves in a cave, or lying in a field, or concealed within shrubs, bodies bruised and aching, often covered in blood.

The druids assumed that they were hunting other animals, part of the Circle of Life.

But mostly, they were hunting people.

The druids didn't want to believe this; they tried to discount or ignore evidence that their ravages were upon villagers or farmers, not deer or sheep. Sometimes they awoke holding an odd shoe or torn clothes that were not theirs, and sometimes they half-remembered seeing men, women and children, sometimes people they knew, running from them, wide-eyed and full of fear.

The Ur-Flan used enchanted crystals to watch the druids wander the night in animal forms.

Werebeasts, they called them. Animal Men, Lycanthropes.

And they cast dark spells upon the druids in secret that caused their corruption to spread, to turn innocent survivors of attacks into werebeasts as well.

The Ur-Flan watched and took notes.

The druids wished to be of One with Oerth, but they became outcasts, corrupted by bloodlust, killing for pleasure, not sustenance.

The wiser druids, those that did not trust the shadowy Ur-Flan, those that knew that the wizards and assassins were lovers of Demons and Devils, the Unnatural, the Uncanny; these druids shunned the lycanthropes and saw them for what they really were.

Instruments against Nature itself.

The werebeasts, no longer druids, found each other and accepted their fate. Many embraced the wildness and learned to control their transformations.

The weretigers loved the thrill of the hunt.

The werewolves lusted for human blood.

The wereboars simply lusted.

Only the werebears kept some semblance of their former personalities, but became terrifying and chaotic if angered.

After around three years, those infected with lycanthropy no longer changed involuntarily with the full moon, and their dual nature became one, that of the beast.

The lycanthropes found that they could mate with the same type of werebeast, and that their offspring would be truly wild, filled with animality, not humanity.

By 520 SD the Curse of Lycanthropy was forevermore a part of Oerth.
