## Part 125

### 635–636 SD

Oeridia was a large island located seventy miles off the coast of Oerik, several hundred miles south of the Singing Lands. Although within the tropics, Oeridia rose high above the sea forming several plateaus that were significantly cooler than the jungles below. At the bottom were a number of sizable lagoons.

The Oeridians were a Mesolithic hunter-gatherer culture that lived primarily on the plateau, but regularly hunted in the lowlands. They shared the heights with centaur tribes, and paleolithic norkers, orcs, ogres, troglodytes and gnolls lived in caves and caverns that dotted the hillsides.

Living in the lagoons and the seashore were the fish people, the kuo-toa. Their young, called fingerlings, lived in the most placid waters away from predators. Peace had long reigned between the Oeridians and the kuo-toa. The Oeridians traded meat, nuts, fruit and shiny stones and the kuo-toa traded back with fish, lobster, oysters, abalone and pearls. Apart from an occasional encounter with wild animals and monsters, life was good.

When the Thytic mariners discovered Oeridia, they claimed to be peaceful and gave away many beautiful presents to the excited kuo-toa, but in 635 SD huge numbers of men came ashore and began cutting down the trees, laying roads and despoiling the lagoons.

The Thytics had decided that this isle would be a good staging ground to launch a more prominent presence in the seas between Oerik, Mystara and Hepmonaland.

The kuo-toa suffered the most. With their bulging, rolling eyes, mouths often agape, and with a comical, waddling walk[^1] and gurgling, burbling way of speaking, the Thytics saw them as primitive jokes. They called the kuo-toa "gogglers" and the laborers taunted and abused them in their spare time. Worse, the shipyard was chosen to be built on one of the sacred spawning grounds. Whole potential generations were wiped out.

Within a year the Oeridians were not spared either. The Thytics raided the plateaus and enslaved thousands for their workforce. Stone age technology was no match against composite bows, long swords and magic.

[^1]: To the Thytic point of view.
