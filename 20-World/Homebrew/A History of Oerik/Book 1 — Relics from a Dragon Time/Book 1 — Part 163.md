## Part 163

Incabulos felt the fire rush down his arm.

He dropped the scroll and it fell onto the bare ground as he clasped his charred hand.

Nerull looked down at the black scroll and somehow he had the feeling that it was gazing back. A tingling burst of energy crackled out from it, but Nerull barely flinched. Impulsively he bent down and snatched it up.

The scroll felt curiously cold. There was no pain.

Incabulos stared at Nerull, clutching his throbbing arm as Nerull tucked the scroll into his skin bag. Nerull saw his friend looking at him, so he grinned and shrugged. It wasn't his fault that the scroll wanted him and not Incabulos.

They stepped out of the vault and were back in the main body of the untidy cave.

<center>* * *</center>

The Scroll of Vile Darkness was pleased with its choice. It had sensed him before, this young man who seemed to possess a brutality, a raw rage waiting to be unleashed. His companion had something of power within too, but the Scroll wasn't interested in him; he was too weak, too unaware of his own darkness.

No, it was Nerull that made the scroll shudder with anticipation. Here was someone who was going to shake things up! The Scroll of Vile Darkness had wanted Nerull to possess it, for it was done with the Queen of Chaos and her ghastly spider children. They'd neglected the Scroll for a while now, and it hadn't been difficult to have the Queen forget to take it to Pesh.

Too bad for them; now someone else could peruse the Secrets.

<center>* * *</center>

Nerull and Incabulos returned to their clan. An elder saw that Incabulos was burned and his hand and arm were treated with a salve, though the pain did not leave the boy.

How had Incabulos come by the injury?

Incabulos said that he had found a red dragon hatchling, and had nearly caught it, when it wriggled free and burst out fiery breath. They had then run away before it alerted its mother.

Incabulos was scolded for being stupid and interfering with dragons. He was lucky not to be dead.

Nerull said that that was what he had told his friend, and he would never be silly enough to bother dragons, no fear!

Incabulos was annoyed that Nerull let him get all of the blame, but Nerull was unrepentant. What did he want from him? At least the elders didn't know the real truth, that they'd been in the Cave of the Gods. Imagine the scolding Incabulos would have got then!

Incabulos could only nod. He grimaced with the continuous pain of his arm, then turned away, leaving Nerull alone with the Scroll.
