## Part 7

> *Mushrooms begin in the dark, but they do quest the light.*
>
> — Dwulf the Prophet, *The Book of the Sombre*, Chapter 17, verse 11

The goblinoids harried the surface folk for decades. Elves, who had little use for combat in general life, now had plenty of time to hone their skills with bow and sword. As well as goblins, kobolds, norkers and ogres, hostile dwarves emerged from the mountains, and they were fearsome indeed with their magical arms. Moradin led his people with wisdom but strength also. After about sixty years of fighting, the goblinoids were scattered but not eradicated. Their fast growth rate meant a couple of dozen hidden goblins could soon become a thousand. The dwarves were not so easily moved.

Corellon Larethian was a young elf warrior, lithe, strong, clever and magically gifted, who had become the best archer of all the elves. When the dwarven armies of Moradin, attired in ring mail and heavily armed, marched through the high countries into the forests of Celadon, Corellon and his company were waiting. The elves felled many dwarves with arrows fired within the trees. It was anger that had mostly motivated Moradin: he sought revenge on his elvish enemies, but now, in the forest that made him feel claustrophobic and disoriented, he saw little point in pushing through. The dwarves had captured chains of hills and mountains, and that was the land they most wanted. The dwur had gained their liberty; they had lifted themselves out of the dirt and into the daylight. The sun was sharp with arrows and it was time to seek shade.

No accurate conversation has been recorded between Moradin the Soul Forger and Corellon Larethian. Elvish and dwarven tales tell different stories, but we know a truce was reached. The dwarves were freed from their enchanted thraldom, and in return there was an acceptance that revenge upon the elves was not to be sought. But for their mutual benefit, and in the face of the common goblinoid enemy that was forever gathering power in the shadows, the two peoples agreed to trade with each other and to respect each other's territories. It didn't mean they had to like each other, but Corellon was a fair and honest leader and so was Moradin.

To this day large conflicts between dwarves and elves are almost unknown, partly in honor of a legendary agreement and partly because both always seem to have much more implacable foes to deal with...
