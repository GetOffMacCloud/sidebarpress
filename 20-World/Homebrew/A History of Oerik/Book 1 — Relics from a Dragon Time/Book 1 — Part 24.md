## Part 24

In the year −53 SD a baby girl was born to a Lowly One family in the servitude of a moderately well-off household in the Singing Lands.

Their masters, kinder than most elves, and rather sympathetic to their slaves, treated the Lowly Ones as compassionately as they felt able. The baby's father could actually read and write and performed the task of secretary.

The girl was called Win-Daled and was exceptionally smart. This would ordinarily be a problem as the Singing Ones regularly sent the more clever Lowly Ones to the torture parks, but instead Win-Daled's natural intelligence was encouraged, which was quite brave of the elven family, who secretly supported emancipation.

When she was four, Win-Daled got to play with the young Ye'Cind, a fifty-year-old elfling with a mischievous streak who was the youngest member of the elven family.

Ye'Cind was as musically gifted as a typical Singing One, but her actual voice was horrible. She had perfect pitch, but the harsh honking sound that came out of her throat was repugnant. Ye'Cind loved to make animal calls and she hooted with laughter whenever she managed to scare Win-Daled with her griffin cries. Fortunately, most of the time the two of them got on extremely well, and would chat and laugh with each other frequently.

One day, Ye'Cind's father managed to form a complex trading scheme that included the Crystalmist dwarves, the Wind Dukes to the northwest, and the Blemu Confederation. He would need to gate to various places to supervise and oversee the trading to make sure everything was in order.

Ye'Cind's mother was a politician who was often working at the Senate or attending concerts or parties, always attempting to swing other Singing Ones to her point of view, which involved more rights for the Lowly Ones as well as reforming to a more centralized government.

There was never enough time for her or her husband to manage the welfare and education of Ye'Cind as well as they would want, and they both feared she would turn out half-wild, so it was agreed that her husband would take Ye'Cind with him on his trading meetings, and his secretary would be allowed to take Win-Daled along too, as Ye'Cind and Win-Daled were almost inseparable.
