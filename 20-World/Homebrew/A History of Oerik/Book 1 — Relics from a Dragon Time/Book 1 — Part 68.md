## Part 68

Scores of the Suel lay scattered, dead on the ground.

Wind Duke Sephar, tall and dignified, strode about the fallen bodies, shaking his head with grim sadness.

As Sephar had expected, the Suel humans had found the Vaati armor impenetrable, the weapons lethal.

The Vaati had let the remainder go, and they ran out of sight.

Still, a lot had died.

"Let this be a lesson for you all," boomed Sephar into the empty air. "There are ways and lore that must be acknowledged. And followed."

Then he stopped. Something else was here apart from Wind Dukes...

Sephar raised his rod.

"Is a human here still? Will you parley with me? Will you fulfill the requirements demanded of you?"

Norebo had gone to ground as soon as he had seen the advancing Wind Dukes. The fighting had been brutal and Norebo had chosen to stay hidden. He should have guessed that the Wind Dukes would smell him out.

Norebo stepped out of the shadows and presented himself.

Sephar soon lost patience. The human with dark hair was speaking too fast to be properly understood, using long sentences that went nowhere, about all kinds of things that seemed irrelevant. Was he trying to deceive the Vaati?

"You tire me," snapped Sephar. "A taste of the Rod will bring you to sense."

Norebo had always been lucky, good Fortune a natural part of his makeup. Attractive, smart, quick and devious, Norebo had helped to found the Oather Society. Of course there had been a few setbacks, but Norebo had always risen to the top like fine wheat.

But he watched, helpless, as Sephar pointed his glowing rod right at him...

A high-pitched wail made Sephar pause. He saw a pink light streak down and explode into brilliance. Ah, he recognized what was happening. Before him was the Suel Goddess called Syrul.

Syrul was plain for a goddess but her eyes were black as pitch. In her hand was a long, corded whip.

Sephar shuddered; he felt the chaotic disturbance of the magic whip like a backhanded slap. That whip contained Madness. The Vaati behind Sephar looked at him for guidance, and he raised his hand at them to wait.

Syrul confidently strode towards the dark, malachite-skinned, hairless beings before her. These Wind Dukes, the Vaati, what could they do against her? She was divine, and they were only puffed-up elementals.

The energy armor around the Wind Dukes flickered and waned as she moved closer. Her very presence made the Vaati retch.

The strongest Wind Duke stepped forward to protect Sephar. Syrul swung her whip, caught the Vaati by the ankle and brought him down. Her eyes pierced him with Darkness, blasting him to ashes.

Fighting nausea, Sephar pointed his rod at the goddess. A lemon-colored bolt caught her in the chest and she gasped. The pain made her writhe but she did not fall.

Syrul was surprised. The rod of the Vaati held a painful bite. Her whip swiped forward and caught around the tip of the rod. Sparks flew everywhere...

...and the Whip left Syrul's hand and wrapped tightly around the Rod, causing it to distort and phase in and out of reality. The Chaos of the Whip and the Law of the Rod were in utter conflict.

Syrul had been wrenched back by the shockwave, but Sephar held on tightly to the Rod, though cramps ran up his arm.

Then Norebo launched out from nowhere and seized the Rod, but couldn't pry it from the Wind Duke.

Sephar had had enough. With a burst of power, he, and the other Vaati, the Whip and the Rod dematerialized and returned to their home.

Norebo was left in the dirt, but glad to be alive. He looked for Syrul, but all that he saw of her was a pink streak in the clouds above.
