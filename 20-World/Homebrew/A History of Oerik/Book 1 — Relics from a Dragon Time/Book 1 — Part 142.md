## Part 142

Lord Dueillig took the Tuatha de Dannan and brought her to the Celenian Court.

Treated with the respect due a Tuatha de Dannan, she was quickly returned to her sisters in the Good Hills, the fortifications they were building growing ever more extensive.

The Celenian elves brought in their own magi to help build a sprawling barrier called Thornspikes, magically created thickets of shrubbery, burned to leave an iron-hard thorny and spiky skeleton that would make climbing through it painful and slow.

While the elves and Tuatha de Dannan worked, patrols of hippogriff cavalry circled in the air, keeping the native gnomes and halflings away.

The gnomes and halflings did not like being pushed aside and demanded information from the Celenians. From what Abyssal plane had the Tuatha de Dannan come? Why were the Celenians helping them?

Lord Dueillig was the elf in charge of the region, and he was responsible for keeping the peace, so he thought it prudent to give them something. He explained that the Tuatha de Dannan were demi-human people from a continent far to the east past the Solnor Sea and Mystara called C'mu Ne'er.

The Tuatha de Dannan were ancestors of today's elves, and were the first beings on Oerth to access the Fey Lands, the dimensions of the Faerie folk.

Why were they here then, taking over land that did not belong to them?

Lord Dueillig smiled. Believe it or not, the Tuatha de Dannan were not here to conquer but to protect.

Protect? From what?

A shriek pierced their ears, then a hippogriff and its rider tumbled and spiraled out of the air, landing heavily on the Thornspikes a few hundred yards away. Elves and Tuatha de Dannan rushed to the scene.

"Look up," cried Lord Dueillig.

High in the sky were great gathering clouds, black and dense, and streaking out from them were large tumbling stones. The stones crashed upon the hill fortifications, smashing into the Thornspikes.

The source of the hurling stones was soon made clear, as atop the clouds, Dueillig could see the moving forms of many giants...
