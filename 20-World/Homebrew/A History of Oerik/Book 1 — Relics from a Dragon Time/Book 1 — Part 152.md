## Part 152

Heward took the mirror off the wall then staggered out into the garden. Lathia followed him, bringing the wine. A midnight picnic would be fun, but what was the mirror for?

Heward balanced the mirror on a hedge then gently took a cup of wine from Lathia. He then began to sing again. Lathia was becoming more annoyed. The way things were going, when Heward woke up in the morning he was unlikely to remember anything.

Now Heward was gurning at his reflection in the mirror. Oh Titania!

Lathia glanced at Heward's mirror image.

It wasn't Heward. Who was this stranger?

Heward spoke to the man in the mirror.

"Hi, I'm Heward, and I think I'm hammered."

<center>* * *</center>

Phizzat wanted to know why his crystal was showing him this lunatic. He was supposed to be investigating the invasion of the giants.

"Why aren't you me?" asked Heward to the image of Phizzat in the mirror.

"What?"

"Don't mirrors usually reflect an image of oneself? You look a little younger, and perhaps a bit more rough around the edges. Do you want to join Lathia and me?"

"What?"

"I mean, I can ask her; she's right here. Lathia, do you like the image of this gentleman?"

Phizzat heard a great crash echo out from somewhere behind Heward and Lathia. Heward turned to Lathia, confused.

<center>* * *</center>

Kavoss and his fire giants had found Freeberger Heward's house and had smashed their way in. Where was the miserable human? Where was the ring?

<center>* * *</center>

Lathia realized that their house was being attacked. The horrids must have discovered the ruse.

What bounded into the garden was no Unseelie. Instead, an enormous animal built like a wildebeest but sporting an ugly red-rimmed badger head.

It was Hucephelus, and the terrible monster charged straight at the nymph.

<center>* * *</center>

Phizzat watched Lathia and Heward run about the garden, narrowly fending off the beast's jaws.

"The mirror!" cried Heward, and then, impossibly, Heward dived into it.

Lathia was just behind Heward, but the beast had snatched her by her long hair, and she was thrown heavily to the ground.

"Good!" grated a voice. A great, orange-skinned cyclops emerged into the garden followed by several tall, red fire giants.

"Don't eat her," said the cyclops. "She will tell us the whereabouts of the Ring of Gaxx."

The monster whined but let Lathia go.

And before anyone could react she had leapt into the mirror too!

<center>* * *</center>

The world seemed to be spinning lights. Flashes of yellow and white and green and blue spun up and down and sideways, saturating Lathia's eyes with color.

Then she saw Heward, thirty feet away and oscillating.

She called out to him, then she saw her companion turn around, grinning.

"Good," said Heward, "I was getting worried."

"It was a close thing," answered Lathia, swimming through the lights towards Heward. "Now can you explain how we are inside a mirror?"

Heward grinned even more widely.

"Someone was spying on us, using a crystal ball probably, and..." he held up the ring, "the Ring of Gaxx opened up a conduit."

Lathia nodded. "I suppose that makes some kind of sense, Heward, but why are we still stuck inside the mirror?"

"We're not stuck; I was waiting for you."

<center>* * *</center>

Phizzat jumped up when he saw two coils of mist emanate out from the crystal, and he ran to the door when the mists solidified into Lathia and Heward.

"Don't worry," called Heward, "we don't bite."

Phizzat hesitated, then bravely faced the interlopers.

"You want to know something, my friend?" slurred Heward. "I usually have a dim opinion of spies, but in this case I think you saved our lives."

<center>* * *</center>

Phizzat poured Heward and Lathia a sobering brew, and as he drank the bitter concoction, Heward looked around, sniffing the air.

"We are in Oerik?" he asked.

"Yes," answered Phizzat. "Eastern Oerik, the Flanaess, in the Sheldomar Valley."

That made sense. The Ur-Flan's dialect was a little strange, but it had been about seven hundred years since Heward was last here after all.

Now Phizzat had a question.

"Why were those monsters after you?"

Heward laughed. It was only because he stole a magic ring. But there was no danger now, because without the ring, the gruesome cyclops couldn't cross into the crystal.

"Unless the conduit is still open," murmured Lathia.

Heward and Phizzat turned to the crystal.

It was smoking, and a curling mist was rising up and...

Heward dashed the crystal ball to the floor, shattering it.

<center>* * *</center>

The sun was hot on the back of Kavoss. The cyclops stood up upon Hucephelus and looked around. Lots of fat, bulbous ferns, and sand. Lots of sand.

Where was the thief? The party moved east, where a light breeze seemed to be blowing. After several hours, Kavoss and his giants saw, below the edge of low cliffs, the sea.

Where were they? What had gone wrong?

<center>* * *</center>

Tonopo had never seen such strange beings. She ran hard upon her stumpy legs to the Great Encampment. All the elders were there, and they needed to know.

Panopo waved his trunk threateningly at Tonopo. This was Grown Up business. Tonopo should be with the other children. Why had she gone to the cliffs again? Why was she always bored?

Tonopo cried in frustration, her little trunk twitching with each sob.

A hulking figure emerged from the back of the tent. It was the Elder Mother, come from the lake. Tonopo knew that she was in for it now.

But the Elder Mother recognized a kindred spirit inside little Tonopo. When she had been a small loxodon, she too was often bored. Of course, Tonopo had probably seen only a couple of ogres; they often roamed this time of year.

Let's scare off the ogres, then we can return to our business.

<center>* * *</center>

Kavoss was finding the breaking waves irritating, because the constant rush of water crashing onto the beach bothered his superior hearing. Eventually he fell into an uneasy slumber.

Hucephelus couldn't sleep well either, for the scents that reached his snout were strange and disturbing. Not food, something else... But he was very tired, and in time he slept standing up.

<center>* * *</center>

The fire giants, the cyclops and the leucrotta were snoring loudly upon the sand of eastern Hepmonaland when the loxoth band, bristling with spears, trudged down to the beach. The elephant people were shocked to see that Tonopo was right after all.
