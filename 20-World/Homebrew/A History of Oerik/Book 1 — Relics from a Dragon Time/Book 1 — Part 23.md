## Part 23

Over the next few thousand years humans expanded across the lands. Northern Hepmonaland, free from most elvenkind (who generally preferred cooler climes), became a unified human nation called Mai. Using gates, the Hepmonalanders traded, fought and eventually joined with the Kara-Turan peoples, forming a huge trading empire called MaiYuan.

The elf nations were concerned but not frightened by the emergence of a human empire that stretched across the tropics of two continents. Their magic was more advanced, their knowledge orders of magnitude greater, and Celene, Blemu and the Singing Lands had plenty of human soldiers fighting for them, either as freemen or as fanatically brainwashed berserkers.

The MaiYuan were not at all warlike in any event. They had stable lands because they were peace-loving and loved the good things in life. Trade was their passion, and it was these trading alliances that eventually cemented the empire. The empire itself was unusual in that a committee was the ultimate arbiter of the law, but most districts were semi-autonomous.

The exotic materials and wealth spread to the elvish lands too, allowing them to build enormous cities within their wild homes.

Celene built vast structures magically held together by bark, silk and spider web. They would be placed high in the trees with walkways and waterfalls. There were dance halls, fairs, concerts, plays and temples.

Other elvish lands would build less impressive, but still beautiful, dwellings made of flowers, vines and timber. Whatever the city, greenery and gardens were everywhere.

At least that was true for Eastern Oerik and Mystara (where Alfheim was founded). The Kara-Turan elves liked to live in mud-brick houses encrusted with jewels and murals, with large flat spaces made of smooth stone. Gardens were usually built in squares surrounded by more stone.

The Singing Ones of Western Oerik had similar architecture to the Kara-Turan elves, but adorned the squares with huge sculptures also, of dancing and cavorting elves and paintings of ki-rin and gold dragons, and they would often perform grand choral works that would rise to the heavens.
