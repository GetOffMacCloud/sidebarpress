## Part 64

Ye'Cind played her recorder, the sonics blasting out like thunder. A pinkish cloud emanated out and gathered around the Pope and her Cardinals, taking the shape and form of a leering face.

The hovering, misty face resembled that of Pope Syrul, and it seemed to bore into the eyes of the worshippers with contempt.

Syrul gasped: what magic was this? The worshippers moaned with awe and fear. The Cardinals looked at the leering face with astonishment.

Then the face spun around and around incredibly fast, and Pope Syrul felt herself grabbed and pulled up into the air.

With a cry, Syrul rose up into the spinning face that enveloped her with a ruddy glow.

A Cardinal desperately reached for her as she rose but he pulled back in pain, his arm afire. Panicked, he ran and knocked down the other Cardinals who found their robes igniting into flame.

Ye'Cind blew out another sonic boom that stirred the cruel giant face. The sound waves forced it to open its vast mouth and swallow the Pope whole.

The cloud re-coalesced and revolved, then dispersed. Another sonic boom and there was no more cloud, just the enormous form of the Pope hovering above the stone formation, four times her true size, floating in the air, looking down upon her people with consternation.

Ye'Cind threw off her cloak and revealed herself to the crowd.

"There's your Goddess," shouted Ye'Cind, brandishing her flute. "I used sound to reveal her true guise! All your prayers, all your rituals, they became divine energy and went into her! You fools! You have made the Pope a god!"

Syrul looked down at the mass of chaos beneath her. Some people were praying, some were laughing. Some were sobbing and some were fleeing. Her power had been growing enormously but the meddling Singing One called Ye'Cind had revealed her secret too soon.

Syrul was indeed the Goddess, but the power she had drawn from her unthinking worshippers had not reached its full potential. Oh well, we will see what will be.

Looking around at her Cardinals, she saw that they were not yet looking at her, too intent on putting the fires out.

She turned back to the sea of shocked faces of her congregation. Some would still follow her, but others would surely turn away, as they were now doing.

And what to do about the slow-witted Thurnfedge and his cunning adviser, Norebo? Thurnfedge was actually weeping; she could see tears glistening in his weak eyes. Norebo was trying to get the monarch to stand up and raise his whip of state, the symbol of his legitimate right of rule.

What to do about that? thought the newborn god and pope, and... hang on, what was Ye'Cind doing?
