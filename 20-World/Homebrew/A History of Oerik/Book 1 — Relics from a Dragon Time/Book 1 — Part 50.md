## Part 50

The hut sat low between two jagged hills as if it were hiding from something. Its wooden frame was dull brown and grey, blending in with the wild bushland. If a hut could look unhappy, then this one did. The lone window peeking out on one side seemed to be frowning somehow, and within one frame of mud-smeared glass was a bright angry glow of red.

A large, black-shawled figure emerged out of the shadows and waddled to the front door. She wrenched at it, but the door resolutely stayed shut. The figure paused for a moment then thumped the door with a heavy fist. The door creaked but stayed shut.

The figure turned away and from beneath her shawl, a warty grey nose poked out, illuminated by the moon. She reached into a deep pouch and pulled out some wood and a tinderbox. Then, wincing, she bent down low and set fire to the bottom of the hut.

The hut shot upwards, four wiry yellow chicken legs sprouting out from beneath it. The legs danced upon the flames, attempting to stamp it out.

"You do make things difficult for yourself," snapped Baba Yaga. "Open up next time."

Baba Yaga blew on the ground and the fire vanished into smoke. The ogress pushed the hut door open and pulled her huge frame inside. At her favorite hearth she put a cauldron of water on the iron and sat down into a gnarled ancient chair.

Once the tea was brewed, the witch went to a square table and pulled her chair up to it. Drinking the sweet tea with large sips, she considered her options. Was this time the best place to bring up the girl? Baba Yaga had her doubts, and her hut did too. Uncharacteristically, it had been more anxious than usual, and that anxiety had turned to resentment. Baba Yaga wasn't too concerned; she knew the hut loved her, deep down. They had seen so much together; their bond would repair in time.

There was something dark and strange about this time, as if the gods were new and confused, as if a great storm had roared over the world, and now, bathed in sunlight, the survivors didn't quite recognize the wreckage that was left.

Baba Yaga and her hut knew all about dark and strange, for they were from the time of Vecna, where that supremely powerful lich king held the Flanaess in his frozen grip. That was why she was looking for somewhere to hide the girl. If the Undead Lord found out her secret...

Several cups later, Baba Yaga pulled back her chair and stretched up to her nine-foot height. This time would not be suitable for the girl. The ogress could taste the magic in the air. There was too much unpredictability here. A mortal could stumble into a hole and emerge a god. And, the Dragons were listening...

"Dear hut on chicken legs," cooed the giant witch, "we will look for a better place."

And down three corridors, four flights of stairs, within a small bedroom, a very little girl slept in a tiny crib, gently rocked by the hut itself.
