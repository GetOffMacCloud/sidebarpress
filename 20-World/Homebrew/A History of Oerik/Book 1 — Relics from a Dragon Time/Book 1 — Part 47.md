## Part 47

### 124 SD

The demons had outstayed their welcome.

After much chaos and pain, suffering and terror, the people of the MaiYuan empire rose up under the leadership of noble heroes willing to sacrifice their lives in ridding the evil from their lands.

The war was short and sharp. Great clerics of good summoned devas and solars, angelic creatures of the Upper Planes, to help rid Oerth of the demonic scourge.

The demons found themselves severely challenged, and their innate cowardice led them to flee back into the festering pits of the Abyss. Still, after the return of MaiYuan to their mercantile ways, some evil humans did skulk into dark, hidden places, and with their lust for domination, built hideous temples to their demonic gods.

The demons had failed to take permanent hold of the Singing Lands too. After the apparent demise of the Whip Lady, the elven Singing Ones lost interest in demonology. There was still a bit of demon worshipping about, but as long as it was only dabbling and not too committed, those individuals were tolerated as mere cranks.

Nonetheless, the Singing Lands continued to be filled with beautiful song and cold hearts.

Another important development was the migration to Oerth of gnomes and halflings. Mysteriously bound to elves in prehistory in the Dragon Times, they had separated from the Olves of Oerth though they had mixed with them on other worlds.

Their appearance in 124 SD was a result of beholder invasions upon one of their idyllic worlds. Forced to flee their homes, a powerful gnome illusionist found a gateway to Oerth.

On Mystara, the continent in the Solnor Sea east of Oerik, the gnomes and halflings settled mainly in the south. The halflings were greeted warmly by the Ierendi elves and were gifted fertile meadows that became the Five Shires.

The gnomes moved further afield occupying parts of the south and west.

On Oerik, the gnomes and halflings settled in the east, in the lands of the Blemu elves, young Sunndi, and the Flinty Hills, but the Sheldomar Valley became their major home. Despite the arrogance of the Celenian elves, the new arrivals were considered distant kin and tolerated, unlike the despised Flan.
