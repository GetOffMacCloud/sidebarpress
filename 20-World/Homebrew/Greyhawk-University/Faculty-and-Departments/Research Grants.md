# Research Grants

The University does not simply post quests and wait. For students with the academic standing and initiative to pursue it, the **Research Grant** system provides formal expedition funding in exchange for specific, documented deliverables. Grants are an investment — the University (or an external patron sponsoring through the University) fronts resources upfront, in exchange for results it needs.

Grants are also the primary path to the University's most prestigious academic recognition: the **Faculty Research Fellowship**.

---

## How Grants Work

### 1. The Grant Listing
Active grants are posted in the **Department Registry** — separate from the general Job Board. Grant listings include:
- The sponsoring department or patron
- The specific deliverable required
- The grant tier (and its stipend value)
- Any eligibility requirements
- The deadline for completion (if applicable)

Unlike standard quests, grants are **not first-come-first-served**. They require an application.

---

### 2. The Application
To apply for a grant, a student (or party) submits a brief **Grant Proposal** to the sponsoring department. This is an in-character document — it need not be long, but it should address:
- **Who is applying** and their relevant qualifications (Major, published work, prior experience)
- **How they intend to pursue the deliverable** (approach, method, estimated timeline)
- **What resources they are requesting** from the stipend

Applications are reviewed by the sponsoring department head (the DM). Multiple parties may apply for the same grant; the DM selects based on qualifications and narrative fit.

> [!tip] **DM Note**
> The application process is a roleplay tool, not a barrier. If a player puts genuine thought into a proposal, accept it. Competition between parties for the same grant is a useful source of campus drama.

---

### 3. The Stipend
Upon acceptance, the student receives the **Grant Stipend** — pre-expedition resources provided by the sponsor. Stipends are not credits; they are direct-issue materials, equipment access, or University services relevant to the expedition.

Stipend resources must be used for the grant expedition. They cannot be stockpiled, traded, or applied to other quests.

---

### 4. Completion & Reporting
Upon return, the student submits a **Grant Report** — more detailed than a standard field report, specifically addressing the deliverable and what was or was not accomplished. The sponsor reviews the report and determines the outcome.

---

## Grant Tiers

Grants are classified into three tiers based on difficulty, scope, and the seniority required to undertake them.

---

### 🟢 Minor Grant
*Available to students of any standing.*

Minor grants are focused, achievable objectives — a single specific deliverable that a student or small party can reasonably accomplish in one expedition.

**Stipend:** One enhanced Loadout Package (standard plus one additional relevant item — a specific tool, a higher-tier potion, a relevant scroll).

**Example Deliverables:**
- Collect three specimens of a specific plant or creature from a documented hex
- Produce a detailed survey map of a specific unexplored area
- Recover a specific category of artifact from a known ruin
- Document the religious practices of a specific settlement
- Identify and report the source of an anomalous environmental phenomenon

---

### 🟡 Standard Grant
*Requires Junior standing (Level 5) or higher.*

Standard grants involve more complex objectives — often multi-stage, requiring both field work and analytical documentation.

**Stipend:** One enhanced Loadout Package plus access to one piece of University specialist equipment for the expedition's duration (survey instruments, a specialist beast's saddle, an alchemical analysis kit, etc.).

**Example Deliverables:**
- Produce a complete structural analysis of a significant ruin, including dating, cultural attribution, and a mapped interior
- Document a previously uncontacted community — culture, governance, relationship to the University
- Trace a trade route suspected of moving stolen University property and provide actionable intelligence
- Identify and sample the source of a regional disease or blight
- Investigate and resolve an archaeological dispute between two competing claims over a site

---

### 🔴 Major Grant
*Requires Senior standing (Level 6) or higher. Faculty Advisor endorsement required.*

Major grants are significant scholarly undertakings — the kind of work that produces publications, changes what the University knows about a region, and occasionally changes the political landscape. They are competitive and demanding.

**Stipend:** One fully customized Loadout Package (the student specifies requested items, subject to DM approval) plus a **monetary stipend** from the external patron (treated the same as a Capstone Quest stipend — a narrative resource, not personal wealth).

**Example Deliverables:**
- Produce a comprehensive field study of a significant unexplored region — geography, inhabitants, resources, threats, and a complete hex map
- Establish first formal contact with a faction of strategic importance to the University
- Recover a specific artifact of major scholarly or magical significance from a high-danger location
- Resolve a long-standing academic question through field investigation and submit the findings as a formal publication
- Conduct a multi-phase investigation into a significant threat (political, environmental, or arcane) and provide the University with actionable intelligence

## Grant Outcomes

### ✅ Full Completion
The deliverable was accomplished as specified. The student retains the stipend, earns the standard quest credits for the expedition, and receives a **Grant Completion Mark** on their Conduct Record.

**Three Grant Completion Marks** (of any tier) unlock eligibility for the **Faculty Research Fellowship** (see below).

> [!info] **Grants and your Major's Boon 3**
> Completing one grant **in your Major's domain** is the research requirement for **Boon 3 — Advanced Study** (attainable at Senior standing, Level 6). See [[Declared Majors|Majors & Minors — Rules]]. A Minor Grant qualifies — the tier doesn't matter, the domain does.

### ⚠️ Partial Completion
The deliverable was substantially but not fully accomplished — the core objective was met but secondary components were not, or the student encountered circumstances that prevented full delivery. The DM determines whether partial completion earns a Completion Mark at their discretion. The student retains the stipend.

### ❌ Failure
The deliverable was not accomplished. The stipend is **not reclaimed** (it was consumed by the expedition) but the student receives no Completion Mark and is **ineligible for grants from the same sponsor for one semester**. A failure is logged in the Conduct Record.

> [!info] **The Incomplete Rule**
> A grant that is abandoned mid-expedition — not failed in the field, but simply not attempted — is treated as an Incomplete on the student's record. The grant may be re-listed. The stipend must be returned in full. The student is ineligible for grants from any department for one semester.

## The Faculty Research Fellowship

The **Faculty Research Fellowship** is the highest academic recognition available to a student before graduation. It is awarded to students who have demonstrated sustained excellence in grant-funded research — three completed grants, of any tier.

**Fellowship Benefits:**
- The title of **Research Fellow** is added to the student's Conduct Record and is publicly recognized within the University
- The Fellow gains **unrestricted Library access** — all tiers, including materials not normally available to students
- The Fellow is eligible to be assigned a **dedicated research budget** by the University — a small recurring stipend (in resources, not credits) for independent study
- Faculty treat the Fellow as a peer in academic contexts — they are consulted, invited to faculty discussions, and given access to ongoing faculty research that would otherwise be confidential
- The Fellow's publications receive the endorsement of the Faculty Research Office, increasing their external circulation and credibility

> [!tip] **DM Note**
> The Fellowship is a milestone, not a conclusion. A Fellow should start receiving attention from external institutions, rival scholars, and patrons who have been watching the University's research output. It is the signal that the student is ready for the campaign's bigger narrative beats.

## Grant Sponsors

Grants may be sponsored by the University's own departments or by external parties who work through the University's institutional framework. The sponsor determines the deliverable, controls the stipend, and reviews the outcome.

**Internal Sponsors** — the [[00 — Department Index|Eight Departments]]:
- [[01 — Department of Arcane Studies|Arcane Studies]] (Magistra Odilia Sternwald) — Arcane Research
- [[02 — Department of Natural Sciences|Natural Sciences]] (Professor Berthold Grasmeyer) — Natural Philosophy
- [[03 — Department of Physics & Alchemy|Physic & Alchemy]] (Doktor Agathe Sauerbrunn) — Medicine & Herbalism
- [[04 — Department of Antiquities|Antiquities]] (Professor Casimir Vogelsang) — Archaeology
- [[05 — Department of Exploration & Cartography|Exploration & Cartography]] (Magister Roswitha Kartner) — Cartography
- [[06 — Department of War Studies & Engineering|War Studies & Engineering]] (Professor Dietrich Mauerbrecher) — Military Theory
- [[07 — Department of Letters & Cultures|Letters & Cultures]] (Professor Ilsabet Federkiel) — Anthropology & Cultural Studies
- [[08 — Department of Statecraft & Commerce|Statecraft & Commerce]] (Professor Aldemar Goldwaage) — Political Philosophy & Diplomacy

**External Sponsors** (examples):
- **The City of Greyhawk** — political, diplomatic, or intelligence-related deliverables
- **The Merchants' Consortium** — trade route mapping, economic investigation
- **The Temple of [Deity]** — religious site documentation, recovery of sacred objects
- **Regional Nobles** — security investigation, disputed territory documentation
- **Private Scholars** — specific research questions or artifact recovery

External sponsors bring higher stipends, broader narrative implications, and occasional complications — their agendas are not always perfectly aligned with the University's values.
