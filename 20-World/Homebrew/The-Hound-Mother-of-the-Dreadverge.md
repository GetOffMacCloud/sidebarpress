# The Hound-Mother of the Dreadverge

> **HOMEBREW — not canon.** This is a Sidebar Press invention for [[SP-PBS-02-The-Bones-of-the-Dreadverge]].

## Summary
Fifteen centuries ago, the Flan buried their great hunters with their beasts. They bound the dead animals to the barrow with a **binding object** laid on the chieftain's body, so the pack would guard the grave and sleep until the object was disturbed. **Kethra, the Hound-Mother,** is the westernmost of nine such chieftain-barrows under the Clatspurs, raised by Flan clans who held these valleys before the Oeridians crossed the passes.

## The Binding Tradition (homebrew rules of thumb)
- Each chief's barrow has **one binding object** on the body (a torc, a girdle, or a spear-head) and **one command object** on the brow or in the hand (a crown, a horn, or a staff).
- If the binding is removed, the pack wakes and hunts for it. It takes livestock as it did in life and drags carcasses home.
- Whoever holds the command object can steer an awake pack.
- Returning the binding puts the pack back to sleep, but its last act is to punish whoever holds the command.
- Bound packs never attack offerings at the barrow's stones (**the Hound-Mother's Law**).
- Bound packs will not cross running water except at a built ford.

## Perrender Folk Memory
Highland Perrenders have half-forgotten the barrows. What survives is a nursery song in Stamtaal and Common, and a habit of not grazing the stones' slopes. When the dead walk, Perrenders reach first for the fears they know: Iggwilv's undead army, and Iuz beyond the ridges. Neither has anything to do with the Hound-Mother.
