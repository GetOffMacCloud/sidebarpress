## Lore
The Orkney islanders called the nuckelavee the most malignant of all the sea's devils. In the account set down by the Orkney folklorist Walter Traill Dennison in the 1890s, it is a horse and rider fused into one body, without skin, its black blood running visibly through yellow veins, its rider's head lolling as if its neck can't hold it, and its arms reaching the ground. Its breath withered crops and sickened livestock, and the islanders blamed it for the cattle plague they called *mortasheen*. Only the Mither o' the Sea, who ruled the waters in summer, could hold it in check. Only fresh running water could stop it on land. The farmer Tammas, in Dennison's telling, escaped it by leaping a stream.

At the table, the nuckelavee is a coastal disaster: the thing that comes ashore when the sea is angry, and the reason the village doesn't burn kelp on the beach.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** cold coasts, island farms, sea-meadows, and the deep water offshore.
- **Encountered as:** alone. There is only ever one nuckelavee on a given coast.
- **Signs:** crops blackening in a band running inland from the shore, horses and cattle sickening overnight, fresh-water streams that the village children are suddenly told never to play away from, and kelp-fires doused in a panic.

## Tactics
The nuckelavee charges up the beach and grabs two victims, one in each arm, then breathes on everyone who comes to help. It stays between its prey and the nearest stream. If it is doused or driven toward running water, it retreats to the sea and returns on the next dry night.

**Running it:** fresh water is the answer, and the players should be able to find it. Put a stream on the map, a rain barrel by the barn, and an old woman with the rhyme. A party that fights it toe-to-toe at level 5 should lose.

## Lair / Regional Effects
A coast where a nuckelavee comes ashore is altered within 3 miles:
- Each dawn, crops in one field (DM's choice) wither and blacken.
- Beasts sicken. A DC 12 Wisdom (Animal Handling) check each day is needed to keep a herd from losing a tenth of its number.
- Burning kelp or seaweed on the shore draws the nuckelavee that same night.

The effects end when it is destroyed. In the old stories, they also ended every summer, when the Mither o' the Sea returned.

## Adventure Hooks
1. A fishing village has burned kelp for its glass-works every spring for a hundred years. This year, a new merchant doubled the burning, and the cattle are dying.
2. An old sea-wife's rhyme gives three ways to beat the "skinless one": the stream, the rain, and a third line nobody remembers. The last person who knew it lives on the next island.
3. The nuckelavee has been seen on a coast with no streams at all. Somebody must have dammed them, and that somebody wanted it to come.

## Spoils
None from the creature, which dissolves into stinking foam when it is destroyed. The grateful island offers a boat, a croft, or the hand of a laird's heir.

## Design Notes
**Folklore source:** the Orcadian nuckelavee as recorded by Walter Traill Dennison (late nineteenth century). That includes its skinless fused body, its blight and *mortasheen*, its aversion to fresh water, the Mither o' the Sea, and Tammas's escape across a stream. Game statistics are original.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274):** *Defensive:* 178 hp is the CR 8 band (176–190). Resistance to cold and necrotic is too narrow to apply a multiplier. AC 15 is one below the expected 16, so defensive CR is about **7.5**. *Offensive:* modeled over three rounds: Blighting Breath on two targets (2 × 36 = 72), then two rounds of Multiattack (13 + 13 + 17 = 43 each). That averages about 53 damage per round, which is the CR 8 band (51–56). +9 to hit is two above the expected +7, and DC 16 is as expected, so offensive CR is about **9**. *Average:* about 8.25, which gives **CR 8**.
