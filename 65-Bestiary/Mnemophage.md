> ***Feeding.*** The mnemophage stores stolen memories as engrams and can hold up to 6. It starts each combat with a number of engrams of the DM's choice. Once per turn, it gains 1 engram (no action required) when a creature fails its saving throw against Memory Drain or when the mnemophage reduces a creature with an Intelligence of 4 or higher to 0 hit points. If it goes 24 hours without gaining an engram, or finishes a long rest, it loses all its engrams.
>
> ***Feeding States.*** The mnemophage's state depends on how many engrams it holds, and spending engrams can drop it to a lower state. The first time it reaches Sated or Gorged during a combat, it gains that state's temporary hit points; reaching the same state again in that combat grants none. *Ravenous (0–1 engrams):* its save DC is 16. *Sated (2–3 engrams):* it gains 25 temporary hit points, its save DC is 17, its Memory Drain can steal spells, and it can use Mind Lash. *Gorged (4–6 engrams):* it gains 25 more temporary hit points, which add to any it already has; its save DC is 18, its Devouring Touch deals extra damage, its Memory Drain also saps Intelligence, and it can use Mnemonic Recoil.
>
> ***Stolen Knowledge.*** For each engram it holds, the mnemophage can know one language and have proficiency in one skill that a creature it has drained knew or had. It loses this knowledge as it loses engrams.
>
> **Actions**
> ***Multiattack.*** The mnemophage makes two Devouring Touch attacks and uses Memory Drain.
>
> ***Devouring Touch.*** *Melee Spell Attack:* +8 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. *Hit:* 17 (3d8 + 4) psychic damage. While the mnemophage is Gorged, this attack deals an extra 4 (1d8) psychic damage.
>
> ***Memory Drain.*** The mnemophage fixes its attention on one creature it can see within 30 feet of it. The target must make an Intelligence saving throw against the mnemophage's current save DC. On a failed save, the target takes 27 (6d8) psychic damage, the mnemophage gains 1 engram, and the target forgets one language or one skill or tool proficiency of the mnemophage's choice. If the mnemophage is Sated or Gorged and the target is a spellcaster, the target also forgets one spell of the mnemophage's choice, which it can't cast, and the mnemophage banks that spell for Stolen Spellcasting. If the mnemophage is Gorged, the target's Intelligence score is also reduced by 1d4 (to a minimum of 3). All of these effects last until the target finishes a long rest. On a successful save, the target takes half as much damage and forgets nothing.
>
> ***Stolen Spellcasting.*** The mnemophage casts one spell, using Intelligence as its spellcasting ability (spell save DC equal to its current save DC, +8 to hit with spell attacks). It can cast *detect thoughts* and *dissonant whispers* at will without expending engrams. It can also cast a spell it banked with Memory Drain by expending a number of engrams equal to the spell's level (1 for a cantrip), but only while it still holds the engram that carries that memory.
>
> ***Fold Space.*** The mnemophage steps through a place someone has forgotten, teleporting up to 30 feet to an unoccupied space it can see.
>
> **Bonus Actions**
> ***Mind Lash (Sated or Gorged Only).*** One creature within 30 feet of the mnemophage must succeed on an Intelligence saving throw against its current save DC or take 11 (2d10) psychic damage and be unable to take reactions until the start of its next turn.
>
> **Reactions**
> ***Mnemonic Recoil (Gorged Only).*** When a creature the mnemophage can see within 30 feet of it hits it with an attack, the mnemophage forces that creature to make a DC 18 Intelligence saving throw. On a failed save, the creature takes 13 (3d8) psychic damage, and the mnemophage regains hit points equal to the damage dealt.

## Lore
Memory is food to a mnemophage. It lifts what a mind knows (a language, a trade, a spell, a name) and stores it as an engram, a pale knot of light that glows behind its blank face. The more it eats, the stronger it grows, and the more it can do with what it stole. It speaks its victims' tongues, uses their skills, and casts their spells back at their friends. Starved, it is merely dangerous. Gorged, it is a thief carrying a whole party's talents.

Sages guess that mnemophages are the last of some vanished people who recorded everything and forgot how to stop. They haunt libraries, archives, academies, and the deep cities of folk with long memories. Their victims wake on the floor with a headache, missing a word they've used all their lives, and most never learn what took it. The few who do describe the same thing: a thin grey figure with a face like an unwritten page, and the feeling of being read.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** libraries, archives, monasteries, academies, deep cities, and ruins where records were kept.
- **Encountered as:** alone. A long-settled mnemophage may keep a few thralls who have forgotten everything but their orders (use **commoner** or **thug** statistics).
- **Signs:** scholars who can't remember their own names, books whose pages have gone blank, guards who don't know why they're standing at their posts, and gaps in conversation that nobody can explain.

## Tactics
The mnemophage opens with Memory Drain on the creature that looks cleverest, ideally a wizard, so it can bank a spell once it is Sated. Its Devouring Touches keep a melee fighter busy in the meantime. Every failed save makes it stronger, so it spreads Memory Drain around to climb toward Gorged. Once Sated, it spends Mind Lash each turn on whoever is about to need a reaction (a caster with *counterspell* or *shield*, or a fighter with Sentinel). It casts a stolen spell when that is the best play, knowing that the memory goes with it. Below half its hit points and still Ravenous, it uses Fold Space and flees to feed elsewhere; Gorged, it fights to the end.

**Running it:** track engrams with tokens and write each banked spell on the token from the drain that stole it. Spending that token, on the spell or on anything else, loses the spell, so most banked spells can be cast once. Apply Memory Drain's effects for the state the mnemophage was in when it used the drain, before the new engram arrives. If it starts a combat Sated or Gorged, give it that state's temporary hit points when combat begins. Tell each player exactly what their character has forgotten; that loss is the creature's whole horror.

## Adventure Hooks
1. The head of a great library hires the party: three apprentices have forgotten how to read. *Twist:* the librarian is feeding the apprentices to the mnemophage in exchange for a single memory it took years ago, the face of his dead wife.
2. A captured enemy spy can't remember her mission, her name, or her handler. *Twist:* a mnemophage drained her on purpose; it works for the other side, and now it holds the mission, the codes, and her face.
3. A party member wakes one morning speaking a language they never learned. *Twist:* a mnemophage has died nearby, and its engrams have scattered into the nearest minds, along with the memory of where its lair, and its hoard of stolen secrets, can be found.

## Spoils
When a mnemophage dies, the memories it holds return to their owners at once, wherever they are. Its blank face-plate peels away as a thin, pearly disk the size of a palm, worth 300 gp to a sage, who can coax one last memory from it (often a clue the DM wants to plant). Its lair holds shelves of blank books and its victims' gear: 2d6 × 100 gp in coin and goods, and one roll on the DMG's Magic Item Table C.

All dice averages, bonuses, and the base DC (8 + 4 + Int 4 = 16) check out.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).** Modeled with the mnemophage starting on 1 engram, a typical fed-recently start.
- *Defensive:* 161 hp plus Sated's 25 temporary hit points (gained after round 1) is 186. Nonmagical weapon resistance is ×1.5 at expected CR 5–10, for 279 effective hp, the CR 14 band (266–280). AC 16 is two below the expected 18, so **defensive CR is 13**. Gorged's further 25, which arrives after the modeled rounds, isn't counted.
- *Offensive:* round 1, two Touches and Memory Drain, 61 (it becomes Sated). Rounds 2 and 3 add Mind Lash, 72 each. That averages about 68, the top of the CR 10 band (63–68). +8 to hit is one above the expected +7, so **offensive CR is about 10.5**.
- *Average:* about 11.75. Read strictly, that rounds to 12, two above the stated CR. But a CR 11 or 12 creature uses the ×1.25 multiplier, under which this same block reads defensive CR 10.5 (232 effective hp, AC one below expected) and an average of 10.5. Re-rated to 12, it would compute back to about 11, so 12 isn't stable. The stable result is **CR 11** (rounding 10.5 up for its uncounted spell theft and Recoil healing). That is within 1 of the stated CR 10, so **CR 10 stands**. It is a hard CR 10; for a party that relies on Intelligence saves or one spellcaster, treat it as CR 11 when budgeting.
