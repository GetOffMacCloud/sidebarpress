# Bell-Wight

*The passing-bell tolls, once for every year of the dead man's life. On the ninth stroke, something drops from the belfry on a rope.*

> ***Bound Bell.*** The wight is bound to one bell (a Huge object with AC 19, 60 hit points, and immunity to poison and psychic damage). While the wight is within 300 feet of its bell, the bell tolls on its own at the start of each of the wight's turns, and the wight gains one **Toll** (maximum 3). If the bell is silenced (its clapper wedged or tied by a creature next to it as an action, a *silence* spell, or anything else that stops its sound), the wight loses all Tolls and gains no more until the bell rings again.
>
> ***Rejuvenation.*** If the wight is destroyed while its bell is whole, it re-forms in the belfry the next time the bell is tolled for a death. Cracking the bell (reducing it to 0 hit points, or dealing 20 or more thunder damage to it in a single hit) destroys the wight forever.
>
> **Actions**
> ***Multiattack.*** The wight makes two Bell-Rope attacks.
>
> ***Bell-Rope.*** *Melee Weapon Attack:* +6 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target. *Hit:* 8 (1d8 + 4) bludgeoning damage plus 3 (1d6) thunder damage for each Toll the wight has. If the target is a Medium or smaller creature and the wight isn't already grappling a creature, the target is grappled (escape DC 14).
>
> ***Knell (Recharge 5–6).*** The wight strikes the air like a bell. Each creature within 30 feet of it that can hear must make a DC 13 Constitution saving throw. On a failed save, a creature takes 14 (4d6) thunder damage and is deafened until the end of its next turn. On a successful save, it takes half as much damage and isn't deafened. Knell has no effect in a silenced area.

## Lore
The passing-bell is tolled when someone in the parish dies: three times three for a man, three times two for a woman, then once for every year of their life. Bell-ringers know the count by heart. When a ringer dies in the tower, tangled in the ropes, fallen from the frame, or hanged for ringing the wrong count, sometimes the bell doesn't let them go.

A bell-wight is that ringer. It sleeps in the belfry until the bell is tolled for a death, then comes down on the ropes to find whoever the bell was rung for. It grows stronger with each stroke. Silence the bell, and it is only a dead man with a rope.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** parish churches, town bell-towers, abbey belfries, and drowned churches whose bells still sound at low tide.
- **Encountered as:** alone, often with the specters or zombies of those it has hanged.
- **Signs:** the bell ringing at odd hours with nobody in the tower, rope burns on the necks of the recently dead, and bell-ropes that are always wet.

## Tactics
The wight drops from the belfry and fights within earshot of its bell. It grapples one target with a rope and keeps striking it, so that a party trying to reach the bell has to leave a friend in its grip. It saves Knell for when three or more enemies bunch up. It won't follow anyone past the range of its bell.

**Running it:** ring the bell aloud at the table, one tap of a glass per Toll. Players will start counting.

## Adventure Hooks
1. The bell tolled last night for a man who isn't dead yet: the town reeve. He's asking for protection.
2. A drowned village's church bell still rings at low tide. Every time it does, a fisherman is found tangled in his own lines.
3. A temple wants its old bell melted down and recast. The bell-founder refuses to light the furnace until something in the belfry is laid to rest.

## Spoils
The bell itself. A sanctified bell (blessed after the wight is destroyed) acts as a *hallow* focus for the church. Cracked bell-bronze is worth 150 gp to a founder.

## Design Notes
**Original**, inspired by the wight (MM p. 300). The Toll mechanic gives the party a clear lever: silence the bell and the wight's damage drops by up to a third. A [[Hush-Moth Swarm]] is the natural, if dangerous, answer.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274):** *Defensive:* 60 hp doubled for broad resistances at expected CR 1–4 gives 120 effective hp, which is the CR 4 band (116–130). AC 13 is one below the expected 14, so defensive CR is about **3.5**. *Offensive:* modeled over three rounds: Knell on two targets (28), then Multiattack at 2 Tolls (2 × 15.5 = 31), then Multiattack at 3 Tolls (2 × 19 = 38). That averages about 32 damage per round, which is the top of the CR 4 band (27–32). +6 to hit is one above the expected +5, so offensive CR is about **5**. *Average:* about 4.25, which gives **CR 4**. **With its bell silenced,** it deals 17 damage per round and plays like a CR 3 creature (700 XP). That is the intended reward.
