## Lore
A penny-risen is what the Reaper's priests call a tithe paid. A priest of Nerull speaks the hours of the dead over a lead coin and drops it in a well. The strong who drink are sick for a day. The weak die within three, and on the fifth night they get up.

Hill folk have no name for them. They are the miller, the neighbor's child, last spring's lamb. That is the cruelty the Grey Sickle intends: a hamlet will bar its doors against wolves, but it hesitates before it burns its own grandmother.

A penny-risen has no hunger and no hatred. It has an errand. A circle of ash on a door ([[Ash Tally]]) marks a house that is "owed", and the dead of that well walk to it and go in.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** hamlets, farmsteads, waystations and churchyards along any road a Nerullite procession has walked.
- **Encountered as:** everyone the well killed, risen on the same night: 3–6 in a hamlet, with whatever livestock drank from the same trough.
- **Signs:** a ring of dead grass around a well; a mass grave that "won't settle"; ash circles on doors; crows that follow strangers; sheep that refuse a trough.

## Tactics
The penny-risen walk at speed 20 toward the nearest ash-marked door and attack only what stands in the way. They ignore a creature that steps aside. Once inside a marked house, they kill the living they find there, then stand still until dawn.

A penny-risen within 30 feet of a [[Reaper's Penny]] turns toward it. A character can use a penny to lead them away from a door.

**Running it:** put the doors on the map before the fight and tell the players where the dead are going. The fight is about getting between, not about damage.

## Variants
- **Animal penny-risen.** Lambs, dogs and foxes: Small, speed 30 ft., Bite in place of Slam (see the import text below). They reach the doors first.
- **Deep-drinkers.** A casting of [[Reaper's Tithe]] at 4th level or higher raises those who drank deep as [[Ghoul Rider|ghouls]] instead.

## Adventure Hooks
1. **The mound that won't settle.** A sexton asks for help watching a new grave on the fifth night. *Twist:* two of the dead were buried in blessed linen and lie still, which tells the wardens that blessing works and that someone here knew to do it.
2. **The trough.** A drover's whole flock dies in three days and he blames a rival. *Twist:* the penny is in the shared trough, and the rival's house has the ash circle.
3. **One penny short.** A corpse is found with its jaw broken open and no coin. *Twist:* a child took the penny as a keepsake, and every penny-risen in the valley is slowly turning toward her house.

## Spoils
A [[Reaper's Penny]] under the tongue of one in three (evidence; 5 gp to a temple scholar). Nothing else. Returning the dead to their families for proper burial earns more goodwill in the hills than any coin.
