## Lore
The Gash-Marked come up out of the deep places: old root-tunnels, flooded mine shafts, and the cracks that open under ancient forests. They call the rift they climbed out of the Gash, and every war-band that has followed it to the surface takes a number. The best known is the Eleventh Gash, led by the warlord [[Grakh Unscarred]]. Each warrior keeps its own tally on its skin, one cut for every foe it has felled, laddered up the forearms and across the chest. To the Gash-Marked, pain is a debt. A wounded raider fights harder, because it means to repay the cut before the round is out.

The raider is the war-band's rank and file. A band holds together through four kinds of warrior: raiders like this one; [[Gash-Marked Houndmaster|houndmasters]], who track for the band and run packs of [[Tunnel Scarhound|tunnel scarhounds]]; and [[Bone-Tally Chanter|bone-tally chanters]], who keep the band's count and mark its next victims. Over them all stands one warlord, who carries the band's iron standard.

Raiders speak Orc and Gash-cant, a clipped trade-tongue whose origin no scholar has traced. Captives who learn it never seem to learn more than the words for "count," "debt," and "kneel."

## Ecology
- **Environment:** tunnel networks and root-caverns below old forests, and the hill country and frontier villages within a night's march of a tunnel mouth.
- **Encountered as:** a raiding party of 4–8 raiders with a houndmaster and 2–4 tunnel scarhounds. A full war-band adds one or two bone-tally chanters and its warlord.
- **Signs:** tally-marks cut into the doorposts of raided farms, one for each of the dead; javelins with a notch on the shaft for every kill; blood-stiff rags left at tunnel mouths, where raiders bind their newest cuts.

## Tactics
Raiders open with javelins, then use Aggressive to close with whoever threw the first spell or arrow. They don't fear being hit: a raider that takes a wound comes back harder on its next swing. They gang up on one target until it falls, then move to the next. A raider flees only when its warlord or chanter falls, and then it runs for the nearest tunnel.

**Running it:** put a die or a token on each raider that took damage this round, so you remember its Blood Tally on the next hit.

## Adventure Hooks
1. A mining camp's lower shaft broke into an old tunnel, and now its night shift goes missing a few at a time. The foreman has been trading the Gash-Marked lamp oil and food for safe passage, and the raiders have come to collect a debt he can't pay.
2. A wounded raider surrenders and offers to lead the party to the war-band's camp. Its tally-cuts are self-inflicted: it is a farmer's son, taken two summers ago, who cut himself to survive and wants to go home. The band will hunt him.
3. A frightened town posts a bounty of one silver piece for every tally-cut arm. The bounty hunters bringing in the most arms are carving the marks onto ordinary corpses, and the real war-band is using the panic to raid the next valley over.

## Spoils
Each raider carries 2d6 sp and a few looted trinkets (a thimble, a child's whistle, a wedding ring). Its gash-axe functions as a greataxe, and it carries 1d4 javelins and hide armor.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *Defensive:* 26 hp is the CR 1/8 band (7–35). AC 13 matches the expected 13, so **defensive CR is 1/8**.
- *Offensive:* the Gash-Axe deals 9 damage a round, and Blood Tally adds 3 on about every other round, for about 10.5: the CR 1 band (9–14). +5 to hit is two above the expected +3, which moves it one step, so **offensive CR is 2**.
- *Average:* (0.125 + 2) ÷ 2 ≈ 1.06, which gives **CR 1**. That matches the stated CR, so it is kept.

**IP substitutions:** Ravnica (in the Languages line) removed. The war-band's own names (the Gash, the Eleventh Gash, Gash-cant) are the contributor's and are kept.
