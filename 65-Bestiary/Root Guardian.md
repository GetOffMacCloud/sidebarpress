## Lore
The root guardian is the third ring of the **Fivefold Watch**, the guardians made by the warden-priests of a temple culture that is now mostly ruins and root-split stairs. The wardens didn't build their guardians from clay or bronze. They raised a hawk, a jaguar, and a viper on offerings and rites until each grew huge and tireless; they coaxed a strangler fig to stand up out of a grave garden; and they called an earth spirit into a carved idol at the tomb door. Into each they set a **heart-sigil**, a thumb-sized carving of green stone, and gave it one post and one rite. A full watch stood in rings: the [[Hawk Guardian]] over the approaches, the [[Jaguar Guardian]] in the precinct after dark, the root guardian in the grave gardens, the [[Viper Guardian]] in the cisterns and treasury, and the [[Stone Guardian]] at the tomb itself. The wardens are long gone. The guardians aren't.

The root guardian keeps the gardens where the wardens buried their dead and stored their seed. It began as a strangler fig planted over a warden's grave, its heart-sigil buried with the body. The fig grew down around the sigil, and the rite taught it to stand. Most of the time it lies across the garden paths as a knot of roots and vines, feeling every footfall through the soil. It knows the slow cadence of a funeral procession, and it knows the sound of a spade.

Any guardian of the watch lets a party pass untouched if one of them openly carries a **warden's seal** (a palm-sized disc of green stone carved with all five beasts) and the party keeps that guardian's rite. The root guardian's rite is to walk the garden path barefoot, in the slow step of a mourner, and never to dig, cut a living plant, or set a fire inside the garden walls. A character who studies the garden's grave markers for 10 minutes can work this out with a DC 13 Intelligence (Religion) check. Without a seal, a heart-sigil taken from another guardian of the same temple will serve, but only for a party that keeps the rite exactly; a single booted foot is enough. Seals turn up in warden graves (often under this guardian) and in the hands of the wardens' descendants.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** temple grave gardens, seed vaults, sacred groves, and swampy burial grounds.
- **Encountered as:** alone, lying across a garden path; with the [[Hawk Guardian]] overhead or the [[Viper Guardian]] in the garden's cistern.
- **Signs:** a garden path crossed by roots that weren't there yesterday; grave plots kept weeded by no one; a spade snapped in half and left upright in the soil.

## Tactics
The guardian waits, motionless and unremarkable, until an intruder breaks the rite or two or more are within 20 feet. Then it uses Grasping Roots, choosing only intruders, and slams restrained targets with its 10-foot reach (attacks against a restrained creature have advantage). It moves only 20 feet a round and doesn't pursue past its garden wall, so it fights from the middle of its bed and lets the roots do the holding. It fears fire. A torch thrust at it doesn't stop it, but it will turn on whoever holds one first.

**Mixed watch by APL** (four characters; DMG p. 82 multipliers). In a mixed fight, the root guardian is the controller: it holds intruders in place for the [[Jaguar Guardian]], the [[Hawk Guardian]], or the [[Viper Guardian]].

**Running it:** ask the players what they're wearing on their feet before they step into the garden, and don't say why.

## Adventure Hooks
1. A farmer digging a new well strikes roots that strike back. The twist: the farm sits on a forgotten warden graveyard, and the guardian goes still once the farmer's own dead, buried there without the rite, are moved and reburied properly.
2. A circle of druids asks the party to free a "captive tree spirit" from a ruin. The twist: there is no spirit to free. The root guardian is a strangler fig bound to a seed vault, and the druids want the seeds.
3. The wardens' descendants want their ancestors' tomb opened for a funeral. The twist: the root guardian has grown through the tomb's foundations and is slowly crushing what it was made to guard. To save the tomb, someone has to cut it, which breaks the rite.

## Spoils
Its heart-sigil, carved as a coiled root, lies at the center of its heartwood. It is worth 50 gp to a collector and can stand in for a warden's seal (see Lore). Its heartwood yields 1d4 pounds of dense, sigil-grained timber, worth 50 gp a pound to a druid or a woodworker. The seed vault it guards holds sealed jars of ancient seed worth 2d6 × 25 gp to the right buyer, and more than that to a farming people in a famine year.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *As submitted:* 68 hp is the CR 1/2 band (50–70). Its resistance covers only nonmagical bludgeoning and piercing, which is narrower than the house method's threshold for the effective-HP multiplier, so we don't apply it. AC 14 is one above the expected 13, so **defensive CR is 1/2**. Over three rounds (Grasping Roots on two targets, then two rounds of Multiattack for 24, with the roots ticking 14 damage in the first round and 7 in each later round as one target breaks free), it averages about 25 damage per round, the CR 3 band (21–26). +6 to hit is two above the expected +4, so **offensive CR is 4**. *Average:* **2.25**.
- *As corrected:* defense is unchanged at **1/2**. +5 to hit is one above the expected +4, and DC 14 is one above the expected 13, so **offensive CR is 3**. *Average:* **1.75**, which rounds to CR 2. That is within 1 of the stated CR 3, so CR 3 stands.

Its partial resistance is why CR 3 holds up at the table: it shrugs off half of most weapon damage before a party has magic weapons. Counting the resistance at half weight (×1.5, about 102 effective hp) puts its defense in the CR 3 band and the average at exactly 3.

**Set check.** The five guardians were submitted as a CR 3–6 ladder. By the numbers:

| Guardian | Stated | Defensive / Offensive / Average | Final |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Root Guardian]] | 3 | 1/2 / 3 / 1.75 | 3 |
| [[Hawk Guardian]] | 3 | 1/2 / 4 / 2.25 | 3 |
| [[Jaguar Guardian]] | 4 | 1 / 6 / 3.5 | 4 |
| [[Viper Guardian]] | 5 | 4 / 8 / 6 | 5 |
| [[Stone Guardian]] | 6 | 11 / 6 / 8.5 | **8 (re-rated)** |

The ladder holds from root to viper. The **stone guardian is the outlier**: its defense alone rates CR 11, so it was re-rated to CR 8 and becomes the set's capstone, with a CR 6 variant on its page. The root guardian computes lowest of the five, but for the resistance reason above it plays as a fair CR 3, level with the hawk.
