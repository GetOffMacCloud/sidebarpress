> ***Bell-Tether.*** A waxed cord runs from the corpse's wrist to the iron bell on its grave. If the corpse drops to 0 hit points while the cord is intact, its bell rings, and on initiative count 0 of the next round the corpse rises with 10 hit points. It doesn't rise if it was reduced to 0 hit points by radiant damage or while it was more than 60 feet from its bell. A creature can cut the cord with a successful attack against AC 10 that deals slashing damage, or snap it with a successful DC 12 Strength check made as an action.
>
> ***Answering Reflex.*** The corpse always moves toward the loudest bell it can hear. If no creature is within 30 feet of it at the start of its turn, it must use its movement to approach the nearest ringing bell.

> ***Multiattack.*** The corpse makes two Slam attacks.

## Lore
In the old days, mourners who feared burying the living tied a cord from the dead hand to a bell above the grave, so that anyone who woke in the coffin could ring for help. Most of those bells never rang. In a churchyard where something has gone wrong, they all do. A curse, a broken vow, or a sexton who sold the bodies can make the dead answer their own bells, and a bell-strung corpse is what climbs out.

The cord is both its leash and its life. As long as it holds, the bell calls the corpse back up every time it falls, and the corpse can't stray much farther than the cord's sound carries. It has no memory and no will of its own, only an answering reflex. It goes to whichever bell is loudest, and it beats on whatever it finds there. Bell-haunted yards often hold worse things, such as a [[Bell-Wight]] in the belfry.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** old churchyards, village burial grounds, and the abandoned cemeteries of drowned or plague-emptied towns.
- **Encountered as:** groups of three to eight in a single yard, rising together whenever a bell is rung; sometimes as the servants of a bell-keeper or sexton who outlived their vow.
- **Signs:** grave-bells on iron posts that ring when there is no wind; cords of waxed string running from the turf to the bells; fresh soil heaped on old graves.

## Tactics
Bell-strung corpses aren't clever. They lurch toward the nearest living creature or the loudest bell and slam whatever they reach. They keep rising until their cords are cut. A party can lure them with any bell, including a handbell or a church bell, and a party that drives them more than 60 feet from their graves can put them down for good.

**Running it:** make the cord visible in the read-aloud and every description. When a corpse drops, ring its bell, then count down to initiative 0. The first time one gets back up, the players will start hunting for cords.

## Adventure Hooks
1. The sexton swears the grave-bells rang all night, and three graves are open by morning. *Twist:* the sexton cut the cords to sell the bells' iron, and the corpses he "lost" are wandering the village, drawn by the bell on the new schoolhouse.
2. A wealthy family insists that their newly buried patriarch be dug up, because his grave-bell rang. *Twist:* he really was buried alive, and he is the only one in the yard who isn't a bell-strung corpse yet.
3. The party must cross a cemetery during a funeral procession that rings handbells all the way. *Twist:* the mourners know exactly what the bells will wake. They are leading the corpses out of the yard toward someone's house.

## Spoils
Each grave-bell is a small iron bell worth 2 gp. A corpse's grave goods are worth 1d6 gp, and the waxed cords are prized by hedge-witches as components for spells of warding and binding.

- *Defensive:* 30 hp, plus 10 for one Bell-Tether rising in a three-round fight, gives 40 effective hp, the CR 1/4 band (36–49). AC 12 is one below the expected 13, which doesn't move it, so **defensive CR is 1/4**.
- *Offensive:* two Slams deal 10 damage a round, the CR 1 band (9–14). +4 to hit is one above the expected +3, which doesn't move it, so **offensive CR is 1**.
- *Average:* **CR 1/2**, which matches the stated CR. If the party never finds the cords, the corpse keeps rising and plays harder than its CR; tell the table about the cords early.
