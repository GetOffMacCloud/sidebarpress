## Lore
When a great working fails, the magic doesn't vanish. A portal collapses, a ward is torn down, or an artifact shatters, and most of the loosed power bleeds away into the air. Sometimes the leftovers knot themselves into a body. An aetherwrack is a tangle of torn light and arcane static as big as an ox. It hangs in the air where the spell broke and feeds on any magic cast near it. It has just enough mind to hunger and to hate, and it understands the languages of the casters whose spell made it, a scrap of their minds caught in the knot. It can answer only in bursts of static.

Colleges of wizardry keep quiet records of wracks, because each one is somebody's failure. A wrack that has fed long enough drifts away from its birthplace toward the strongest magic it can sense: a mage's tower, a temple's relic vault, or an adventuring party with a wizard in it. Casting near one is like throwing oil on a fire, and the old advice is to fight it with steel and patience, or not at all.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** ruined towers, collapsed portals, academies of magic, and deep places where old magic broke.
- **Encountered as:** alone; two or three only after a catastrophic failure.
- **Signs:** hair standing on end, compass needles that spin, spell components that crumble to grey dust, the smell of struck flint, and stone etched with frost-like filaments that glow faintly in the dark.

## Tactics
The wrack hunts spellcasters. It opens with Arcane Discharge on the largest group, then Lashes whoever cast most recently. The frayed connection gives that creature disadvantage on its next save, which sets up a recharged Discharge. It uses Arcane Step every turn to stay within reach of casters and out of reach of fighters. Its truesight and blindsight mean that invisibility and darkness don't hide anyone from it. It never flees, and when it dies, it bursts, so a party that has been crowding it pays at the end.

**Running it:** keep a tally of spells cast within 30 feet and announce the hit points the wrack regains each time, so the players learn quickly. The puzzle is to fight it with weapons, cantrips, and spells cast from more than 30 feet away. Roll the wild surge in the open; the table enjoys it. Warn the party, through a bloodied wrack's swelling glow, before Unstable Core goes off.

## Variants
- **Scaling Spell Siphon** (the contributor's variant). Spell Siphon restores 5 × the spell's level in hit points instead of a flat 10. It punishes high-level casting harder (a 5th-level spell restores 25) and low-level casting less (a 1st-level spell restores 5). Use it against parties of 11th level or higher; against typical 1st- to 3rd-level spells, it heals about the same and doesn't change the CR.

## Adventure Hooks
1. A college's teleportation circle misfired three days ago, and the apprentice who cast it hasn't been seen since. *Twist:* the wrack in the circle-room understands the apprentice's dialect perfectly, because part of the apprentice is in it, and killing it may kill the apprentice's chance of coming back.
2. Every spell cast in a mountain town fizzles a little, and the town's healer can't close a wound. *Twist:* a wrack has drifted into the town's well cistern and is drinking the magic from the whole valley, and it's getting bigger.
3. A wizard hires the party to capture an aetherwrack, not kill it, using a lead-lined cage. *Twist:* the wizard wants it to power the very ritual that created it in the first place, and a second failure will make a far larger one.

## Spoils
An aetherwrack leaves no body. When Unstable Core goes off, a fist-sized knot of cooled, glassy light drops where it hung. It hums faintly and is worth 500 gp to a wizard or a college, which will also want to know where it was found. A wrack's birthplace usually holds whatever was left of the working that made it: 2d6 × 100 gp in components, notes, and instruments, plus one roll on the DMG's Magic Item Table C.

**One attack or two?** The submitted Multiattack contains a single attack. We've printed it as written. If the contributor meant two Lash attacks, its offense rises to about 67 damage a round (CR 10 band), and its average comes out at about 11–12. At that point it should be re-rated to CR 11 or 12. *Contributor to confirm.*

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *Defensive:* 178 hp with resistance to nonmagical weapons, force, and psychic is ×1.5 at expected CR 5–10, for 267 effective hp, the CR 14 band (266–280). Magic Resistance counts as +2 AC, so its effective AC 19 is one above the expected 18, which leaves **defensive CR at about 14.5**. Spell Siphon and the healing surge aren't counted.
- *Offensive:* round 1, Arcane Discharge on two targets, 70. Rounds 2 and 3, one Lash, 19. Unstable Core on two creatures (54, once) adds 18 a round. That averages 54, the CR 8 band (51–56). +7 to hit and DC 16 both match the CR 8 expectation, so **offensive CR is 8**.
- *Average:* 11.25, which gives CR 11. That is within 1 of the stated CR 10, so **CR 10 stands**. (At the CR 11+ multiplier of ×1.25, defense reads 12 and the average 10, so the result holds either way.)
