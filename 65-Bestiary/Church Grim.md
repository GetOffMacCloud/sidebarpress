## Lore
English and Scandinavian folklore tells of the church grim (the Swedish *kyrkogrim*), the guardian spirit of a churchyard. The old belief held that the first person buried in new ground had to stay and guard it against the Devil and the restless dead. So when a church was founded, a black dog was buried alive on the north side, to keep the watch in a person's place. The grim is seen in stormy weather and at night. It is said to toll the church bell at midnight before a death, and to be seen by the priest at a funeral as a sign of whether the soul has gone up or down.

A church grim isn't a monster. It is a sentry. It turns on the living only when they come to rob, desecrate, or raise the dead it guards.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** parish churchyards, especially old ones.
- **Encountered as:** alone; one to a churchyard.
- **Signs:** large paw prints on the north side and nowhere else, graves untouched in a parish where grave-robbing is common, and a sexton who leaves a bone on the step at dusk.

## Tactics
The grim watches first. It lets mourners, priests, and honest visitors pass. It confronts trespassers at the lych-gate with a silent stare. It fights anyone who digs, desecrates, or uses necromancy. It opens with Death-Knell Howl, then knocks the leader prone and holds them there. It doesn't chase anyone past the churchyard wall.

**Ally use:** a grim can fight alongside a party defending its churchyard against undead, and that makes a fine climax for a graveyard siege.

## Adventure Hooks
1. A necromancer needs a body from a particular churchyard. The party is hired to get it out. The grim is the obstacle, and it isn't wrong.
2. The grim has been howling every night for a week, and nobody has died. Then the party arrives.
3. A new church is being built on a hill. The builders want the party to find "a proper black dog," and the bishop doesn't want to know.

## Spoils
None from the grim. A grim that is befriended, or that the party fights beside, may guide them to a grave or a buried secret within its churchyard, once.

## Design Notes
**Folklore source:** the church grim of English and Scandinavian tradition, including the black dog buried on the north side, its role as guardian against the Devil and the dead, and its bell and death-omens. Game statistics are original.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274):** *Defensive:* 76 hp is the CR 1 band (71–85). AC 14 is one above the expected 13, so defensive CR is about **1.5**. *Offensive:* modeled over three rounds (the Howl, then two rounds of two Bites at 26 each), it deals about 17 damage per round, which is the CR 2 band (15–20). +6 to hit is three above the expected +3, so offensive CR is about **3.5**. *Average:* about 2.5, which rounds to **CR 3**. The Howl's party-wide fear, which the formula doesn't count, justifies rounding up.
