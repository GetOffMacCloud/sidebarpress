## Lore
The viper guardian is the fourth ring of the **Fivefold Watch**, the guardians made by the warden-priests of a temple culture that is now mostly ruins and root-split stairs. The wardens didn't build their guardians from clay or bronze. They raised a hawk, a jaguar, and a viper on offerings and rites until each grew huge and tireless; they coaxed a strangler fig to stand up out of a grave garden; and they called an earth spirit into a carved idol at the tomb door. Into each they set a **heart-sigil**, a thumb-sized carving of green stone, and gave it one post and one rite. A full watch stood in rings: the [[Hawk Guardian]] over the approaches, the [[Jaguar Guardian]] in the precinct after dark, the [[Root Guardian]] in the grave gardens, the viper in the cisterns and treasury, and the [[Stone Guardian]] at the tomb itself. The wardens are long gone. The guardians aren't.

The viper keeps the temple's water and its wealth: the sacred cistern, the flooded stair, and the treasury vault beneath them. It is the only guardian the wardens taught to listen. It can't speak, and it isn't clever, but it understands what is said near it, and it remembers. The wardens' treasurers came and went by a spoken **watchword**, and the viper still waits, head raised above the water, for someone to say it.

Any guardian of the watch lets a party pass untouched if one of them openly carries a **warden's seal** (a palm-sized disc of green stone carved with all five beasts) and the party keeps that guardian's rite. The viper's rite is the watchword, spoken by the seal-bearer at the water's edge, and a cup of clean water poured into the cistern. The wardens carved the word into the cistern steps in their cipher; a character who studies the steps for 10 minutes can find and read it with a DC 15 Intelligence (Religion) check. Without a seal, a heart-sigil taken from another guardian of the same temple will serve, but only with the word spoken exactly. Because the viper understands Common, a party can also simply talk to it. It won't let them pass on talk alone, but it listens, and what it has overheard over the centuries can be coaxed out of it with gestures and patience. Seals turn up in warden graves and in the hands of the wardens' descendants, who sometimes don't know what they hold.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** temple cisterns, flooded stairwells and treasuries, jungle rivers below ruined shrines, and underground pools.
- **Encountered as:** alone, submerged in its cistern; with the [[Root Guardian]] where a grave garden surrounds the water.
- **Signs:** a shed skin the length of a canoe snagged on the cistern steps; still water with no fish, frogs, or insects; a faint bitter smell above the surface; offering cups lined up neatly at the water's edge.

## Tactics
The viper waits underwater, where its Stealth and darkvision serve it best, until someone reaches for the treasury or enters the water without the rite. It rises, bites with its 10-foot reach, and constricts the strongest-looking intruder so the others must come close to free them. When three or more enemies crowd it, it uses Poison Cloud, then Disengages with Shifty and slides back into the water, dragging its restrained victim with it. It fights to the death inside its cistern but won't follow anyone up the stair.

**Mixed watch by APL** (four characters; DMG p. 82 multipliers). In a mixed fight, the viper is the heaviest hitter of the four living guardians; the [[Root Guardian]] holds intruders in place for it.

**Running it:** have it react to what the players say out of character, a word at a time. It is listening for the watchword, and the players will start choosing their words carefully.

## Adventure Hooks
1. The oldest cistern under a city has a guardian that has started attacking its maintenance crews. The twist: the watchword was never lost. A water-sellers' consortium recut the steps with a new word years ago so that only its people could draw the clean water, and the old crews never learned it.
2. A scholar has deciphered a viper guardian's watchword and hires the party as escorts. The twist: the word is right, but she has no seal. She means for one of the party to wear the heart-sigil she plans to cut from the jaguar guardian on the way in.
3. A smuggling ring stashes its goods in a sunken temple because its members know the watchword. The twist: the viper understands every word they've said for ten years, and with patience and gestures it can lead the party to where the ring hid the temple's stolen gold.

## Spoils
Its heart-sigil, carved as a coiled serpent, sits behind its fangs. It is worth 150 gp to a collector and can stand in for a warden's seal (see Lore). Its venom sacs hold 1d3 doses of poison (a DC 14 Constitution save, or 22 (4d10) poison damage and poisoned for 1 minute), worth 200 gp each. The flooded treasury holds 3d6 × 50 gp in coin, jade, and offering bowls, and one roll on the DMG's Magic Item Table B.

Its attacks run on Dex (+4), as the source's +7 to hit and +4 damage on both bite and constrict already assume. That is unusual for a constrictor, but it is consistent, so we kept it.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *As submitted:* 105 hp is the CR 3 band (101–115). AC 15 is two above the expected 13, so **defensive CR is 4**. Reading Savage Assault as +5, the three rounds are Poison Cloud on two targets for 54, then bite 33 + constrict 13 + Savage Assault 5 = 51, twice. That averages 52 damage per round, the CR 8 band (51–56). +7 to hit is exactly the expected +7, so **offensive CR is 8**. *Average:* **6**.
- *As corrected:* 119 hp is the CR 4 band (116–130). AC 15 is one above the expected 14, which doesn't move it, so **defensive CR is 4**. Offense is unchanged at **8**; DC 14 is two below the expected 16, but the attack bonus dominates. *Average:* **6**. That is within 1 of the stated CR 5, so CR 5 stands.

**It runs hot.** The viper computes highest of the four living guardians, a full step above its printed CR. Its bite alone deals about what a CR 5 creature deals in a whole round. If it is overperforming at your table, drop Savage Assault: its offense falls to about CR 7, and the average to 5.5.

**Set check.** The five guardians were submitted as a CR 3–6 ladder. By the numbers:

| Guardian | Stated | Defensive / Offensive / Average | Final |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Root Guardian]] | 3 | 1/2 / 3 / 1.75 | 3 |
| [[Hawk Guardian]] | 3 | 1/2 / 4 / 2.25 | 3 |
| [[Jaguar Guardian]] | 4 | 1 / 6 / 3.5 | 4 |
| [[Viper Guardian]] | 5 | 4 / 8 / 6 | 5 |
| [[Stone Guardian]] | 6 | 11 / 6 / 8.5 | **8 (re-rated)** |

The ladder holds from root to viper, with the viper at the hot end of its CR. The **stone guardian is the outlier**: its defense alone rates CR 11, so it was re-rated to CR 8 and becomes the set's capstone, with a CR 6 variant on its page.

No IP substitutions; the source block is setting-neutral. The source note came from the contributor's CR 5 homebrew list and names no campaign of origin. The "(Striker)" tag and second-person wording on two traits suggest they were adapted from a player-facing sidekick or class template.
