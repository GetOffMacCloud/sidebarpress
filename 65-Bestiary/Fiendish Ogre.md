## Lore
Ogres sell themselves cheap. A cult that wants muscle, a warlock that wants a bodyguard, or a devil that wants a soldier can buy an ogre with a cart of meat. The fiendish ogre is what that bargain becomes after the buyer offers more than meat. A few drops of a fiend's blood, a brand burned into the hide, or a night spent in a summoning circle remakes the ogre into something with a furnace in its belly and skin like slag. It isn't any smarter, but it is much harder to hurt, and its new master's language comes out of its mouth in a voice like a bellows.

Most fiendish ogres don't remember the bargain. They remember being hungry and then being hot. They serve whoever holds the brand's other half (a ring, a rod, or a cult's altar-stone) and they serve it without question until it is broken.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** hills and mountains near the lairs of fire cults; ruined forts and caverns where a fiend's servants make their camp.
- **Encountered as:** alone as a guard, or in a group of 1d3 with a cult fanatic or an imp who holds their brands. Ordinary ogres sometimes travel with them, and they resent it.
- **Signs:** scorched handprints on stone; charred bones gnawed down to the marrow; trees with their bark burned off at shoulder height (about 10 feet up); and a brimstone stink that lingers for days.

## Tactics
A fiendish ogre opens with Fire Breath on the largest cluster of enemies it can reach, then wades in and swings its club. It isn't clever, but it knows that most weapons barely hurt it, so it charges straight at the party and ignores anyone who isn't hurting it. It uses its breath again whenever it recharges. If its master's brand is destroyed during the fight, it stops, looks around, and usually wanders off or turns on whoever is closest.

**Running it:** describe the party's mundane weapons skidding off its slag-hard skin. Players should learn quickly that they need magic weapons, and that fire won't help.

## Adventure Hooks
1. A mountain village has been paying tribute to a fire cult. The cult's enforcer is a fiendish ogre, and the villagers want it gone. The ogre is the son of a local ogre clan, and his mother wants him back, not dead.
2. A merchant has bought a fiendish ogre as a bodyguard, and he carries its brand-ring on his hand. The ring has been stolen, and the ogre has gone looking for whoever holds it.
3. A cult is turning ogres into fiendish ogres at a forge in the hills. The party can stop the cult, but each ogre they "free" by destroying a brand is still a fire-breathing ogre with nowhere to go.

## Spoils
The ogre's brand is set in iron on its hide; the matching half (a ring, rod, or altar-stone) is worth 100 gp to a collector and points the way to the ogre's master. Its greatclub stays hot for a day after it dies and can be used as a torch. It carries a sack holding 2d10 × 10 gp in coin and a few charred trinkets.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *As submitted:* 80 hp × 2 (nonmagical weapon resistance at expected CR 1–4) gives 160 effective hp, the CR 6 band (146–160). AC 15 matches the expected 15, so **defensive CR is 6**. Over three rounds (Fire Breath on two targets for 28, then two club hits for 15 each), it averages about 19 damage per round, the CR 2 band (15–20). +6 to hit is three above the expected +3, so **offensive CR is about 3.5**. *Average:* about 4.75, which gives **CR 5**.
- *Now:* 85 × 2 = 170 effective hp, the CR 7 band (161–175). AC 15 matches the expected 15, so **defensive CR is 7**. Over three rounds (Fire Breath for 28, then two club hits for 16 each), it averages 20 damage per round, the CR 2 band. +7 to hit is four above the expected +3, so **offensive CR is 4**. *Average:* 5.5. No uncounted feature justifies rounding up, so it gives **CR 5**.
- CR 5 is within 1 of the stated CR 4, so per house policy the **stated CR 4 stands**. Its resistance to nonmagical weapons is what pushes it up: a low-level party with no magic weapons will find it a long fight. If your party has none, budget it as CR 5.

**IP substitutions:** none. Ogres are SRD.
