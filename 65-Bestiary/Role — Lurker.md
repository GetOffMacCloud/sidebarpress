# Lurker

*The ceiling moves. Then the scout is gone, and so is the thing that took him.*

A **lurker** waits, strikes once with everything it has, and disappears. It's fragile in a straight fight, so it never offers one. Lurkers make the party watch the shadows, split their attention, and fear the quiet rounds.

> [!example]+ Applying the Lurker Role
> Adjust any statblock:
> - **Hit Points** −10%.
> - **Stealth** expertise (double its proficiency bonus), and a climb speed or darkness-friendly senses where it fits.
> - **Damage** unchanged, but see Ambush and Sneak Strike.
>
> **Signature traits (pick one or two):**
> - ***Ambusher.*** In the first round of combat, the lurker has advantage on attack rolls against any creature that hasn't taken a turn.
> - ***Sneak Strike.*** Once per turn, when the lurker hits a creature it has advantage against, or a creature that is within 5 feet of one of its allies, the attack deals extra damage equal to its normal damage.
> - ***Fade.*** As a bonus action while it is in dim light or darkness, the lurker takes the Hide action.
> - ***Drop from Above.*** If the lurker drops onto a creature from at least 10 feet above it, the target must succeed on a Strength saving throw (DC 8 + proficiency bonus + Strength modifier) or be knocked prone, and the lurker's first attack against it this turn has advantage.
>
> **CR:** usually **+1** with Sneak Strike, because doubling damage once per turn raises offense by about two steps while the HP cut lowers defense by about one.

## Running a Lurker
- Place it before the fight: on the ceiling, in the dark, among the dead.
- Strike the weakest or most isolated creature, then Fade or withdraw out of sight.
- A lurker that's been spotted and cornered fights badly. Let it try to flee.

## Lurker Minions
A lurker minion keeps **Drop from Above** or **Ambusher**. A pack of them springs from the ceiling in round one, and the fight is about surviving that first moment.

## Design Notes
Lurkers concentrate their threat into one moment. Sneak Strike is priced in the three-round damage model by counting the extra damage on every round it plausibly triggers (usually rounds one and two). On minions, keep the burst small: a fixed-damage pack that all gets advantage in round one is already frightening.
