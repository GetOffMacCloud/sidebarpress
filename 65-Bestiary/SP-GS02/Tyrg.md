## Lore
Tyrgs are the white wolves of the far cold: pony-sized, silent, with a howl that locks a listener's legs. Herders of the Tors and the Crystalmist foothills know them as winter's hounds and say they follow storms the way crows follow armies. They hunt in packs of four to eight behind a breeding female, the alpha, who is larger and fears nothing.

They are animals, but not ordinary ones. A tyrg pack will follow a powerful cold creature (a frost giant, a white dragon or an immoth) and hunt for it in return for the kills it leaves. Folk in the Yeomanry say a tyrg seen south of the hills means "the winter has a master this year."

## Ecology
- **Environment:** snowfields, high passes, the cold slopes of the Tors and the Crystalmists. They come lower only behind a storm.
- **Encountered as:** packs of 3–6 (4 at APL 6), with an alpha in packs of 5 or more.
- **Signs:** huge pawprints that don't break the crust; frozen mule carcasses opened and left; no birdsong; a howl that makes the listener's knees go weak, heard from miles off.

## Tactics
- **Silence, then the howl.** A pack approaches in silence, then one tyrg howls as the rest charge, to stun as many prey as it can.
- **Trip and rake.** Tyrgs bite to knock prey down, then rake the fallen. Two tyrgs on one prone target is their favorite fight.
- **Fire holds them off.** Tyrgs circle a lantern line rather than cross it, and go for whoever is outside the light. They won't howl near a flame. Only the alpha leads them through fire.
- **Retreat.** A pack breaks and runs when it is down to half its number or the alpha falls, unless something stronger is driving it.

## Named Example
**The Grey Bitch of the Tors** (an alpha) leads Hrimvald's pack in [[SP-GS02-The-Long-Night-at-Candlecrown]]: she has a torn left ear and a bell-collar of old iron from some herder's long-dead dog. She leads the gate wave on Midwinter Night.

## Adventure Hooks
1. A caravan master offers 50 gp a pelt for white tyrg hides, delivered before the spring thaw.
2. A tyrg pack has denned in a shepherds' winter fold. The shepherds want it gone and their flock back.
3. A tyrg wears a collar with a noble house's crest. Someone has been keeping one, and it got loose.

## Spoils
- **Tyrg pelt:** 25 gp (an alpha's, 75 gp). A cloak made from one gives advantage on saving throws against extreme cold.
- **Alpha's bell-collar:** a curiosity (5 gp), and proof of the kill for the Candlecrown elder.

## Design Notes
**Converted from** the 2e tyrg (MC5 *Greyhawk Appendix*; the *Monstrous Manual*), 5–8 HD: a large, silent wolf-like predator with a stunning howl that hunts in packs. No 2e text is reproduced. The bite-and-trip is the wolf's (MM p. 341).

| 2e feature | 5e version |
|---|---|
| Large, 5–8 HD (females bigger) | Tyrg (CR 2) and Tyrg Alpha (CR 4) |
| Bite 2d8 | Bite 12 (2d8 + 3) / 13 (2d8 + 4), with the wolf's trip |
| Stunning howl | Stunning Howl / Great Howl (Recharge 5–6, Wis save, 1-hour immunity so packs can't chain it) |
| Rear claws against downed prey | Rake (bonus action, prone targets only) |
| Silent movement | Stealth proficiency, Snow Runner |
| — | Flame-Shy (new), so the lantern line matters; Fire-Hardened for the alpha |

The howls' save DCs are Constitution-based (8 + proficiency + Con), on purpose: the howl is lungs, not willpower.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *Tyrg. Defensive:* 52 hit points is the CR 2 band (41–55); AC 13 is expected: **2**. *Offensive:* bite 12 plus Rake against prone targets about half the time (4) is 16, the CR 2 band (15–20); +5 is two above the expected +3, and the howl adds control: **offensive 3**. *Average:* 2.5, kept at **CR 2** (within 1 of the converted HD band).
- *Tyrg Alpha. Defensive:* 102 hit points is the CR 4 band (101–115); AC 14 is expected: **4**. *Offensive:* 24 a round (bite 13 + claws 11), the CR 3 band (21–26); +6 is one above the expected +5, with Pack Tactics and the Great Howl: **offensive 4**. *Average:* **CR 4**.
