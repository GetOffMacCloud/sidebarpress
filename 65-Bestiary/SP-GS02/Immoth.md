## Lore
Before the Rain of Colorless Fire, a mad sorcerer of the Suel Imperium bound a host of cold elementals with a curse spoken aloud, and the curse's words scattered across the world on the winter wind. The bound spirits became the immoths. Each one is driven to gather the words of that curse back to the Mountain of Ultimate Winter, and in the long search each has learned to love words for their own sake.

An immoth collects language the way a dragon collects coin. It takes voices, sayings, names and secrets and freezes them into runes on its body, and it will trade, bully and kill for a word it doesn't have. It is clever, patient and vain, and it speaks beautifully in whatever tongue it took most recently.

In the Flanaess, immoths are tales told in the Crystalmists, the Rakers and the Griff Mountains. Barbarian skalds say an immoth that hears a lost verse will walk a thousand miles to take it.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** high cold country: glaciers, snowfields, the ice caves of the high Tors and the Crystalmists. It brings its own storm when it travels.
- **Encountered as:** alone, almost always, with tyrgs or other cold beasts driven before it as hunting dogs.
- **Signs:** a storm that won't break; snow falling up a cliff; frost runes on stones that hum when touched; people who have stopped speaking.

## Tactics
- **It talks first.** An immoth opens with an offer and a threat (*message* across the snow, or its own voice from a cliffside). It wants words, not corpses.
- **In a fight** it stays on cliff faces and rooftops, where melee can't reach it easily, and uses *sleet storm* and *ice storm* to break up a group before it closes. It uses Word-Theft on spellcasters and singers first.
- **It spends its oldest words first.** Ice Runes are a resource it would rather not use: each Spend a Word gives a voice back. It uses them when hurt or cornered.
- **Fire changes its mind.** After it takes fire damage twice, it retreats up the cliff, out of reach, and offers to bargain again.
- **It flees at 50 hit points or fewer,** up the nearest ice face, with whatever runes it still has. It doesn't fight to the death for anything but the curse's own words.

## Named Example
**Hrimvald the Gatherer** is the immoth of [[SP-GS02-The-Long-Night-at-Candlecrown]]. It has heard the seventh verse of Candlecrown's Long Night Carol, a curse-fragment hidden in a hymn to Telchur, and it has come down out of the high Tors to collect it. Hrimvald speaks a slow, courtly Common it took from a dead Keoish envoy. It would trade the verse for a rarer word: a true name, a secret no one living has spoken, or the apprentice's counter-verse. Its siege actions on Midwinter Night are in the lair block above.

## Adventure Hooks
1. A village in the Rakers has stopped speaking. Its people write on slates; the snow outside has words in it.
2. A sage in Greyhawk will pay for any fragment of the "Winter Curse of the Suel" and doesn't say why the last three copyists left the city.
3. An immoth offers the party a deal: one secret each, freely told, in exchange for safe passage through a pass it holds.

## Spoils
- **Ice runes:** while the immoth lives, each rune holds a voice. Once it dies, any runes left shatter in 1 minute unless kept below freezing. A *dispel magic* (DC 15) on a rune, or 10 fire damage to it, frees the voice.
- **Heart-rune:** a fist-sized rune of blue ice that never melts below the summer solstice. Worth 1,000 gp to a sage, or used as an *ice knife* spell scroll that never needs a check.
- **Word-hoard:** the immoth's cave (if found) holds frozen phrases in dozens of tongues; a party that spends a day there with *comprehend languages* learns one secret per character (DM's choice).

## Design Notes
**Converted from** the 3e *Monster Manual II* immoth (pp. 127–128, CR 9): a Huge cold creature that hoards words, casts as a sorcerer, carries ice runes it triggers as quick spells, has a freezing tail poison that paralyzes, takes half damage from piercing and slashing, is vulnerable to fire, and walks on ice. No 3e text is reproduced.

| 3e feature | 5e version |
|---|---|
| Sorcerer casting (12th level), no fire | Innate Spellcasting, DC 17, 3/day *ice storm*, *sleet storm*, *counterspell*; no fire spells |
| Ice runes as triggered spells | Ice Runes + Spend a Word (bonus action); each spent rune frees a stolen voice |
| Freezing poison, paralysis | Freezing Tail: restrained, then paralyzed on a second failure; fire ends it |
| Half damage from piercing and slashing | Resistance to piercing and slashing (magical included) |
| Fire vulnerability | Damage Vulnerabilities fire, plus Fire Melts Words |
| Icewalking | Icewalking (climb and ignore ice terrain) |
| Hoards words | Word-Theft (Recharge 5–6), new |

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).** *Defensive:* 184 hit points sits in the CR 9 band (176–190). Resistance to two physical types is offset by fire vulnerability, so no adjustment. AC 17 is one above the expected 16: **defensive CR 9–10**. *Offensive:* over three rounds, *ice storm* on two targets (46) plus *ice knife* (12), then Multiattack (37) plus *ice knife* (12), then *ice storm* again (58) averages 55 a round, the CR 8 band (51–56). +9 to hit is two above the expected +7: **offensive CR 9**. *Average:* **CR 9** (5,000 XP). The siege's lair actions raise the climax's effective CR to about 10; the encounter math in [[SP-GS02 — Bestiary]] counts them as part of the climax's difficulty.

**Scaling:** for 4 PCs, 152 hit points (16d12 + 48 with Con 16, saves −2); for 6 PCs, Word-Theft recharges on 4–6.
