## Lore
The hawk guardian is the outermost ring of the **Fivefold Watch**, the guardians made by the warden-priests of a temple culture that is now mostly ruins and root-split stairs. The wardens didn't build their guardians from clay or bronze. They raised a hawk, a jaguar, and a viper on offerings and rites until each grew huge and tireless; they coaxed a strangler fig to stand up out of a grave garden; and they called an earth spirit into a carved idol at the tomb door. Into each they set a **heart-sigil**, a thumb-sized carving of green stone, and gave it one post and one rite. A full watch stood in rings: the hawk over the approaches, the [[Jaguar Guardian]] in the precinct after dark, the [[Root Guardian]] in the grave gardens, the [[Viper Guardian]] in the cisterns and treasury, and the [[Stone Guardian]] at the tomb itself. The wardens are long gone. The guardians aren't.

The hawk keeps the sky and the approaches. It roosts on the highest point of the temple, sees a traveler on the causeway a mile out, and watches every step of the climb. Pilgrims were meant to come up the processional stair, on foot, in daylight. Anything that climbs the walls, flies over the precinct, or comes up the stair after dark is, to the hawk, a thief. It doesn't leave its post to hunt; it eats what the offering bowls give it, and when they are empty, what the causeway gives it.

Any guardian of the watch lets a party pass untouched if one of them openly carries a **warden's seal** (a palm-sized disc of green stone carved with all five beasts) and the party keeps that guardian's rite. The hawk's rite is the simplest: walk the processional stair by daylight, and don't climb, fly, or cut across the terraces. A character who studies the stair's carvings for 10 minutes can work this out with a DC 12 Intelligence (Religion) check. Without a seal, a heart-sigil taken from another guardian of the same temple will serve, but only for a party that keeps the rite exactly; one slip, and the hawk dives. Seals turn up in warden graves and in the hands of the wardens' descendants, who sometimes don't know what they hold.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** jungle and highland temple ruins, cliff-top shrines, and the causeways that lead to them.
- **Encountered as:** alone on the temple's crown, or in pairs at a large site. It is the first guardian of a full watch that a party meets, and the one most likely to fight beside a sibling, because it alone can't be caught by the [[Root Guardian]]'s roots or the [[Stone Guardian]]'s Tremor.
- **Signs:** a single huge feather, banded rust and white, on the stair; small bones dropped in a ring around the temple's base; birds that won't nest anywhere on the ruin.

## Tactics
The hawk opens from height. It dives at least 30 feet onto the first creature that breaks the rite, strikes with beak and talons, and uses Flyby to climb back out of reach without provoking. It favors anyone climbing a wall (a fall is half its work) and spellcasters standing in the open. With a land speed of 10 feet, it never lands by choice. Below a quarter of its hit points, it climbs out of range and circles, waiting for the party to leave its stair. It never pursues past the foot of the causeway.

**Mixed watch by APL** (four characters; DMG p. 82 multipliers). In a mixed fight, the hawk is the flier that pulls ranged attackers' attention off the ground guardians.

The [[Stone Guardian]]'s Tremor and the [[Viper Guardian]]'s Poison Cloud hit everything near them, siblings included, which is why the wardens paired the heavy guardians with the hawk.

**Running it:** describe its shadow crossing the stair a round before it dives. Players who read that as a warning, and step back onto the rite, should be rewarded.

## Adventure Hooks
1. Pilgrims to a ruined hill temple keep being struck from the sky on the last stretch. The twist: the hawk attacks only those who take the cliff shortcut, and a rival expedition has blocked the processional stair to force everyone up the cliff.
2. A collector offers a fortune for a hawk guardian's egg. The twist: guardians don't breed. The "egg" in the nest is the hawk's own heart-sigil, set there by the wardens, and lifting it wakes the whole watch.
3. A hawk guardian has started ranging miles from its temple, taking goats. The twist: its temple has fallen, and it is searching for the last warden's descendant, a herder child whose family still keeps a seal in a cookpot.

## Spoils
Its heart-sigil, carved as a hawk with spread wings, is worth 50 gp to a collector and can stand in for a warden's seal (see Lore). 2d4 of its primary flight feathers survive intact; each is worth 25 gp to a fletcher or a priest. The temple's crown, where it roosts, holds the bones and belongings of past climbers: 3d6 × 10 gp in coin and gear.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *As submitted:* 68 hp is the CR 1/2 band (50–70). AC 14 is one above the expected 13, which isn't enough to move it, and its flight doesn't count without a ranged attack, so **defensive CR is 1/2**. Flyby lets it dive every round, so each round is beak 11 + talons 13 + Dive Attack 7 = 31 damage, the CR 4 band (27–32). +6 to hit is one above the expected +5, so **offensive CR is 4**. *Average:* about **2.25**, which rounds to CR 2. That is within 1 of the stated CR 3, so CR 3 stands.
- *As corrected:* 60 hp is still the CR 1/2 band, and nothing else changed. **Defensive CR 1/2, offensive CR 4, average 2.25.** CR 3 stands.

**Set check.** The five guardians were submitted as a CR 3–6 ladder. By the numbers:

| Guardian | Stated | Defensive / Offensive / Average | Final |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Root Guardian]] | 3 | 1/2 / 3 / 1.75 | 3 |
| [[Hawk Guardian]] | 3 | 1/2 / 4 / 2.25 | 3 |
| [[Jaguar Guardian]] | 4 | 1 / 6 / 3.5 | 4 |
| [[Viper Guardian]] | 5 | 4 / 8 / 6 | 5 |
| [[Stone Guardian]] | 6 | 11 / 6 / 8.5 | **8 (re-rated)** |

The ladder holds from root to viper. The **stone guardian is the outlier**: its defense alone rates CR 11, so it was re-rated to CR 8 and becomes the set's capstone, with a CR 6 variant on its page. The hawk itself runs a little soft for CR 3 on paper, because the DMG method ignores flight without ranged attacks. Against a party short on ranged attacks, its 90-foot fly speed and Flyby make it play at CR 3 or better, so we kept it there.
