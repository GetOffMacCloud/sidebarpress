## Lore
Iuz is free with his fiends (WGR5 p. 15), and his Boneheart servants rent the lesser ones as other masters hire bounty-hunters. The leash fiend is one of these: an eyeless, hunched demon whose throat ends in a living chain. It is bound to one quarry and one destination. It feels its quarry the way a dog smells its master, and it doesn't stop.

It isn't clever, and it can't be bargained with. It doesn't care whom it hurts on the way. That makes it the tool a Boneheart master sends when he no longer trusts his own agent, and the agent usually knows what it means.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** wherever its quarry is.
- **Encountered as:** alone, on the trail of its quarry. Sometimes it walks beside a servant of Iuz who thinks the fiend is his, and doesn't know he is the quarry.
- **Signs:** chain-scored ice and trees; a pressure in the ears, like weather coming; a missing man's belongings dragged toward the north.

## Tactics
The fiend goes straight for its quarry, leashes it, and drags it toward its destination, biting anyone who gets in the way. If the quarry is protected, it leashes the protector and throws them aside first.
**Running it:** the fiend makes a three-way fight. Let the party learn who its quarry really is. In SP-SQ-05 the quarry can be Liesl (Vesharan's prize) or Vesharan himself (Kermin's recall), at the DM's choice. Either way, protecting the quarry is a better win than killing the fiend.

## Named Example
**The Hound of Return** (SP-SQ-05). Kermin Mind-Bender sends it after Vesharan's second failure, telling the agent it is "an escort for the girl." Its quarry is **Vesharan himself**, and its destination is the Boneheart Citadel. Vesharan learns this on the ice. It becomes the moment he can be turned.

## Adventure Hooks
1. A hunter offers the party a share of a bounty on a man the fiend is chasing. *Twist:* the man is the hunter.
2. A leash fiend walks into Ritterstadt and stops outside the Long Dormitory. *Twist:* its quarry is a student who doesn't know she's anyone worth hunting.
3. The party finds a leash fiend chained in a cellar. *Twist:* its quarry died years ago, and it can't go home.

## Spoils
The hook on its leash: a black iron hook, ice-cold, that can be made into the [[Leash-Chain of Return]] by a smith willing to work it.

## Design Notes
**New creature** (Sidebar Press, 2026-11-05). The "hired fiend" of SP-SQ-05, built as a single tracking threat at the top of the APL 4 budget.

| Change | — | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Statblock | — | new | A bound recall-hunter, with the quarry twist built in |

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).** Internals: 16d10 averages 88, plus 32 from Con is 120; Str +4 with proficiency +2 gives +6 and DC 14; saves and skills check out; passive Perception 14; 2d8 + 4 averages 13 and 2d6 + 4 averages 11.
- *Defensive:* 120 hp is the CR 4 band (116–130). AC 14 is expected. A single resistance doesn't count toward the multiplier. **Defensive CR 4.**
- *Offensive:* bite and leash for 24 a round, the CR 3 band (21–26). +6 is 2 above the expected +4, which moves it one step. **Offensive CR 4.**
- *Average:* **CR 4.**

**Encounter caution:** at APL 4 with four PCs (thresholds: medium 1,000, hard 1,500, deadly 2,000), the fiend alone is medium (1,100 XP). If Vesharan fights beside it, the fight is deadly (1,550 × 1.5 = 2,325). In the intended play Vesharan is its quarry, not its ally. Don't add fog-drowned to the same fight.
