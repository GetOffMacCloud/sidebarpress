## Lore
In Russian and wider Slavic folk belief, the kikimora is a household spirit: a tiny, thin woman, sometimes with a bird's beak or a head the size of a thimble, who lives behind the stove or in the cellar. She spins at night. In a well-kept house, she is a quiet blessing who finishes the spinning and minds the hens. In a slovenly or quarrelsome house, she tangles the thread, breaks the pots, sours the milk, and sits on the chests of sleepers until they wake gasping. Folk tradition also knows a *swamp kikimora* who lives in the marshes and is less forgiving still.

A kikimora is not evil. She is a *judge*. She keeps the house to the standards it once had, and she punishes the household for falling short.

### Keeper's Due
| The household… | The kikimora… |
|---|---|
| sweeps the hearth, leaves her a bowl of milk or porridge, and doesn't quarrel at table | finishes the spinning, keeps the hens laying, and wards the house (the household has advantage on Perception checks to notice intruders) |
| neglects the hearth or quarrels | snarls thread, sours milk, hides keys, and breaks one dish a night |
| insults her, or tries to drive her out | Night-Rides the worst offender every night until amends are made |

## Ecology
- **Environment:** farmhouses, town houses, mills, and bathhouses. The swamp variant lives in marsh hummocks.
- **Encountered as:** alone; one to a house.
- **Signs:** thread found spun overnight, or snarled beyond saving; hens' feathers plucked into patterns; small wet footprints by the stove; and a household that wakes exhausted.

## Tactics
A kikimora would rather be appeased than fight. Once a fight starts, she uses Tangle-Thread on the biggest group, then hops from shadow to shadow with Behind the Stove, stabbing with her spindle. She casts *sleep* on low-level parties and *bane* on strong ones. She never leaves the house, so a party can simply walk out, though she'll be waiting when they come back.

**Running it:** the best ending is a negotiation. Find out what the house did wrong and fix it.

## Variants
- **Swamp Kikimora.** Neutral evil. Her speed is 30 ft. and swim 30 ft. Hearth-Bound and Behind the Stove apply to her marsh hummock and any patch of reeds. She replaces *sleep* with *fog cloud* and doesn't care whether anyone is appeased.

## Adventure Hooks
1. The new owners of a farmhouse hire the party to get rid of a "rat." The rat has been finishing the old widow's spinning for thirty years.
2. A merchant's whole household is falling ill with exhaustion. The cause is a kikimora that came with the stove they bought secondhand.
3. A village elder needs her kikimora moved to her grandson's new house before she dies. Moving a kikimora means carrying her across the threshold in a bast shoe, and she has opinions about the grandson.

## Spoils
An appeased kikimora gives a gift on the party's departure: a spool of thread that never tangles, a spindle that works as a *+1 dagger*, or a single *charm of restoration* (removes one level of exhaustion; one use).

## Design Notes
**Folklore source:** the kikimora of Russian and East Slavic household belief, including her spinning, her stove-corner home, her nighttime chest-sitting, and her swamp cousin. Game statistics are original.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274):** *Defensive:* 63 hp is the CR 1/2 band (50–70). AC 14 is one above the expected 13, and Behind the Stove makes her hard to pin down, so defensive CR is about **1**. *Offensive:* two Spindle attacks deal 13 damage per round, which is the CR 1 band (9–14). +6 to hit is three above the expected +3, so offensive CR is about **2.5**. Tangle-Thread's restraint sets up advantage for allies. *Average:* about 1.75, which gives **CR 2**.
