## Lore
Drowned guests are the dead of a gathering that went into the water together: a wedding party on a capsized barge, revelers in a flooded cellar, a ferry's full complement. They rise still dressed for the occasion, and they keep to their parts. Footmen hold their trays, bridesmaids hold their posies, and fiddlers hold bows that have long since lost their hair. The water keeps them. Most never stray more than a hundred feet from where they went under.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** flooded cellars and crypts, mill-ponds, lake shallows, sunken ships, marsh causeways.
- **Encountered as:** two to eight, usually still standing in the roles they held when they died.
- **Signs:** wet footprints that lead *out* of the water and back again, the smell of old wine and pond-weed, and party favors floating in still water.

## Tactics
Guests are slow and mindless, but they have their orders. They hold a stair, a doorway, or a causeway. They grapple whoever comes closest and walk backward into the water, so the water does the killing.

## Named Example
**The Marrow wedding guests** ([[SP-HAL-02-The-Hollow-Lantern]]). Two drowned guests in fifty-year-old livery hold the cellar stair for [[Lord Edric Vane]].

## Adventure Hooks
1. Every year on the anniversary of the barge disaster, the drowned wedding party wades ashore and goes looking for the groom, who didn't drown.
2. A flooded wine cellar holds a priceless vintage. The cellar staff are still on duty.
3. A ferryman refuses the night crossing. His passengers from twenty years ago are still waiting on the far bank.

## Spoils
Guests often wear the jewelry of the occasion, such as rings, brooches, and a silver tray or two: 2d10 gp per guest. Taking a guest's wedding favor without leaving another in its place offends the rest of the dead in the house.

## Design Notes
**Changes from the Zombie block:** a swim speed of 20 ft.; Waterlogged and Drag Under added. CR is unchanged at 1/4, because the water does the work and the creature does not.

**CR verification:** The zombie (MM p. 316) is CR 1/4. The changes add no damage, and the drowning threat depends on the terrain, so the CR stays **1/4**. When guests fight in deep water, the DM can price the encounter one step harder.
