> *1. Battle Song.* Each affected creature gains a +2 bonus to attack rolls and damage rolls.

## Lore
Goblins don't march in step by nature. They shove, squabble, and scatter at the first sign of real trouble, which is why a war-chief who can afford one keeps a drummer. The battle-drummer is usually a scrawny, half-starved runt, too weak to fight and too clever to be eaten, that has found its one talent. When it beats its drum, the pack moves as one: faster, braver, and hungrier than any goblin has a right to be.

Each drummer knows a handful of songs, passed down from the last drummer it served under, and each song has its own ugly purpose. The drum itself is the drummer's pride. It is stretched with the hide of something the band killed together, and a drummer will sooner lose a hand than its drum. Goblins say that when the drum stops, the band dies, and they are usually right: without its beat, a goblin pack remembers how frightened it is.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** goblin warrens in forests, hills, mountains, caves, and ruins.
- **Encountered as:** one drummer with 4–8 goblins, often with a goblin boss, hobgoblins, or worgs.
- **Signs:** a distant rhythm that rises and falls at dusk; a circle of trampled ground where goblins drilled to a beat; a cracked drumstick of carved bone left on a trail.

## Tactics
The drummer stays at the back, behind cover, and uses Nimble Escape to Hide whenever it can. It opens with Battle Song (or rolls, if the band's war-chief trusts luck), then re-rolls the song whenever the fight turns: Courage Song if a spellcaster starts frightening goblins, Haste Song if the band needs to chase or flee. Its allies protect it, because they know what happens when the drum stops.

**Running it:** play the drum. Tap the table in rhythm while the song lasts and stop the moment the drummer is incapacitated. The players will learn to go for the drummer.

## Adventure Hooks
1. Drumming has been heard beneath a town's streets every night for a week. It is a lone drummer whose band was wiped out, beating its drum to call a new one, and the sewer's kobolds have started answering.
2. A bard pays well for a true goblin war-song for her ballad. The song she learns works on more than goblins, and her first tavern performance ends in a riot.
3. A goblin band attacks a caravan in perfect formation. Its drummer is a human child, raised by goblins since infancy, with a genuine gift for music and a family two valleys away that has never stopped searching.

## Spoils
A drummer carries a dagger, leather armor, 1d6 cp, and its war drum (worth 5 gp). A bard or other creature proficient with drums can learn one of the drummer's songs by practicing for a month; the song has no magical effect for anyone but a goblin drummer, but goblins who hear it played by a stranger hesitate for a moment before attacking.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *Defensive:* 11 hp (7 as submitted) is the CR 1/8 band (7–35). AC 13 matches the expected 13, so **defensive CR is 1/8**.
- *Offensive:* the Dagger deals 4 damage a round, the CR 1/4 band (4–5). +4 to hit is one above the expected +3, not enough to move it, so **offensive CR is 1/4**.
- *Average:* (0.125 + 0.25) ÷ 2 ≈ 0.19, exactly halfway between CR 1/8 and CR 1/4. We round up, because Battle Song adds +2 to every ally's attack and damage rolls (with four goblins, about 8 extra damage a round that the drummer's own line doesn't show). That gives **CR 1/4**, matching the stated CR, so it is kept.

**IP substitutions:** none.
