## Lore
The jaguar guardian is the second ring of the **Fivefold Watch**, the guardians made by the warden-priests of a temple culture that is now mostly ruins and root-split stairs. The wardens didn't build their guardians from clay or bronze. They raised a hawk, a jaguar, and a viper on offerings and rites until each grew huge and tireless; they coaxed a strangler fig to stand up out of a grave garden; and they called an earth spirit into a carved idol at the tomb door. Into each they set a **heart-sigil**, a thumb-sized carving of green stone, and gave it one post and one rite. A full watch stood in rings: the [[Hawk Guardian]] over the approaches, the jaguar in the precinct after dark, the [[Root Guardian]] in the grave gardens, the [[Viper Guardian]] in the cisterns and treasury, and the [[Stone Guardian]] at the tomb itself. The wardens are long gone. The guardians aren't.

The jaguar keeps the night. By day it sleeps along a lintel or in the dark of a colonnade, and the rosettes on its hide (which the wardens trained into the shapes of their glyphs) are easy to mistake for carving. After sunset it walks the precinct's courts and colonnades, and it treats anything moving there as prey. It was made to hunt, and it hunts like a cat: it stalks, it waits, and it goes for whatever runs.

Any guardian of the watch lets a party pass untouched if one of them openly carries a **warden's seal** (a palm-sized disc of green stone carved with all five beasts) and the party keeps that guardian's rite. The jaguar's rite is to enter the precinct only between dawn and dusk; a party caught inside after dark must carry a flame, walk, and never run. A character who studies the colonnade's carvings for 10 minutes can work this out with a DC 13 Intelligence (Religion) check. Without a seal, a heart-sigil taken from another guardian of the same temple will serve, but only for a party that keeps the rite exactly; one character breaking into a run is enough. Seals turn up in warden graves and in the hands of the wardens' descendants, who sometimes don't know what they hold.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** jungle temple precincts, colonnades, overgrown courtyards, and ruined ball courts.
- **Encountered as:** alone, patrolling the precinct by night; with the [[Root Guardian]] and the [[Hawk Guardian]] at a large site.
- **Signs:** claw marks scored high on pillars; the carcasses of deer or pigs dragged up onto a lintel; an offering bowl licked clean; total silence in the courtyards after dark, without even insects.

## Tactics
The jaguar stalks first. It climbs to a lintel or wall top, waits for an intruder to separate from the group, and pounces from at least 20 feet away. A prone target gets the bonus bite and the whole Multiattack, with advantage from the prone condition. It chases anyone who runs, which is usually the weakest member of the party. When it drops below half its hit points, it breaks off into the dark, climbs out of reach, and comes back a few rounds later from another direction for a second pounce. It doesn't leave the precinct.

**Mixed watch by APL** (four characters; DMG p. 82 multipliers). In a mixed fight, the jaguar is the striker: it pounces on whatever the [[Root Guardian]] has restrained or the [[Hawk Guardian]] has knocked off a wall.

**Running it:** have it pounce first and appear second. Describe the glyph-patterned wall coming loose only as the claw lands.

## Adventure Hooks
1. Treasure hunters camped at a ruin lose one of their number each night. The twist: they sleep inside the precinct, and their guide knows the rite. He picks who sleeps nearest the colonnade.
2. A noble wants a jaguar guardian captured alive for her menagerie. The twist: the binding holds only inside its precinct. Outside, it becomes an ordinary great cat, still huge and now furious, and it hunts its captors first.
3. A village leaves meat on an old altar every new moon. The twist: they think they feed a god. They feed the jaguar guardian, and the offering is the only thing that keeps it inside the ruin. This year's herd has sickened.

## Spoils
Its heart-sigil, carved as a crouching cat, is worth 100 gp to a collector and can stand in for a warden's seal (see Lore). Its pelt, with glyphs plain in the rosettes, is worth 250 gp intact, and twice that to a scholar who can read it. Its lintel den holds 2d6 × 10 gp in coin and gear from those who were caught after dark.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *As submitted:* 85 hp is the top of the CR 1 band (71–85). AC 14 is one above the expected 13, which isn't enough to move it, so **defensive CR is 1**. Over three rounds (a pounce on the first, so bite 13 + two claws 22 + bonus bite 13 = 48, then Multiattack for 35 and 35), it averages 39 damage per round, the CR 6 band (39–44). +7 to hit is one above the expected +6, so **offensive CR is 6**. *Average:* **3.5**.
- *As corrected:* defense is unchanged at **1**. The damage is unchanged, and +6 to hit is exactly the expected +6, so **offensive CR is 6**. *Average:* **3.5**. We round up to **CR 4** because Pounce's prone condition grants advantage on every follow-up attack against that target, which the damage model doesn't count. Either way it is within 1 of the stated CR 4, which stands.

**Set check.** The five guardians were submitted as a CR 3–6 ladder. By the numbers:

| Guardian | Stated | Defensive / Offensive / Average | Final |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Root Guardian]] | 3 | 1/2 / 3 / 1.75 | 3 |
| [[Hawk Guardian]] | 3 | 1/2 / 4 / 2.25 | 3 |
| [[Jaguar Guardian]] | 4 | 1 / 6 / 3.5 | 4 |
| [[Viper Guardian]] | 5 | 4 / 8 / 6 | 5 |
| [[Stone Guardian]] | 6 | 11 / 6 / 8.5 | **8 (re-rated)** |

The ladder holds from root to viper, and the jaguar sits squarely at CR 4. The **stone guardian is the outlier**: its defense alone rates CR 11, so it was re-rated to CR 8 and becomes the set's capstone, with a CR 6 variant on its page. Like the hawk and the root, the jaguar is a glass cannon (CR 1 defense, CR 6 offense), and a party that wins initiative can drop it before it lands a second Multiattack.
