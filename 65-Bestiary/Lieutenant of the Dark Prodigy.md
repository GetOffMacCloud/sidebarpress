## Lore
Every fiendish warlord who wants to conquer the mortal world needs officers. The one called **the Dark Prodigy** (a title, not a name, given to whichever fiendish conqueror the DM chooses) makes its lieutenants from the best of Hell's hunters, then gives them weapons forged with its own curses. A lieutenant leaves its old duties behind. It serves the Prodigy alone, as its herald, its general, and its executioner.

Lieutenants are courteous, formal, and utterly loyal. They deliver terms of surrender in person, and they bow before fighting. Their weapons do the rest. Each blow weakens a different part of the victim: their strength, their wits, their will, or their spells. A lieutenant's curses outlast the fight, so a company that beats one off is still marked, and the next lieutenant will know exactly where they are weak.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** the ruined fortresses, conquered cities, and mountain strongholds of the Dark Prodigy's campaign; wherever its army is marching.
- **Encountered as:** at the head of a company of the Prodigy's troops (cultists, fiendish ogres, or lesser devils), or in a pair as the Prodigy's honor guard.
- **Signs:** banners bearing the Prodigy's sigil; formal letters of surrender left nailed to a town's gate; and survivors of a skirmish who can no longer cast spells reliably, swing a sword straight, or keep their nerve.

## Tactics
A lieutenant opens with its longbow from long range to spread curses among the party, aiming for casters first. It closes to melee against creatures that are already cursed, then parries the strongest melee attacker. It fights in formation with its troops and keeps them between itself and the party's heavy hitters. It won't retreat without orders, but it will offer terms to a party that is clearly losing: surrender, and serve the Prodigy.

**Running it:** keep a list of curses on each character, and roll them openly. The party should see the curses pile up and know that *remove curse* is the answer.

## Adventure Hooks
1. A lieutenant arrives at a city's gates under a flag of truce to deliver the Dark Prodigy's terms. The city council will pay the party to kill it on the way out. Killing an envoy under truce is exactly what the Prodigy needs to justify its invasion.
2. A paladin cursed with three of a lieutenant's curses asks the party to escort her to a temple. The curses are spreading through her company, and they aren't from the lieutenant anymore; they are from its sword, which she took as a trophy.
3. A lieutenant wants to defect from the Dark Prodigy's army and offers the party the Prodigy's battle plans in exchange for sanctuary. Its curses still answer to the Prodigy, and it doesn't know it's being used to find the party.

## Spoils
The **Dark Prodigy's longsword** is a +1 longsword that deals an extra 1d6 fire damage on a hit. It requires attunement by a creature of evil alignment; any other creature that attunes to it gains one of the lieutenant's curses, chosen by the DM, each dawn until it ends the attunement. The lieutenant carries the Prodigy's orders under seal, a map of the army's next targets, and 2d6 × 100 gp in the Prodigy's war coin.

## Design Notes
**Contributed by GrimmGrixis** (Realms / Writers Guild). The lore, ecology, tactics, hooks, and spoils were drafted by Sidebar Press and need the contributor's review. The source file names no campaign or session.

**The Dark Prodigy.** The submitted name refers to a specific master, likely a villain from the contributor's campaign. We kept "the Dark Prodigy" as an in-world title but made it generic: it is whatever fiendish warlord the DM chooses. If the contributor wants to publish the original villain, it can be added later as a Rogues' Gallery entry and linked here.

**Why CR 8, not 11.** The lieutenant shares the [[Lesser Erinyes]] chassis (the submitted block is identical, and its Parry still said "the lesser erinyes"). Like that block, its defense rates about CR 10, but its two attacks deal only 32 damage a round, the damage of a CR 4 creature. Its stacking curses are strong control, but they don't make up three CRs. We kept every attack and curse and moved it to CR 8, adjusting its proficiency-derived numbers.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *As submitted:* 120 hp × 1.25 (nonmagical, nonsilvered weapon resistance at expected CR 11–16) gives 150 effective hp, the CR 6 band (146–160). AC 18 is three above the expected 15, and Magic Resistance adds 2 more, so **defensive CR is about 8.5**. Two two-handed longsword hits deal 16 each (9 + 7), or 32 damage per round, the CR 4 band (27–32). +8 to hit is three above the expected +5, so **offensive CR is about 5.5**. *Average:* 7. That is four below the stated CR 11, so it is re-rated.
- *Retuned:* at expected CR 8, the resistance multiplier is ×1.5, so 120 × 1.5 = 180 effective hp, the CR 8 band (176–190). AC 18 plus 2 for Magic Resistance is four above the expected 16, so **defensive CR is 10**. Two longsword hits still deal 32 damage per round, the CR 4 band. +7 to hit is two above the expected +5, so **offensive CR is 5**. *Average:* 7.5. We round up to **CR 8** because the formula doesn't count the curses: they stack up to three, last until *remove curse*, and impose disadvantage on saves or make spells fail, which is lasting control.

**IP substitutions:** none from WotC. "The Dark Prodigy" (a campaign villain's name) became a generic in-world title, as described above.
