## Lore
Grakh is warlord of the Eleventh Gash, the eleventh war-band of the Gash-Marked to climb out of the deep (see [[Gash-Marked Raider]]). He is called Unscarred because no enemy has ever cut him. Every mark on his body he carved himself. The eleven tally-lines across his chest are not kills: each one is a settlement, a hold, or a valley that his band broke and counted, and that his [[Bone-Tally Chanter|chanters]] agreed was full. Below them runs a hand's width of clean skin that he oils every morning. It is reserved, he says, for a world he has not yet taken.

Unlike most of his kind, Grakh is patient and lawful. He keeps his word exactly, demands the same of his captives, and treats the count as sacred law rather than a war-cry. He prefers to send his [[Gash-Marked Houndmaster|houndmasters]] and their [[Tunnel Scarhound|scarhounds]] to empty a village quietly, and to fight only when the count demands it. When he does fight, he plants the iron standard of the Eleventh Gash at his feet, and the ground thunders.

His warriors believe that the first enemy to draw his blood will be marked by the deep. Some say that warrior will inherit the band. Grakh has never let anyone find out.

## Ecology
- **Environment:** the Eleventh Gash's camps, in root-caverns and tunnels below old forests, and the frontier villages it raids.
- **Encountered as:** at the heart of the full war-band: 6–10 raiders, 1–2 houndmasters with their scarhounds, and 1–2 bone-tally chanters.
- **Signs:** an iron standard topped with eleven notches; villages emptied without a body left behind; a written list of names, in Common, pinned to the door of the next village on the band's road.

## Tactics
Grakh fights from the front, but he lets his raiders engage first. When three or more enemies are bunched within 20 feet of him, he uses Plant the Standard. That knocks them prone and shields his own warriors with temporary hit points. He opens his strongest round with Carve the Tally: the self-inflicted wound also triggers Blood Tally, so his next hit lands for an extra 2d6 with advantage and an expanded critical range. Answer in Kind punishes anyone who trades blows with him. He spends his legendary actions on Hold the Line, so his raiders strike out of turn, and he uses Advance only to reach a fallen standard or a fleeing captive.

**Running it:** the first time a player character damages Grakh, have the whole war-band fall silent for a heartbeat. It tells the table that they've done something no one else has.

## Variants
**Ascendant Grakh (CR 7, 2,900 XP).** The full-strength version, for a table that is running hot. Make these changes:
- **Hit Points** 102 (12d8 + 48); or raise Constitution to 20 (+5) and use 114 (12d8 + 60).
- **Proficiency** +3: Saving Throws Str +7, Con +7 (+8 with Con 20); Skills Athletics +7, Intimidation +5, Perception +4; passive Perception 14.
- **Multiattack:** Grakh makes three Gash-Cleaver attacks.
- **Gash-Cleaver:** +7 to hit; *Hit:* 10 (1d12 + 4) slashing damage plus 3 (1d6) necrotic damage.
- **Plant the Standard:** DC 15 (16 with Con 20).
- **Challenge** 7 (2,900 XP).

Don't use Ascendant Grakh at the end of an attrition gauntlet, where the party has already fought the war-band in waves without a rest.

## Adventure Hooks
1. Grakh sends an envoy under a white rag: he will spare the town if it gives him one line, a single named person to add to his count. The person he names has already volunteered: a dying elder who wants the town spared. The party must decide whether to honor the bargain.
2. A rival warlord of the Ninth Gash offers the party guides and safe passage if they kill Grakh. If an enemy blade ever cuts him, his warriors believe the clean band of skin belongs to whoever drew the blood, and the whole Eleventh Gash may swear itself to the party.
3. The party learns which settlement Grakh's eleventh line stands for: a village they thought was simply abandoned. Its people live in his camp as counted captives, and some of them don't want to be rescued.

## Spoils
Grakh's battered plate is plate armor that gives AC 17 until a smith spends 100 gp and a week repairing it. His gash-cleaver functions as a greataxe. The iron standard of the Eleventh Gash is worth 250 gp to a collector, and far more to a rival war-band. Any Gash-Marked creature that sees an enemy carrying it has disadvantage on its first attack roll against that enemy. His war chest holds 3d6 × 10 gp, 2d6 × 10 sp, the band's tally-cords, and one roll on the DMG's Magic Item Table B.

**CR verification (DMG p. 274).**
- *Defensive:* 85 hp is the CR 1 band (71–85). AC 17 is four above the expected 13, which moves it two steps, so **defensive CR is 3**.
- *Offensive* (action, bonus action, and Blood Tally): Round 1 is Multiattack (20) plus Plant the Standard on two targets (28) plus Blood Tally (7), or 55. Rounds 2 and 3 are Multiattack plus Blood Tally, 27 each. The average is 36.3, the CR 5 band (33–38). +6 to hit matches the expected +6, and DC 14 is one below the expected 15, so **offensive CR is 5**.
- *Average:* (3 + 5) ÷ 2 = **CR 4**, matching the stated CR. It is kept. Answer in Kind adds up to 10 damage a round when Grakh is hit in melee; counting it raises his offense to about CR 7 and the average to about 5, still within 1. Run him as a hard CR 4.
- *Ascendant Grakh:* 114 hp (or 102) is the CR 3 band (101–115), and AC 17 is four above the expected 13, so defensive CR is 5. Round 1 is three Gash-Cleavers (39) plus Plant the Standard on two targets (28) plus Blood Tally (7), or 74; rounds 2 and 3 are 46 each. The average is 55.3, the CR 8 band (51–56), and +7 to hit matches the expected +7, so offensive CR is 8. The average is 6.5. We round up, because Answer in Kind (a fourth cleaver attack most rounds) and Hold the Line are uncounted, which gives **CR 7**. That is two above the submitted CR 5, so the variant is re-rated. CR 7 keeps proficiency +3, so the variant's bonuses are as submitted.

**IP substitutions:** Ravnica removed, and "the Closed Fist attrition column" (a campaign encounter reference) generalized. The Eleventh Gash, the Ninth Gash, and Gash-cant are the war-band's own names and are kept.
