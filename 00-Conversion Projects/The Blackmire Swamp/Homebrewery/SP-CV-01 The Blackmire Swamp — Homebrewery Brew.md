# Using This Adventure
*A complete regional adventure built around an escalating crocodile ecosystem and an ancient druidic curse.*

**The Blackmire Swamp: The Curse of the Druids** is a fifth-edition fantasy adventure for a party beginning at 1st level and reaching 5th level by milestone advancement. It combines wilderness survival, investigation, faction conflict, flooded ruins, and escalating predator encounters. The adventure is written for four to six characters, but encounter guidance is included for smaller or larger groups.

The defining principle of Blackmire is ecological escalation. Small predators test travelers. Crocodiles control the waterways. Alpha crocodiles control entire regions. Beneath all of it, an ancient druidic force is changing what the creatures can become. Every chapter advances that hierarchy until the characters face the Thorn-Heart Archdruid Crocodile in the Sunken Druid Grove.

{{note
##### Adventure Identity
Blackmire should feel observant before it feels hostile, hostile before it feels organized, and organized before it reveals itself as intelligent. The swamp does not suddenly become the antagonist in the final chapter; the characters gradually discover that it has been present in every ripple, retreat, ambush, and territorial boundary.
}}

## Content Considerations

The adventure contains predatory animal attacks, forced movement into water, drowning pressure, body transformation, undead soldiers, and a final scene in which a humanoid body dissolves into roots, mist, and swamp water. Discuss any boundaries that would make those themes more enjoyable for the group.

# Adventure at a Glance
*The campaign path, chapter milestones, central mystery, and the choices that shape Blackmire’s fate.*

## The Core Story

Travel through Blackmire has always been dangerous, but its dangers once followed familiar patterns. Old routes could be trusted. Crocodile territories could be marked. Guides knew which waterways belonged to which predators. That order is failing.

A supply barge disappears along a route that Old Marrow Finch has used for decades. Juvenile crocodiles test travelers without committing to a kill. Hunting crocodiles coordinate across channels that should belong to rival packs. Alpha crocodiles guard ruins they once ignored. Deep within the Black Core, the legendary Thorn-Heart is losing its ability to contain the collective memory of a vanished druidic order.

The characters begin by searching for missing travelers. Their investigation leads through Fort Drowned Spear, the Rooted Archive, the Crocodile Throne Pool, the Rootmaw Labyrinth, and the Sunken War Church. Along the way they learn that the Curse of the Druids is not a punishment placed upon outsiders. It is the cost of preserving Blackmire by binding generations of druidic memory into the swamp—and into one ancient guardian.

By the time the characters reach the Sunken Druid Grove, they must decide whether that memory should be preserved, severed, restored to its guardian, or allowed to awaken throughout the swamp.

- **Which faction gains influence.** The Iron Stakes, Circle of the Submerged Root, Mudrunner Guild, and Veiled Syndicate all provide useful assistance, but each wants a different future for Blackmire.

# The Truth of Blackmire
*This section contains the hidden history behind the curse, the Thorn-Heart, and the evolving crocodiles.*

## The Rite of Living Memory

Long before Grimwater Docks or Fort Drowned Spear existed, the Circle of the Submerged Root protected Blackmire as a sacred ecosystem. The Circle believed memory was a form of life. A body could die, but knowledge, instinct, grief, and devotion could be returned to the roots and waters that had sustained it.

When outside armies attempted to drain Blackmire and seize the mineral-rich earth beneath it, the Circle performed the **Rite of Living Memory**. Dying druids willingly surrendered fragments of themselves to the swamp. Their memories entered reeds, roots, water, and beasts. The rite allowed Blackmire to anticipate invaders, close routes, move predators, and preserve knowledge that would otherwise be lost.

The ritual worked too well. Thousands of memories entered the swamp faster than the living ecosystem could absorb them. Without a central mind to hold the fragments together, Blackmire would have become a storm of competing instincts.

## The Thorn-Heart Vessel

The Archdruid of Blackmire accepted the accumulated memories into one body. Through a permanent shapechange and a binding rite, the Archdruid became the Thorn-Heart: an ancient crocodile that could survive beneath the water, move through every territory, and contain the will of the Circle.

The Thorn-Heart was intended to be a temporary vessel. Three ritual anchors divided the burden: the **Root Anchor** bound the swamp’s physical growth, the **Tide Anchor** bound its predatory instinct and movement, and the **Memory Anchor** preserved identity. Together they prevented the Archdruid from being erased by the countless lives held within.

Centuries passed. Fort Drowned Spear was built over a lesser binding site, wars shattered the outer wards, and the Sunken War Church introduced corrupted divine magic into the network. The Circle dwindled. The Thorn-Heart remained.

## Why the Crocodiles Are Evolving

Every crocodile born in Blackmire is exposed to the druidic current. Most absorb only instinct: where to wait, when to strike, which route a traveler will choose. Older crocodiles absorb more. They become stronger, more territorial, and increasingly capable of coordination.

The crocodiles are not transforming according to ordinary age alone. They are becoming expressions of preserved druidic behavior. A Juvenile Crocodile tests. A Hunting Crocodile coordinates. An Alpha Crocodile controls territory. A Swamp Tyrant controls the boundary between land and water. The Thorn-Heart controls the ecosystem itself.

The change has accelerated because agents of the Veiled Syndicate removed a splinter from a damaged binding beneath Fort Drowned Spear. They believed it was residual magic from a failed spell. The extraction weakened the three-anchor network and caused preserved memories to leak into the crocodile population at a much faster rate.

## The Curse of the Druids

The titular curse is the price of the Circle’s preservation. The druids succeeded in making Blackmire remember, but the memory cannot die, rest, or remain contained forever. The Thorn-Heart is both guardian and prisoner. Its humanoid identity survives only in fragments, while its crocodilian instincts grow stronger with each generation of memory it receives.

The curse creates three simultaneous truths:

**The Thorn-Heart protects Blackmire.** It attacks those who would control, drain, harvest, or expose the heart of the swamp.

**The Thorn-Heart endangers Blackmire.** As its identity erodes, predatory instinct increasingly determines how the shared memory responds.

**The Thorn-Heart wants release.** The remaining Archdruid understands that the vessel is failing. If the crocodile body is destroyed while the anchors remain bound, the Archdruid will use Wish to transfer the burden into the entire swamp.

## The Meaning of the Final Wish

The Wish does not create the network. The network has always existed. The spell removes the final separation between the Thorn-Heart and the rest of Blackmire.

\page

If the Wish succeeds in its default form, every crocodile, root system, and deep waterway receives a portion of the stored will. The Archdruid dissolves because there is no longer a single body carrying the curse. Crocodilian cries answer one another across the swamp because they are no longer isolated minds. The final note—“It was never just one.”—reveals that the Thorn-Heart was only the most visible expression of a much larger system.

This is why the Archdruid’s final line is not a threat or a victory boast. It is a statement of certainty: **“The swamp remembers.”**

# Running Blackmire
*Travel, visibility, water combat, Drowning Pressure, faction reputation, and the crocodile escalation system.*

## The Swamp as an Active Presence

Blackmire should rarely feel empty. When no creature is visible, use ripples, moving reeds, distant warning bells, bubbles beneath a log, or a sudden silence among insects. These signs do not always lead to combat. Their purpose is to make the characters consider where they stand, what touches the water, and who might be watching.

Do not treat every crocodile as suicidal. Juveniles retreat when injured. Hunting Crocodiles withdraw if they lose their advantage. Alphas defend a boundary rather than chasing prey across the entire region. This behavior makes later coordination more alarming because the characters learn what “normal” Blackmire predation looks like first.

## Regional Travel Procedure

A **travel segment** represents roughly four hours of movement through one region. Resolve each segment in four steps:

- **1. Choose the route.** Safe routes take longer, direct routes increase encounter risk, and hidden routes usually require a Mudrunner or local guide.
- **2. Choose a navigator.** The navigator makes a Wisdom (Survival) check, an Intelligence check using navigator’s tools, or another check justified by the route.
- **3. Resolve the result.** On a success, the party reaches the next point and chooses whether to resolve a beneficial discovery or faction opportunity. On a failure, roll or choose a regional complication. On a failure by 5 or more, also trigger a predator encounter or serious hazard.
- **4. Mark Pressure.** Each failed segment adds 1 Pressure. At 3 Pressure, the swamp initiates an encounter at the next vulnerable moment, then Pressure returns to 0.

## Resting in Blackmire

A short rest is possible anywhere the characters can remain out of immediate danger. A long rest outside a settlement requires a secure platform, elevated ruin, defensible barge, or prepared camp. One character makes the region’s Long-Rest Camp check using Wisdom (Survival), carpenter’s tools, or water vehicles.

On a success, the rest proceeds normally. On a failure, the group still completes the rest, but the DM chooses one cost: a resource is spoiled, the next travel segment begins with 1 Pressure, one character gains no benefit from spent Hit Dice during the rest, or the camp is discovered immediately after the rest ends. Avoid repeatedly denying long rests; use the procedure to create consequences and urgency.

## Water, Mud, and Visibility

\page
{{pageNumber,auto}}
{{footnote RUNNING BLACKMIRE}}

- **Shallow water (1–3 feet).** Difficult terrain for creatures without a swim speed unless a location states otherwise.
- **Deep water.** A creature without a swim speed follows the normal rules for swimming. Heavy equipment and restrained conditions may make Athletics checks necessary at the DM’s discretion.
- **Mud.** Difficult terrain. A creature knocked prone in deep mud must spend 5 additional feet of movement to stand.
- **Murky water.** Underwater space is lightly obscured beyond 5 feet and heavily obscured beyond 15 feet in the Reedmarch or Broken Fen. In the Black Core, those distances become 5 feet and 10 feet.
- **Low fog.** Creatures standing above water see normally for 30 feet; space beyond that is lightly obscured unless a location gives a different range.

## Drowning Pressure

Blackmire crocodiles are frightening because they separate characters and control their breathing space. The following procedure supplements the normal rules for grappling, swimming, and suffocation without making a single bite cause immediate drowning.

When a crocodile begins its turn in water while grappling a creature, it can drag that creature as it moves. Unless the crocodile has a feature stating otherwise, it moves at half speed while dragging a creature of its size or smaller.

A grappled creature pulled completely beneath the surface begins holding its breath. It is not automatically restrained. It can use its action to escape the grapple, and an adjacent ally can use the Help action or attempt to break the grapple with a Strength (Athletics) check against the escape DC.

Whenever a submerged, grappled creature takes damage from **Death Roll**, it must succeed on a DC 10 Constitution saving throw or lose 1 minute of remaining breath. A creature with less than 1 minute of breath remaining instead loses one remaining round. This makes repeated death rolls dangerous while preserving the normal time scale of suffocation.

A creature can deliberately release held breath to speak a verbal component, but doing so reduces its remaining breath to a number of rounds equal to its Constitution modifier, minimum 1 round. This choice should be clear to the player before the spell is cast. If depleted of oxygen, spellcasters are unable to cast spells that require a verbal component.

## Unstable Logs and Narrow Platforms

A creature that moves more than half its speed across an unstable log, is shoved while standing on one, or takes 10 or more damage from one source while on it must succeed on a DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check or fall prone. If the log is over water, the creature falls into the water instead. A creature can move at half speed to avoid the check.

# Blackmire Gazetteer

## The Swamp That Remembers

Blackmire is a vast inland swamp where land, water, and magic blur. Crocodiles are not simply beasts here; they are territorial expressions of an older druidic will that still lingers beneath reality. Travel is dangerous, trade is profitable, and nothing stays buried forever.

The four regions form rough concentric zones, but their boundaries are ecological rather than political. A flooded season can push Deepwater predators into the Broken Fen. A damaged anchor can turn a familiar Reedmarch route into hunting water overnight.

## The Reedmarch

The outer wetlands contain reed farms, fish traps, shallow channels, and the most reliable raised roads. Civilization believes it controls the Reedmarch, but the region is only safe because its predators have historically treated travelers as unimportant. Chapter 1 shows that this assumption is failing.

## The Broken Fen

The Broken Fen is a field of unstable ruins, collapsed forts, half-drowned roads, bandit camps, and channels that change after every storm. Hunting Crocodiles use the debris to divide travelers. Fort Drowned Spear stands here as both a warning and the first physical evidence that the druidic bindings are damaged.

## The Deepwater Reach

Deepwater Reach consists of long channels, broad basins, and floating settlements that must be re-tethered whenever crocodile territories shift. Alpha Crocodiles control entire routes. The Drift survives by moving before the predators decide it has become part of their territory.

## The Black Core

The Black Core is the forbidden druidic heart of the swamp. Water and roots respond to emotion and magic. Corrupted creatures, drowned spirits, and ancient guardians occupy sites connected to the three ritual anchors. The Sunken Druid Grove lies at the center.

## Grimwater Docks

\page
{{pageNumber,auto}}
{{footnote BLACKMIRE GAZETTEER}}

![](https://YOUR-IMAGE-HOST/blackmire/col-grimwater.jpg){width:100%}

Grimwater Docks is a half-built settlement raised on stilts and reclaimed stone. Mosquito nets hang over streets like low ceilings. Cargo moves from flat-bottom barges to warehouses that lean toward the water. The settlement serves as the principal trade hub for swamp goods, antidotes, smoked fish, resin, medicinal moss, and legal or illegal crocodile products.

**Key locations:** the bounty board, Old Marrow’s weathered guide shack, the Lantern Net market, the Reedwake landing, and a fortified Iron Stakes watch platform.

**Immediate tension:** merchants want routes reopened quickly, the Iron Stakes want visible kills, Mudrunners want the authorities kept away from missing cargo, and Sister Vell wants the disturbance investigated before more creatures are slaughtered.

## The Drift

The Drift is a city made from connected barges, rafts, houseboats, and scavenged platforms. It is chaotic, lively, and always one bad current away from disaster. Crews re-tether entire neighborhoods to avoid Alpha Crocodile territory.

The Drowned Cup Tavern is the best place to hear stories of drowned explorers, buy fermented reed wine, meet smugglers, hire a guide, or become involved in a crocodile-tooth gambling ring. Illegal spellcasting stalls operate beneath patched awnings, and the Veiled Syndicate maintains at least one concealed laboratory among them.

## Mossfall Outpost

![](https://YOUR-IMAGE-HOST/blackmire/col-mossfall.jpg){width:100%}

Mossfall Outpost is a fortified checkpoint at the edge of Blackmire. Its wooden wall stands on stilts and is reinforced with iron stakes driven into the mud. Warning bells connect to submerged trip lines. The outpost tracks monster movement, stages expeditions, and provides the closest thing Blackmire has to an official border.

The Iron Stakes consider Mossfall proof that the swamp can be pinned down. The Circle considers it proof that outsiders mistake temporary resistance for control.

## Food, Drink, and Culture

- **Fermented reed wine:** dry, grassy, and stronger than it tastes.
- **Smoked crocodile tail jerky:** common in Iron Stakes territory and offensive to many members of the Circle.
- **Mossbread:** dense, bitter, and reliable during wet travel.
- **Mud ale:** dark, mineral-heavy, and surprisingly popular.
- **Boiled eel stew:** served in communal bowls aboard the Drift.
- **Spicy swamp pepper soup:** a favored remedy for cold rain and insect bites.
- **Crocodile egg omelets:** rare, expensive, and illegal in some zones because taking eggs destabilizes territories.

## Chapter Summary

The characters arrive at Grimwater Docks as the supply barge **Reedwake** is declared missing. Captain Rusk offers an extermination bounty, Old Marrow asks them to discover what changed along his route, Sister Vell warns that the crocodiles are listening, and Silt quietly asks for a sealed package aboard the barge.

The search teaches the party how Blackmire travel works. At the wreck, Juvenile Crocodiles test isolated characters while survivors struggle beneath collapsed cargo. The characters recover a crocodile tooth marked with a druidic root-sigil and evidence that the creatures deliberately drove the barge into shallow water.

The party reaches 2nd level after returning with the survivors, the marked tooth, or a credible account of the attack.

## Fort Drowned Spear

The fort is a compact flooded dungeon. Use the following five areas in order or allow the party to move between them as the tilted structure permits.

### 1. Tilted Gatehouse

The gatehouse floor slopes into chest-deep water. Cut bell lines drift near the ceiling. A character who succeeds on a DC 12 Wisdom (Perception) check notices that the cuts are clean and recent; the crocodiles did not remove them.

A hidden Mudrunner symbol marks a dry crawlspace. Following it avoids the first flooded corridor but reveals evidence that someone transported heavy equipment through the fort.

### 2. Drowned Barracks

Two drowned soldiers remain tangled in their bunks. They rise if the party removes military records or disturbs the bronze command seal. Use two **Drowned Sentries** from Appendix A, or omit the combat and present the corpses as a warning if the group has already taken significant damage.

The military records show that Fort Drowned Spear was built over “root-stones that resist excavation.” The final entry reports that crocodiles gathered in silence whenever workers struck the stones.

### 3. Rusted Armory

The armory contains one reinforced net, twenty feet of chain, two usable spears, and a sealed iron tube containing an old map of the fort. A DC 12 Intelligence (Investigation) check reveals scratch marks showing that something large entered through the flooded wall but did not disturb the weapon racks.

### 4. Flooded Courtyard

\page
{{pageNumber,auto}}
{{footnote THE BROKEN FEN HUNTS}}

{{descriptive

}}

Two Hunting Crocodiles occupy the courtyard for a group of four characters. Use three for six characters, or one Hunting Crocodile and two Juveniles for a less dangerous encounter. The crocodiles coordinate: one attempts to grapple, while the other occupies the nearest exit or attacks a character helping the victim.

- Characters on the central platform have high ground but limited space.
- A hanging chain can be cut to drop a section of walkway, creating a temporary bridge or dealing 2d6 bludgeoning damage to a creature beneath it with a DC 12 Dexterity save for half.
- A crocodile that begins its turn grappling a creature in water uses Drowning Pressure and drags toward the anchor vault passage.
- If both crocodiles fall below half hit points, one retreats toward Deepwater Reach unless the party blocks the submerged exit.

### 5. Anchor Vault

{{descriptive

The chamber contains a damaged lesser binding connected to the three ritual anchors. The missing wedge is the shard that passed through the Reedwake. The extraction frame bears the symbol of the Veiled Syndicate. Characters examining the site can learn the following:

- A DC 12 Intelligence (Arcana or Nature) check reveals that the stone did not store magic; it directed magic through the roots.
- A DC 13 Wisdom (Survival) check shows crocodile tracks entering the chamber only after the extraction.
- The tuning fork from Sister Vell produces three distinct tones, one for each distant anchor.
- The arcane lens from Aster Vale reveals pulses moving east toward Deepwater Reach.
- A field note admits that removing “Specimen One” caused simultaneous movement in six separate waterways.

## The Anchor Splinter

If the party brought the shard from the Reedwake, placing it into the split stone briefly stabilizes the roots and shows a vision: a humanoid hand pressing into black water, then becoming a crocodile’s claw. If the shard is absent, the party can trace its residue toward the Drift and the Rooted Archive.

## Concluding the Chapter

The evidence establishes that the crocodile changes began immediately after the binding was disturbed. The fort itself cannot explain the ritual, but an old military map identifies the **Rooted Archive** as the source of the original root-stone records.

## The Rooted Archive

![](https://YOUR-IMAGE-HOST/blackmire/col-archive.jpg){width:100%}

The archive is a half-sunken library enclosed by enormous roots. Resin has preserved doors, tablets, and scrolls that would otherwise have dissolved centuries ago. The site contains six areas.

### 1. Root Bridge

A narrow living root spans a deep channel. Characters moving at half speed cross safely. A character moving faster must succeed on a DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check or fall into the water. A submerged scrape on the root shows where an enormous crocodile regularly passes beneath.

### 2. Resin Vestibule

Amber resin seals a circular door. Heat softens it, cold makes it brittle, and druidic magic causes it to withdraw. Forcing the door requires a DC 16 Strength check and alerts the Alpha.

An inscription reads: **“Memory enters by consent. Power taken without witness becomes hunger.”** Speaking the Circle’s phrase opens the door quietly.

### 3. Hall of Remembered Names

Thousands of names appear beneath the resin floor. When a character steps on a name, a brief memory rises: rain on leaves, a funeral song, the sight of soldiers cutting reeds, the feeling of teeth closing around a spear.

\page
{{pageNumber,auto}}
{{footnote THE DEEPWATER REACH}}

Each character can choose to receive one memory. Doing so grants advantage on one Intelligence (History), Intelligence (Nature), or Wisdom (Survival) check made in the archive, but the character dreams of Blackmire during the next long rest.

### 4. The Scriptorium

The Scriptorium contains preserved records. A careful search reveals the following account:

{{descriptive
*We gave the mire our names so it would know whom it defended. We gave it our fear so it would know what approached. We gave the vessel our memories so the waters would not drown beneath them. Root holds the body. Tide holds the instinct. Memory holds the self. Should the vessel break while all three remain bound, the burden will seek every living channel.*
}}

This is the first clear warning that killing the Thorn-Heart without addressing the anchors could release the curse.

### 5. Memory Well

A still pool fills the central chamber. A character who touches the water experiences the Rite of Living Memory from the Archdruid’s perspective: hundreds of hands releasing light into the swamp, one figure kneeling, bones becoming scale, and three distant obelisks igniting.

A character who succeeds on a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw remains an observer and can ask the well one question about the rite. On a failure, the character is overwhelmed until the end of their next turn and speaks one sentence in a dead druid’s voice. Either result reveals the locations of the Root, Tide, and Memory Anchors.

### 6. The Root Vault

{{descriptive
*The final vault opens onto a roofless chamber of black water and hanging roots. A stone map rises from the center. Before you reach it, two yellow eyes open beside the wall. What looked like a fallen beam lifts, revealing the armored back of a crocodile large enough to divide the chamber in half.*
}}

One Alpha Crocodile guards the vault. It does not attack immediately if the party entered quietly. Its behavior depends on the party’s actions:

- **Witnesses.** If a character speaks “We enter as witnesses, not owners” and no one has damaged the archive, the Alpha allows one character to approach the map. Removing more than one object causes it to attack.
- **Intruders.** If the door was forced, the archive was burned, or an extraction lens is active, the Alpha attacks from beneath the roots with advantage on its first bite.
- **Challengers.** If a character displays the sigiled crocodile tooth and succeeds on a DC 14 Wisdom (Animal Handling or Survival) check, the Alpha accepts a territorial challenge. It focuses that character until reduced below half hit points, then withdraws if allowed.
- **Predators.** If combat begins, the Alpha uses the water and hanging roots to separate the party. It does not pursue beyond the Root Bridge unless the characters steal the entire stone map.

*Three ritual anchors offer three ways to weaken the curse—and three different futures for Blackmire.*

## Chapter Summary

The party enters the Black Core and visits the Root Anchor, Tide Anchor, and Memory Anchor in any order. Each site can be purified, severed, extracted, or left unchanged. The choices alter the final boss and determine which endings remain possible.

The characters need to resolve at least two anchor sites to reveal a stable route to the Sunken Druid Grove. Resolving all three provides the strongest control over the final encounter. # Chapter Five: The Heart of Blackmire

## Encounter Summary

The final encounter takes place in the Sunken Druid Grove, a circular basin of deep water, unstable logs, dead trees, and a half-submerged stone circle. The Thorn-Heart begins as a submerged ambush predator, becomes an open terrain controller when injured, and enters a fully awakened final phase below one-third of its hit points.

The battle is not intended to be a simple damage race. The party’s anchor choices determine which parts of the swamp can still aid the boss. Position, rescue, forced movement, and control of the central stone circle matter more than standing still and exchanging attacks.

## Phase 1: The Swamp Is Watching

The Thorn-Heart begins hidden beneath the water. Its first attack is a Bite against an isolated target, followed by movement toward a deep channel. It uses Vanish Beneath Water to change position rather than remaining beside the entire party.

At initiative count 20, use **Undertow Pulse** if the Tide Anchor remains bound: each creature in water must succeed on a DC 15 Strength saving throw or move 10 feet toward the nearest deep channel and fall prone. If the Tide Anchor was resolved, use **Veiling Fog** instead: a 15-foot-radius area becomes heavily obscured until initiative count 20 on the next round.

The boss retreats after attacking and seeks another angle. This phase teaches the party that grouping too tightly makes the circle hard to occupy, while spreading too far invites isolation.

## Phase 2: The Swamp Defends Its God

{{descriptive
*When the Thorn-Heart’s blood reaches the water, roots erupt from beneath its scales. Green-gold marks ignite across its armored back. The basin answers with a single violent pulse, and the crocodile rises onto the stone circle as though the swamp itself has lifted it.*

The Thorn-Heart stops hiding and contests the central circle. If the Root Anchor remains bound, it gains Swamp Grasp. If the Memory Anchor remains bound, it regenerates 10 hit points at the start of its turn while touching water or mud, provided it has at least 1 hit point.

At initiative count 20, choose one lair action and do not repeat the same action on consecutive rounds: Rising Mire, Root Spear, Undertow Pulse, Whisper of the Druid, or Veiling Fog. Ignore any action disabled by an anchor choice.

## Phase 3: The Heart of the Swamp

{{descriptive
*The crocodile’s outline breaks apart. Vines replace muscle. Water runs through gaps where bone should be. Stone roots push from beneath its scales, and the old druidic light inside the creature becomes visible through every wound. It is no longer an animal standing in the swamp. It is the swamp trying to stand.*
}}

The Thorn-Heart gains one use of Legendary Resistance. If the Memory Anchor remains bound, regeneration increases to 15 hit points. The boss also gains **Vinebound Assault**: the first creature hit by one of its melee attacks on each of its turns must succeed on a DC 16 Strength saving throw or have speed 0 until the start of the Thorn-Heart’s next turn.

At initiative count 20, resolve one available lair action, then target one creature touching water with spectral roots. That creature must succeed on a DC 15 Strength saving throw or be restrained until initiative count 20 on the next round. This additional restraint does not occur if the Root Anchor was resolved.

The Thorn-Heart no longer retreats. It prioritizes characters who can free allies, control movement, or attack from outside the water.

## Running the Boss Fairly

- Telegraph forced movement. Describe the current tightening, roots turning, or a log beginning to roll before the saving throw.
- Do not repeatedly target the same character unless that character is deliberately holding the boss’s attention.
- A grappled character should have multiple rescue options: escape, ally assistance, forced movement, control magic, or an anchor boon.
- The Thorn-Heart is intelligent enough to recognize tactics but does not know every class feature or magic item.
- If the party resolved no anchors, remind them that the echoes around the basin can still be attacked or disrupted during combat. Each echo has AC 15, 20 hit points, and immunity to poison and psychic damage; destroying one counts as severing it.

When the Thorn-Heart reaches 0 hit points, it does not die normally. Initiative pauses unless an ending specifically calls for immediate action. The crocodile body becomes the broken shell of the humanoid Archdruid within.

{{descriptive
*The ancient crocodile shudders violently. Vines split apart from its scales as its immense body collapses into the black water. Silence falls over Blackmire. From the broken shell rises a humanoid figure wrapped in moss and roots. Water streams upward instead of down. A crown of antler-like driftwood rests above eyes burning with emerald light.*
}}

The Archdruid does not immediately attack. Without acknowledging the party, the figure raises one hand toward the heart of the swamp and begins an unknown casting.

A character who can see or hear the casting may make a DC 17 Intelligence (Arcana) check. A character who studied the Memory Well or purified the Memory Anchor succeeds automatically. On a success, the character recognizes **Wish**, but not a controlled or conventional form. It is primal, spoken rather than shaped, and directed into Blackmire itself.

The ending now depends on the anchor choices and the party’s actions.

## Ending One: The Swamp Remembers

This is the default ending and preserves the original conclusion. It occurs if fewer than two anchors share another outcome, or if the party allows the casting to proceed.

{{descriptive
*The Archdruid speaks one word in a language older than kingdoms. The water stops moving. Even the wind hesitates. The figure turns translucent like wet moss as roots peel away from bone. Then a primal scream crosses the basin—not pain, but rage directed at something far older than you. Across Blackmire, crocodilian cries erupt at once. They are not ordinary calls. They answer one another beneath the water. The druid drops a small waterlogged bag into the mud, laughs with the certainty of something accepting an inevitable truth, and dissolves into mist and roots. “The swamp remembers.”*
}}

The Wish transfers the druidic memory into the entire swamp. Crocodiles become parts of a networked will. Travel grows more difficult, and the campaign continues into an awakened Blackmire.

## Ending Two: The Last Root Is Severed

This ending occurs if at least two anchors were severed. The Archdruid attempts the Wish, but the broken network cannot carry it.

{{descriptive
*The word leaves the Archdruid’s mouth, but the swamp cannot answer as one. Light races toward the severed anchors and vanishes into the wounds you left behind. The figure exhales, not in anger, but in release. Crocodile cries rise across Blackmire as separate voices—confused, fading, mortal. The Archdruid places the waterlogged bag at your feet. “You have made it forget,” the figure says, and then becomes ordinary water.*
}}

The coordinated evolution slows and eventually stops. Sections of Blackmire suffer ecological collapse according to which anchors were severed. The Iron Stakes gain influence, while the Circle considers the result a spiritual catastrophe.

## Ending Three: The Guardian Restored

This ending requires at least two purified anchors and the Rite of Clear Water from Sister Vell or the Memory Well. As the Archdruid begins Wish, the party can perform the rite with 3 successes before 2 failures using DC 15 Arcana, Nature, Religion, Performance, or Persuasion checks.

{{descriptive
*Your voices enter the casting before the final word is complete. The purified anchors answer one by one. Root holds the body. Tide releases the instinct. Memory returns the self. The Archdruid’s shape steadies. For the first time, the eyes looking at you belong to one person rather than a thousand. The figure lowers one hand and touches the water gently. The Archdruid survives as a diminished guardian or passes the role to Sister Vell, according to the party’s relationship with the Circle. Crocodile evolution stabilizes, though the creatures retain unusual intelligence.

## Ending Four: The Swamp Unbound

This ending occurs if at least two anchors were extracted, or if the party deliberately channels the Wish through Veiled Syndicate equipment.

{{descriptive

}}

The Syndicate gains portable fragments of the network, but Blackmire begins sending memory and predatory instinct through any water touched by those fragments. This ending creates the most immediate external campaign threat.

## The Dropped Bag

Unless the Archdruid survives physically, the waterlogged bag remains. It contains the following objects:

- A druidic focus carved from living wood that still pulses faintly.
- A map fragment showing unknown deeper zones beneath the Blackmire Descent.
- A crocodile tooth marked with arcane sigils.
- A sealed vial of Blackmire essence.
- A folded note bearing only the words: **“It was never just one.”**

# Aftermath and Further Adventures
*Blackmire does not return to normal. The ending determines what changes, who gains power, and what follows the characters beyond the swamp.*

## Immediate Aftermath

When the final scene ends, the basin remains quiet long enough for the characters to recover the dropped bag, search the stone circle, and decide whether to leave immediately. The Sunken Druid Grove no longer produces lair actions, but its water reflects the condition of the anchors.

If the party purified an anchor, its reflected light is steady and clear. A severed anchor appears as a dark break in the reflection. An extracted anchor appears as a shard-shaped absence around which the water bends. A bound anchor continues to pulse green-gold beneath the surface.

The route back through the Black Core is safer than the approach for one travel segment. After that, the consequences of the ending begin to shape the region.

## Regional Consequences by Ending

\page

{{footnote AFTERMATH AND FURTHER ADVENTURES}}

### The Swamp Remembers

- Crocodiles pause before attacking as though receiving distant instruction.
- Navigation checks in the Black Core increase by 1 until the characters learn how the network communicates.
- The note and map fragment point toward deeper layers beneath the Blackmire Descent.
- The Circle cannot agree whether the distributed Archdruid is liberated or lost.

### The Last Root Is Severed

- Organized crocodile activity declines over several weeks, although ordinary predators remain dangerous.
- One ecological cost manifests for each severed anchor: aggressive plant die-off for Root, chaotic flooding for Tide, and loss of ancestral knowledge for Memory.
- The Iron Stakes expand patrols and begin surveying channels for drainage.
- Surviving Circle druids may ask the party to repair what can still be saved without restoring the curse.

### The Guardian Restored

- The restored Archdruid or Sister Vell establishes marked passages that crocodiles will not cross without provocation.
- Predator evolution stabilizes; unusual intelligence remains, but it no longer accelerates each generation.
- The Circle becomes more visible and must negotiate with settlements that once considered it extinct.
- The guardian asks the party to investigate whatever the final scream was directed toward.

### The Swamp Unbound

- Extracted fragments carry Blackmire’s awareness into wells, ships, laboratories, and distant waterways.
- The Veiled Syndicate fractures between those who want to destroy the shards and those who believe control is still possible.
- Predators outside Blackmire begin demonstrating remembered behaviors.
- The Blackmire Descent opens as a region-scale current draws water downward into unknown chambers.

## Rewards and Recognition

Each surviving character earns the story reward **Marked by Blackmire**. Creatures shaped by the swamp recognize the character’s scent and memory. The mark is not automatically beneficial; it explains why Blackmire notices the party first in future adventures.

In addition, the party receives any promised bounty, faction reward, and treasure recovered during the adventure. If the Thorn-Heart was defeated, Captain Rusk pays the full public bounty even when he disapproves of the ending, provided the characters can present credible evidence. Sister Vell instead offers knowledge, safe passage, and a Blackmire magic item from Appendix C.

# Appendix A: The Crocodile Evolution Bestiary
*Every generation of Blackmire crocodiles grows stronger through exposure to ancient druidic magic. Hunters call it age. Druids know it is evolution.*

## Using the Evolution Line

The creatures in this appendix are not separate species. They represent known stages of adaptation within Blackmire’s crocodile population. A creature’s stage reflects age, territory, and the amount of druidic memory it has absorbed.

The progression is deliberately behavioral as well as numerical. Juveniles test and retreat. Hunting Crocodiles coordinate. Alphas control waterways. Swamp Tyrants dominate the boundary between land and water. The Thorn-Heart controls the ecosystem itself.

Challenge ratings assume the creatures use their terrain and signature control features. An encounter on dry, open ground is easier than the same encounter in murky water, flooded ruins, or narrow channels.

# Appendix B: Hazards and Encounter Tables
*Use these tables to make travel feel active without turning every segment into an unrelated combat.*

## Using Random Events

Roll only when an event would improve the pace. A failed travel segment, 3 Pressure, a noisy camp, or an exposed crossing are natural triggers. A result can be presented as a full encounter, a clue, a resource cost, or a choice between speed and safety.

Not every creature result begins with initiative. Blackmire predators assess risk, factions watch before approaching, and many hazards can be recognized before anyone enters them. Whenever possible, show the sign of danger first.

## Environmental Hazards

### Quickmire

A quickmire patch is usually a 10- or 15-foot area disguised as firm vegetation. A creature entering it notices the danger with a passive Perception of 13 or a successful DC 12 Wisdom (Survival) check.

A creature that enters the patch must succeed on a DC 13 Strength saving throw or become restrained. A restrained creature sinks 1 foot at the end of each of its turns. It can use an action to make a DC 13 Strength (Athletics) check, escaping on a success. An adjacent creature on stable ground can pull it free with the same check. A rope, pole, or solid platform grants advantage.

### Biting Insect Cloud

The cloud fills a 20-foot-radius area for 1d4 × 10 minutes. A creature entering or starting its turn in the cloud must succeed on a DC 11 Constitution saving throw or have disadvantage on its next ability check before the end of its next turn. Smoke, strong wind, or 5 points of fire damage disperses the cloud.

### Toxic Bloom

\page
{{pageNumber,auto}}
{{footnote HAZARDS AND ENCOUNTER TABLES}}

A giant mushroom or floating flower releases spores when disturbed. Creatures within 10 feet make a DC 12 Constitution saving throw. On a failure, a creature is poisoned for 10 minutes. It can repeat the save at the end of each minute. A character who recognizes the bloom with a DC 12 Nature check can harvest one dose of poison or antidote material.

### Collapsing Ruin

When a flooded structure shifts, every creature on or beneath the affected section makes a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw. On a failure, a creature takes 7 (2d6) bludgeoning damage and is knocked prone or pushed into adjacent water. On a success, it takes half damage and chooses a safe adjacent space.

### Warning-Line Bells

Submerged cords ring bells at an outpost when disturbed. A creature can spot a line with a DC 13 Perception check and disable or bypass it with a DC 12 Dexterity check using thieves’ tools, water vehicles, or an appropriate cutting tool. Triggering the line does not always summon allies; abandoned bells may attract crocodiles.

### Druidic Current

A visible green-gold current runs through water for 1 minute. A creature entering it must make a DC 13 Wisdom saving throw. On a success, it receives one clear memory connected to the current’s destination and has advantage on its next navigation check. On a failure, it receives overlapping memories, takes 5 (2d4) psychic damage, and cannot take reactions until the start of its next turn.

## Encounter Scaling

| **Party Condition** | **Adjustment** |
|:--|:--|
| Three characters or depleted resources | Remove one minor creature, reduce a hazard DC by 2, or allow the warning signs to reveal a safe alternative. |
| Four characters | Use listed encounters and DCs. |
| Five or six characters | Add one minor creature, place a second objective in the encounter, or increase terrain pressure rather than simply increasing hit points. |
| Highly optimized party | Let predators coordinate, use cover and retreat routes, and threaten objectives or positions. Avoid increasing every saving throw DC. |
| New players | State the consequences of water entry, grapples, and anchor choices before they commit. Blackmire should be tense, not opaque. |

## Adventure Close

Blackmire can be survived, changed, wounded, restored, or awakened. It cannot be reduced to a single monster or one final roll.

However the characters leave the swamp, the people of Grimwater Docks will tell the story differently. The Iron Stakes will count the predators killed. The Mudrunners will mark the routes that opened. The Veiled Syndicate will measure the residue that remains. The Circle of the Submerged Root will listen to the water.

And somewhere beneath them all, Blackmire will remember.
