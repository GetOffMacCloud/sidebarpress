# Part 1A: The Ruins of the Moathouse — Dungeon Level

> Beneath the ruins, Lareth the Beautiful is gathering men and humanoids to restore the Temple. Undead and an ogre guard the west; recruits squabble in the south; his black-clad guards hold the north wing. **Map 8.** Party level **3–4**.

## Residents and Factions

| Group | Count | Where | Wants | Relation to others |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lareth and his lieutenant** | 2 | 35, 34 | Rebuild a force; loot far afield | Commands everyone below |
| **The guardsmen** | 18 + 3 sergeants | 33, 34 | Obey; hold the north | Alert and fearless; the hoot and the howl bring help |
| **Lubash** the ogre | 1 | 24 | Fresh meat, baubles | Serves those who show the sign |
| **The bugbears** | 6 | 27 | Favor with the New Master | Newly recruited; resented by the gnolls |
| **The gnolls** | 9 | 29 | Better pay, better loot | Unhappy; open to a bribe |
| **The ghouls** | 4 | 31 | Corpses, and live "sport" | Fed by the Master from area 22 |
| **The zombies** | 12 | 21 | — | Placed as guards |
| **The crayfish** | 1 | 30 | Food | Held at bay by the guards' polearms |

## The Alarm

> [!warning] How the north wing reacts
> 1. **Hoot.** A guard attacked at any X (area 33) sends up a low hooting sound while engaging. This brings the other guards of 33 and their sergeant.
> 2. **Reinforcements.** The hoot also brings six spearmen and their sergeant from area 34. They take **1d2 + 3 rounds** (4–5) to arrive: time to ready themselves, one round to reach area 33, and another to reach the north passage if needed there.
> 3. **Howl.** If the guards are losing the fray, they start howling. This brings **every guard in the area, the lieutenant and Lareth**, on the same timing.
>
> Once the howl goes up, the north wing is Deadly at any level this adventure is played at. The right play is to split the guards up or avoid them. Gygax built it that way, and it stays that way.

## Dungeon Level Encounter Key

### 18. STAIRWAY ARCH

Two nauseous blobs of green slime have been carefully placed and fed in a position over the central arch at the base of the stairway. The first creature to step off the stairs onto the dungeon floor must make a **DC 15 Dexterity save** or one of the two slimes dislodges itself and falls squarely upon it. Any cautious approach (either looking up or moving along the walls) reveals the slime (passive Perception 13) and avoids this, but then anyone passing through the northern portion of the 10-foot square will run the same risk of being attacked.

The room contains no treasure. A few torn sacks, broken barrels, and shattered weapons racks show that this large area was once a storage place and armory for the fortress. Great heaps of worthless rubble and broken containers and furniture are at the western end of the room, all obviously junk (but concealing the doors to rooms 19 and 20).

- **Green slime (2 patches):** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Green Slime]].

### 19. DOOR

The lock of this portal is secured, obviously new, and well-oiled (thieves' tools, DC 15). If not picked, the door can be chopped or smashed down by axe or hammer (AC 15, 50 hp). Inside the room are 30 shields, 12 suits of leather armor, and several barrels. Most of the latter contain salted meat, but two in the rear are 5-gallon kegs of an average brandy, worth 25 gp each.

### 20. DOOR

This portal is also locked, as is area 19. Behind it are 50 spears, 16 glaives (ten glaives and six guisarmes in the 1985 text), three battleaxes, 70 black capes (each with a yellow eye of fire sewed on the center), more containers of provisions, a crate of 120 arrows, and a crate of 200 crossbow bolts.

The capes matter: see area 24.

### 21. CORRIDOR AND CELLS

Two zombies are hiding behind the northernmost pillar, at the end of the hall. They move to attack any intruders approaching the entrance to area 22. One round after they are seen, another pair of zombies appears from the southernmost cubicle (nearest the stairs), attacking the party from the rear. Each of the remaining cubicles also contains two zombies, and one pair enters the engagement each round (12 zombies in all).

Try to corner the party in area 22. The zombies attack until slain or turned, of course. They have no treasure, but a peridot (50 gp) was long ago hidden behind a loose stone in the northernmost cell (the last to disgorge zombies). If this cell is searched, the loose piece in the wall is found with a **DC 13 Intelligence (Investigation)** check (one check per searcher per round).

- **Zombies (12):** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Zombie]]; hp 22 each. They act last in every round and resist piercing damage.

### 22. TORTURE CHAMBER

At first glance this place seems long abandoned; dust, dirt, and cobwebs abound. A careful perusal shows, however, that several of the skeletons in the place are not old remnants, and some of the instruments and mechanisms have been used very recently indeed.

Only if exacting care is taken in inspecting the floor (a **DC 15 Intelligence (Investigation)** check, made only by characters who say they are searching the floor) can some drops of blood be noticed, leading south (the trail of a sloppy ghoul). Without this clue the secret door in the pillar takes a **DC 20 Wisdom (Perception)** check to find; each character looking gets one check only. If the trail is discovered, the secret door is found with a normal **DC 15** check.

Inside the pillar is a shaft some 30 feet deep, with iron rungs set into the stonework for easy descent. The rungs are obviously used with some frequency.

### 23. LITTERED STOREROOM

The mess of filth and broken junk is utterly unremarkable. The door opens easily, and makes no noise. If the characters are cautious as they open the door, a **DC 12 Wisdom (Perception)** check notes the grease on the door hinges.

### 24. LARGE ROOM

Lubash, an ogre, has been lured here and into service by baubles and the promise of fresh human meat. He will immediately attack intruders who do not wear the symbol of the new master (the yellow eye of fire). Those properly clad must still make the secret sign (crossed arms before the face) to prevent the ogre's attack. A character who has seen the sign made can copy it with a **DC 10 Wisdom (Insight)** check.

Lubash will devour the slain at the first opportunity, tossing wounded and prisoners into room 25. He will pursue a fleeing party, but only as far as the stairs up (area 18).

The ogre has a heap of old clothing and skins for a bed, and a large wooden chest for his treasures—worthless glass beads, brass candlesticks and other trash, 823 cp, 46 sp, and 3 gp. However, by some trick of fate, a small-sized [[Cloak of Elvenkind (T1)|cloak of elvenkind]] is intermixed with the old carpeting and rags of his bedding.

- **Lubash:** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Lubash]] and [[Lubash]]; hp 59. Bardiche, reach 10 ft.

### 25. OAK DOOR

This portal is heavily barred, for this is the grisly pantry of Lubash the ogre. It currently houses a pair of humans who have undergone only mild torture, plus a badly beaten gnome upon whom Lubash intended to soon dine—a mere snack, and tough, but a nice change nonetheless.

If rescued, the humans will admit to being merchants. Both will promise large rewards for their release, vowing to send the monies to Hommlet as soon as they return to their homes in Dyvers. Four weeks after their release, a passing caravan will deliver 100 sp per merchant to the rescuers.

If the merchants are rescued, award **50 XP each**. If they die through the characters' choices, there is no award, and Hommlet hears of it. Apply the same principle to the gnome (**50 XP**).

The gnome was caught spying by some gnolls, and he too will ask to be freed, giving a plain [[Gnome's Iron Ring (T1)|iron ring]] to whomever first so agrees. Gnomes within 100 miles of the area recognize this ring as a sign that the bearer is an ally: the bearer has **advantage on Charisma checks** to make them friendly and helpful.

- **Merchants (2):** commoner; hp 4 each (currently 2). **Gnome:** commoner with *Gnome Cunning*; hp 4 (currently 1). See [[The Moathouse Prisoners]].

### 26. UNDETECTED TRAP

The party will not notice that a huge iron grate could fall from above at this point in the corridor. The underside is even with the tunnel ceiling 15 feet above, and is painted a grayish brown to blend with the stone. The trap's two triggers are false doors, described as area 28 but located separately.

- **Trap: falling grate.** Triggered by opening either false door (28). It deals no damage, but blocks the corridor. Lifting it takes a **DC 25 Strength (Athletics)** check; up to three creatures can help, and each helper with Strength 19 or higher adds +5 to the check. The clang of its fall is heard throughout the level.

**26a.** The secret door (DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) to find) leads to a winch, which is used to haul the grate back up.

### 27. RECRUITS

This is the lair of six bugbears, who have recently been recruited by the New Master (room 35). If any lights are shown in the chamber to the north, these creatures sneak forth quietly to investigate. They are hidden, and their *Surprise Attack* applies if they catch the party unaware.

The bugbears know only that the Master is quartered somewhere to the north. Each has 5 gp. The leader (the toughest) wears a bracelet, which is actually a silver necklace set with five chrysoprases (250 gp for the piece).

- **Bugbears (6):** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Bugbear]]; hp 27 each. For four characters, use four bugbears (see [[T1-00 Introduction#Adjusting the Moathouse]]).

### 28. DOOR

This portal is a fake. When pulled open, a bare stone wall is revealed behind. At the same time, a distant clangor sounds (the grate, falling in area 26).

### 29. ODD SIDE ROOM

Herein is the lair of nine gnolls recruited by the New Master (area 35). They attack intruders, fighting fiercely and to the death if necessary. However, they are open to a parley, for they are not satisfied with their lot. They have lost six members while raiding to the east and south of the ruins, are dissatisfied with their loot, and are irate about the greater status accorded the newly recruited bugbears (area 27).

**Parley.** If an offer to parley is made, a **DC 8 Charisma (Persuasion)** check makes them stop and listen; the check has advantage if the offer is coin. The gnolls speak Gnoll, Goblin (bugbear), and Giant (ogre), but no Common.
- If offered 6 or more gp each (triple for the leader, 18 gp), they desert their current station.
- If double this fee is offered and paid, they lead the characters to the north passage toward the Master's room, and even tell of human guards.
- Either way, the gnolls then proceed west to the exit and never return. Award their full XP if they desert.

Each gnoll has 3d6 sp and 1d6 gp. The leader has 11 gp and a pretty lump of blue quartz worth 10 gp.

- **Gnolls (9):** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Gnoll]]; hp 22 each. Two have longbows, each with 20 arrows.

### 30. POOL

When the dungeons were dug, this natural pool of water was found. As it is considerably below floor level, the area was dug lower and the entrance to the pool enlarged. Some connection to an underground stream must exist, for some time ago, a giant crayfish took the pool as its own. It now disputes with any creatures entering, hoping to make a meal in the bargain. (The men-at-arms from area 33 hold it at bay by prodding with polearms; all others take their chances.)

The monster has been fairly successful, and there are bones and litter in and around the pool. If badly wounded (below half its hit points), the crayfish retreats into a very deep central section of the pool, and does not return for 24 or more hours.

Near the back (northeast) edge of the pool are 17 cp, 30 sp, 19 gp, and a normal longsword. In the water on a ledge is a platinum pin set with a smallish ruby (worth 250 gp) and a bone tube. The pin is under a skull, and the tube rests under some bones. Unless searchers use their hands, each treasure slips off the ledge and is lost below unless the searcher succeeds on a **DC 10 Dexterity check**. The tube is watertight and contains [[Spell Scrolls, Crayfish Pool (T1)|three wizard spell scrolls]] (*thunderwave*, *stinking cloud*, *fly*), but does not float.

- **Giant crayfish:** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Giant Crayfish]]; hp 45.

### 31. BURIAL CRYPTS

The builders of the dungeon planned for a long tenancy (though their expectations were not met). Most of the niches are empty, but a few contain splintered coffins, wrappings, and gnawed and split bones (a sure sign that all is not well).

Squatting in the north end of the room are four ghouls, who attack as soon as they see intruders. These creatures get corpses to eat from the Master—and sometimes even prey on those not yet dead (from area 22), so they can have some "sport" before dining. They have no treasure (not here, at least).

- **Ghouls (4):** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Ghoul]]; hp 22 each. Elves are immune to their paralysis.

**31a.** The back of a crypt is broken out, leading to an earth tunnel, which is foul and damp and only about 5 feet high. The tunnel leads to a noisome den (A) where a heap of bones and skulls indicates the ghouls' nest. Their treasure is scattered in the mess—800 sp, seven vials of holy water, a [[Potion of Undead Control (T1)|potion of undead control]], and a [[Scroll of Protection (Undead) (T1)|scroll of protection from undead]]. A trail of gold pieces (1d4 per 10 feet of tunnel) begins at area B, leading generally eastward.

**31b.** A warren of small burrows (3 feet in diameter and smaller) starts here. Mapping is not possible. After about 30 feet of travel, the trail of gold ends. If the characters follow the tunnels off the mapped portion, allow them to proceed another hundred feet, and then tell them they are hopelessly lost.

### 32. PASSAGE

The corridor here begins to slant gradually upwards, and a slight breeze can be felt after 100 feet beyond the edge of the map. After another 300 feet, the tunnel ends in a narrow opening amidst rocks, thick briars and brambles—open air.

### 33. ROOM AND CORRIDOR

At each position marked X stands a guard, garbed all in black with gold eyes of fire embroidered upon tunic and cloak. These humans are the alert and fearless guards of the "New Master" (room 35). Their sergeant is usually in area 33, but he patrols the whole area regularly.

When attacked, a guard sends up a low hooting sound while engaging. This brings the others, plus reinforcements from area 34 (six more guards with spears, and their sergeant). The reinforcements take 1d2 + 3 rounds to arrive: to ready themselves, one round to move to area 33, and another to reach the north passage (if needed there).

If the guards are losing the fray, they start howling. This brings all guards in the area, and the Master as well (on the same timing). See [[#The Alarm]].

Each guardsman has 2d4 sp and 1d6 gp. The sergeant has double those amounts of coin, and wears a gold neckchain of office (worth 25 gp).

- **Guardsmen (6):** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Lareth's Guardsmen]]; guard; AC 16 (scale mail and shield); hp 11 each; longsword, dagger, light crossbow and 30 bolts.
- **Sergeant:** thug; AC 18 (chain and shield); hp 32; morningstar, warhammer, dagger.

### 34. BARRACKS CHAMBER

Quartered here are 12 guards and two sergeants, all armed and armored as those in area 33, except that six guards bear spears instead of crossbows. These six and their sergeant are the reinforcements arriving in area 33 if fighting occurs. Also here is the New Master's lieutenant, an evil hero. He stays near his liege lord always, and will accompany him wherever he goes.

In the 20-by-40-foot room to the northeast are supplies for the guards—salted meat, weak wine in a barrel, water, hard biscuits, dried fruit, and so forth. There are also mounds of fresh rushes and grass, for bedding and for two riding horses stabled at the north end of the place. Saddles, tack, and a lance are nearby.

The guards' pockets are as at area 33, and each sergeant wears a gold neckchain (25 gp). The lieutenant has 29 gems in his purse, small stones worth 10 gp each (three amethysts, eight pieces of fine coral, nine red garnets, six pearls, and three tourmalines), and wears a platinum chain of office in which a topaz is prominently set (250 gp for the piece).

- **Guards (12):** as area 33 (six with spears instead of crossbows).
- **Sergeants (2):** as area 33.
- **Lieutenant:** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#The Lieutenant]] and [[Lareth's Lieutenant]]; AC 20 (plate and shield); hp 58; longsword, longbow, handaxe, dagger.

### 35. CHAMBER OF THE NEW MASTER

The Master's chamber is lavishly furnished, with thick rugs, wall hangings, and soft chairs, couch, and cushions. Wines, liquors, and dishes of sweetmeats abound. A brazier continually burns, warming the place and sending a fragrant incense into the air. Silver serving pieces and goblets (worth 250 gp for all) and an alabaster box filled with unguents (total value 25 gp) are in plain sight. Hidden in a cabinet (DC 15 Wisdom (Perception) to find) is an exquisite piece of jewelry, made with matched fire opals on a gold chain and settings with diamond chips (750 gp for the piece). The New Master, Lareth, is described more fully below. He carries but 9 pp and one small black opal (100 gp). On his left arm is bound a [[Phylactery of Action (T1)|phylactery of action]], a clerical magic item which makes the wearer impervious to paralysis of any sort, including all *hold* spells and similar powers.

Lareth will aid his guards' melee with his spells, keeping a defensive posture while commanding his troops' attacks. He will melee if necessary, and probably after using his spells. He wields a mace in his left hand and a [[Staff of Striking (T1)|staff of striking]] in his right (10 charges; he can spend up to 3 on a hit for extra force damage). He wears [[Plate Armor +1 (T1)|plate armor +1]].

If seriously threatened, Lareth will offer all his nonmagical treasures—jewelry, coins, and all else—as ransom for his life. (If the characters accept, award his full XP.) If Lareth is slain, there will be cautious and discreet inquiries in Hommlet, as the servants of evil therein make every attempt to find out what happened. If any of Lareth's possessions turn up, the slayers are revealed. An assassin who can speak the languages of the individuals, as well as those of major allies, will come to the village in **3d6 + 2 days** to eliminate the offender(s). DM, handle this as you see fit. Jaroo and Terjon of Hommlet will certainly aid those attacked. If the assassin is killed, nothing further will be attempted.

- **Lareth:** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Lareth the Beautiful]] and [[Lareth the Beautiful]]; AC 19; hp 58.
- **The avenger:** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#The Avenger]].

### Lareth the Beautiful

Lareth the Beautiful is the dark hope of chaotic evil—young, handsome, well endowed in abilities and aptitudes, thoroughly wicked, depraved, and capricious. Whoever harms Lareth had best not brag of it in the presence of one who will inform the Demoness Lolth!

Lareth has been sent to this area to rebuild a force of men and humanoid fighters to gather loot and restore the Temple of Elemental Evil to its former glory. He is but one of many so charged, of course, but is looked upon with special favor and expectation. He and his minions have been careful to raid far from this area, never nearer than three or four leagues, travelling on foot or riding in wagons of the traders from Hommlet. None of the victims are ever left alive to tell the tale, and mysterious disappearances are all that can be remarked upon. No trace of men, mounts, goods, wagons or draft animals is ever found.

Evil to the core, Lareth is cunning. If a situation appears in doubt, he uses bribery and honeyed words to sway the balance to his favor. He is not averse to gaining new recruits of all sorts, and will gladly accept adventurers into the ranks (though he will test and try them continually). Those who arouse suspicion will be quietly murdered in their sleep. Those with too much promise will be likewise dealt with, for Lareth wants no potential usurpers or threats to his domination.
