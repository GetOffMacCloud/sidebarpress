# Part 1A: The Ruins of the Moathouse — Upper Level

> The shattered outpost of the Temple in the marsh, five leagues from Hommlet. Brigands squat in the old lord's Black Chamber, and vermin hold the rest. Below, unknown to all, the New Master is rebuilding. **Map 7.** Party level **1–2**.

## What Hommlet Knows

The following information may be gleaned, piece by piece, through conversation with the villagers of Hommlet. Old-timers know far more of the tale than newcomers, and certain special individuals (notably those on the Town Council) could relate the whole story as found here, given sufficient provocation and trust in those inquiring. It is unlikely that such trust will be cause for revelation even so, unless seriously interested characters attend a special Council meeting for the express purpose of aiding the village through their explorations.

The appended boxed information is to be read as the characters head for the ruins.

This place was once the outpost of the Temple of Elemental Evil, its watchtower and an advance base for raids, looting, and destruction. From this area, servants of the Temple were to bring the Village of Hommlet and all the lands around it into subjection. The conquered folk were then to be used as slaves to construct yet another fortress further west, spreading the evil power of the Temple in ever-growing rings to encompass all of the land around its base.

This outpost was ignored during the destruction of the Temple, for the army of good which came against the wicked hordes was so strong as to be totally immune to any pinpricking from the garrison of the moathouse. Only after the end of the battle which destroyed the main armies of the Temple of Elemental Evil was attention turned to this place. A detachment of horse and foot with a small siege train then came to the marshlands, to lay the castle low. The common folk from miles around came to help, and the moathouse was surrounded, cut off, and battered into extinction. The place is now shunned by the people of Hommlet, who hate its former evil and the memory of the terror brought to them by the black lord of the fortress—a vile cleric of damnation—and his evil men and humanoid troops.

## The Approach

> A scrub of thorns, thistles, weeds, and shrubs grows thickly along the edge of the track which leads to the ruins. Even the track is mostly overgrown and cluttered with fallen branches and trees. Here and there it is washed out, in other places a mire.
>
> Some game evidently still follows the pathway, however, for after a mile or so faint traces can be seen. But even considering this, going is slow, and it takes some five hours to reach the place on horseback, or the whole of a long day to trudge along on foot. Considerable hacking and clearing is necessary to make the way passable, so double the time required for the first trip. After a dozen miles, as the track turns more northerly, the land begins to sink and become boggy. Tall marsh plants grow thickly where cattails and tamaracks do not. Off to the left can be seen the jagged silhouette of the moathouse.
>
> A side path, banked high to cross over the wetland to either side, juts north to the entrance of the ruin. The track here is only about 15 feet wide or so, with crumbling embankments making travel near the edge dangerous. The bogs stink. The vegetation appears dense and prolific, but somehow sickly and unhealthy, creepers and vines throwing their strangling loops over the skeletons of dead saplings and living bushes alike. The rushes and cattails rustle and bend even to a slight zephyr, and weird birdcalls, croakings, and other unwholesome sounds come faintly across the fen.

The track continues past the ruins for many miles—seven leagues, in fact, to Nulb. (Map 1 governs these distances; see [[T1-00 Introduction#Development Notes]].)

## Random Encounters (Upper Level)

Check once every **10 minutes** in all areas: an encounter occurs on a **1 on a d12**. Roll a d6 for the type. These random encounters do not deplete the numbers of others in the ruins. References to encounter areas are given for aid in handling of the type.

| d6 | Encounter |
|---|---|
| 1 | Giant rats (1d4 + 1), or 1 swarm of rats (see area 13) |
| 2 | Scraping noise (shifting materials above) |
| 3 | Giant tick overhead (see area 16); it is hidden and has advantage on its Stealth check |
| 4 | Squeaking and rustling (rats in the floor underneath) |
| 5 | Brigands (1d4 + 1 bandits, reinforcements for area 7) |
| 6 | Footsteps (the party's own, a trick of echoes) |

**Statistics:** brigands are **bandits** ([[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Brigands]]); giant rats and swarms of rats with the filth fever rider ([[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Giant Rats and Swarms of Rats]]); the [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Giant Tick (Moathouse)]].

## Upper Level Encounter Key

### 1. POOL

The following encounter should challenge a party of four or five 1st-level characters. If fewer characters are present, or if they are weak, the DM should decrease the number of monsters accordingly, first omitting one of the larger creatures, then the smaller, leaving one large monster at all times. Do not, however, add to the number. If the characters are all 2nd or higher level, convert small monsters to large specimens. (See also [[T1-00 Introduction#Adjusting the Moathouse]].)

Six giant frogs lurk in the tall weeds near the edge of the pool. They have **advantage on their Dexterity (Stealth) checks** to surprise intruders. These nasty things leap from their lurking places to attack, and they attack madly, for they are voraciously hungry at all times.

**Mounts.** If characters are mounted, each rider makes a **DC 15 Wisdom (Animal Handling)** check to keep the mount from panicking (a warhorse panics only on a 1 or 2 on a d20; no check needed). If a mount panics, check two things for it: the rider makes a **DC 10 Dexterity save** or is thrown (1d6 bludgeoning damage, prone), and the mount plunges into the marsh and becomes **mired** on a roll of 1–3 on a d6 (a DC 12 Strength (Athletics) check by the mount, with help, pulls it free). Both may happen to one victim.

**Tongues.** Each of the two larger frogs has a tongue 15 feet long; the smaller tongues are a mere 10 feet. If its prey is more than 5 feet away, a frog uses its tongue to attack (+5 to hit, no damage). Any victim hit by the tongue may attack it (AC 11), and if any damage is dealt the frog withdraws it and does not use it thereafter. Otherwise, the victim is pulled in 10 feet (escape DC 11). A victim weighing more than the frog is pulled only 5 feet, and gains another chance to strike the tongue. Each of the small frogs weighs about 100 pounds; each of the larger, 200.

For added fun, each large frog can swallow a Small creature, using its *Swallow* action (MM). A swallowed creature can attack the frog from inside; if the frog dies, the victim crawls out.

The largest frog has swallowed an amethyst (worth 100 gp), but the others are penniless.

- **Giant frogs (2):** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Giant Frog]]; hp 18 each.
- **Giant frogs, small (4):** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Giant Frog, Small]]; hp 9 each.

### 2. ROTTING DRAWBRIDGE

When the moathouse was taken, part of the drawbridge was battered through, and men crossed on planks to gain access to the inner gates. The chains of the drawbridge were broken, and it fell; it has remained thus since. It is strong enough to bear a man, but any mount crossing over may break through and injure a leg. Roll a d10: a horse breaks through on 1–5, a mule on 1–3, and a pony on 1–2. The injury reduces the mount's hit point maximum by a third (rounded up) and halves its speed and carrying capacity, until it receives magical healing.

### 3. BROKEN GATES

> The door is hanging open on one great hinge, the other is splintered and holed but still in position, wedged and shored closed from the inside.

A careful examination of the ground here (DC 10 Wisdom (Survival)) reveals boot prints in the ground, heading for the building steps at area 5.

It is 50% likely that a pair of brigands (from area 7) are here, see the party, and retreat to their lair, giving warning to the group. If so, the party will be aware of none of this, unless using magical or other invisible advance scouting (a familiar, for instance).

### 4. TOWER

> The upper portion of the tower has collapsed, and the interior is dark.

Even if an arrow slit is peered through, darkvision will reveal nothing. The occupant lurks above. A scattering of husks and a few bones lie on the floor, possibly giving warning to the wary, but the gleam of coins therein may tempt investigation.

A huge spider is crouched on the remains of an upper floor some 15 feet overhead. It leaps (up to 30 feet) and bites as soon as anyone steps into the tower. It is hidden: it makes its Dexterity (Stealth) check with **advantage, +5**, against the party's passive Perception.

Amidst the rubbish are 71 cp and 38 sp, and an ivory box about the size of a large book (worth 25 gp to the right buyer).

- **Huge spider:** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Huge Spider]]; hp 26.

### 5. LITTERED STEPS

This staircase leads to the house portion of the fortress. A careful examination of these steps (DC 10 Wisdom (Survival)) reveals that some traffic (human, animal, and perhaps otherwise) comes this way. The doors at the head of the stairs are broken—one flat on the floor, the other sundered.

### 6. GREAT HALL

> This was once a great audience chamber, as shown by the tattered banners and tapestries on the walls, destroyed furniture, and heaps of rotting cloth thrown into corners. Once richly appointed, it has been thoroughly searched, sacked, and despoiled. Leaves and dirt cover the floor, and cobwebs hang from walls and the ceiling above. Looking up, you see that pieces of beams and chunks of stone poke through, indicating that the upper stories of the place are totally destroyed and likely to be impassable to anything larger than a rat.

### 7. BRIGANDS

Holed up in this "Black Chamber," the quarters of the former Lord of the castle, are eight brigands and their leader. The heavy door has been repaired by these outlaws, and it is usually barred and bolted as well (DC 15 Strength to force; AC 15, 25 hp). They are not aware of the secret door nor the stairway down.

The chamber is floored in black flagstones, and has ebon-colored wall hangings (now burnt and tattered) in addition to the jumbled wreckage noted. The brigands pitch bedrolls in odd corners, and the remains of fires can be noted in the northern fireplace.

If brigand sentries were at area 3 when the party approached (a 50% chance), they have retreated here, and the others are warned of the party's approach. At least one brigand always watches the escape route (the ruined wall in the southeast corner, and the stepping stones across the moat).

The brigands' actions vary by the party's apparent strength:
- **Weak:** The brigands hide in the clutter of wrecked furniture. They make their Dexterity (Stealth) checks with **advantage, +5**.
- **Capable:** The brigands lock their door, and leave if it is battered. They take only their petty loot, leaving the rest safely hidden.
- **Overwhelming:** The brigands sneak out as soon as the party is detected, taking all of their loot and heading for greener pastures.

- **Leader:** thug ([[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Brigands]]); AC 18 (chain and shield); hp 32; spear or longsword.
- **Aide:** bandit; AC 16 (ring mail and shield); hp 11; longsword or flail.
- **Others (7):** bandit; hp 11 each; armor and weapons below.

| Armor | Weapons | AC |
|---|---|---|
| Padded & shield | longsword, 3 javelins | 13 |
| Studded leather | glaive (1e halberd) | 12 |
| Leather & shield | spear, handaxe | 13 |
| Leather | light crossbow, longsword | 11 |
| Leather | light crossbow, club | 11 |
| Leather | light crossbow, mace | 11 |
| Leather | glaive (1e voulge) | 11 |

**Treasure:** Each brigand has 3d6 sp and 1d6 gp. The aide has an additional 1d6 ep. The leader has 1d4 pp and a citrine (50 gp), and wears a gold chain worth 25 gp.

The brigands have buried a chest under the rubble where the wall collapsed. Thirty minutes of digging can uncover the loot. The chest holds 2,000 cp, two bolts of fine cloth worth 25 gp each, a crystal flagon and four goblets (the set worth 25 gp), an inlaid wooden box with ivory handles and decorations (25 gp), and four [[Arrows +1 (T1)|arrows +1]].

### 8. STAIRWAY UP

After a few steps the risers are covered with dust, crushed stone, broken wood, and burned heaps of cloth. At the landing halfway up they are completely choked with rubble, and it is impossible to clear the loose stone and huge wall section to allow passage. The entire upper floor is collapsed and ruined.

### 9. LITTERED ROOM

This room was once a conference chamber, but is now empty. Its dirt and wreckage show no signs of any recent occupant, though a couple of small rats are seen scurrying away. There is nothing of any value within except a very fine longsword (1e broadsword; worth 50 gp, not magical) wedged behind a splintered wall case, the contents of which are long gone.

### 10. EMPTY BEDCHAMBER

Once the quarters of a castle troop leader or some other petty official, this place is now a total wreck. The bed is chopped to pieces, and the furniture is smashed or missing.

### 11. SALON

This room was once very opulent, obviously a place of many expensive furnishings. The remaining bits and tatters still appear rich (though none are of value). If the party investigates the room, scores of bats flutter angrily around. In the confusion, each creature holding a light source must make a **DC 13 Dexterity save** or drop it, and a flame goes out. In the darkness, anyone who misses a bat with a melee attack hits an adjacent ally instead on an **11 or higher on a d20**; if that ally was behind the attacker, it takes an extra weapon die of damage.

- **Swarms of bats (2):** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Swarm of Bats]]; hp 22 each.

### 12. CORNER ROOM

After one of their number was slain here a few weeks ago, the brigands gave this room and the entry corridor a wide berth. A huge adder, over 12 feet long, dwells here. It crawled up the rubble spilled into the moat, and found a nice safe lair where it could hole up after hunting. In the litter of its nesting place is a jeweled dagger worth 250 gp.

- **Giant snake:** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Elder Adder]]; hp 33.

### 13. STOREROOM AND STAIRWAY

Some rustling and squeaking will betray the presence of a hungry horde of giant rats. Any bite may inflict disease (DC 10 Constitution save; see the bestiary). These rodents live on the shelves along the walls, and some lurk around the staircase. They are hungry and attack without fear. Though they have no treasure, an overlooked jug of oil (10 flasks' worth) is on the upper shelf of the storage area, and on the fifth stair down is a gold ring worth 25 gp. However, if the party does not search, the ring will probably pass unnoticed: a character using the stairs notices it only with a **passive Perception of 18** or higher, or with a **DC 18 Wisdom (Perception)** check if searching.

- **Swarms of rats (2):** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Giant Rats and Swarms of Rats]]; hp 24 each.
- **Giant rats (3):** hp 7 each.

### 14. TROPHY ROOM

Only a few mangy pelts, stuffed heads, and shattered antlers indicate the former status of the chamber. All worthwhile items are looted. Considerable time may be spent searching the litter herein, but nothing of value will be found.

### 15. DOMICILE

This place was the domicile of the majordomo of the castle, but is now stripped of everything save broken and ruined furnishings. One wall cresset remains near the outer wall, and its nondescript torch stub is a silver baton worth 25 gp.

### 16. KITCHEN

The remains of moldering foodstuffs and kitchen work tables are visible. A wooden cask near the fireplace on the north wall looks inviting, but anyone approaching it is attacked by a giant tick! If the bite hits, the victim takes the bite's damage and then loses 1d6 hit points at the start of each of the tick's turns from blood drain, with no attack roll needed. This horror came in through the chimney. No treasure is here.

- **Giant tick:** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Giant Tick (Moathouse)]]; hp 22.

### 17. BARRACKS

A giant lizard is in the south end of the room, near an intact chest. The monster attacks anyone invading its lair. It has previously swallowed a [[Shield +1 (T1)|shield +1]], easily found if appropriate actions are taken after the battle. The chest holds 33 cp, a scale mail tunic, a light crossbow, and a case of 24 quarrels.

In this partially collapsed chamber, the remains of cots and plain wooden chests indicate that this was once a barracks room for castle guards—undoubtedly the human sort.

- **Trap: falling rubble.** If the overhead rubble is forcibly moved, it may fall further (50% chance; check for each round of disturbance). Each creature in the room makes a **DC 12 Dexterity save**, taking **11 (2d10) bludgeoning damage** on a failure, or half as much on a success.
- **Giant lizard:** [[T1 - Bestiary (5e)#Giant Lizard]]; hp 19.
